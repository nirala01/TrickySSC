Series — Arithmetic & Geometric Progressions

Concepts, formulas, solved examples and practice · Quantitative Aptitude

Every AP or GP question uses one of a handful of formulas. Know which one fits — nth term, number of terms or sum — and the rest is substitution.

1. Concepts in brief

How to recognise the series

Subtract each term from the next. If the gap is always the same, it is an arithmetic progression (AP) and the gap is the common difference d. If instead you divide each term by the one before and always get the same number, it is a geometric progression (GP) and that number is the common ratio r.

3, 7, 11, 15 → gaps all 4 → AP with d = 4.   2, 6, 18, 54 → each term × 3 → GP with r = 3.

Why the AP sum formula works

Write the series forwards and backwards and add them term by term. Every pair adds to (first + last), and there are n pairs, so twice the sum = n(first + last). That is why Sₙ = n/2 × (a + l).

When a GP has an infinite sum

If the ratio is a fraction between −1 and 1, the terms keep shrinking towards zero, so the total settles at a fixed value: a/(1 − r). If |r| ≥ 1 the terms do not shrink and there is no finite sum.

3. Shortcuts that save time

  • Number of terms: n = (l − a)/d + 1. Multiples of 7 between 100 and 500: first 105, last 497 → (497 − 105)/7 + 1 = 57.
  • Sum = number of terms × middle term in any AP (the middle term is the average).
  • From the sum to a term: Tₙ = Sₙ − Sₙ₋₁.
  • Swap rule: if the pth term is q and the qth term is p, then the (p+q)th term is 0.
  • AM ≥ GM for positive numbers, equal only when the numbers are equal.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 Sum of all two-digit numbers divisible by 3?
12, 15, …, 99 → n = (99 − 12)/3 + 1 = 30. Sum = 30/2 × (12 + 99) = 15 × 111 = 1665.
Example 2 3 + 6 + 12 + … up to 8 terms = 3(2⁸ − 1)/(2 − 1) = 3 × 255 = 765.
Example 3 11² + 12² + … + 20² = (sum to 20) − (sum to 10) = 2870 − 385 = 2485.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 4 The 7th term of an AP is 20 and the 12th term is 35. Five steps of d add 15, so d = 3 and a = 20 − 6 × 3 = 2. The 20th term = 2 + 19 × 3 = 59.
Example 5 Sum of the first 20 terms of 5, 9, 13, …: 20/2 × (2 × 5 + 19 × 4) = 10 × 86 = 860.
Example 6 1 + 1/3 + 1/9 + … to infinity = 1/(1 − 1/3) = 3/2.
Example 7 A GP has 3rd term 12 and 6th term 96. Three steps of r multiply by 8, so r = 2 and a = 3. The 8th term = 3 × 2⁷ = 384.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. Find the 15th term of 3, 7, 11, …

Answer: 59 (a = 3, d = 4: 3 + 14 × 4).

Q2. How many terms are there in 7, 11, 15, …, 139?

Answer: 34 ((139 − 7)/4 + 1).

Q3. Find 1 + 2 + 3 + … + 50.

Answer: 1275 (50 × 51/2).

Q4. Find 1³ + 2³ + … + 10³.

Answer: 3025 ((10 × 11/2)² = 55²).

Q5. Find the geometric mean of 4 and 25.

Answer: 10 (√100).

6. Traps to avoid

  1. Forgetting the +1 when counting terms.
  2. "Between 100 and 500" excludes the end points; "from 100 to 500" includes them.
  3. The infinite-GP formula works only when |r| < 1.
  4. Sum of the first n odd numbers is n²; of the first n even numbers it is n(n+1) — don't swap them.
  5. In a GP with r < 1, use a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r) to keep the signs positive.
