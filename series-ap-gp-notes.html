Every AP or GP question uses one of a handful of formulas. Know which one fits — nth term, number of terms or sum — and the rest is substitution.

1. Key formulas

2. Shortcuts that save time

  • Number of terms: n = (l − a)/d + 1. Multiples of 7 between 100 and 500: first 105, last 497 → (497 − 105)/7 + 1 = 57.
  • Sum = number of terms × middle term in any AP (the middle term is the average).
  • From the sum to a term: Tₙ = Sₙ − Sₙ₋₁.
  • Swap rule: if the pth term is q and the qth term is p, then the (p+q)th term is 0.
  • AM ≥ GM for positive numbers, equal only when the numbers are equal.

3. Solved examples

Example 1 Sum of all two-digit numbers divisible by 3?
12, 15, …, 99 → n = (99 − 12)/3 + 1 = 30. Sum = 30/2 × (12 + 99) = 15 × 111 = 1665.
Example 2 3 + 6 + 12 + … up to 8 terms = 3(2⁸ − 1)/(2 − 1) = 3 × 255 = 765.
Example 3 11² + 12² + … + 20² = (sum to 20) − (sum to 10) = 2870 − 385 = 2485.

4. Traps to avoid

  1. Forgetting the +1 when counting terms.
  2. "Between 100 and 500" excludes the end points; "from 100 to 500" includes them.
  3. The infinite-GP formula works only when |r| < 1.
  4. Sum of the first n odd numbers is n²; of the first n even numbers it is n(n+1) — don't swap them.
  5. In a GP with r < 1, use a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r) to keep the signs positive.
