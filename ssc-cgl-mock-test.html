The SSC CGL Tier-I examination provides limited time to solve a large number of questions. Mock tests train candidates to distribute time efficiently across all sections. Analyse Performance

Performance analysis is equally important as attempting the test itself. After every mock test, review incorrect answers, understand the solutions, and identify recurring mistakes. Continuous analysis leads to steady improvement in scores over time.

SSC CGL Tier-II Exam Pattern 2026

The SSC CGL Tier-II Examination is conducted in multiple papers depending on the post applied for. Paper-I is compulsory for all candidates, while Paper-II is conducted only for candidates applying for the Junior Statistical Officer (JSO) post.

  • Paper-I is mandatory for all candidates.
  • The Computer Knowledge Test (Module-III) is qualifying in nature, but candidates should prepare it seriously as it is compulsory.
  • There is negative marking in objective-type papers: 1 mark for every incorrect answer in Mathematical Abilities, Reasoning, English, and General Awareness, and 0.50 mark for every incorrect answer in the Computer Knowledge Test.

  • Candidates applying for other SSC CGL posts are not required to appear for Paper-II.

How to Use SSC CGL Mock Tests Effectively

Simply attempting mock tests is not enough. Candidates should follow a structured strategy to maximise the benefits of every test.

  1. Step 1: Complete the basic concepts of all subjects before starting full-length mock tests.
  2. Step 2: Attempt one mock test without interruptions and follow the actual examination time limit.
  3. Step 3: Analyse every incorrect answer carefully. Understanding mistakes is more valuable than simply checking the score.
  4. Step 4: Maintain an error notebook containing formulas, grammar rules, reasoning concepts, and factual mistakes.
  5. Step 5: Revise weak topics before attempting the next mock test.
  6. Step 6: Compare your scores regularly to measure improvement in speed and accuracy.

Consistent practice combined with detailed analysis produces far better results than attempting a large number of tests without reviewing mistakes.
