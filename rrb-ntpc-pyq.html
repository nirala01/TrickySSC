What is RRB NTPC?

RRB NTPC (Railway Recruitment Board, Non-Technical Popular Categories) is the recruitment of the Indian Railways for its non-technical posts. It is notified in two parts: the Graduate level, for posts such as Goods Train Manager, Station Master, Chief Commercial-cum-Ticket Supervisor, Senior Clerk-cum-Typist and Junior Accounts Assistant-cum-Typist, and the Undergraduate (12th pass) level, for posts such as Commercial-cum-Ticket Clerk, Accounts Clerk-cum-Typist, Junior Clerk-cum-Typist and Trains Clerk.

The exam is among the largest in the country by number of applicants, and the first stage, CBT-1, is held in many shifts spread over several weeks. Because marks are normalised across shifts and CBT-1 only screens candidates for CBT-2, a few marks decide who moves ahead — which is exactly what previous year paper practice protects.

RRB NTPC Exam Pattern

First Stage Computer Based Test (CBT-1)

SubjectQuestionsMarks

⏱ Duration: 90 minutes (120 minutes for eligible PwBD candidates with a scribe)

Key highlights:

  • Objective-type computer based test, offered in English, Hindi and the regional languages listed in the notification.
  • 100 questions carrying 100 marks, 1 mark each, in one mixed section — there is no sectional timing.
  • Negative marking of one-third of a mark for each incorrect answer.
  • Marks are normalised across shifts. CBT-1 is a screening stage: its marks shortlist candidates for CBT-2 and are not counted in the final merit.

Stages after CBT-1

  • Second Stage CBT (CBT-2): 120 questions in 90 minutes — General Awareness 50, Mathematics 35, General Intelligence & Reasoning 35 — with the same one-third negative marking. The final merit is prepared from CBT-2 marks.
  • Skill test: Computer Based Aptitude Test for Station Master and Traffic Assistant, and a Computer Based Typing Skill Test (qualifying) for the typist posts.
  • Document verification and medical examination according to the medical standard of the post.

RRB NTPC Syllabus

RRB NTPC CBT-1 tests candidates in three subjects, with General Awareness carrying the highest weight.

Mathematics

Number system, decimals and fractions, LCM and HCF, ratio and proportion, percentage, mensuration, time and work, time and distance, simple and compound interest, profit and loss, elementary algebra, geometry and trigonometry, and elementary statistics. Maths PYQs

General Intelligence & Reasoning

Analogies, completion of number and alphabetical series, coding and decoding, mathematical operations, similarities and differences, relationships, analytical reasoning, syllogism, jumbling, Venn diagrams, puzzles, data sufficiency, statement-conclusion, statement-courses of action, decision making, maps and interpretation of graphs. Reasoning PYQs

General Awareness

Current events of national and international importance, games and sports, art and culture of India, Indian literature, monuments and places of India, general science and life science (up to Class 10), history of India and the freedom struggle, physical, social and economic geography of India and the world, Indian polity and governance, scientific and technological developments including the space and nuclear programme of India, UN and other important world organisations, environmental issues, basics of computers and computer applications, common abbreviations, transport systems in India, Indian economy, famous personalities, flagship government programmes, flora and fauna of India and important government and public sector organisations. GK PYQs

RRB NTPC Preparation Strategy

A workable RRB NTPC strategy combines concept building, daily General Awareness revision, previous year paper practice and full-length mock tests.

Finish the basics first, then move to previous year papers to learn how the questions are actually framed. The paper gives you 54 seconds per question on average, and General Awareness alone is 40 of the 100 questions — so the time saved on GA is the time you get for Maths. With one-third negative marking, a blind guess among four options does not pay; attempt a doubtful question only when you can rule out at least one option.

A recommended approach:

  1. Complete the three-subject syllabus topic-wise.
  2. Give General Awareness and current affairs thirty minutes every day; static GK and general science are the most repeated areas.
  3. Solve RRB NTPC previous year papers consistently, newest shifts first.
  4. Attempt a full-length mock test every week, then one a day in the final three weeks.
  5. Analyse every mistake after each test — concept gap, careless slip or time loss.
  6. Fix accuracy before chasing attempts.
  7. Decide your order of attempt in practice: the questions are mixed, so use the palette to move between subjects instead of going strictly from Q1 to Q100.

Consistent practice and honest analysis usually contribute more to a selection than starting yet another new book.
