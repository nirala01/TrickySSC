Partnership

Profit is shared in the ratio of capital × time. If everyone invests for the same time, it is simply the ratio of capital. If capital is the same, it is the ratio of time.

1. Concepts in brief

Simple and compound partnership

In a simple partnership all partners invest for the same period, so profit is shared only by capital. In a compound partnership the periods differ, so profit is shared by capital × time — what is often called "capital-months".

Why capital × time

₹1000 kept in a business for 12 months does as much work as ₹2000 for 6 months: both equal 12,000 rupee-months. Profit rewards this combined contribution, so it is split in the ratio of the products.

Working and sleeping partners

A working partner manages the business and may take a salary or a share of profit first. The remaining profit is then split by capital × time among all partners, including him. A sleeping partner only puts in money.

2. Key rules

3. Shortcuts that save time

Always convert to capital-months A puts in ₹5000 for 12 months = 60,000. B puts in ₹6000 for 8 months = 48,000. Ratio 60 : 48 = 5 : 4.
  • When someone joins after x months, his time is (12 − x) months.
  • If A's investment is k times B's and A's time is m times B's, A's profit is km times B's.
  • A salary or commission is deducted before the remaining profit is divided.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 A invests ₹6000 and B invests ₹9000 for a year. Profit ₹7500 → ratio 2 : 3 → A gets ₹3000.
Example 2 A starts with ₹3500. B joins after 5 months with ₹4500. Ratio = 3500 × 12 : 4500 × 7 = 42000 : 31500 = 4 : 3.
Example 3 A, B, C invest ₹2000, ₹3000, ₹5000. C works and gets 10% of the profit as pay. Profit ₹8000: C's pay = 800; remaining 7200 split 2 : 3 : 5 → C gets 800 + 3600 = ₹4400.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 4 A, B and C invest ₹20,000, ₹30,000 and ₹50,000. After 6 months A takes out half his money. Year-end profit is ₹57,000. Capital-months: A = 20000 × 6 + 10000 × 6 = 1,80,000; B = 3,60,000; C = 6,00,000 → 3 : 6 : 10. One part = 3000, so A gets ₹9000, B ₹18,000, C ₹30,000.
Example 5 A invested for 8 months and B for 12 months; their profits are in the ratio 3 : 2. Their capitals are in the ratio (3/8) : (2/12) = 9 : 4.
Example 6 Capitals in the ratio ½ : ⅓ : ¼. Multiply by the LCM 12: 6 : 4 : 3.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. A puts ₹4000 for 12 months and B ₹6000 for 8 months. Profit ratio?

Answer: 1 : 1 (48,000 : 48,000).

Q2. A invests ₹5000 for 9 months and B ₹6000 for 6 months. Ratio?

Answer: 5 : 4 (45 : 36).

Q3. Profit ₹4500 is shared in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. C's share?

Answer: ₹2000.

Q4. A starts with ₹12,000; B joins after 3 months with ₹16,000. Ratio at year-end?

Answer: 1 : 1 (1,44,000 each).

Q5. Capitals are 2 : 3 and times 4 : 3. Profit ratio?

Answer: 8 : 9.

6. Traps to avoid

  1. Using capital alone when the time periods differ.
  2. Counting a late partner's time as 12 months — subtract the months before he joined.
  3. Splitting the whole profit by capital when a working partner's salary should come out first.
  4. Applying a mid-year withdrawal to the whole year instead of only the remaining months.
