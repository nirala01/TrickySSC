Every cloze question asks one thing: which word fits this blank in this passage? A cloze test is a short passage with five words taken out. For each blank there are four options, and the right one must fit the grammar of the sentence and the meaning of the whole passage — a word that sounds fine in the sentence alone can still be wrong because of what comes next.

Marks are lost in three places. The first is reading the sentence, not the passage — the clue for a blank is often in the next sentence, and skipping it lets a plausible wrong word through. The second is the look-alike — affect and effect, adapt and adopt, eminent and imminent. The third is the linking word: although, therefore, however and unless each set up a different relation between two ideas, and the wrong one turns the passage’s logic upside down.

What You Will Practice in the Cloze Test Chapter

Vocabulary blanks

  • The content word — noun, verb, adjective or adverb — whose meaning fits the idea of the passage
  • Collocations and fixed phrases: pay attention, carry out, take a toll, play a part, heavy rain
  • Look-alike and sound-alike words: affect / effect, adapt / adopt, endemic / epidemic
  • Near-synonyms that differ in shade: reduce / curtail / diminish, prevent / prohibit

Grammar blanks

  • Prepositions after verbs and adjectives: depend on, prone to, aware of, in spite of
  • Verb forms: tense agreement, the present perfect with since and for, active or passive, gerund or infinitive
  • Linking words: although, because, however, therefore, unless, whereas, despite
  • Articles, pronouns, relative pronouns and quantifiers: a / the, who / whose / which, few / little, each / every

What the five blanks test — and the clue to look for

Blank typeThe clue to look for
Vocabulary in contextThe idea of the sentence and the one after it — positive or negative, cause or result
Collocation / phrasal verbThe word the blank partners with — “a heavy ___”, “___ attention”
PrepositionThe verb or adjective just before the blank — depend on, prone to
Verb formThe subject, a time word (since, yesterday, every year) and whether the subject acts or is acted on
Linking wordHow the two ideas relate — contrast, reason, result, condition
Article / pronounThe noun after the blank and what the pronoun points back to

In these tests about half the blanks in every passage are vocabulary and half are grammar — the same mix as the exam.

The three mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Filling blanks before reading the passage. The topic and tone decide many blanks; read the whole passage once first.
  • Stopping at the end of the sentence. “Although” and “because” can both look right until the next sentence shows which relation the writer means.
  • Choosing the word that sounds familiar. A word students have seen often — or one that looks like the right word — is the commonest trap; check that its meaning really fits.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Cloze Test

Read the passage, then the four blanks below it.

Many Indian cities are now planting small “pocket forests” on empty plots. Dozens of native saplings are planted very close together, (1)________ they compete for light and grow quickly. Within three years the plot needs almost no care, because the young forest has become (2)________. Residents say the forests have (3)________ birds and butterflies back to their neighbourhoods. The idea is simple, but its success depends (4)________ choosing species that belong to the local soil.
Q1. Blank (1)
(a) although  (b) unless  (c) lest  (d) so
Planting close together is the cause; competing for light is the result. A result needs “so”; although (contrast) and unless (condition) break the logic. Answer: (d) so
Q2. Blank (2)
(a) self-centred  (b) self-sustaining  (c) self-conscious  (d) self-employed
The forest “needs almost no care”, so it must look after itself. Self-centred, self-conscious and self-employed describe people, not a forest. Answer: (b) self-sustaining
Q3. Blank (3)
(a) drawn  (b) dragged  (c) pushed  (d) thrown
The forests attract birds back — “draw back” means attract. Dragged, pushed and thrown all mean using force, which trees cannot do. Answer: (a) drawn
Q4. Blank (4)
(a) in  (b) at  (c) with  (d) on
“Depend” always takes “on” (or “upon”). Answer: (d) on

How to Prepare the Cloze Test for SSC CGL

  1. Read the whole passage first. Thirty seconds on the topic and tone pays back on all five blanks.
  2. Name the blank. Does it need a word of meaning, or a word of grammar — a preposition, a verb form, a linker? That decides how you check the options.
  3. Read one sentence past the blank. The clue is often there — especially for linking words and for words with a positive or negative tone.
  4. Check grammar and meaning together. The right option fits both; a word with the right meaning in the wrong form is still wrong.
  5. Learn words in partnerships. Note collocations (take a toll, pay attention) and preposition pairs (prone to, depend on) as you meet them — cloze blanks test them constantly.
  6. Do Level 1 untimed first, then with the 15-minute clock. Move to Level 2 once Level 1 is steady, and then check yourself on a previous year paper.

SSC CGL Cloze Test — Frequently Asked Questions

How many cloze test questions are asked in SSC CGL?
One passage with five blanks, so five questions worth ten marks, in most SSC CGL and CHSL Tier I shifts. In some shifts a reading comprehension passage takes its place, and the Tier II English paper has cloze passages as well.
What does a cloze test check?
Vocabulary and grammar together. Roughly half the blanks need the content word whose meaning fits the passage; the rest test prepositions, verb forms, linking words such as although and therefore, articles and pronouns, and fixed word partnerships such as pay attention or carry out.
How should I solve a cloze test?
Read the whole passage once before filling any blank, so you know the topic and the writer’s tone. Then for each blank decide whether it needs a word of meaning or a word of grammar, read the full sentence and the next one, and pick the option that fits both the meaning and the grammar.
Are the passages in these tests taken from previous year papers?
No. All 100 passages are written fresh for TrickySSC in the SSC pattern, on new topics, and none repeats or adapts a previous year cloze passage, so every test is unseen practice.
What is the most common trap in cloze test questions?
A word that fits the sentence on its own but not the passage. The clue often sits in the next sentence, so a word that looks right in isolation can contradict what follows. Look-alike words such as affect and effect, and linkers that reverse the logic, are the other common traps.
How many questions are in each test?
Twenty-five questions per test, from five passages of five blanks each, with an optional 15-minute timer and the real exam marking of +2 for a correct answer, −0.5 for a wrong one and 0 for an unattempted question.
Are the tests in English or Hindi?
The cloze tests are in English, as the English Language section is in SSC CGL and CHSL. Every solution quotes the sentence, names the clue, applies the rule and explains why each wrong option fails, in simple English.
What is the difference between Level 1 and Level 2?
Level 1 is set at real SSC CGL difficulty, with everyday topics, common exam words and one clear clue for each blank. Level 2 is harder than the exam: denser analytical passages, Mains-level words and near-miss options such as mitigate and alleviate, where the clue may sit in the next sentence.
