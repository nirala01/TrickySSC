A quadratic has two roots. You rarely need to find them — the sum and product of roots, which come straight from the coefficients, answer most questions.

1. Concepts in brief

What a quadratic is

A quadratic equation has the highest power 2: ax² + bx + c = 0 with a ≠ 0. Its roots are the values of x that make it true. A quadratic has at most two real roots.

Three ways to find the roots

  1. Factorisation — split the middle term into two numbers whose product is ac. Fastest when the numbers are small.
  2. The quadratic formula — works every time, even when factors are ugly.
  3. Sum and product — when the question only needs a combination of the roots, use −b/a and c/a directly without finding them.

The discriminant

D = b² − 4ac sits under the square root in the formula. Positive D gives two different real roots, zero gives one repeated root, and negative D gives no real roots. Questions about "equal roots" or "real roots" are really questions about D.

3. Shortcuts that save time

Factor by splitting the middle term x² − 7x + 12 = 0: find two numbers with sum −7 and product 12 → −3 and −4. Roots are 3 and 4.
  • If a + b + c = 0, one root is 1; if a − b + c = 0, one root is −1.
  • Roots reciprocal of each other: c = a.
  • Roots equal and opposite: b = 0.
  • For a nest like x = √(k + √(k + …)), square it and form x² − x − k = 0.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 Roots of x² − 5x + 6 = 0: sum 5, product 6 → 2 and 3.
Example 2 Roots of 2x² − 3x − 5 = 0: a − b + c = 2 + 3 − 5 = 0, so x = −1 is a root; product = −5/2, so the other root = 5/2.
Example 3 For x² − 6x + 8 = 0, α² + β² = 36 − 16 = 20.
Example 4 For which k does x² + kx + 9 = 0 have equal roots? D = k² − 36 = 0 → k = ±6.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 5 Form the equation whose roots are 3 and −5: sum = −2, product = −15 → x² + 2x − 15 = 0.
Example 6 If α and β are the roots of x² − 5x + 6 = 0, then 1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/αβ = 5/6.
Example 7 Roots of 3x² − 10x + 3 = 0 are reciprocals (a = c): 3 and 1/3.
Example 8 In x² − 8x + k = 0 one root is 3 times the other. α + 3α = 8 → α = 2, roots 2 and 6, so k = 12.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. Solve x² − x − 12 = 0.

Answer: x = 4 or −3.

Q2. Sum and product of the roots of 2x² + 7x − 4 = 0?

Answer: Sum −7/2, product −2.

Q3. Does x² + x + 1 = 0 have real roots?

Answer: No (D = 1 − 4 = −3).

Q4. For what k does 4x² − kx + 9 = 0 have equal roots?

Answer: k = ±12 (k² = 144).

Q5. If α, β are roots of x² − 4x + 1 = 0, find α² + β².

Answer: 14 (16 − 2).

6. Traps to avoid

  1. Sum of roots is −b/a, not b/a — keep the minus sign.
  2. Divide through by a before reading sum and product if a ≠ 1.
  3. A negative discriminant means no real roots, not no roots.
  4. When squaring both sides to remove a root, check every answer in the original equation.
