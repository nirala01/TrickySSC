Every antonym question asks one thing: which option means the opposite of the given word? The word may stand alone, sit underlined inside a sentence, or appear in brackets next to a blank. In the sentence forms, the opposite must match the word in the sense the sentence uses it — and in the blank form, the sentence must make sense with the opposite filled in.

Marks are lost in three places. The first is the synonym trap — SSC almost always puts a word with the same meaning among the options, and a hurried reader picks it. The second is the look-alike — a word that shares the first letters or the sound of the given word. The third is the half-opposite: an option that contrasts with the word in some way, but is not its true opposite.

How Many Antonym Questions Are Asked in SSC CGL?

Antonyms are a fixed part of the English section — usually one or two questions in every SSC CGL Tier I shift, with more in CHSL and the Tier II English paper. SSC repeats its words heavily across years and exams, which is why a good share of every test here is built on words already asked in SSC papers. SSC often hides the true opposite as a plain word instead — the opposite of frugal is extravagant, not “unfrugal”.

The three mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Picking the synonym. Underline the word “antonym” in the instruction before reading the options — a word with the same meaning is almost always there.
  • Matching the look, not the meaning. An option that shares the first letters of the word is usually a trap, not a clue.
  • Taking a half-opposite. The opposite of generous is stingy, not poor — check that the option reverses the exact quality, not just something near it.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Antonyms

Q1. Select the most appropriate ANTONYM of the given word.
Frugal
(a) Thrifty  (b) Extravagant  (c) Careful  (d) Fragile
Frugal means careful not to waste money. Thrifty is the synonym trap and fragile is a look-alike. Answer: (b) Extravagant
Q2. Select the most appropriate ANTONYM of the underlined word.
The new scheme will augment the income of small farmers.
(a) increase  (b) diminish  (c) secure  (d) reveal
Augment means to make larger. Increase is the synonym trap; the opposite is to make smaller. Answer: (b) diminish
Q3. Select the most appropriate ANTONYM of the word given in brackets to fill in the blank.
The doctor said the tumour was __________(malignant), so no surgery was needed.
(a) cancerous  (b) benign  (c) painful  (d) swollen
Malignant means harmful and spreading. “No surgery was needed” calls for its opposite, harmless — and cancerous is the synonym trap. Answer: (b) benign
Q4. Select the most appropriate ANTONYM of the given word.
Ephemeral
(a) Transient  (b) Ethereal  (c) Permanent  (d) Fleeting
Ephemeral means lasting a very short time. Transient and fleeting are both synonyms, and ethereal only sounds similar. Answer: (c) Permanent

How to Prepare Antonyms for SSC CGL

  1. Read the instruction first. Antonym or synonym? The synonym is placed in the options precisely to catch the reader who skips this step.
  2. Find the meaning, then flip it. Say the meaning in plain words, think of its opposite, and only then look at the options.
  3. Strike out the synonym and the look-alike. Most questions have one of each; removing them usually leaves two options, and one of those is only a half-opposite.
  4. In a sentence, read the whole sentence. The opposite must reverse the word in the sense used there, and fit the blank in the right grammatical form.
  5. Learn words in pairs. Note a synonym and an antonym for every new word — one is your answer in this chapter, the other in Synonyms.
  6. Revise daily, not weekly. Ten minutes a day on one test beats one long session, and then check yourself on a previous year paper.

SSC CGL Antonyms — Frequently Asked Questions

How many antonym questions are asked in SSC CGL?
Usually one or two in every SSC CGL Tier I shift, with more in CHSL and the Tier II English paper. The same words also appear in Synonyms, Fill in the Blanks and Cloze Test questions.
Does SSC repeat antonym words from previous papers?
Yes, often. SSC reuses its vocabulary heavily across years and across exams, which is why 40% of the words in every test here are taken from SSC CGL and CHSL previous year papers.
What are the different types of antonym questions in SSC?
Three: the word on its own, an underlined word inside a sentence, and a blank where you fill in the opposite of the word given in brackets. Every test here mixes all three.
What is the most common trap in antonym questions?
The synonym. SSC usually places a word with the same meaning among the options, so a candidate who reads 'antonym' as 'synonym' picks it. Look-alike words and half-opposites are the next two traps.
Is adding un-, in- or dis- always the right antonym?
No. It often works — willing and unwilling, honest and dishonest — but SSC usually gives the opposite as a separate word, such as frugal and extravagant. And some words break the rule: inflammable means the same as flammable.
How many questions are in each test?
Twenty-five questions per test, with an optional 15-minute timer and the real exam marking of +2 for a correct answer, −0.5 for a wrong one and 0 for an unattempted question.
Are the tests in English or Hindi?
The antonyms tests are in English, as the English Language section is in SSC CGL and CHSL. Every solution gives the meaning of the word, a root or context clue, why each wrong option fails and a memory trick, in simple English.
What is the difference between Level 1 and Level 2?
Level 1 is set at real SSC CGL difficulty, with exam-level words and the classic synonym trap. Level 2 is harder than the exam: Mains-level words, look-alike traps, near-opposites that are not true opposites and more sentence questions where the sense of the word decides the answer.
