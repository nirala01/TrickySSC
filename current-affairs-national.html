October 2026Monthly notes

UPSC at 100

  • Centenary – PM Modi attended UPSC's centenary function at Bharat Mandapam on 1 October 2026, the close of the two-day 'Shatabdi Samaroh – Manthan'.
  • Releases – a commemorative coin, a postage stamp and the book '100 Landmark Judgements'.
  • Roots – the first Public Service Commission was formed on 1 October 1926 on the Lee Commission's advice, with Sir Ross Barker as chairman.
  • Renamed – it became the Union Public Service Commission on 26 January 1950.
  • Head – Dr Ajay Kumar is the present UPSC Chairman.
UPSC: constitutional body under Article 315; chairman and members are appointed by the President for six years or up to age 65; it reports on its work to the President every year (Article 323).

Ladakh in focus

  • Organic UT – LG Vinai Kumar Saxena declared Ladakh India's first 100% organic Union Territory on 25 October.
  • Groundwork – a July 2026 order banned chemical and synthetic fertilisers; 207 revenue villages were certified under PKVY.
  • Map names – the Home Ministry cleared standard names for 28 places and features in Ladakh for Survey of India maps.
  • HC bench – CJI Surya Kant opened a Ladakh Bench of the J&K and Ladakh High Court at Leh.
  • Snow leopards – Ladakh holds 477 of India's estimated 718; a first high-altitude snow leopard safari began there.
Sikkim: India's first fully organic state, declared in 2016; Ladakh became a Union Territory on 31 October 2019.

Heritage and GI tags

  • Tentative List – three entries added on 20 October take India's UNESCO Tentative List to 81.
  • Andaman sites – the Karst-Mud-Volcano Complex of Baratang, and the Andaman Subduction Volcanoes on Barren and Narcondam islands.
  • Sirpur – cultural landscape on the Mahanadi in Mahasamund, Chhattisgarh; famous for the 7th-century brick Lakshmana Temple.
  • Mandar – Jharkhand's red-clay folk drum got a GI tag from the GI Registry, Chennai; the applicant was a producer company of Gumla district.
  • Ambaji Mohanthal – the gram-flour sweet offered at Gujarat's Ambaji temple also won a GI tag.
Barren Island: India's only confirmed active volcano, in the Andaman Sea about 138 km north-east of Port Blair.

Wildlife comebacks

  • Wild water buffalo – eight animals from Kaziranga, Assam, were released into a 17-hectare enclosure in Kanha Tiger Reserve, MP, on 11 October.
  • After a century – the species had vanished from the area about 100 years ago; MP plans to bring in 50 over three years.
  • Ramsar – Sukhna Wetland (Chandigarh) and Sauj Jheel (UP) took India's Ramsar sites to 103.
  • Barda – a study found Asiatic lions have returned to Gujarat's Barda Wildlife Sanctuary after about 144 years.
  • Buxa – a tigress from Bihar's Valmiki Tiger Reserve was shifted to Buxa Tiger Reserve, West Bengal.

Courts, rules and literacy

  • Supreme Court – held that access to the internet is a fundamental right under Articles 19(1)(a) and 21.
  • Census rules – the Census (Amendment) Rules, 2026 allow private-sector staff and teachers to serve as enumerators.
  • Frontier Nagaland – the Frontier Nagaland Territorial Authority Bill, 2026 received Presidential assent; it covers six eastern districts.
  • Lakshadweep – declared fully literate under ULLAS (New India Literacy Programme).
  • Jantar Mantar – Delhi Police withdrew its status as a protest site.

September 2026Monthly notes

UNESCO tentative list grows

  • Nine new entries – India put nine cultural sites on its UNESCO Tentative List, taking it to 78.
  • Andaman and Nicobar – the old penal colony sites (Cellular Jail, Viper, Ross and Chatham islands) and Kamorta Harbour with Nicobarese culture.
  • Assam – the Rangpur–Sivasagar group, linked with the Ahom capital.
  • Madhya Pradesh – four entries – Bagh caves paintings, Gupta-era Udayagiri caves, the Gond fort of Ginnorgarh and the Chambal valley temples (Bateshwar, Padavali, Kakanmath).
  • Others – the painted havelis of Shekhawati, and a joint India–South Africa entry on sites of Gandhi's Satyagraha.

A temple and a new law

  • Gollala Gudi – temple at Palampet village, Mulugu district, Telangana, declared a Monument of National Importance by the ASI.
  • Style – a Trikutalaya (three-shrine) temple of the Kakatiya period, about the 12th–13th century.
  • Births and Deaths law – the Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Act, 2026 comes into force on 1 October 2026; it amends the 1969 Act.
  • 1–2 years late – a birth or death can be registered only on the order of a DM, SDM or an Executive Magistrate the DM authorises.
  • Over 2 years late – registration needs an order of a Judicial Magistrate First Class.
AMASR Act: Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act, 1958; under it the ASI (set up 1861, first DG Alexander Cunningham) protects monuments of national importance.

Clean-up drives

  • Special Campaign 6.0 – run by DARPG to clean offices and clear pending files; prep phase 16–30 September, main phase 2–31 October; held every year since 2021.
  • Swachhata Hi Seva 2026 – fortnight held every year since 2017 in the run-up to 2 October, Swachh Bharat Diwas.
  • PRAGATI-50 – launched by MoHUA on 29 September to turn 50 cities into cleanliness models under SBM-Urban 2.0.
  • Test bed – CSIR-CMERI and MoHUA opened the Swachh Bharat Test Bed and Innovation Centre at Durgapur, West Bengal.
  • Swachh Sagar, Surakshit Sagar 5.0 – Earth Sciences Ministry's coastal clean-up (12–19 September), with an Ocean Olympiad.

Youth, children and workers

  • Nasha Mukt Yuva – a 100-week youth drive began on 18 September (Youth Affairs Ministry, via MY Bharat and NSS); its pillars are Prevent, Protect, Recover and Lead.
  • Adoption campaign – CARA began a year-long legal adoption awareness drive on 1 September; CARA works as a statutory authority under the Juvenile Justice Act, 2015.
  • Poshan Maah – the 9th National Nutrition Month (9 September–8 October) was launched in Varanasi; theme 'Hamari Anganwadi, Hamari Zimmedari'.
  • Shram Samvad – a 90-day awareness campaign on the Labour Codes from 28 September, covering 316 districts.
  • SADHANA – regional consultations by the Capacity Building Commission under Mission Karmayogi (29 September–17 November).

Wildlife and climate

  • Project Cheetah – completed four years on 17 September 2026; it began on 17 September 2022 when eight cheetahs from Namibia were released at Kuno National Park, MP.
  • Sites – cheetahs now live in Kuno and Gandhi Sagar (MP); Banni grasslands (Gujarat) and Nauradehi (MP) are the next sites.
  • Later batches – 12 came from South Africa (February 2023) and 9 from Botswana (February 2026).
  • Great Indian Bustard – a GIB soft release centre opened at Naliya, Kutch; a Gujarat Action Plan for the bird was made by the Wildlife Institute of India.
  • Net Zero Portal – launched with the NAPCC Dashboard on World Ozone Day (16 September) in Mumbai.
International Big Cat Alliance: launched by India in April 2023 to mark 50 years of Project Tiger; covers seven big cats; signed an MoU with the Asian Development Bank at a Delhi workshop on 2–3 September 2026.

Water-sharing pacts

  • Kishau MoU – signed on 15 September in New Delhi, with Amit Shah and C.R. Patil present, by six states.
  • Six partners – Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Rajasthan and Delhi.
  • Dam – a 232.6 m concrete gravity dam on the Tons, a Yamuna tributary along the Uttarakhand–Himachal border.
  • Funding – declared a National Project – the Centre bears 90% of the water-part cost and the states 10%.
  • Son river – Bihar and Jharkhand agreed (31 August) to split 7.75 MAF – Bihar 5.75 and Jharkhand 2 – a dispute rooted in the 1973 Bansagar agreement.

Laws, orders and portals

  • AFSPA – extended for six months from 1 October 2026 in notified areas of Manipur, Nagaland and Arunachal Pradesh.
  • FCRA Bill – the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026 went to a 31-member JPC headed by MP Sanjay Jaiswal.
  • Jan Soochna Seva – Supreme Court initiative (14 September) giving simple Hindi summaries of rulings and daily audio-video bulletins.
  • Pehchaan – Punjab became the first state with a portal to identify unclaimed and unknown bodies.
  • Durga Yojana – Delhi scheme giving women and transgender persons 1,300 pink e-autos in phase one.

Days, stamps and books

  • Hyderabad Liberation Day – the 79th was marked on 17 September at Secunderabad; on that day in 1948 Hyderabad joined India.
  • Sant Malukdas – a stamp on the saint-poet was released on 15 September by Jyotiraditya Scindia.
  • Indomitable – memoir of former SBI chief Arundhati Bhattacharya; its Hindi version is titled 'Aparajita'.
  • Bhupen Hazarika – a book for his birth centenary was released in Assam by the Vice-President; he is called the 'Bard of the Brahmaputra'.
  • Adi Darpan – second edition of a compendium by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs on its schemes and tribal communities.
Operation Polo: Indian Army's police action of 13–17 September 1948 that ended the Nizam's rule and brought Hyderabad State into India.

August 2026Monthly notes

80th Independence Day

  • Theme – the 2026 celebrations ran on two ideas: 'Yuva Shakti for Viksit Bharat@2047' and 150 years of Vande Mataram.
  • Red Fort first – the National Song Vande Mataram was sung by a mass gathering at the Red Fort for the first time.
  • Youth pledges – AI skilling for one crore young people, online coaching at no cost for children of poor and middle-class families, and a sports talent hunt for children of 5–15 years.
  • Nuclear target – 100 GW of nuclear power by 2047, with five Small Modular Reactors to be set up within this decade.
  • Shakti ki Saptdhara – a seven-pillar plan for national strength, covering manufacturing, farming and food processing, Gati Shakti, defence, green and blue economy, and soft power.

Har Ghar Tiranga 2026

  • Dates – the campaign ran from 9 to 17 August 2026 and was dedicated to 150 years of Vande Mataram.
  • Drone delivery – India Post used a drone to carry a Tiranga from its Mandi head office to the Reharadhar branch office in Himachal Pradesh.
  • Suryapath relay – the Ministries of Culture and External Affairs ran a global flag relay that began in Suva (Fiji) and ended in San Francisco, with 54+ Indian missions sharing Tiranga images.
  • Concert – a Har Ghar Tiranga concert at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, starred singer B Praak.
  • Abroad – New York, New Jersey and Boston formally named 15 August 2026 'India Independence Day'.

New laws passed by Parliament

  • Supreme Court strength – the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Act, 2026 raised the sanctioned strength from 34 to 38 judges, counting the CJI.
  • How – it changes Section 2 of the 1956 Act from 33 to 37 judges besides the CJI, replaces the May 2026 ordinance and counts from 16 May 2026.
  • Vande Mataram – the Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Act, 2026 now protects the National Song too; a first offence can bring up to 3 years in jail, and a repeat one at least 1 year.
  • Exam cheating – the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Act, 2026 sets jail of 5–10 years and fines up to Rs 50 lakh for individuals; organised syndicates face 7–10 years.
  • Speed – police must finish the probe within 2 months, trials are to end within 3 months of the chargesheet, with daily hearings and fast-track courts.
Supreme Court strength: Article 124 lets Parliament fix the number of judges; the court began in 1950 with 8 judges (CJI + 7) and grew to 11 (1956), 14 (1960), 18 (1977), 26 (1986), 31 (2009), 34 (2019) and 38 (2026).

Kerala becomes Keralam

  • Law – the Kerala (Alteration of Name) Act, 2026 renames the state Keralam, its name in Malayalam.
  • Passage – Lok Sabha cleared it on 11 August and Rajya Sabha on 12 August; the President signed it on 14 August.
  • In force – the Home Ministry's notice brought the new name into effect on 25 August 2026, the eve of Thiruvonam.
  • Records – all central and state records are to switch to Keralam within a year.

New portals and apps

  • SAMAVESH – a single portal of the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment, built by NeGD, bringing SC/OBC welfare, senior citizens, disability, transgender and scholarship services to one place; the Nasha Mukt Bharat portal moved to it.
  • RSVC AMRIT – launched on 15 August by Principal Scientific Adviser Ajay Kumar Sood; built by NABARD with the PSA's office for village tech hubs called RuTAGe Smart Village Centres.
  • AppCoMS 2.0 – Chief Information Commissioner Raj Kumar Goel launched it on 17 August to handle RTI second appeals and complaints online; version 1 came in 2016.
  • India Code – the revamped repository of central and state laws was launched by Arjun Ram Meghwal; it is run by the Legislative Department and NIC.
  • Dak Mitra and Dak Sewa – India Post apps launched by Jyotiraditya Scindia on 24 August; Dak Mitra is for postal staff, Dak Sewa for citizens.

Environment

  • Glaw Lake – became India's 101st Ramsar site and the first in Arunachal Pradesh, announced by Bhupender Yadav on 3 August 2026.
  • Where – a freshwater lake inside the Kamlang Tiger Reserve, Lohit district, sacred to the Mishmi people; it supports 150+ tree and 49 orchid species.
  • Elephant corridors – Tamil Nadu notified three corridors inside Cauvery North Wildlife Sanctuary (Hosur division).
  • Prakriti Gyan Dham – an eco-education hub at CSIR-NBRI's Maharishi Parashar Bioresource Centre, Banthra, Lucknow, dedicated by Dr Jitendra Singh.
  • Black-necked crane – Ladakh held its first festival for the bird, which is Near Threatened on the IUCN list and in Schedule I of the Wildlife Act.
Ramsar Convention: treaty on wetlands signed at Ramsar, Iran, in 1971; India joined in 1982 and its first sites were Chilika Lake and Keoladeo; World Wetlands Day is on 2 February.

Campaigns and milestones

  • Nasha Mukt Yuva – PM Modi launched the 'Nasha Mukt Yuva for Viksit Bharat' Sankalp Abhiyan on 2 August; the Sports Ministry runs it through MY Bharat as a 100-week drive with events every Sunday.
  • Mission Samriddha Gaon – Shivraj Singh Chouhan's model for poverty-free, job-rich villages; pilots run in Budni (Sehore) and Khategaon (Dewas) of Madhya Pradesh.
  • Organ Donation Day – the 16th edition on 3 August recalled India's first heart transplant at AIIMS in 1994; the 'Jug Jug Jiyo' village campaign was launched.
  • Literacy – Uttarakhand became India's sixth state to be declared fully literate under ULLAS, with 98.7% literacy.
  • Sujal Gaon ID – unique codes for rural habitations under Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 to track drinking water supply.

Places and persons

  • New airport – PM Modi opened the Sri Alluri Sitarama Raju International Airport at Bhogapuram, Vizianagaram (Andhra Pradesh), on 1 August.
  • Mysuru – the Swami Vivekananda Cultural Youth Centre opened at Nirjana Math, where Vivekananda stayed in 1892.
  • Arunachal names – the Centre and the state, through the Survey of India, gave official names to 27 places, including four passes and Sancho Sarovar lake.
  • Ladakh bench – the Cabinet on 20 August cleared a Ladakh bench of the High Court of J&K and Ladakh.
  • Obituary – D.Y. Patil, former Governor of Tripura and Bihar and Padma Shri awardee, died on 4 August in Kolhapur; a stamp marked painter Satish Gujral's birth centenary.

July 2026Monthly notes

Sarnath and Rashtrapati Bhavan

  • Sarnath – the 'Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath' near Varanasi became India's 45th World Heritage property on 25 July 2026, at the 48th World Heritage Committee session in Busan, South Korea.
  • Parts listed – the Chaukhandi Stupa and the main ruins with the Dhamek and Dharmarajika stupas; it is Uttar Pradesh's fourth World Heritage Site.
  • Long wait – Sarnath, where the Buddha gave his first sermon, had been on the tentative list since 1998; India still has 69 sites on that list.
  • Four years in office – on 28 July President Droupadi Murmu (in office since 25 July 2022) opened 'Bachpan Bal Parisar', a children's space at Rashtrapati Bhavan.
  • Books – the Vice President released four books, one on each year of her term, plus an Odia collection, 'Ama Rashtrapati–Ama Gaurav'.
World Heritage Committee: 21-member UNESCO body that picks sites under the 1972 Convention; each country may put up one site a year; the ASI is India's nodal agency. India hosted the 46th session in New Delhi in 2024.

Exam reforms and panchayats

  • Minister quits – Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned on 25 July 2026 after weeks of student protests over the NEET-UG paper leak; the President accepted it under Article 75.
  • Extra charge – Pralhad Joshi got the Education Ministry on top of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution, and New and Renewable Energy.
  • Task force – on 26 July the PM set up a six-member high-powered task force on exam reforms led by Nandan Nilekani; it includes ex-ISRO chief S. Somanath and IIT Madras director V. Kamakoti.
  • Aatmanirbhar Panchayat – launched on 27 July by the Panchayati Raj Ministry to help panchayats earn their own revenue through bankable projects; the aim is 350 projects in four years.
  • New portals – 'Samarth Panchayat' digitises panchayat tax collection, and 'DAANVEER' lets citizens give computers to village panchayats through the Meri Panchayat app.

Rail, ports and places

  • Hydrogen train – on 17 July 2026 PM Modi flagged off 'Namo Green Rail', India's first home-built hydrogen train, from Jind in Haryana.
  • Train facts – the 10-coach train uses a 1,200 kW fuel-cell system on the 89-km Jind–Sonipat stretch of Northern Railway, with a top speed of 75 km/h.
  • Electrification – electric traction now covers 99.6% of broad-gauge routes.
  • Deep port – Kamarajar Port, Chennai, reached an 18-metre draft, the second Indian port after Visakhapatnam to do so, and can now take capesize ships.
  • Geothermal and Central Vista – India's first and deepest geothermal wells were opened in Ladakh's Puga Valley; the Central Vista area is now called the 'Kartavya Bhavan Complex'.

Rural jobs and farm schemes

  • VB-G RAM G Act – the Viksit Bharat – Guarantee for Employment and Livelihood Mission (Rural) Act, 2025 took effect on 1 July 2026, replacing MGNREGA (2005).
  • Launch – it was launched nationally on 2 July at Mukkavaripalli in Tirupati district, Andhra Pradesh; the minimum wage under it is Rs 300 a day.
  • PRAGATI – an Agriculture Ministry project launched on 7 July to help 20,000 young villagers start farm businesses that will serve 20 lakh small farmers.
  • High-seas fishing – vessel-specific 'Letters of Authorisation' launched in Bhubaneswar on 9 July allow Indian-flagged boats to fish beyond the EEZ.
  • Ayurveda roadmap – NITI Aayog released a plan up to 2047 to make Ayurveda a globally accepted, evidence-based system.
VB-G RAM G Act: rural jobs law of the Ministry of Rural Development that replaced MGNREGA; passed by Parliament in December 2025, got Presidential assent on 21 December 2025 and took effect on 1 July 2026.

People, PSUs and other news

  • Teejan Bai – the celebrated Pandavani singer of Chhattisgarh died in Raipur on 5 July 2026; she held the Padma Vibhushan (2019) and the Fukuoka Prize (2018).
  • 'Bridge Man' – Girish Bharadwaj of Sullia, Karnataka, who built over 140 low-cost footbridges in remote areas, passed away; he got the Padma Shri in 2017.
  • Navratna – Chennai Petroleum Corporation Ltd became India's 28th Navratna; MECON of Ranchi was made a Miniratna Category-I company.
  • TribeX – Tribal Affairs Minister Jual Oram launched this digital learning platform on tribal art, culture and skills in Bhubaneswar.
  • Space gallery – the country's first 'Space Exploration Gallery' opened at the Regional Science Centre, Bhopal.
Navratna status: given by the Centre to profit-making Miniratna Category-I, Schedule 'A' CPSEs; their boards can put as much as Rs 1,000 crore or 15% of net worth in one project without government approval.

June 2026Monthly notes

Governance milestones

  • Longest-serving elected PM – on 10 June 2026 Narendra Modi finished 4,399 straight days in office, one day past Jawaharlal Nehru's elected run of 1952–64.
  • Start of tenure – Modi was first sworn in on 26 May 2014; the Union Cabinet passed a resolution hailing 10 June as historic.
  • NITI Aayog – the 11th Governing Council meeting, chaired by the PM in New Delhi on 11 June, focused on 'Inclusive Human Development for Viksit Bharat@2047'.
  • Full attendance – Chief Ministers of all 28 states attended – a first.
  • NeVA – the West Bengal Assembly became the 33rd legislature to adopt the National e-Vidhan Application for paperless functioning.
NeVA: National e-Vidhan Application of the Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs, a centrally sponsored scheme to digitise state legislatures; hosted on NIC's 'MeghRaj' cloud.

Courts and justice delivery

  • Footpaths – the Supreme Court ruled that walking on safe, well-kept footpaths is part of the right to life under Article 21, and pedestrians come before vehicles.
  • DISHA 2.0 – the Law Ministry recast its access-to-justice scheme for 2026–31 with about Rs 255 crore; it keeps Tele-Law and Nyaya Bandhu and adds a dashboard called 'Vidhi Sanjeevani'.
  • Judgment timeline – the Supreme Court set a three-month limit for all High Courts to deliver reserved judgments.
  • Aravalli – a Supreme Court expert panel under ICFRE Director General Kanchan Devi will re-examine the Centre's definition of the Aravalli range.
  • GSICC – the Supreme Court's gender sensitisation and internal complaints committee was reconstituted with Justice B.V. Nagarathna as chair.
DISHA: 'Designing Innovative Solutions for Holistic Access to Justice', a central sector scheme of the Department of Justice; the new phase targets 30 lakh fresh beneficiaries.

Wetlands, wildlife and literacy

  • 100th Ramsar site – Surha Tal, also called the Jai Prakash Narayan Bird Sanctuary, of Ballia district, Uttar Pradesh, was listed on World Environment Day, 5 June 2026.
  • About Surha Tal – an oxbow lake of the middle Ganga plain, a bird sanctuary since 1991; UP now has 13 Ramsar sites, behind only Tamil Nadu.
  • Hargila Army – in Mann Ki Baat the PM lauded biologist Purnima Devi Barman, whose women's group guards the greater adjutant stork in Assam.
  • Kerala HAWK – Kerala became the first state to connect its wildlife-crime tracking system with e-courts.
  • Sikkim – President Droupadi Murmu declared it a fully literate state under the ULLAS adult-literacy mission.
ULLAS: 'Understanding of Lifelong Learning for All in Society', the Ministry of Education's literacy programme for people aged 15 and above.

Connectivity and border projects

  • Zojila tunnel – the last breakthrough blast came on 9 June; the tunnel of about 13 km at nearly 11,578 ft will join Sonamarg (J&K) with Kargil district (Ladakh).
  • Why it matters – it will be India's longest road tunnel and give Ladakh year-round road access; NHIDCL is the agency and MEIL the builder.
  • Easy Connect – India's first hub-and-spoke international flight began from Varanasi airport on 25 June; passengers finish immigration and customs at the starting airport.
  • Vinimay – Amit Shah launched the Land Ports Authority of India's digital Land Port Management System on 9 June.
  • Chumur – Ladakh's first model border village under the Vibrant Villages Programme was started at about 16,700 ft near the China border.

Youth, health and other initiatives

  • Vande Mataram Camps – 20 youth camps under MY Bharat; the first, in Kolkata from 24–30 June, marked Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay's birth anniversary on 27 June.
  • Drug control – Amit Shah released a Vision Document on Drug Control 2026–29 at the 10th apex meeting of NCORD.
  • SSBSK – the new Samagra Shishu Bal Swasthya Karyakram joins home-based newborn and young-child care for children up to 36 months.
  • Anaemia Mukt Bharat – revised guidelines widen the 6x6x6 plan to 7x7x7, adding low-birth-weight babies as a target group.
  • Drone post – India Post began drone mail in Himachal Pradesh on 12 June, starting from Mandi head post office.
Anaemia Mukt Bharat: launched in 2018 under the National Health Mission; its original 6x6x6 design meant six target groups, six interventions and six institutional mechanisms.

May 2026Monthly notes

Courts and laws

  • Supreme Court strength – President Droupadi Murmu issued the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Ordinance, 2026 on 16 May under Article 123.
  • New number – judges other than the CJI go up from 33 to 37, so the full bench strength is now 38.
  • Background – the Union Cabinet cleared it on 5 May; it changes Section 2 of the 1956 Act; the last rise was in 2019 (30 to 33).
  • Assam UCC – on 27 May the Assam Assembly passed the Uniform Civil Code, Assam, 2026 Bill by voice vote.
  • Where it stands – Assam is the third state after Uttarakhand and Gujarat and the first in the North-East; Scheduled Tribes are kept outside it.

Voter rolls and states

  • SIR Phase III – the Election Commission ordered the third phase of the Special Intensive Revision of rolls in 16 states and UTs.
  • Power used – Article 324; parties were told to appoint Booth Level Agents to work with Booth Level Officers.
  • Sikkim – celebrated its 51st Statehood Day on 16 May; it became the 22nd state in 1975 through the 36th Constitutional Amendment.
  • Sikkim Organics – a new mission was launched; Sikkim was declared India's first fully organic state in 2016.
  • Ladakh – officers were posted to five new districts (Nubra, Sham, Changthang, Zanskar, Drass), taking the UT to seven districts.

Heritage comes home

  • Chola plates – Leiden University Library handed back 11th-century Chola copper plates (21 large, 3 small) on 16 May.
  • What they say – royal charters in Tamil and Sanskrit recording the gift of Anaimangalam village to a Buddhist vihara at Nagapattinam.
  • Bronzes – the Smithsonian's National Museum of Asian Art (USA) returned three temple bronzes from Tamil Nadu.
  • The pieces – a Chola Shiva Nataraja (about 990 CE), a 12th-century Somaskanda and a 16th-century Vijayanagara image of the saint Sundarar.
  • Padma Doree – NEHHDC (DoNER ministry) launched a textile line that blends North-East Eri (Ahimsa) silk with Madhya Pradesh's Chanderi weave.

Roads, rail and dams

  • Barrier-free toll – India's first Multi-Lane Free Flow (MLFF) toll began at Chorayasi plaza on the Surat–Bharuch stretch of NH-48, Gujarat.
  • How it works – number-plate cameras (ANPR) and FASTag collect the fee without stopping; about 41,500 vehicles crossed on the first day.
  • Hydrogen train – Indian Railways' first indigenous hydrogen train is cleared for the Jind–Sonipat line in Haryana; 10 cars, top speed 75 km/h.
  • Semi-high-speed line – the Cabinet cleared Railways' first such line, about 134 km in Ahmedabad district, Gujarat.
  • Dam safety – the National Dam Safety Authority released the National Register of Specified Dams 2026 and the DHARMA portal.

Wildlife

  • Kanha deaths – tigress T-141 and her four cubs died within nine days in Kanha Tiger Reserve, Madhya Pradesh.
  • Suspected cause – canine distemper virus (CDV), carried by stray dogs; about eight tigers died there in April–May and the MP High Court asked for reports.
  • Lions – a Lion Species Spotlight was held at Sasan Gir on 14 May before the big cat summit; Gir lions numbered about 891 in 2025.
  • Cheetahs – Bhupender Yadav reviewed Project Cheetah (2022); Gandhi Sagar Sanctuary is the second home after Kuno.
Kanha Tiger Reserve: in the Mandla and Balaghat districts of Madhya Pradesh, in the Maikal hills of the Satpura range; a Project Tiger reserve since 1973, known for the hard-ground barasingha.

April 2026Monthly notes

Seat expansion bill defeated

  • Lost vote – the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026 failed in the Lok Sabha on 17 April with 298 ayes and 230 noes.
  • Shortfall – it needed two-thirds of members present and voting (352 of 528) and fell 54 votes short.
  • Plan – raise Lok Sabha strength from 543 to about 850 and redraw seats on the 2011 Census, so the women's quota could apply in 2029.
  • Dropped – the linked Delimitation Bill, 2026 and the Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 were not pursued.
  • Women's quota law – the Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam (106th Amendment, 2023) took effect on 16 April 2026; one-third seats for women will follow the next delimitation.
Article 368: a constitutional amendment needs, in each House, a majority of total membership plus two-thirds of those present and voting; there is no joint sitting for such bills.

Parliament: new law and a third term

  • Jan Vishwas 2.0 – MoS Commerce Jitin Prasada moved the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Bill, 2026, touching 784 provisions in 79 Central Acts of 23 ministries.
  • Split – 717 provisions are decriminalised to ease business; 67 are changed for ease of living.
  • Earlier law – the 2023 Jan Vishwas Act covered 42 Acts, removing jail terms from 183 provisions.
  • Deputy Chairman – Harivansh was elected Rajya Sabha Deputy Chairman unopposed on 17 April, a third straight term.
  • Nominated – no nominated member had held the post before him; President Murmu nominated him on 10 April in place of ex-CJI Ranjan Gogoi.

Change at the top in Bihar

  • New CM – Samrat Choudhary took oath as Bihar's 24th Chief Minister on 15 April, the first from the BJP in the state.
  • Why – Nitish Kumar stepped down after shifting to the Rajya Sabha.
  • Deputies – JD(U) leaders Vijay Kumar Choudhary and Bijendra Prasad Yadav became Deputy CMs.
  • Oath – Governor Syed Ata Hasnain swore them in at Lok Bhavan, Patna.
  • Profile – Choudhary, son of Shakuni Choudhary, had been Deputy CM under Nitish Kumar.

Census begins and NCERT upgraded

  • Census 2027 – Phase I, the Houselisting and Housing Census, started on 1 April 2026 and runs till 30 September.
  • Self-enumeration – people can fill their own details online for the first time; President Droupadi Murmu was the first to do so.
  • Digital count – it is India's first digital census, the 16th overall and 8th since Independence.
  • NCERT – the Education Ministry, on UGC advice, made NCERT a deemed-to-be university (distinct category), so it can award its own degrees.
  • Coverage – the status also covers six NCERT constituent units, among them the Regional Institutes of Education.
NCERT: National Council of Educational Research and Training, an autonomous body set up in 1961 under the Education Ministry, headquartered in New Delhi; it frames National Curriculum Frameworks and school textbooks.

Wetlands and climate

  • 99th Ramsar site – Shekha Jheel Bird Sanctuary in Aligarh (UP) got the Ramsar tag on 22 April.
  • UP tally – Uttar Pradesh now has 12 Ramsar sites; the lake lies on the Central Asian Flyway.
  • HFC curbs – MoEFCC told regulators not to clear new or extra HFC production after 31 December 2027.
  • Kigali path – India, which ratified the Kigali Amendment in 2021, will cut HFC use 10% by 2032 and 85% by 2047.
  • COP host – India pulled out of the race to host COP33 (2028); its only COP so far was COP8 at New Delhi in 2002.

Culture, courts and languages

  • Art record – Raja Ravi Varma's 1890s oil 'Yashoda and Krishna' fetched Rs 167.2 crore at a Saffronart auction in Mumbai on 1 April, the highest for any Indian artwork.
  • Buyer – Cyrus S. Poonawalla of the Serum Institute; the old mark was M.F. Husain's 'Gram Yatra' at about Rs 118 crore.
  • Sindhi – VP C.P. Radhakrishnan released the latest Constitution in Sindhi on 10 April; Sindhi joined the Eighth Schedule by the 21st Amendment (1967).
  • Moh-Juj – Assam was told by the Gauhati High Court not to stage its traditional buffalo fights, citing the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960.
  • Meghalaya – an ordinance gave official-language status to Khasi and Garo.
Raja Ravi Varma (1848–1906): born at Kilimanoor in the Travancore state (now Kerala); blended Indian myth with European realism and ran a lithographic press in Bombay from 1894.

Lives remembered

  • Asha Bhosle – the playback legend died in Mumbai on 12 April aged 92; she sang about 12,000 songs.
  • Her honours – Dadasaheb Phalke Award (2000) and Padma Vibhushan (2008); she was Lata Mangeshkar's younger sister.
  • Raghu Rai – the celebrated photojournalist, known for his Bhopal gas tragedy images, passed away; Padma Shri in 1972.
  • Mohsina Kidwai – former Union Minister and Congress leader from Uttar Pradesh died on 8 April.
  • Col. Sonam Wangchuk – Maha Vir Chakra hero of the 1999 Kargil war from Ladakh passed away.

March 2026Monthly notes

Census 2027 goes digital

  • Launch – four digital census platforms were soft-launched by Home Minister Amit Shah on 5 March 2026.
  • Mascots – 'Pragati' (a woman enumerator) and 'Vikas' (a man enumerator) are the friendly faces of the drive.
  • Builder – C-DAC made the tools: a houselisting block creator, an HLO mobile app, a self-enumeration portal and the CMMS monitoring portal.
  • Self-enumeration – for the first time families can enter their own details online, in 16 languages, before the door-to-door visit.
  • Phase one – house listing begins on 1 April 2026 with over 30 lakh field staff.
Census in India: a Union List subject (Entry 69), held under the Census Act, 1948 by the Registrar General and Census Commissioner, Ministry of Home Affairs.

Right to die with dignity put into practice

  • Case – in Harish Rana v. Union of India (11 March 2026) the Supreme Court, for the first time, allowed life support to be withdrawn for a patient.
  • Patient – Harish Rana, about 32, had been in a permanent vegetative state for nearly 13 years after a fall.
  • Feeding tube – the court held that clinically assisted nutrition and hydration is medical treatment that can be stopped.
  • Base – it applied the 2018 Common Cause verdict, which read a right to die with dignity into Article 21; the rules were eased in 2023.
  • Limit – active euthanasia, such as a lethal injection, is still illegal.

Gujarat's common civil code and other rulings

  • Gujarat UCC – the state Assembly passed the Uniform Civil Code Bill on 24 March 2026 after a seven-hour debate.
  • Second state – Gujarat follows Uttarakhand (Feb 2024); Scheduled Tribes are left out of the law.
  • Main rules – compulsory registration of marriages and live-in ties, a ban on bigamy and equal inheritance for daughters.
  • Constitution – Article 44, a Directive Principle, asks the State to try for a UCC for all citizens.
  • Adoptive mothers – the Supreme Court held they get 12 weeks' paid maternity leave whatever the child's age, striking down the age bar in the Social Security Code, 2020.

Rivers, ports and passes

  • River lighthouses – foundation stones were laid for four lighthouses on the Brahmaputra in Assam – Pandu, Bogibeel, Silghat and Biswanath Ghat.
  • First – they will be India's first lighthouses on a river, built by IWAI with the Directorate General of Lighthouses and Lightships.
  • NW-2 – the Brahmaputra waterway runs 891 km from Dhubri to Sadiya.
  • Digital twin – V.O. Chidambaranar Port, Thoothukudi, became the first Indian port with a digital twin, a live virtual model of the port.
  • Lipulekh – the Centre issued rules to reopen India–China border trade through the Lipulekh Pass (Pithoragarh district, Uttarakhand).

Records in public life

  • Modi – on 22 March 2026 Narendra Modi became India's longest-serving elected head of government, adding his Gujarat CM and PM years.
  • Old mark – he passed the 8,930-day record of former Sikkim CM Pawan Kumar Chamling.
  • Nitish Kumar – the Bihar Chief Minister was elected to the Rajya Sabha from Bihar.
  • Menaka Guruswamy – the senior advocate, elected to the Rajya Sabha from West Bengal, is India's first openly gay MP.
  • Section 377 – she had argued the 2018 case in which the Supreme Court decriminalised homosexuality.

Heritage in the news

  • Sarnath – the old Buddhist site near Varanasi is India's World Heritage nomination for the 2025–26 cycle.
  • Root bridges – Meghalaya's Jingkieng Jri landscape of living root bridges, grown from rubber fig roots in the Khasi and Jaintia Hills, is the 2026–27 entry.
  • Tally – India has 44 World Heritage Sites: 36 cultural, 7 natural and 1 mixed.
  • Kalinjar – the Geological Survey of India declared Kalinjar Fort in Banda, UP, a national geo-heritage site.
  • Mahad – a convention marked the 99th year of Dr Ambedkar's Mahad Satyagraha (20 March 1927) for Dalits' right to draw water from Chavdar Tank.

India's climate pledge for 2035

  • Approval – the Union Cabinet cleared India's NDC for 2031–2035 on 25 March 2026, to go to the UNFCCC.
  • Emissions – cut the emissions intensity of GDP by 47% by 2035 from 2005 (36% was achieved by 2020).
  • Clean power – 60% of installed power capacity from non-fossil sources by 2035; it was about 52.6% in Feb 2026.
  • Forests – an extra carbon sink of 3.5 to 4 billion tonnes of CO2-equivalent from trees.
  • Long goal – net-zero emissions by 2070.

Biodiversity bodies at work

  • Nagoya report – India sent its first national report on the Nagoya Protocol to the CBD Secretariat on 27 Feb 2026.
  • Protocol – adopted at Nagoya, Japan, in 2010 and in force since 2014; it covers access to genetic resources and fair sharing of benefits.
  • Invasive species – the National Biodiversity Authority formed an expert panel on invasive alien species under ex-IFS officer Dhananjay Mohan.
  • HANUMAN – Andhra Pradesh launched the 'Hanuman' project to rescue and treat wildlife and reduce human–animal conflict.
  • NBA – a statutory body under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002, based in Chennai.

February 2026Monthly notes

Government moves to new buildings

  • Seva Teerth – opened by the PM on 13 Feb 2026 on Kartavya Path; home of the PMO, Cabinet Secretariat and National Security Council Secretariat.
  • Ministries – two new office blocks for key ministries, Kartavya Bhavan-1 and -2, also opened that day.
  • Goodbye South Block – the PMO left South Block after about 95 years; the day also marked 95 years of New Delhi as capital.
  • Motto – the complex follows the idea of 'Nagrik Devo Bhava' (the citizen is god).
  • First files – approval of PM RAHAT, a larger Lakhpati Didi target and a bigger Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (see Economic).
Central Vista Redevelopment: plan to rebuild the 3.2-km stretch between Rashtrapati Bhavan and India Gate; includes the new Parliament (2023) and Kartavya Path (2022); architect Bimal Patel.

Assam gets a runway on a highway

  • Moran ELF – the first highway Emergency Landing Facility in the Northeast, a 4.2-km highway stretch on the Moran bypass, Dibrugarh; the PM landed there in a C-130J on 14 Feb.
  • New bridge – Kumar Bhaskar Varma Setu – a six-lane extradosed concrete bridge over the Brahmaputra joining Guwahati and North Guwahati.
  • Cost and first – built for about Rs 3,030 crore; it is the first extradosed bridge in the Northeast.
  • Also opened – IIM Guwahati and the National Data Centre for the North-Eastern Region.
  • Earlier ELF – India's first highway landing strip came up on NH-925A in Barmer, Rajasthan, in 2021.

Chips and rapid rail

  • Micron – the PM opened Micron's ATMP (assembly, testing, marking, packaging) unit at Sanand, Gujarat, on 28 Feb.
  • Jewar – the PM laid the foundation of the HCL–Foxconn chip unit near Jewar, Uttar Pradesh, on 21 Feb – the state's first semiconductor unit.
  • Namo Bharat – the full 82-km Delhi–Meerut RRTS corridor opened; trains can run at up to 180 km/h.
  • Operator – the corridor is run by the National Capital Region Transport Corporation (NCRTC).
  • Meerut Metro – it shares the same track as Namo Bharat, a first in India.

Law, courts and states

  • PRAHAAR – on 23 Feb the MHA issued India's first national counter-terrorism policy and strategy.
  • Speaker – the opposition gave notice to remove Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla; such a motion comes under Article 94(c) and needs 50 members to back it.
  • Menstrual health – in Dr Jaya Thakur v. Union of India, the SC read it into the right to life under Article 21; schools must give free sanitary pads and separate toilets.
  • Keralam – on 24 Feb the Union Cabinet cleared renaming Kerala; the Bill goes to the state Assembly for views under Article 3.
  • Manipur – President's Rule, imposed on 13 Feb 2025, ended on 4 Feb 2026 and a new government took office.
PRAHAAR: 'zero tolerance' plan resting on prevention, quick response, rule of law, cutting off radicalisation and global cooperation; the NSG is the national counter-terror force.

Culture and heritage

  • Vande Mataram – new MHA rules: the official version runs about 3 minutes 10 seconds; when sung with the anthem, the national song comes first.
  • Puppet stamps – India Post issued a set of 8 stamps on 'Puppets of India' on 13 Feb, linked to the Ishara puppet festival.
  • Rajaji – on 23 Feb the President unveiled a statue of C. Rajagopalachari at Rashtrapati Bhavan where a bust of Edwin Lutyens stood.
  • About Rajaji – the last Governor-General of India (1948–50) and a Bharat Ratna (1954).
  • Ol Chiki – the President opened the centenary celebrations of the Santhali script on 16 Feb.

Cheetahs, waste and wildlife

  • Cheetahs – nine cheetahs (6 female, 3 male) from Botswana entered quarantine at Kuno, Madhya Pradesh, on 28 Feb; India's count rose to 48.
  • Third batch – earlier groups came from Namibia (2022) and South Africa (2023).
  • Waste rules – Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026 (from 1 April 2026) make four-way sorting compulsory – wet, dry, sanitary and special-care waste.
  • Bee corridors – NHAI will grow strips of flowering plants along national highways for pollinators.
  • Hornbill 'restaurant' – fruit-tree patches for the Malabar pied hornbill in Udanti–Sitanadi Tiger Reserve, Chhattisgarh.
Project Cheetah: started 17 Sep 2022 at Kuno; run by NTCA with technical help from the Wildlife Institute of India; the world's first intercontinental move of a large wild carnivore.

People in the news

  • Thanya Nathan C – from Kerala, she cleared the judicial service exam to become India's first fully visually impaired woman judge.
  • Kabak Yano – the Arunachal climber scaled Mount Aconcagua, South America's highest peak.
  • André Béteille – sociologist and author of 'Caste, Class and Power', died on 3 Feb.
  • Mukul Roy – former Railway Minister and a founder of the TMC, died on 23 Feb.
  • Eastern Nagaland – a three-way pact will set up a Frontier Nagaland Territorial Authority for six eastern districts.

January 2026Monthly notes

Sleeper version of Vande Bharat rolls out

  • Route – Howrah–Kamakhya (Guwahati), about 972 km in roughly 14 hours, cutting about 2.5 hours.
  • Launch – PM flagged it off from Malda Town on 17 Jan 2026; return train flagged off virtually.
  • Train – 16 coaches, 823 berths, fully AC, up to 180 km/h, Kavach safety, regional food in fare.
  • Train numbers – 27575/27576; commercial runs from 22–23 January.
  • Also in Assam – foundation for the 86 km Kaziranga Elevated Corridor on NH-715 (Kaliabor–Numaligarh) to protect wildlife crossings.

Republic Day 2026: what was new

  • Theme – 150 years of 'Vande Mataram'.
  • Chief guests – EU leaders António Costa and Ursula von der Leyen, a first for the EU.
  • Army debuts – 'Battle Array' marching format; Bhairav Light Commando Battalion; Shaktibaan regiment; Suryastra rocket launcher on display.
  • CRPF – Simran Bala led an all-male contingent, a first for a woman officer.
  • Seating names – parade stands named after rivers; Beating Retreat stands after musical instruments.

Governance tools and portals

  • PRAGATI – 50th review meeting held; projects and PM SHRI schools checked.
  • PANCHAM – Panchayati Raj Ministry's WhatsApp chatbot with UNICEF, in many languages via Bhashini.
  • Land Stack – single GIS window for land records under DILRMP; pilots in Chandigarh and Tamil Nadu.
  • IDs and licences – Aadhaar mascot 'Uday' and a new app for up to five family profiles; DGCA's Electronic Personnel Licence for pilots on the eGCA app.
  • SVANidhi card – UPI-linked credit card for street vendors, limit up to Rs 30,000.
PRAGATI: Pro-Active Governance and Timely Implementation – ICT platform started in 2015 and chaired by the PM; links PMO, Union Secretaries and state Chief Secretaries.

Languages, scripts and the past

  • Granth Kutir – manuscript and book room at Rashtrapati Bhavan, opened 23 Jan by the President, covering all 11 classical languages.
  • Classical texts – 55 works in five classical languages released by the Education Minister.
  • Santhali Constitution – released in Ol Chiki script, created in 1925 by Pandit Raghunath Murmu.
  • Kashmir find – 2,000-year-old Kushan-period Buddhist site at Jehanpora, Baramulla.
  • Freedom heroes recalled – Tilka Manjhi (Jabara Paharia), who killed Bhagalpur collector Augustus Cleveland and was hanged in 1785; Rani Velu Nachiyar of Sivaganga.
Classical languages: 11 – Tamil (2004, first), Sanskrit, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Odia, and in Oct 2024 Marathi, Pali, Prakrit, Assamese, Bengali.

Wetlands list reaches 98

  • New sites – Patna Bird Sanctuary (Etah, UP) and Chhari-Dhand (Kutch, Gujarat), announced 30–31 Jan before World Wetlands Day.
  • Patna sanctuary – UP's smallest bird sanctuary, an Important Bird Area.
  • Chhari-Dhand – seasonal salty shallow lake between Banni grassland and the Rann; 'chhari' means salty.
  • Growth – India had 26 Ramsar sites in 2014; India joined the convention in 1982.
  • Other green news – second Project Dolphin survey from Bijnor; Mission Mausam for sharper forecasts; BNHS project for the Indian skimmer.

State and city firsts

  • Jharkhand – notified PESA rules.
  • Tamil Nadu – first DeepTech Startup Policy.
  • Delhi – IGI became the first water-positive airport; Atal Canteens launched.
  • Visakhapatnam – country's longest glass skywalk.
  • Tripura – Matabari Tourism Circuit around Tripura Sundari temple.

People in the news

  • Kamya Karthikeyan – at 18, the youngest Indian to ski to the South Pole.
  • Ajit Pawar – six-time Deputy CM of Maharashtra, died in a plane crash at Baramati.
  • Madhav Gadgil – ecologist, head of the Western Ghats Ecology Expert Panel (2011), died.
  • Also died – Suresh Kalmadi, former IOA chief; Mark Tully, BBC's voice of India.
