Every spot the error question asks one thing: which part of this sentence breaks a grammar rule? The sentence is split into parts, and exactly one part is wrong — or none is, when a No error option is given. The fastest route is a fixed checking order: find the subject and its verb, check the tense, then the pronouns, articles and prepositions, and only then the smaller points of comparison and word order.

Marks are lost in three places. The first is letting a nearby noun decide the verb — “The quality of the mangoes are” sounds right because “mangoes” sits next to the verb. The second is trusting the ear: “discuss about”, “senior than” and “I wish I was” sound normal in speech but are wrong in exam English. The third is marking a correct part out of suspicion — “The news is” and “A number of students are” look wrong and are right.

How Many Spot the Error Questions Are Asked in SSC CGL?

Spot the error is a fixed part of the English Language section — usually two or three questions in a Tier I shift, more in the Tier II English paper, and the same type turns up in CHSL, MTS and CPO. The grammar areas behind those questions are listed below, most common first.

Check the subject and its verb first — that one step catches the largest share of errors.

The three mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Agreeing with the wrong noun. In “One of my cousins live in Pune”, the subject is “One”, so the verb is “lives”.
  • Trusting what sounds normal. “He insisted for a receipt” is common in speech; the exam wants “insisted on”.
  • Distrusting a correct part. “Mathematics is my favourite subject” has no error — the -s does not make it plural.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Spot the Error

Q1. Each of the boys / have submitted / his project / on time.
(A) Each of the boys   (B) have submitted   (C) his project   (D) on time
The subject is ‘Each’, not ‘boys’, and ‘each’ takes a singular verb. ‘have submitted’ must be ‘has submitted’. Answer: (B)
Q2. The branch manager / is senior than / all the other clerks / in the office.
(A) The branch manager   (B) is senior than   (C) all the other clerks   (D) in the office
Senior, junior, superior, inferior and prefer take ‘to’, never ‘than’. ‘is senior than’ must be ‘is senior to’. Answer: (B)
Q3. No sooner did the bell ring / when the students / rushed out of the classroom.
(A) No sooner did the bell ring   (B) when the students   (C) rushed out of the classroom   (D) No error
‘No sooner’ is always paired with ‘than’; ‘when’ goes with ‘hardly’ and ‘scarcely’. ‘when the students’ must be ‘than the students’. Answer: (B)
Q4. Neither the captain nor the players / were happy / with the umpire’s decision.
(A) Neither the captain nor the players   (B) were happy   (C) with the umpire’s decision   (D) No error
With neither…nor, the verb agrees with the nearer subject. ‘the players’ is plural, so ‘were’ is right, and the other parts are correct too. Answer: (D) No error

How to Prepare Spot the Error for SSC CGL

  1. Find the subject and the verb first. Strike out phrases such as ‘of the mangoes’ or ‘along with his friends’ and match the verb to what is left.
  2. Check the tense against the time words. Since, for, yesterday, ago, before, by the time — each one fixes a tense.
  3. Learn the fixed pairs as pairs. Senior to, comply with, insist on, no sooner…than, not only…but also, both…and.
  4. Test every part, not just the suspicious one. Choose No error only when all parts have passed a rule.
  5. Write the corrected sentence in your head. If the fix needs words from two parts, look again — the error usually sits in one.
  6. Do Level 1 untimed first, then with the 15-minute clock. Move to Level 2 once Level 1 is steady, and then check yourself on an SSC CGL English previous year paper.

SSC CGL Spot the Error — Frequently Asked Questions

How many spot the error questions are asked in SSC CGL?
Usually two or three in an SSC CGL Tier I shift, inside the 25-question English section, and several more in the Tier II English paper. SSC CHSL, MTS and CPO ask the same question type, so the practice carries across exams.
How do I find the error quickly?
Find the subject and its verb first, then check the tense, then the pronouns, articles and prepositions. Most errors sit in one of those places. Test each part on its own and only choose No error after every part has passed.
Which grammar rules come up most often?
Subject-verb agreement comes first by a distance — each, every, one of, along with, neither…nor, the number of. After that come tense, prepositions after particular words, pronoun case, articles, and comparison words such as senior to and more better.
When is No error the right answer?
Only when every part is correct. No-error items usually carry one construction that looks wrong but is right — ’The news is’, ’Mathematics is’, ’A number of students are’, ’had better go’. Choose No error only after checking each part against a rule.
Does SSC follow formal grammar or everyday usage?
Formal grammar. SSC keys ’I wish I were’, ’between you and me’, ’senior to’, ’each + singular verb’ and ’among’ for more than two, even where casual English is looser. The solutions on this page follow the same rules.
How many questions are in each test?
Twenty-five questions per test, with an optional 15-minute timer and the real exam marking of +2 for a correct answer, −0.5 for a wrong one and 0 for an unattempted question.
Are the tests available in Hindi?
No. Spot the Error is an English Language chapter, so the questions, options and solutions are in English, just as they are in the exam. Every solution is written in short, simple sentences.
What is the difference between Level 1 and Level 2?
Level 1 is set at real SSC CGL difficulty — shorter sentences and one clear rule. Level 2 is harder than the exam: longer sentences, tempting parts that are actually correct, more No-error items, and subtler rules such as inversion, conditionals, any other and that of.
