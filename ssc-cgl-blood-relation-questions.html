Blood relations is a chapter of one small family tree. Every question — a direct statement, a man pointing at a photograph, a woman introducing someone, a coded line such as P + Q × R, or a puzzle about six family members — describes a few links between people. Draw those links once, generation by generation, and the asked relation can be read straight off the tree.

Marks are lost in three places. The first is assuming a gender — a name, a profession or the word ‘child’ does not tell you whether a person is male or female, and ‘cannot be determined’ is often the right answer. The second is mixing up the two sides of the family — a father’s brother and a mother’s brother are both ‘uncle’ in English but chacha and mama in Hindi, and SSC asks for the side. The third is reading a pointing sentence from the front — start from the speaker’s end and work backwards instead.

Blood relations gives one question in a typical Tier I shift, sometimes two, and a few more in Tier II Paper I. It is one of the surest marks in reasoning — a well-drawn tree answers it in under a minute.

What You Will Practice in the Blood Relations Chapter

Family-tree chains

  • Short and long direct chains — two links up to six — across three and four generations
  • In-law steps: father-in-law, mother-in-law, brother-in-law, sister-in-law, son-in-law and daughter-in-law
  • Paternal versus maternal side — father’s brother or mother’s brother, father’s sister or mother’s sister, and the cousins on each side
  • Generation gaps, the relation between two derived people, and reverse relations (if A is B’s nephew, B is A’s …)

Pointing, introducing and ‘only’-clauses

  • Pointing at a person or a photograph, with one, two or three nested possessives
  • Introducing someone — ‘her mother is the only daughter of my mother’
  • Self-reference clauses — ‘my father’s only son’, ‘my mother’s only daughter’
  • Speaker–listener chains — ‘your mother is the sister of my father’

Coded relations and puzzles

  • Symbol codes (+, −, ×, ÷, $, #) with three or four people, and long expressions with six or seven
  • Which expression means the given relation, which statement is (NOT) correct, and the missing symbol
  • Digit and word codes — ‘P 3 Q’, ‘P ka Q’
  • Family paragraphs, married-couple puzzles, age order, counting members, and statement sufficiency

Rules worth knowing by heart

RuleWhat to remember
Tree symbols+ male, − female; double line = married couple; single vertical line = parent to child; horizontal line = siblings
‘Only son’ / ‘only daughter’‘My father’s only son’ is the speaker himself when the speaker is male; ‘my mother’s only daughter’ is the speaker herself when the speaker is female
Pointing sentencesRead from the speaker’s end: start at ‘my’ and collapse one possessive at a time

Brother-in-lawSpouse’s brother, sister’s husband — and in many questions, spouse’s sister’s husband

Coded expressionsDecode left to right, one pair at a time, and mark each gender as soon as a symbol fixes it

Draw the tree before you look at the options — the options are made from the relations you get by misreading one link.

The three mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Assuming a gender. ‘A is the child of B’ says nothing about A — wait for a word like son, wife or brother.
  • Taking the wrong side of the family. A mother’s brother is mama, not chacha; a father’s sister’s son is a phupera bhai, not a mamera bhai.
  • Assuming sibling means the same gender. A is the sibling of B and B is the brother of C — A can still be a sister.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Blood Relation Questions

Q1. Pointing to a man, Riya says, “He is the son of my father’s father’s only son.” How is the man related to Riya?
Riya’s father’s father is her paternal grandfather, and his only son is Riya’s father. The son of Riya’s father is Riya’s brother. Answer: Brother
Q2. A is the brother of B, B is the sister of C, and C is the father of D. How is A related to D?
A, B and C are siblings, and A is male. C is D’s father, so A is the brother of D’s father — D’s paternal uncle. Answer: Uncle (chacha or tau)
Q3. ‘P + Q’ means P is the father of Q, ‘P − Q’ means P is the wife of Q and ‘P × Q’ means P is the brother of Q. In M + N × O − K, how is M related to K?
M is the father of N; N is the brother of O, so M is O’s father too; O is the wife of K. The father of K’s wife is K’s father-in-law. Answer: Father-in-law
Q4. Introducing a woman, Arun says, “Her mother is the only daughter of my mother.” How is Arun related to the woman?
Arun is male, so the only daughter of Arun’s mother is Arun’s sister. The woman’s mother is Arun’s sister, so Arun is her mother’s brother. Answer: Maternal uncle (mama)
Q5. In a family of five — K, L, M, N and O — K is the husband of L, and M and N are their children. O is L’s son-in-law and is married to N. How is O related to M?
O is a son-in-law, so O is male and N is his wife. M is N’s sibling, and the husband of one’s sister is one’s brother-in-law. Answer: Brother-in-law

How to Prepare Blood Relations for SSC CGL

  1. Learn the relation names on both sides. Chacha, tau and bua on the father’s side; mama and mausi on the mother’s side; and the six in-law terms. Most wrong options are the right person on the wrong side.
  2. Draw the tree every time. Generations on separate rows, + and − for gender, a double line for couples — it takes ten seconds and removes guesswork.
  3. Mark a gender only when it is proved. Leave a question mark beside a person whose gender is not given, and check it before you choose.
  4. Work pointing sentences backwards. Begin at ‘my’, replace each possessive with the person it names, and the chain shrinks to one link.
  5. For coded relations, decode one pair at a time. Write each pair as a link on the tree as you go, then read the asked relation from the first person to the last.
  6. Do Level 1 untimed first, then with the 15-minute clock. Move to Level 2 once Level 1 is steady, and then check yourself on a previous year paper.

SSC CGL Blood Relation Questions — Frequently Asked Questions

What is blood relation in SSC CGL reasoning?
It is the verbal reasoning chapter in which you work out how two people are related from a chain of family links — a direct statement, a person pointing at a photograph, someone introducing another person, a coded expression such as P + Q × R, or a short family puzzle. You draw a small family tree and read the asked relation off it.
How many blood relation questions come in SSC CGL?
Usually one question in a Tier I shift, sometimes two, with a few more in Tier II Paper I. Pointing and introducing statements and coded (symbol) relations are the most common forms; family puzzles and in-law questions appear regularly in CGL, CHSL and MTS.
What is the fastest way to solve a pointing or introducing question?
Start from the far end of the sentence — the speaker’s own relatives — and work backwards one possessive at a time. ‘My father’s only son’ is the speaker himself if the speaker is male, so a long chain often collapses to one or two steps.
How do I draw a family tree for blood relation questions?
Put each generation on its own row, join married couples with a double line and parents to children with a single vertical line, and mark every person + for male and − for female. Write the gender only when the question proves it — an unknown gender is often the whole point of the question.
When is the answer ‘cannot be determined’?
When the relation depends on a gender the question never states. If A is the child of B’s brother and A’s gender is not given, A is B’s nephew or niece — so an option that says only nephew is not proved.
How many questions are in each blood relation practice test?
Twenty-five questions per test, with an optional 15-minute timer and the real exam marking scheme of +2 for a correct answer, −0.5 for a wrong one and 0 for an unattempted question.
Are the blood relation practice tests available in Hindi?
Yes. Questions, options, detailed solutions and shortcut tricks are available in Hindi, with the correct Hindi terms for each side of the family — chacha, mama, bua, mausi, and the in-law terms — and you choose English or Hindi before the test starts.
What is the difference between Level 1 and Level 2?
Level 1 is set at real SSC CGL difficulty — two to four links per question and coded expressions with three or four people. Level 2 is harder than the exam: chains of five or six links, nested possessives, coded expressions with six or seven people, ‘which statement is NOT correct’, statement sufficiency and family puzzles where every gender has to be deduced.
