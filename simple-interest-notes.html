Simple Interest

Simple interest is the same amount every year. That one fact — equal yearly interest — solves most questions faster than any formula.

1. Concepts in brief

Principal, rate, time

The principal (P) is the money lent or borrowed. The rate (R) is the interest charged per ₹100 per year. The time (T) is measured in years. Interest is the extra money paid for using the principal.

Why simple interest grows in a straight line

In simple interest, interest is calculated only on the original principal — never on interest already earned. So the interest is the same every year: ₹1000 at 10% earns ₹100 in year 1, ₹100 in year 2, ₹100 in year 3. This straight-line growth is what makes questions with two amounts so quick.

Mixing two rates

When a sum is split into two parts lent at different rates, the overall rate is a weighted average of the two. Use alligation to find how much was lent at each rate.

2. Key formulas

QuantityFormula
Simple interestSI = P × R × T / 100
AmountA = P + SI = P(1 + RT/100)
Principal, rate, timeP = 100·SI/(RT), R = 100·SI/(PT), T = 100·SI/(PR)
Sum doubles in T yearsR = 100/T
Sum becomes n times in T yearsR = 100(n − 1)/T
n times in T years → m times inT(m − 1)/(n − 1) years
Equal annual instalment x clearing A in n yearsx = 100A / [100n + n(n − 1)R/2]

3. Shortcuts that save time

Use the yearly interest If a sum amounts to A₁ in t₁ years and A₂ in t₂ years, the interest for one year is (A₂ − A₁)/(t₂ − t₁). Subtract t₁ years of it from A₁ to get the principal.
  • Interest for T years at R% = RT% of the principal. 5 years at 8% = 40% of P.
  • Months → divide by 12; days → divide by 365.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 SI on ₹8000 at 6% for 3 years = 8000 × 6 × 3/100 = ₹1440.
Example 2 A sum doubles in 8 years. Rate = 100/8 = 12½%. It triples in 8 × (3 − 1)/(2 − 1) = 16 years.
Example 3 A sum becomes ₹7200 in 2 years and ₹7800 in 3 years. One year's interest = 600, so P = 7200 − 1200 = ₹6000 and R = 600/6000 = 10%.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 4 SI on ₹5000 at 8% for 9 months = 5000 × 8 × 9/(100 × 12) = ₹300.
Example 5 In how many years will ₹2500 earn ₹1000 at 8%? T = 1000 × 100/(2500 × 8) = 5 years.
Example 6 ₹10,000 is lent, part at 5% and the rest at 8%; one year's total interest is ₹680, an overall rate of 6.8%. By alligation, 5% part : 8% part = (8 − 6.8) : (6.8 − 5) = 2 : 3 → ₹4000 at 5%, ₹6000 at 8%.
Example 7 ₹4000 is lent at 6% for the first 2 years and 9% for the next 3. Interest = 4000 × (12 + 27)/100 = ₹1560.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. SI on ₹12,000 at 7.5% for 4 years?

Answer: ₹3600.

Q2. A sum becomes 4 times itself in 15 years at SI. Rate?

Answer: 20% (100 × 3/15).

Q3. SI is one-fourth of the principal in 5 years. Rate?

Answer: 5% (25% in 5 years).

Q4. ₹6000 amounts to ₹7200 in 4 years. Rate?

Answer: 5% (1200 × 100/(6000 × 4)).

Q5. In how many years does a sum double at 10% SI?

Answer: 10 years (100/10).

6. Traps to avoid

  1. "Doubles" means interest = P (n = 2), not 2P.
  2. Convert months and days to years before using the formula.
  3. If the rate changes midway, add the interest for each period separately.
  4. Don't confuse amount with interest in the question — read which one is given.
