Ratio & Proportion

Concepts, formulas, solved examples and practice · Quantitative Aptitude

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind. Most questions reduce to one idea: turn the ratio into parts, find what one part is worth, then answer.

1. Concepts in brief

A ratio is a comparison in parts

"Boys to girls is 3 : 2" does not say there are 3 boys. It says that for every 3 boys there are 2 girls — the class is split into 5 equal parts, 3 of them boys. Turn every ratio into parts, find what one part is worth from the data, and multiply back.

Proportion is two equal ratios

When a : b = c : d, the four numbers are in proportion. Cross-multiplying gives ad = bc, which finds any one missing term. The mean, third and fourth proportionals in the table are just this rule with one value unknown.

Direct and inverse variation

If doubling one quantity doubles the other (cost and number of items), they vary directly. If doubling one halves the other (workers and days for a fixed job, speed and time for a fixed distance), they vary inversely.

3. Shortcuts that save time

Join two ratios with a common term A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5. Make B equal: multiply first by 4, second by 3 → A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15.
Change after adding or removing If the ratio is a : b and x is added to both, new ratio c : d, then x = (bc − ad)/(d − c). This handles every "added the same number" problem in one line.
  • Divide a total T in ratio a : b : c → shares are T·a/(a+b+c) and so on.
  • Ratio of incomes and expenditures: savings = income − expenditure, so put both on a common-term basis first.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 Divide ₹1760 among A, B, C in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5. One part = 1760/10 = 176. A = 352, B = 528, C = 880.
Example 2 What number should be added to both terms of 7 : 11 to make it 3 : 4?
x = (11 × 3 − 7 × 4)/(4 − 3) = (33 − 28)/1 = 5. Check: 12 : 16 = 3 : 4 ✓.
Example 3 If (x + y)/(x − y) = 5/3, find x : y. By componendo–dividendo, x/y = (5 + 3)/(5 − 3) = 8/2 = 4 : 1.
Example 4 Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 5 and their LCM is 150. The numbers are 3k and 5k with LCM 15k = 150, so k = 10 and the numbers are 30 and 50.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 5 The incomes of A and B are in the ratio 5 : 4 and their expenditures 3 : 2. Each saves ₹1600. Incomes 5x and 4x, expenditures 3y and 2y: 5x − 3y = 1600 and 4x − 2y = 1600 give x = 800. Incomes are ₹4000 and ₹3200.
Example 6 Fourth proportional to 4, 9 and 12 = 9 × 12/4 = 27.
Example 7 Mean proportional of 0.08 and 0.18 = √0.0144 = 0.12.
Example 8 A bag has ₹1, 50 p and 25 p coins in the number ratio 1 : 2 : 4, worth ₹60 in all. Their values are in the ratio 1 : 1 : 1, so each kind is worth ₹20: 20, 40 and 80 coins.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. A : B = 3 : 4 and B : C = 6 : 7. Find A : B : C.

Answer: 9 : 12 : 14 (make B = 12).

Q2. Divide ₹900 in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4.

Answer: ₹200, ₹300, ₹400.

Q3. Find the third proportional of 9 and 12.

Answer: 16 (12²/9).

Q4. What must be added to each of 5, 9, 7 and 12 to make them proportional?

Answer: 3 (8 : 12 = 10 : 15).

Q5. If x : y = 3 : 4, find (2x + 3y) : (3x + 2y).

Answer: 18 : 17 (put x = 3, y = 4).

6. Traps to avoid

  1. Adding the same number to both terms does not keep the ratio equal — it moves it toward 1 : 1.
  2. Join ratios on the common term; do not multiply straight across.
  3. For ratio of squares or areas, square the ratio of sides, and take the root to go back.
  4. Check what is asked: a share, the whole, or the difference between two shares.
  5. Do not reduce a ratio before using it with actual quantities — reduce only to compare.
