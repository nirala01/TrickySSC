Ancient Indian History is a fact chapter. SSC does not ask you to explain why the Mauryan empire declined; it asks who wrote the Indica, where the Great Bath was found, or which Gupta king issued the first silver coins. The options are always four names from the same table — four Harappan sites, four Buddhist councils, four Gupta rulers — so a half-remembered fact is exactly what the paper is built to catch.

Marks are lost in three places. The first is look-alike names: Chandragupta Maurya and Chandragupta I and II, Kalashoka and Ashoka, Kanishka's council and Ashoka's council. The second is site–state–river pairs: Kalibangan on the Ghaggar in Rajasthan, Lothal on the Bhogava in Gujarat, Manda on the Chenab. The third is text–author pairs: Mudrarakshasa and Vishakhadatta, Harshacharita and Banabhatta, Silappadikaram and Ilango Adigal. The tests here are built around exactly these three.

The three traps that cost the most marks

  • Same name, different king. Chandragupta Maurya founded the Mauryan empire; Chandragupta I started the Gupta era; Chandragupta II was Vikramaditya. Read the period in the question before the name.
  • The extreme answer in a statement question. “Only”, “first”, “largest” and “northernmost” are where SSC hides the wrong statement — check those words first.
  • Mixing up the councils. Four Buddhist councils and two Jain councils, each with its own place, king and president — learn them as one table, not as six separate facts.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Ancient History

Q1. Who wrote the Ashtadhyayi, the oldest surviving work on Sanskrit grammar?
(a) Panini
(b) Patanjali
(c) Yaska
(d) Pingala
Panini wrote the Ashtadhyayi, a grammar of Sanskrit in about 4,000 short rules (sutras). Patanjali later wrote the Mahabhashya, a commentary on it; Yaska wrote the Nirukta on Vedic word meanings, and Pingala wrote on metre. Answer: (a)
Q2. Consider the following statements about the Ellora caves:
1. They are in Maharashtra.
2. They include Buddhist, Hindu and Jain caves.
3. The Kailasa temple there is carved out of a single rock.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3
All three are correct. Ellora, in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad) district of Maharashtra, has 34 caves of three faiths side by side, and the Kailasa temple (Cave 16) was cut downward from one rock under the Rashtrakutas. Answer: (d)
Q3. Read the Assertion (A) and Reason (R) carefully.
Assertion (A): The Gupta period is often called the Classical Age of ancient India.
Reason (R): Sanskrit literature, art and science reached a high level in this period.
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
(c) A is true, but R is false
(d) A is false, but R is true
The Gupta age gave Kalidasa's plays and poems, Aryabhata's astronomy and the Ajanta paintings — that flowering is the very reason it is called the Classical Age. Answer: (a)
Q4. ‘सुश्रुत संहिता’ का मुख्य संबंध किस विषय से है?
(a) शल्य चिकित्सा (सर्जरी)
(b) खगोल विज्ञान
(c) व्याकरण
(d) संगीत
सुश्रुत संहिता प्राचीन भारत का शल्य चिकित्सा (सर्जरी) का प्रमुख ग्रंथ है; इसीलिए सुश्रुत को ‘शल्य चिकित्सा का जनक’ कहा जाता है। चरक संहिता का संबंध औषधि (मेडिसिन) से है। उत्तर: (a)

How to Prepare Ancient History for SSC CGL

  1. Learn it as tables. Harappan site – river – state – excavator; Buddhist council – place – king – president; Mahajanapada – capital. SSC questions come straight out of such tables.
  2. Keep a timeline in front of you. Harappa, Vedic, Mahajanapadas, Maurya, post-Maurya, Gupta, post-Gupta — placing every fact on it stops you mixing up rulers with the same name.
  3. Pair every text with its author. Indica – Megasthenes, Arthashastra – Kautilya, Harshacharita – Banabhatta, Tolkappiyam – Tolkappiyar. Text–author pairs are among the most repeated questions.
  4. Read the solutions, not just the answer. Each solution here carries six or seven related facts — reading them is how 300 questions cover the whole chapter.
  5. Revise in short rounds. One test a day keeps the earlier periods fresh while you move ahead, and then check yourself on a previous year paper.
