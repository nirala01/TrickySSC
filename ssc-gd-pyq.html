What is SSC GD Constable?

SSC GD (Staff Selection Commission, Constable General Duty) is the largest uniformed-services recruitment in the country for 10th-pass candidates. The Commission recruits Constables (GD) for the Central Armed Police Forces — BSF, CISF, CRPF, SSB and ITBP — along with the Secretariat Security Force, Rifleman (GD) in Assam Rifles and, in some cycles, Sepoy in the Narcotics Control Bureau and posts in the NIA.

The 2026 cycle alone notified 25,487 vacancies and drew several lakh candidates to a single computer-based examination held from 27 April to 30 May 2026; 1,97,408 of them were shortlisted for the physical tests when the result came on 12 October 2026. Because the written stage is one 80-question paper, the difference between selection and rejection is usually a handful of marks — which is exactly what previous year paper practice protects.

⏱ Duration: 60 minutes (80 minutes for eligible candidates with a scribe)

Key highlights:

  • Objective-type computer-based examination, offered in Hindi, English and 13 regional languages (Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada, Konkani, Malayalam, Manipuri, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Tamil, Telugu and Urdu).
  • 80 questions carrying 160 marks, 2 marks each.
  • Negative marking of 0.25 marks for each incorrect answer.
  • Marks are normalised across shifts; the CBE score decides the final merit for candidates who clear PET/PST and the medical examination.

Selection stages after the CBE

  • Physical Efficiency Test (PET): male candidates run 5 km in 24 minutes, female candidates 1.6 km in 8½ minutes (Ladakh: 1.6 km in 7 minutes and 800 m in 5 minutes).
  • Physical Standard Test (PST): height 170 cm (male) and 157 cm (female) for the general category, chest 80 cm with 5 cm expansion for male candidates, with the relaxations the notification lists for ST and hill-area candidates.
  • Detailed Medical Examination and document verification; the final merit is prepared from the CBE marks, category-wise and force-wise.

SSC GD Syllabus

SSC GD tests candidates across four parts of equal weight.

General Intelligence & Reasoning

Analogies, similarities and differences, spatial visualisation and orientation, visual memory, discrimination, observation, relationship concepts, arithmetical reasoning, figural classification, number and alphabet series, coding-decoding, blood relations, direction sense, order and ranking, and figure questions (mirror images, paper folding, embedded figures). Reasoning PYQs

General Knowledge & General Awareness

India and its neighbouring countries, sports, history, culture, geography, economic scene, general polity, the Indian Constitution, scientific research and current events of national and international importance. GK PYQs

Elementary Mathematics

Number systems, computation of whole numbers, decimals and fractions, relationships between numbers, fundamental arithmetical operations, percentages, ratio and proportion, averages, interest, profit and loss, discount, mensuration, time and distance, ratio and time, time and work. Maths PYQs

English / Hindi

Basic comprehension and writing ability: spot the error, fill in the blanks, synonyms, antonyms, spelling, idioms, one-word substitution, sentence improvement, voice, narration, cloze passage and reading comprehension (the Hindi paper tests the equivalent Hindi vyakaran, shabd gyan and gadyansh). SSC GD Preparation Strategy

A workable SSC GD strategy combines concept building, daily GK revision, previous year paper practice, full-length mock tests and a running plan for the PET.

Finish the basics first, then move to previous year papers to learn how SSC actually frames questions. Because the CBE gives you only 45 seconds per question on average, speed practice matters as much as knowledge — and the −0.25 penalty means a guess has to be better than one-in-eight odds to be worth it.

A recommended approach:

  1. Complete the four-part syllabus topic-wise.
  2. Give GK and current affairs fifteen minutes every day.
  3. Solve SSC GD previous year papers consistently, newest shifts first.
  4. Attempt a full-length mock test every week, then one a day in the final three weeks.
  5. Analyse every mistake after each test — concept gap, careless slip or time loss.
  6. Fix accuracy before chasing attempts.
  7. Track your score trend against the previous cut-off (146 out of 160 for UR male in 2026).
  8. Run three or four times a week from the start so the PET is a formality, not a hurdle.

Consistent practice and honest analysis usually contribute more to a selection than starting yet another new book.
