Compound interest is interest on interest. Learn the effective-rate figures in section 2 and you can skip the power calculations in most two- and three-year questions.

1. Concepts in brief

Interest on interest

In compound interest, the interest earned each period is added to the principal, and the next period's interest is calculated on this bigger amount. ₹1000 at 10%: year 1 interest ₹100 (amount 1100), year 2 interest ₹110 (amount 1210), year 3 interest ₹121 (amount 1331).

SI and CI compared

For the first year, SI and CI are always equal. From the second year on, CI is bigger because it also earns on the earlier interest. The CI − SI difference formulas in the table come straight from this.

Compounding more often

"Compounded half-yearly" means interest is added every 6 months. Each half-year uses half the yearly rate, and the number of periods doubles. Quarterly uses a quarter of the rate and four times the periods.

Successive-year amounts

If you know the amount after n years and after n + 1 years, their ratio is 1 + R/100. This gives the rate in one step.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 CI on ₹10,000 at 10% for 2 years = 21% of 10,000 = ₹2100.
Example 2 CI − SI for 2 years at 5% is ₹25. P = 25 × (100/5)² = 25 × 400 = ₹10,000.
Example 3 A sum doubles in 4 years at CI. It becomes 8 times (2³) in 12 years.
Example 4 ₹16,000 at 10% per annum, compounded half-yearly, for 1 year: 16000 × 1.05² = 17640, so CI = ₹1640.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 5 CI on ₹8000 at 5% for 3 years: 8000 × 1.05³ = 9261, so CI = ₹1261.
Example 6 An amount of ₹12,100 is due after 2 years at 10% CI. Principal = 12100/1.21 = ₹10,000.
Example 7 A sum becomes ₹4840 in 2 years and ₹5324 in 3 years at CI. Rate = 484/4840 = 10%; principal = 4840/1.21 = ₹4000.
Example 8 CI − SI on ₹10,000 for 3 years at 10% = 10000 × (0.1)² × 3.1 = ₹310.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. CI on ₹5000 at 20% for 2 years?

Answer: ₹2200 (44% of 5000).

Q2. CI on ₹10,000 at 10% for 2½ years?

Answer: ₹2705 (10000 × 1.21 × 1.05 = 12705).

Q3. CI on ₹1000 for 2 years at 10% for year 1 and 20% for year 2?

Answer: ₹320 (1000 × 1.1 × 1.2 = 1320).

Q4. A sum triples in 5 years at CI. In how many years will it become 27 times?

Answer: 15 years (27 = 3³).

Q5. CI − SI for 2 years at 10% is ₹50. Find the principal.

Answer: ₹5000 (50 × 100).

6. Traps to avoid

  1. Half-yearly means halve the rate and double the time — both.
  2. For 2½ years, the half year uses R/2 simple — don't raise to the power 2.5.
  3. P(R/100)² is the CI−SI difference for 2 years only.
  4. "Becomes 8 times" is 2³, so the time triples, it doesn't become 4 times.
