Chemistry in SSC is a fact chapter. The paper almost never asks you to balance an equation or calculate; it asks the chemical name or formula of a common substance, which element, ore or alloy is used for what, or why an everyday effect happens. Curd tasting sour, an iron nail rusting, chips packed with nitrogen, milk of magnesia easing acidity — each is one line of theory turned into a question.

Marks are lost in three places. The first is common names and formulas: baking soda against washing soda, plaster of Paris against gypsum, and the three vitriols. The second is ores and alloys: which ore belongs to which metal, and which metals make brass, bronze or German silver. The third is look-alike facts in the periodic table and in acids and bases: Mendeleev against Moseley, which indicator turns which colour, and which natural acid is found in which food. The tests here are built around exactly these three.

The three traps that cost the most marks

  • Baking soda or washing soda. Baking soda is sodium hydrogen carbonate, NaHCO3, used in baking powder, antacids and soda-acid fire extinguishers. Washing soda is sodium carbonate decahydrate, Na2CO3·10H2O, used to remove the permanent hardness of water. SSC often swaps the two formulas.
  • Plaster of Paris or gypsum. Plaster of Paris is CaSO4·½H2O; gypsum is CaSO4·2H2O. Heating gypsum at 373 K gives plaster of Paris, and mixing plaster of Paris with water sets it back into hard gypsum.
  • Mendeleev or Moseley. Mendeleev arranged the elements by atomic mass and left gaps for undiscovered elements; Moseley showed in 1913 that atomic number is the more fundamental property, which is the basis of the modern periodic law.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Chemistry

Q1. What is the chemical formula of washing soda?
(a) NaHCO3
(b) Na2CO3·10H2O
(c) CaOCl2
(d) NaOH
Washing soda is sodium carbonate decahydrate, Na2CO3·10H2O. NaHCO3 is baking soda, CaOCl2 is bleaching powder and NaOH is caustic soda. Answer: (b)
Q2. Consider the following statements:
1. Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc.
2. Bronze is an alloy of copper and nickel.
3. Solder is an alloy of lead and tin.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3
Bronze is an alloy of copper and tin, not nickel — statement 2 is wrong. Brass (copper and zinc) and solder (lead and tin) are correct; solder melts at a lower temperature than either of its metals, which is why it is used to join electrical wires. Answer: (c)
Q3. Read the Assertion (A) and Reason (R) carefully.
Assertion (A): Iron articles are galvanised to protect them from rusting.
Reason (R): Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it corrodes in preference to iron even if the zinc coating is scratched.
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
(c) A is true, but R is false
(d) A is false, but R is true
Galvanisation is coating iron or steel with a thin layer of zinc. Zinc lies above iron in the reactivity series, so it is oxidised first and keeps protecting the iron even where the coating is broken. R explains why galvanising works. Answer: (a)
Q4. ओज़ोन (O3) किस तत्व का अपररूप है?
(a) नाइट्रोजन
(b) ऑक्सीजन
(c) हाइड्रोजन
(d) कार्बन
ओज़ोन ऑक्सीजन का अपररूप है; इसके एक अणु में ऑक्सीजन के तीन परमाणु होते हैं, जबकि साधारण ऑक्सीजन (O2) द्विपरमाणुक होती है। समतापमंडल की ओज़ोन परत सूर्य की पराबैंगनी किरणों को पृथ्वी तक पहुँचने से रोकती है। उत्तर: (b)

How to Prepare Chemistry for SSC CGL

  1. Keep one common-names table. Common name – chemical name – formula – use, for baking soda, washing soda, bleaching powder, plaster of Paris, gypsum, quicklime, slaked lime, the vitriols, dry ice and laughing gas. This single page answers a large share of SSC chemistry questions.
  2. Learn metals as ore, alloy and use. Bauxite for aluminium, haematite for iron, cinnabar for mercury; brass, bronze, solder and German silver; zinc for galvanising and tungsten for bulb filaments — SSC asks the pairing, not the theory.
  3. Pair every acid and indicator with one example. Lactic acid in curd, citric acid in lemon, tartaric acid in tamarind, formic acid in an ant sting; phenolphthalein pink in a base, blue litmus red in an acid.
  4. Read the solutions, not just the answer. Each solution here carries six or seven related facts — reading them is how 150 questions cover the whole chapter.
  5. Check yourself on real questions. One test a day keeps the earlier topics fresh while you move ahead, and the Chemistry previous year questions show exactly how SSC words them.

SSC CGL Chemistry — Frequently Asked Questions

How many Chemistry questions are asked in SSC CGL?
Usually one or two of the 25 General Awareness questions in an SSC CGL Tier I shift. General Science as a whole gives four to six, and chemistry is asked mostly as common names, formulas and everyday uses. CHSL, MTS and the Tier II General Awareness paper follow the same pattern.
Which Chemistry topics are most important for SSC?
Acids, bases and salts, common names and formulas of compounds, and metals, ores and alloys come up most often, followed by carbon compounds and polymers, atomic structure and isotopes, the periodic table, and fuels and combustion.
How many tests and questions are in this chapter?
Six tests of 25 questions each, 150 questions in all, in chapter order: chemical reactions and matter, mixtures and atomic structure, carbon compounds and the early periodic tables, the modern periodic table and fuels, acids, bases, salts and metals, and ores, alloys and everyday chemicals. Very rarely. SSC asks chemistry mostly as names, formulas, properties and uses, so the tests do the same; where a formula or value appears, such as the formula of plaster of Paris or the pH of pure water, it is a standard fact you are expected to remember.
What do the solutions contain?
Six to seven short points for every question: the answer, the formula or reaction behind it, the related compounds, uses and examples, and what each wrong option actually refers to.
