A mensuration question is a formula waiting to be picked. Once you know which solid the question is about — a cylinder, a cone, a frustum, a hemisphere, or two of them joined — the working is usually two lines. The skill is naming the solid and the quantity before you calculate: curved surface or total surface, volume or capacity, the outer radius or the inner one.

The marks are lost in three places. The first is using the height where the slant height is needed — a cone's or frustum's curved surface needs l, not h. The second is counting the wrong surfaces: an open bucket has no top, a pipe has no ends, and joining two solids hides the faces that touch. The third is mixing units — centimetres with metres, litres with cubic centimetres. Each test puts twenty-five mensuration questions in front of you in one sitting, which is what makes those checks automatic.

The three mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Height in place of slant height. The curved surface of a cone or frustum needs l. With radius 7 and height 24, the slant is 25 — using 24 gives a wrong option that is always there.
  • Wrong set of surfaces. Open tanks and buckets have no top, pipes have no ends, and a hemisphere on a cylinder hides the circle where they join. List the faces before adding them.
  • Mixed units. 1 m³ = 1000 litres and 1 litre = 1000 cm³. Convert every length to one unit before multiplying.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Mensuration Questions

Q1. A cone has base radius 7 cm and height 24 cm. What is its curved surface area? (π = 22/7)
Slant height l = √(7² + 24²) = 25 cm. Curved surface = πrl = 22/7 × 7 × 25 = 550 cm². Using the height 24 instead of 25 gives 528, a trap option. Answer: 550 cm²
Q2. A solid metal sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into a cylinder of radius 4 cm. How tall is the cylinder?
Volume is kept: ⁴⁄₃π × 216 = π × 16 × h, so 288 = 16h and h = 18 cm. The π cancels, so no value of π is needed. Answer: 18 cm
Q3. A ball of radius 3 cm is dropped into a cylindrical jar of radius 6 cm partly filled with water. By how much does the water rise?
The rise holds the ball's volume: π × 36 × h = ⁴⁄₃π × 27 = 36π, so h = 1 cm. Answer: 1 cm
Q4. A bucket is a frustum 6 cm deep with end radii 14 cm and 7 cm. What is its capacity? (π = 22/7)
V = ⅓πh(R² + r² + Rr) = ⅓ × 22/7 × 6 × (196 + 49 + 98) = 44/7 × 343 = 2156 cm³. Answer: 2156 cm³

How to Prepare Mensuration for SSC CGL

  1. Learn the formulas in families. Cylinder, cone and hemisphere on the same base and height hold volumes in the ratio 3 : 1 : 2. Memorise them side by side, not one at a time.
  2. Write down r, h and l first. Most questions give two of them; the third comes from a right triangle before any area or volume.
  3. Know the slant-height triples. 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 7-24-25, 8-15-17 and 20-21-29 turn most cone and frustum questions into one line.
  4. Keep volume for recasting. When one solid becomes another, equate volumes and let π and ⁴⁄₃ cancel; never equate surfaces.
  5. Scale with powers. Lengths × k, surfaces × k², volumes × k³ — and for partial changes, multiply the separate factors.
  6. List the visible faces. For joined, cut or hollowed solids, count what is added (new cut faces, inner walls) and what disappears (joined faces, holes).
  7. Convert units at the start. Put every length in the same unit, and remember 1 m³ = 1000 litres.
  8. Estimate before choosing an option. πr²h with r = 7 and h = 10 is about 1540 — a quick estimate rules out two options.
  9. Do Level 1 untimed, the same day you finish the theory. It is set at real SSC CGL difficulty; the aim is picking the right formula, not speed.
  10. Repeat Level 1 with the 15-minute clock on, then move to Level 2. After that, verify against a previous year paper and a full mock test under locked sectional timing.

SSC CGL Mensuration Questions — Frequently Asked Questions

How many mensuration questions are asked in SSC CGL?
Usually two or three in Tier I out of the 25 maths questions, and three or four in Tier II Paper I. Most are surface area or volume of a cylinder, cone, sphere or frustum, or a melting-and-recasting question, and each should take about a minute once the formulas are automatic.
What topics come under mensuration for SSC CGL?
Cubes and cuboids, cylinders and hollow cylinders, cones and frustums, spheres and hemispheres, prisms and pyramids, combined solids, solids cut from or placed inside other solids, melting and recasting, water displacement and flow through pipes, wells and embankments, Euler's formula, and 2D work on sectors, segments, rings, paths and wheels.
Which mensuration formulas should I memorise first?
The curved surface and volume of the cylinder (2πrh, πr²h), cone (πrl, ⅓πr²h) and sphere (4πr², ⁴⁄₃πr³), the hemisphere's total surface 3πr², the frustum volume ⅓πh(R² + r² + Rr), the cuboid diagonal √(l² + b² + h²) and the slant height l = √(r² + h²). These cover most Tier I questions.
How do I solve melting and recasting questions?
Volume is conserved, so set the volume of the original solid equal to the total volume of the new ones and solve for the unknown. The π and the 4⁄3 usually cancel. Surface area is not conserved — it almost always changes, which is why many questions ask for the surface after recasting.
What happens to volume and surface area when dimensions are scaled?
If every length is multiplied by k, surface areas multiply by k² and volumes by k³. When only some dimensions change, multiply the separate factors — for a cylinder, volume goes as r²h and curved surface as rh, so the radius factor is squared for volume but not for curved area.
Are there figure-based questions in the mensuration tests?
Yes. Five questions in every test come with an original diagram — composite solids, frustums and buckets, solids with holes and cavities, cut cubes, water in vessels, and 2D paths and sectors. Each figure is drawn for TrickySSC and shown alongside the question in both English and Hindi.
How many questions are in each mensuration practice test?
Twenty-five questions per test, with an optional 15-minute timer and the real exam marking scheme of +2 for a correct answer, −0.5 for a wrong one and 0 for an unattempted question.
Are the mensuration tests available in Hindi?
Yes. Questions, options, figures, detailed solutions and shortcut tricks are available in Hindi, and you choose English or Hindi before the test starts.
Which chapters should I study along with mensuration?
Geometry supplies the lengths, right triangles and circle facts that mensuration starts from, squares, cubes and roots speed up radius and edge work, powers and surds handle answers such as 5√10, ratio and proportion underlies scaling questions, and pipes and cisterns shares the flow-rate idea used in canal and tank questions.
