Time, Speed, Distance & Train

One equation runs the chapter: distance = speed × time. Everything else — relative speed, trains, average speed — is that equation used cleverly.

1. Concepts in brief

Speed, time and distance are tied together

Distance = speed × time. Fix any one of the three, and the other two are linked: for a fixed time, distance grows with speed; for a fixed distance, time falls as speed rises (inverse proportion).

Units

1 km/h = 1000 m ÷ 3600 s = 5/18 m/s. So multiply by 5/18 to go from km/h to m/s, and by 18/5 to go back. Train questions usually give lengths in metres and speeds in km/h — convert first.

Relative speed

When two objects move, what matters is how fast the gap between them changes. Moving towards each other, the gap closes at the sum of their speeds; moving the same way, at the difference.

Trains

A train has length. To pass a pole or a person it must travel its own length. To pass a platform, bridge or another train, it must travel its own length plus the other length.

3. Shortcuts that save time

Late or early by the same distance Walking at S₁ makes you t₁ late; at S₂ makes you t₂ early: D = S₁S₂ (t₁ + t₂)/(S₂ − S₁) (speeds in consistent units).
  • If speed rises by a ratio a : b, time falls by b : a — use it to avoid equations.
  • A man covers 1/n of distance at one speed and the rest at another: weighted average by distance.
  • Meeting problems: they meet after D/(S₁ + S₂) hours.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 72 km/h = 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s.
Example 2 A 150 m train at 54 km/h (15 m/s) crosses a 250 m platform in (150 + 250)/15 = 26⅔ seconds.
Example 3 Two trains of 120 m and 80 m run in opposite directions at 40 and 50 km/h. Relative speed = 90 km/h = 25 m/s. Time = 200/25 = 8 seconds.
Example 4 A man covers a journey at 30 km/h one way and 20 km/h the other. Average = 2 × 30 × 20/50 = 24 km/h.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 5 Walking at 4 km/h a man is 10 minutes late; at 5 km/h he is 5 minutes early. Distance = 4 × 5 × (15/60)/(5 − 4) = 5 km.
Example 6 Travelling at ¾ of his usual speed, a man is 20 minutes late. New time = 4/3 of usual, so the extra ⅓ of usual time = 20 minutes. Usual time = 60 minutes.
Example 7 A train passes a pole in 15 s and a 200 m platform in 25 s. The extra 10 s covers 200 m, so speed = 20 m/s and length = 20 × 15 = 300 m.
Example 8 Two people 60 km apart walk towards each other at 4 km/h and 6 km/h. They meet after 60/10 = 6 hours.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. Convert 90 km/h into m/s.

Answer: 25 m/s.

Q2. A 240 m train at 72 km/h passes a pole in how long?

Answer: 12 seconds (240/20).

Q3. A car goes 60 km/h for 2 hours and 40 km/h for 3 hours. Average speed?

Answer: 48 km/h (240 km in 5 hours).

Q4. Trains of 100 m and 150 m run the same way at 60 and 42 km/h. Time to cross?

Answer: 50 seconds (relative 5 m/s, 250 m).

Q5. A car covers a distance in 6 hours at 50 km/h. Speed needed to do it in 5 hours?

Answer: 60 km/h.

6. Traps to avoid

  1. Convert km/h and m/s consistently before using the formula.
  2. For a platform or bridge, add the train length; for a pole, don't.
  3. Average speed is not (a + b)/2 unless the times are equal.
  4. Same direction → subtract; opposite → add.
  5. If a train overtakes a moving person, use relative speed, not the train's speed alone.
