This is Time & Work with taps. Inlet pipes are workers who add; outlet pipes and leaks are workers who undo. Use the LCM of the times as the tank capacity.

1. Key formulas

SituationFormula
Inlet pipe filling in x hours+1/x of the tank per hour
Outlet pipe emptying in y hours−1/y of the tank per hour
Fill and empty togetherxy/(y − x) hours (y > x)
Two inlets togetherxy/(x + y) hours
Leak: fills in x hours; with leak y hoursLeak empties full tank in xy/(y − x) hours

2. Shortcuts that save time

LCM as capacity Pipe A fills in 12 hours, pipe B fills in 15 hours, pipe C empties in 20 hours. Capacity = LCM = 60 units. A = +5, B = +4, C = −3. Net = 6 units/hour → 60/6 = 10 hours.
  • Positive rate = filling; negative rate = emptying. Add them all.
  • If pipes are opened alternately, add one full cycle and handle the rest separately.

3. Solved examples

Example 1 A fills a tank in 6 hours, B empties it in 8 hours. Both open: (6 × 8)/(8 − 6) = 24 hours.
Example 2 A tank fills in 4 hours. Because of a leak it takes 5 hours. Leak empties a full tank in (4 × 5)/(5 − 4) = 20 hours.
Example 3 Two pipes fill a tank in 20 and 30 hours. Together: (20 × 30)/50 = 12 hours.

4. Traps to avoid

  1. A leak gives a negative rate — subtract it, don't add it.
  2. If the tank is already part full, work with the remaining part.
  3. Check which pipes are open at which time — questions often close one midway.
  4. Convert minutes and hours to the same unit first.
