This is Time & Work with taps. Inlet pipes are workers who add; outlet pipes and leaks are workers who undo. Use the LCM of the times as the tank capacity.

1. Concepts in brief

Pipes are workers

An inlet pipe that fills a tank in 6 hours does 1/6 of the job each hour — exactly like a worker who finishes a job in 6 days. An outlet pipe or a leak does negative work. So everything from Time & Work carries over.

Capacity as LCM

Give the tank a capacity equal to the LCM of all the times given. Each pipe then has a whole-number rate per hour or minute: positive for filling, negative for emptying.

When pipes open and close at different times

Break the timeline into stretches. In each stretch, add the rates of the pipes that are open and multiply by the length of the stretch. Keep a running total of how much of the tank is filled.

3. Shortcuts that save time

LCM as capacity Pipe A fills in 12 hours, pipe B fills in 15 hours, pipe C empties in 20 hours. Capacity = LCM = 60 units. A = +5, B = +4, C = −3. Net = 6 units/hour → 60/6 = 10 hours.
  • Positive rate = filling; negative rate = emptying. Add them all.
  • If pipes are opened alternately, add one full cycle and handle the rest separately.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 A fills a tank in 6 hours, B empties it in 8 hours. Both open: (6 × 8)/(8 − 6) = 24 hours.
Example 2 A tank fills in 4 hours. Because of a leak it takes 5 hours. Leak empties a full tank in (4 × 5)/(5 − 4) = 20 hours.
Example 3 Two pipes fill a tank in 20 and 30 hours. Together: (20 × 30)/50 = 12 hours.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 4 Pipes A and B fill a tank in 12 and 15 minutes. Both are opened, and A is closed after 3 minutes. Capacity 60: A = 5, B = 4 per minute. In 3 minutes they fill 27; the remaining 33 takes B 33/4 = 8¼ minutes more.
Example 5 Three pipes fill a tank in 10, 12 and 15 hours. Capacity 60: 6 + 5 + 4 = 15 per hour → 4 hours together.
Example 6 A tank is half full. An inlet fills a full tank in 6 hours and an outlet empties it in 9 hours. Capacity 18: net +3 − 2 = 1 per hour; half is 9 units → 9 hours.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. Two pipes fill a tank in 6 and 9 hours. Together?

Answer: 3.6 hours (54/15).

Q2. A pipe fills in 5 hours and a leak empties in 20 hours. Both open?

Answer: 6⅔ hours (20/3).

Q3. Pipes fill in 10 and 15 hours; a third empties in 6 hours. All open?

Answer: The tank never fills (3 + 2 − 5 = 0 per hour).

Q4. A tank fills in 6 hours, but with a leak it takes 8. Leak alone empties it in?

Answer: 24 hours.

Q5. Pipes fill in 20 and 30 minutes. Both run for 4 minutes, then the first is closed. Time still needed?

Answer: 20 minutes (60 units: 20 done, 40 left at 2 per minute).

6. Traps to avoid

  1. A leak gives a negative rate — subtract it, don't add it.
  2. If the tank is already part full, work with the remaining part.
  3. Check which pipes are open at which time — questions often close one midway.
  4. Convert minutes and hours to the same unit first.
