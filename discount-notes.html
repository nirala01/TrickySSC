Discount & Marked Price

Formulas, tricks and traps on one page · Quantitative Aptitude

On this sheet

  1. Key formulas
  2. Shortcuts that save time
  3. Solved examples
  4. Traps to avoid

Discount is always calculated on the marked price; profit is always calculated on the cost price. Most errors in this chapter come from mixing those two bases.

1. Key formulas

QuantityFormula
DiscountMP − SP
Discount %Discount ÷ MP × 100
SP after discount d%MP × (100 − d)/100
Successive discounts a%, b%a + b − ab/100
Link between MP and CPMP/CP = (100 + P%)/(100 − D%)
Buy x, get y freeDiscount % = y/(x + y) × 100
Marked up m%, discount d%Profit % = m − d − md/100

2. Shortcuts that save time

Multiply the factors Mark up 25% and give 12% off → 1.25 × 0.88 = 1.10 → 10% profit. Successive discounts 20% and 10% → 0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72 → 28% total.
  • The order of successive discounts does not change the final price.
  • To find the single discount equal to three discounts, apply the formula twice.

3. Solved examples

Example 1 A trader marks goods 25% above CP and allows 12% discount. Profit = 25 − 12 − 300/100 = 10%.
Example 2 Single discount equal to 20% and 10% successive = 20 + 10 − 2 = 28%.
Example 3 "Buy 4, get 1 free" is a discount of 1/5 × 100 = 20%.
Example 4 A shopkeeper wants 20% profit after giving 20% discount. How far above CP should he mark?
MP/CP = 120/80 = 3/2 → mark up 50%.

4. Traps to avoid

  1. Discount % on MP, profit % on CP — never mix the bases.
  2. Successive discounts of 20% and 10% are 28%, not 30%.
  3. "Buy 3 get 1 free" is 25% (1 of 4), not 33⅓%.
  4. If a discount is given on a discounted price, the second discount is on the reduced price.
  5. When CP is the unknown, set CP = 100 and work forward.
