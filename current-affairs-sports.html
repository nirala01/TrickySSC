October 2026Monthly notes

Asian Games 2026 – the tally

  • Games – the 20th Asian Games were held in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan, from 19 September to 4 October.
  • India – 4th with 85 medals (21 gold, 27 silver, 37 bronze), its second-best haul after Hangzhou (106).
  • Leaders – China topped with 341 medals (169 gold); Japan came second.
  • Flag bearer – trap shooter Neeru Dhanda, winner of two golds, carried the tricolour at the closing.
  • Next – Doha, Qatar, hosts the 2030 Games.
Asian Games: run by the Olympic Council of Asia; first held at New Delhi in 1951; held every four years. Japan hosted earlier in 1958 (Tokyo) and 1994 (Hiroshima).

Golden moments in Nagoya

  • Women's hockey – beat China 1–0 for India's first women's Asian Games hockey gold since 1982.
  • Men's hockey – beat Malaysia 5–1; both teams earned places at LA 2028.
  • Cricket – India won both golds; the men beat Pakistan by 19 runs in the final.
  • Golf – Pranavi Urs became the first Indian woman to win Asian Games golf gold.
  • Other golds – Kumkum Mohod (women's recurve archery), Lovlina Borgohain (75 kg boxing), Aman Sehrawat (57 kg wrestling).

Asian Para Games 2026

  • Event – 5th Asian Para Games, Aichi-Nagoya, 18–24 October.
  • India – record 114 medals (30 gold, 35 silver, 49 bronze) and 4th place, its best ever.
  • Ahead – China, Japan and Uzbekistan.
  • Squad – only 168 athletes; para athletics gave 65 medals.
  • Flag bearers – Pramod Bhagat and Simran Sharma at the opening; Nishad Kumar and Mona Agarwal at the closing.

World titles and records

  • Manisha Bhanwala – first Indian woman to win a senior world wrestling title: 59 kg gold at Astana on 30 October, beating USA's Abigail Nette 8–6.
  • Only two – Sushil Kumar (66 kg, 2010) is the only other Indian senior world wrestling champion.
  • Abhishek Sharma – 30-ball T20I hundred against Afghanistan in Delhi (17 September), the fastest by an Indian and by any full-member batter.
  • Old record – Rohit Sharma's 35-ball century against Sri Lanka (2017).
  • Pankaj Advani – claimed his 29th world title in cue sports.

Football, chess and hosting

  • Ballon d'Or 2026 – Harry Kane won, the first Englishman since Michael Owen (2001); the ceremony was held in London.
  • Women – Alexia Putellas won her third Ballon d'Or Féminin; Lamine Yamal took the Kopa Trophy.
  • Chess – Nihal Sarin and Koneru Humpy won individual golds at the European Chess Club Cup in Budva, Montenegro.
  • Women's Champions Trophy – the first edition (14–28 February 2027) moved from Sri Lanka to India; venues Mumbai's CCI and Vadodara.
  • Weightlifting – Ahmedabad will host the 2029 Senior World Weightlifting Championships, a first for India.
Ballon d'Or: begun in 1956 by France Football magazine; Stanley Matthews was the first winner; the women's award started in 2018.

September 2026Monthly notes

Cricket

  • Women's Asia Cup – India beat Sri Lanka by 72 runs in Dubai on 13 September for a record eighth Women's Asia Cup title.
  • Final – India 183/5, with 68 by Shafali Verma; Shree Charani took 4/20 and Sri Lanka were all out for 111.
  • Record – Shafali's 24-ball fifty is the fastest in the Women's T20 Asia Cup.
  • Duleep Trophy – East Zone won on first-innings lead over South Zone in Chennai.
  • Ishan Kishan – the East Zone captain made 270 and 80 – 350 runs, a Duleep Trophy record for one match.

Badminton and tennis

  • China Masters – Satwiksairaj Rankireddy and Chirag Shetty won the Super 750 title at Shenzhen on 6 September.
  • History – they are the first Indians to win the China Masters since it began in 2005; they beat China's He Jiting and Ren Xiangyu.
  • US Open men – Alexander Zverev beat Ben Shelton – his second major after the 2026 French Open.
  • US Open women – Elena Rybakina beat two-time defending champion Aryna Sabalenka for her first US Open.
  • Prize – each singles champion got $5.5 million, the biggest in Grand Slam history.

Hockey and archery

  • Junior Asia Cup – India won both titles – men beat South Korea 4–1, women beat China 1–0.
  • Records – men's sixth title (a record) and fourth in a row; women's third straight; both teams booked spots at the FIH Junior World Cup 2027.
  • Dhiraj Bommadevara – won men's recurve gold at the Archery World Cup Final in Saltillo, Mexico, beating Japan's Junya Nakanishi in a shoot-off.
  • Only second – the first Indian man to win it; Dola Banerjee (2007) was the only Indian before him.
  • Para archery – India hosted a World Archery Para Series leg for the first time, at Ahmedabad's Sabarmati Riverfront; Sheetal Devi took her maiden individual title.

Athletics and para sports

  • Ultimate Championship – World Athletics held the first edition in Budapest, with a record $10 million prize pool.
  • Hammer records – at the Indian Athletics Final Series, Tanya Choudhary threw 68.35 m and Nirbhay Choudhary 71.02 m – the first Indian past 71 m.
  • WSPS shooting – at Changwon, Korea, India won 5 medals, including team gold by Mona Agarwal, Avani Lekhara and Mili Shah; a quota event for LA 2028.
  • Para badminton – India won 9 medals at the Japan Para Badminton International; Pramod Bhagat took SL3 gold.
  • Nomad Games – at the 6th World Nomad Games in Kyrgyzstan, India debuted in classic tug of war (Nagaland team, 6th).

Chess, football and more

  • Bodhana Sivanandan – 11-year-old of Indian origin playing for England became the youngest woman Grandmaster ever at the 46th Chess Olympiad, Samarkand.
  • AFC U20 – India beat Bangladesh 3–0 in Tashkent to reach the AFC U20 Asian Cup, a first in 20 years (last in 2006).
  • Cycling – The 2032 UCI Road World Championships go to Pune – a first for an Indian city.
  • Sports genomics – Gujarat became the first state to start a Sports Genomics Programme, building a DNA database of athletes in about 24 sports.
  • Handball – Pooja Rathore became the first Indian woman with an IHF badge; she will referee at the 4th World Wheelchair Handball Championship in Spain.
Women's Asia Cup: run by the Asian Cricket Council; first held in 2004 (ODI), in T20 format since 2012; India's eight titles are four in each format.

August 2026Monthly notes

Commonwealth Games 2026, Glasgow

  • Edition – the 23rd Games ran in Glasgow, Scotland, from 23 July to 2 August 2026, with only 10 sports; mascot Finnie, a unicorn.
  • India's place – 4th with 39 medals (13 gold, 17 silver, 9 bronze) from a 122-member team; Australia topped the table.
  • Boxing best – Indian boxers won 10 medals, 7 of them gold, making boxing India's strongest sport.
  • Judo first – Asmita Dey (48 kg) and Harsh Singh (60 kg) won India's first-ever CWG judo golds.
  • Stars – Mirabai Chanu won a third straight CWG gold (2018, 2022, 2026); Gulveer Singh won two medals – 10,000 m silver and 5,000 m bronze.
Commonwealth Games: first held in 1930 at Hamilton, Canada, as the British Empire Games; held every four years; India hosted in Delhi in 2010 and will host the centenary edition at Ahmedabad in 2030.

FIH Hockey World Cup 2026

  • Hosts – Belgium and the Netherlands, at Wavre and Amstelveen, from 15 to 30 August.
  • Men – Germany beat Spain 1-0 in the final to keep the title; it is Germany's fourth crown.
  • Women – Argentina beat the Netherlands 3-1 in a shoot-out after a 1-1 draw, for their third title.
  • India women – Salima Tete's side beat Germany 3-1 to finish fifth, their best since fourth place in 1974.
  • India men – finished 8th; captain Harmanpreet Singh was joint top scorer with 8 goals.

Badminton

  • World Championships – held at the Indira Gandhi Indoor Stadium, New Delhi, from 17 to 23 August.
  • India's medal – a women's doubles bronze for Treesa Jolly and Gayatri Gopichand, India's first in the event since the 2011 bronze of Jwala Gutta and Ashwini Ponnappa.
  • Champions – Alex Lanier became the first Frenchman to win the men's singles world title; Korea's An Se-young won her second women's crown.
  • Teen title – 17-year-old Tanvi Sharma won the Taipei Open Super 300, her first World Tour title.
  • Korea Masters – Assam's Ashmita Chaliha won the women's singles at the Super 300 event.

Tennis, chess and cricket

  • Canada – Iga Świątek won the women's title in Toronto and Ben Shelton the men's in Montreal.
  • Cincinnati – Arthur Fils is now the youngest Frenchman with a Masters 1000 title; Coco Gauff beat Jessica Pegula in the first all-American women's final there since 1970.
  • Grand Chess Tour – R. Praggnanandhaa is the first Indian winner of the tour, beating Fabiano Caruana in the St. Louis final.
  • Esports chess – Magnus Carlsen won the Esports World Cup chess in Paris for a second straight year.
  • Test sixes – Rishabh Pant became the first Indian and fourth player to hit 100 sixes in Tests.

Wrestling, athletics and squash

  • U20 wrestling – at Bratislava, Kajal Dhokchak won 76 kg gold, her second U20 world title in a row; Indian women were second in the team table.
  • U20 athletics – at Eugene, USA, Basant Kumar Meghwal won silver in men's high jump, India's first medal in that event at the U20 Worlds.
  • Lausanne – Neeraj Chopra was second at the Diamond League with a season's best 88.05 m; Sri Lanka's Rumesh Pathirage won.
  • Squash – Velavan Senthilkumar won the PSA Budapest Open and Abhay Singh the Tuanku Muhriz Trophy in Malaysia.
  • Fencing – India won the Commonwealth Fencing Championship (Lagos) overall for the first time.

Football and more

  • Durand Cup – East Bengal beat Mohun Bagan Super Giant in the 135th final at Kolkata for their 17th title.
  • Messi – Lionel Messi, 39, quit international football on 31 August after 21 years; he signs off with Argentina records of 207 caps and 125 goals.
  • Footballer of the Year – the FPAI picked Mizoram's Lallianzuala Chhangte as its Footballer of the Year 2026.
  • Formula E – Pascal Wehrlein (Porsche) won his second world title, the 2025-26 season.
  • FIBA U18 Asia Cup – held in Ahmedabad; Australia won, beating China in the final.
Durand Cup: Asia's oldest football tournament, first played in 1888 at Dagshai near Shimla and named after Sir Mortimer Durand, Foreign Secretary of British India.

July 2026Monthly notes

Cricket

  • Women's T20 World Cup – Australia beat hosts England by 7 wickets at Lord's on 5 July 2026 for a record 7th title, with the biggest successful chase in a final.
  • Final star – Beth Mooney made 64 off 49 balls and was named both Player of the Match and Player of the Tournament; Sophie Molineux led Australia.
  • India at Lord's – India's women won their first Test at Lord's, beating England by 270 runs under Harmanpreet Kaur; Yastika Bhatia scored 113.
  • Zimbabwe tour – India's men won the T20I series 3-0 in Harare and went back to No. 1 in the ICC T20I rankings.
  • Youngest debut – Vaibhav Sooryavanshi of Bihar made his India debut against England at Old Trafford aged 15 years 99 days, beating Sachin Tendulkar's record.

Football and tennis

  • FIFA World Cup 2026 – Spain beat holders Argentina 1-0 after extra time in the final at MetLife Stadium, New Jersey, on 19 July – their second title after 2010.
  • Winning goal – substitute Ferran Torres scored in the 106th minute; Spain now hold both World Cup trophies, men's and women's, at once.
  • Awards – Rodri won the Golden Ball, Unai Simón the Golden Glove and Pau Cubarsí the Best Young Player award; Kylian Mbappé took the Golden Boot (10 goals), the first man to win it twice.
  • Wimbledon men – Jannik Sinner (Italy) beat Alexander Zverev in four sets to keep his title; it was his fifth Grand Slam.
  • Wimbledon women – Czech Linda Nosková beat compatriot Karolína Muchová for her first major; Guo Hanyu and Kristina Mladenovic took the women's doubles.
FIFA World Cup 2026: first 48-team edition, co-hosted by the USA, Canada and Mexico; the 2030 edition will be held in Spain, Portugal and Morocco.

Badminton, squash and chess

  • Japan Open – P.V. Sindhu beat world champion Akane Yamaguchi 21-17, 21-17 in Tokyo on 19 July, becoming the first Indian to win this Super 750 event.
  • World Junior Squash – 18-year-old Anahat Singh beat Egypt's Ruqayya Salem 3-0 in Canada to become India's first world junior squash champion.
  • Egypt's run ended – her win broke Egypt's 16-year grip on the girls' title, and she did not drop a game in the event.
  • Hockey5s – at the FIH Youth Hockey5s Asian Championship in Muscat, India's under-16 boys beat Pakistan 3-1 in the final; India's girls lost to China.
  • FIDE – five-time world champion Viswanathan Anand became acting (interim) President of the world chess body.

Shooting, archery and wrestling

  • Trap gold – Army's Neeru Dhanda won women's trap at the Shotgun World Cup in Lonato, Italy – the first individual ISSF World Cup gold by an Indian woman in trap.
  • Hangzhou World Cup – Esha Singh won women's 25m pistol gold and Manu Bhaker took bronze.
  • Archery – India's women's compound team (Jyothi Surekha Vennam, Prithika Pradeep, Chikitha Tanipathi) won silver at World Cup Stage 4 in Madrid; Colombia won gold.
  • Para archery – India won 10 medals, 5 of them gold, at Stage 2 of the World Para Archery Series in Czechia.
  • Wrestling – India won 20 medals, including 4 gold, at the U-20 Asian Championship in Pattaya, Thailand.

Other events

  • Tour de France – the 113th edition ran from 4 to 26 July 2026, starting in Barcelona and ending in Paris.
  • Boxing – India's women won 18 medals (3 gold) at the Asian U-19 and U-23 Championships in Jakarta.
  • Athletics – Annu Rani won women's javelin at the 65th National Senior Athletics Championships in Bhubaneswar.
  • Youth chess – Pratiti Bordoloi won silver in the girls' U-18 section of the World Youth Chess Championship in Italy.
  • Para judo – Kokila won bronze in the J2 52 kg women's class at the IBSA Grand Prix in São Paulo.

June 2026Monthly notes

Cricket

  • IPL 2026 – Royal Challengers Bengaluru beat Gujarat Titans by 5 wickets in the final at Ahmedabad on 31 May to keep their title; captain Rajat Patidar.
  • Final star – Virat Kohli made 75 not out and was Player of the Match.
  • Season awards – Vaibhav Sooryavanshi (RR) won the Orange Cap with 776 runs plus the MVP award; Kagiso Rabada (GT) took the Purple Cap with 29 wickets.
  • Afghanistan Test – Mullanpur (New Chandigarh) became India's 31st Test venue; debutant Manav Suthar was Player of the Match and India's winning margin was an innings and 300 runs.
  • ODI World Cup 2027 – to be held from 4 October to 21 November 2027 in South Africa, Zimbabwe and Namibia.

Chess and tennis

  • Norway Chess – R. Praggnanandhaa became the first Indian to win it, beating Vincent Keymer in the last round in Oslo on 5 June; he also beat Magnus Carlsen twice in classical games.
  • Women's section – won by Kazakhstan's Bibisara Assaubayeva.
  • French Open men – Alexander Zverev (Germany) beat Flavio Cobolli in five sets for his first Grand Slam title.
  • French Open women – 19-year-old Mirra Andreeva beat Polish qualifier Maja Chwalinska; Roland Garros is the only major played on clay.
  • New Grandmasters – Goa's Ethan Vaz and Chennai's G.B. Harshavardhan took India's GM count to 97.

Hockey and football

  • Women's Nations Cup – India beat hosts New Zealand 2-0 in Auckland on 21 June to win a second title (first in 2022) and earn a return to the FIH Pro League.
  • Final details – goals by Navneet Kaur and Sunelita Toppo; the team, led by Salima Tete, lost no match.
  • Men's U-18 Asia Cup – India beat hosts Japan 4-1 in the final at Kakamigahara.
  • SAFF Women's Championship – India beat Bangladesh 3-1 in Goa to take the title for the sixth time.
  • Most caps – Manpreet Singh became India's most-capped hockey player, going past Dilip Tirkey.

Shooting, badminton and other events

  • ISSF World Cup, Munich – Esha Singh won women's 25m pistol gold with a world record.
  • Asian Fencing Championships – held at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi (19–24 June); Japan topped the medal table.
  • Singapore Open – Satwiksairaj Rankireddy and Chirag Shetty won the men's doubles title (Super 750).
  • Asian Doubles Squash – Abhay Singh and Velavan Senthilkumar won men's doubles gold in Malaysia.
  • Yogasana – the first World Yogasana Sports Championship was held in Ahmedabad and India topped the table.

Athletics

  • Doha Diamond League – Neeraj Chopra finished fourth in the javelin.
  • Hurdles world record – American Ja'Kobe Tharp ran the 110m hurdles in 12.75 s at the NCAA meet, bettering Aries Merritt's 12.80.
  • Parul Chaudhary – lowered her own 5,000 m national record at a meet in Nice, France.
  • Indian Athletics Awards – the first edition by AFI named Neeraj Chopra and Parul Chaudhary the top athletes of the year.
  • National Games – Meghalaya will host the 39th edition in 2027.

May 2026Monthly notes

Athletics records at Ranchi

  • Meet – the 29th National Senior Athletics Federation Competition (Federation Cup) was held at Birsa Munda Stadium, Ranchi.
  • 100 m – Gurindervir Singh ran 10.09 s on 23 May, the first Indian man under 10.10 s; Animesh Kujur had held the record a day earlier.
  • 400 m – Tamil Nadu's Vishal T.K. clocked 44.98 s, the first Indian under 45 seconds.
  • Decathlon – Tejaswin Shankar became the first Indian to pass 8,000 points.
  • Why it mattered – the meet was a main trial for the 2026 Commonwealth Games in Glasgow.

Badminton and tennis

  • Thomas Cup – China beat first-time finalists France 3–1 at Horsens, Denmark, on 3 May.
  • India – lost the semi-final 0–3 to France and shared bronze with Denmark; India's only title came in 2022.
  • Uber Cup – South Korea beat holders China 3–1 for their third title (after 2010 and 2022).
  • Rome – Jannik Sinner beat Casper Ruud 6-4, 6-4 in the Italian Open final, the first Italian men's winner since Adriano Panatta in 1976.
  • Golden Masters – Sinner is the second man after Novak Djokovic to win all nine Masters 1000 events; Elina Svitolina beat Coco Gauff for the women's title.

Golf and football

  • PGA Championship – England's Aaron Rai, of Indian heritage, won the 108th edition at Aronimink, Pennsylvania, on 17 May.
  • Record – first Englishman to win it since Jim Barnes in 1919; three shots clear of Jon Rahm; he jumped from 44th to 15th in the world.
  • ISL – East Bengal won their first Indian Super League title on 21 May, beating Inter Kashi 2–1 in Kolkata on the last day.
  • Close finish – they tied Mohun Bagan Super Giant on 26 points but won on goal difference, ending a 22-year wait for a league title.
  • Team – coach Oscar Bruzón; Miguel Figueira won the Golden Ball.

Other highlights

  • Wrestling – at the U23 Asian Championships, Indian women won 10 medals with 6 golds; the Greco-Roman squad had its best haul.
  • CWG 2030 – the Commonwealth Games Federation handed the host baton to Ahmedabad for the centenary Games in 2030.
  • Yogasana – Ahmedabad will also hold the first World Yogasana Championship on 4–8 June 2026.
  • Ranchi meet – nearly 800 athletes took part, according to the AFI.
Commonwealth Games: first held in 1930 at Hamilton, Canada; India hosted in Delhi in 2010; the 2026 Games are in Glasgow and the 2030 centenary Games in Ahmedabad.

April 2026Monthly notes

Chess and badminton breakthroughs

  • Candidates – Uzbekistan's Javokhir Sindarov won the FIDE Candidates in Cyprus with a round in hand (10/14) and will face champion D. Gukesh.
  • Women's event – India's R. Vaishali took the Women's Candidates with 8.5/14 after beating Kateryna Lagno in the final round.
  • Next match – Vaishali, the first Indian winner of the event and elder sister of R. Praggnanandhaa, will challenge China's Ju Wenjun.
  • Asia silver – Ayush Shetty won silver at the Badminton Asia Championships in Ningbo, losing the final to China's Shi Yuqi.
  • 61-year gap – he was the first Indian man in the singles final since Dinesh Khanna's 1965 title, after upsetting Kunlavut Vitidsarn in the semis.

Boxing, shooting and archery

  • Asian boxing – at Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, India won 16 medals (5 gold, 3 silver, 8 bronze), the most by any team; Kazakhstan led on golds.
  • Men's gold – Vishvanath Suresh (50 kg) beat Daichi Iwai of Japan 5-0, ending a five-year wait for an Indian male Asian champion.
  • Women on top – Indian women headed their medal table with 10 medals, including golds for Minakshi, Preeti, Priya and Arundhati.
  • Junior shooting – India topped the ISSF Junior World Cup at Cairo with 16 medals, five of them gold.
  • Para archery – India led the World Archery Para Series in Bangkok with 13 medals, seven gold.

Distance running

  • Under two hours – Kenya's Sabastian Sawe won the London Marathon on 26 April in 1:59:30, the first sub-two-hour marathon in a record-eligible race.
  • Old mark – he took 65 seconds off the late Kelvin Kiptum's 2:00:35 (Chicago, 2023).
  • Runner-up – Ethiopia's Yomif Kejelcha also dipped under two hours (1:59:41) on his debut.
  • Indian record – Sawan Barwal clocked 2:11:58 at the Rotterdam Marathon, erasing Shivnath Singh's 1978 national record of 2:12:00.
  • Tribal Games – the first Khelo India Tribal Games in Chhattisgarh closed on 6 April with Karnataka on top, ahead of Odisha and Jharkhand.

Tennis, golf and football

  • Masters – Rory McIlroy won golf's 90th Masters at Augusta for a second year running; Scottie Scheffler was second.
  • Monte Carlo – Jannik Sinner beat Carlos Alcaraz in the final and regained the world No. 1 spot.
  • Stuttgart – Kazakhstan's Elena Rybakina retained the Porsche Tennis Grand Prix title.
  • SAFF U-20 – Bangladesh beat India 4-3 on penalties in the final at Malé; India's Omang Dodum was named best player.
  • Bundesliga – Bayern Munich won a record 35th German league title.

Asian Beach Games and Laureus

  • Sanya 2026 – the 6th Asian Beach Games were held at Sanya, China (22–30 April); India's haul was six medals: three gold, two silver and one bronze.
  • Kabaddi – India's women beat Sri Lanka 47-31 for a sixth straight beach kabaddi gold; the men lost the final to Iran.
  • Wrestling – Pushpa Yadav and Priya Malik won beach wrestling golds.
  • Laureus – at Madrid on 20 April, Carlos Alcaraz and Aryna Sabalenka were named Sportsman and Sportswoman of the Year.
  • Other Laureus – Paris Saint-Germain won Team of the Year, Lando Norris Breakthrough and Lamine Yamal Young Sportsperson.
Laureus World Sports Awards: given every year since 2000; winners are chosen by the Laureus World Sports Academy of sporting legends; often called the 'Oscars of sport'.

March 2026Monthly notes

India's third T20 World Cup

  • Final – India beat New Zealand by 96 runs at the Narendra Modi Stadium, Ahmedabad, on 8 March 2026.
  • Firsts – first side to win the men's T20 World Cup three times, the first to defend it and the first to win at home.
  • Score – India's 255/5 is the highest total in a T20 World Cup final; NZ were bowled out for 159.
  • Awards – Jasprit Bumrah (4/15) was player of the final; Sanju Samson (321 runs) player of the tournament.
  • Captain and hosts – Suryakumar Yadav led India; India and Sri Lanka co-hosted; the 2028 edition goes to Australia and New Zealand.
Men's T20 World Cup: ICC event first held in 2007, which India won; India's other titles came in 2024 and 2026.

J&K lift the Ranji Trophy

  • Title – Jammu and Kashmir won their first Ranji Trophy in 2025-26, some 67 years after their debut in 1959-60.
  • Final – the drawn final with Karnataka at Hubballi was settled on J&K's 291-run first-innings lead (28 Feb).
  • Captain – Paras Dogra led the side.
  • Bowler – pacer Auqib Nabi took 60 wickets and was player of the season.
  • Record – Mumbai hold the most Ranji titles (42); J&K are the 18th different champion.

Winter Paralympics in Italy

  • Host – the 14th Winter Paralympics were held in Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo, 6–15 March 2026.
  • Size – 79 medal events in six sports.
  • Top – China led with 44 medals (15 gold), ahead of the USA and Russia.
  • Hosts – Italy had its best ever Winter Paralympics haul.
  • New event – wheelchair curling mixed doubles made its debut.

Events at home

  • Tribal Games – the first Khelo India Tribal Games began on 25 March 2026 in Chhattisgarh (Raipur, Jagdalpur, Sarguja).
  • Mascot – 'Morveer', from Chhattisgarhi words for 'mine' and 'brave'.
  • Sports – seven medal sports such as archery, hockey and wrestling; mallakhamb and kabaddi as demonstration sports.
  • Para athletics – New Delhi hosted the World Para Athletics Grand Prix (8–13 March) and India topped the medal list.
  • ASMITA – Achieving Sports Milestone by Inspiring Women Through Action – the women's league ran a special athletics edition on Women's Day.

Other names in sport

  • Prague chess – Uzbekistan's Nodirbek Abdusattorov won the Prague Masters; India's Aravindh Chithambaram shared second.
  • Squash – Anahat Singh won the women's title at the JSW Indian Open in Mumbai.
  • Hockey – forward Gurjant Singh, part of the Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 bronze teams, retired.
  • Cycling – the logo of the Cycling League of India, pitched as a franchise-based pro road league, was unveiled.

February 2026Monthly notes

Winter Olympics in Italy

  • Games – the 25th Winter Olympics were held in Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo, Italy, 6–22 Feb 2026.
  • Table – Norway topped with 18 gold and 41 medals; the USA came second with 12 gold.
  • Record – Norway's Johannes Høsflot Klæbo won six golds, the most by anyone at one Winter Games.
  • India – two athletes – Arif Khan (alpine skiing) and Stanzin Lundup (cross-country skiing); no medal.
  • Mascots – two stoats, Tina (Olympics) and Milo (Paralympics); the 2030 Games go to the French Alps.

Cricket titles

  • U-19 World Cup – India beat England by 100 runs in the final at Harare on 6 Feb for a record sixth title; captain Ayush Mhatre.
  • Star – Vaibhav Sooryavanshi hit 175 off 80 balls (15 sixes) in the final; player of the final and of the tournament.
  • Hosts – Zimbabwe and Namibia staged the event.
  • WPL – RCB beat Delhi Capitals by 6 wickets in Vadodara on 5 Feb for their second title (first in 2024).
  • Record chase – RCB's 204 was the highest chase in a WPL final; Delhi lost a fourth straight final.

Shooting, athletics and wrestling

  • Asian Shooting – the Asian Shooting Championship was held at the Dr Karni Singh Range, New Delhi, and India topped the table.
  • Elavenil Valarivan – she won three golds there.
  • Indoor athletics – at the Asian Indoor Championships in Tianjin, China, Tejaswin Shankar won heptathlon gold.
  • Zagreb Open – Sujeet Kalkal (65 kg) and Manisha Bhanwala (57 kg) won wrestling golds in Croatia.

Tennis, football and more

  • Davis Cup – India beat the Netherlands 3–2 and moved to Qualifier Round 2 (Sept 2026).
  • Santosh Trophy – Services beat Kerala 1–0 in the 79th final, played in Assam.
  • Para badminton – Pramod Bhagat won SL3 singles gold at the BWF Para World Championships in Manama, Bahrain.
