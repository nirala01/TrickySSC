Venn diagram questions test one skill: seeing how groups sit inside, across or apart from each other. In the classic SSC form you get three classes — say mothers, women and doctors — and pick the picture that shows them correctly. Compare the classes two at a time: one inside the other, partly overlapping, or nothing in common. Three pairs, one picture.

The figure form gives you overlapping shapes with numbers in each part and asks how many people fit a condition. The rule never changes: a number counts only if its part is inside every shape named and outside every shape excluded. Word problems use the same idea with the formula n(A or B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(both).

The three mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Missing a partial overlap. Women and doctors overlap: some women are doctors, and many doctors are men. Drawing one inside the other is wrong.
  • Letting circles touch when nothing is shared. No cobra lives in the sea, so in Cobras, Snakes, Sea animals the sea-animal circle cuts the snake circle but stays away from the cobra circle.
  • Counting the centre in a “but not” question. The middle part of a figure is inside every shape, so it never fits a question that excludes one of them.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Venn Diagram

Q1. Select the Venn diagram that best represents: Mothers, Women, Doctors.
Every mother is a woman, so the mothers circle sits inside the women circle. Some women are doctors and some doctors are men, and some mothers are doctors — so the doctors circle cuts both. Answer: a small circle inside a big one, with a third circle cutting both
Q2. Select the Venn diagram that best represents: Tea, Coffee, Beverages.
Tea and coffee are both beverages, and nothing is both tea and coffee. Answer: two separate circles inside a bigger circle
Q3. In the figure the circle stands for students who play cricket and the rectangle for students who play football. How many students play football but not cricket?
The part inside the rectangle and outside the circle holds 24. The 9 is inside both shapes, so it does not count. Answer: 24
Q4. In a class of 60 students, 35 like tea, 30 like coffee and 12 like both. How many like neither?
At least one = 35 + 30 − 12 = 53. Neither = 60 − 53 = 7. Answer: 7

How to Prepare Venn Diagram for SSC CGL

  1. Learn the eleven pictures above. Most questions are recognised in seconds once you know them.
  2. Always compare in pairs. Inside, overlapping or apart — write a quick symbol for each of the three pairs.
  3. Test an overlap with a real example. “A woman doctor” proves women and doctors share members; “a male doctor” proves neither is inside the other.
  4. In figures, mark IN and OUT first. Then add only the parts that satisfy both.
  5. For word problems, fill the diagram from the centre outwards. All three first, then the “two only” parts, then the “only one” parts.
  6. Do Level 1 untimed first, then with the 15-minute clock. Move to Level 2 once Level 1 is steady, and then check yourself on a previous year paper.

SSC CGL Venn Diagram — Frequently Asked Questions

How many Venn diagram questions are asked in SSC CGL?
Usually one question in a Tier I shift, sometimes two. It is one of the quickest marks in the reasoning section once the method is fixed, and the same forms appear in SSC CHSL, MTS and CGL Tier II.
How do I find the right Venn diagram for three classes?
Compare the classes two at a time. For each pair decide whether one lies inside the other, whether they partly overlap, or whether they have nothing in common. Then pick the only picture that shows all three of those relations together.
What does it mean when two circles partly overlap?
Some members belong to both classes, some belong only to the first and some only to the second — for example women and doctors. If every member of one class is in the other, draw it fully inside instead; if no member is shared, keep the circles apart.
How do I answer ‘how many’ questions from a figure?
Mark the shapes the people must be inside and the shapes they must be outside. Add only the numbers in parts that satisfy both. A part counts once, even if it lies inside several shapes.
What is the formula for two- and three-group word problems?
For two groups, n(A or B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(both). For three groups, n(A or B or C) = n(A) + n(B) + n(C) − the three pair counts + n(all three). Those in none = total − those in at least one.
How many questions are in each test?
Twenty-five questions per test, with an optional 15-minute timer and the real exam marking of +2 for a correct answer, −0.5 for a wrong one and 0 for an unattempted question.
Are the tests available in Hindi?
Yes. Questions, options, detailed solutions and shortcut tricks are available in Hindi, and you choose English or Hindi before the test starts.
What is the difference between Level 1 and Level 2?
Level 1 is set at real SSC CGL difficulty — common class relations, three-shape figures and two-group word problems. Level 2 is harder than the exam: subtle relations (a class inside the overlap of two others, a third class cutting only the outer circle), four-shape figures with ‘exactly two’, ‘at least two’ and difference questions, and three-group word problems.
