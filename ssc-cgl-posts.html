SSC CGL Posts 2026: Complete List, Salary, Job Profile, Promotion & Perks

Last updated: 7 August 2026

The Staff Selection Commission Combined Graduate Level examination is the single largest gateway into Central Government service for graduates in India. Every cycle, lakhs of candidates compete for posts spread across Ministries, Departments, Constitutional Bodies, Tribunals and Government Organisations — and in the 2026 cycle alone, more than 28 lakh applications were received against roughly 12,256 tentative vacancies.

What makes SSC CGL unusual is that one examination opens more than thirty different career paths. Clear the same Tier 1 and Tier 2 papers and you could end up auditing the Union Government's accounts for the C&AG, investigating financial crime at the Directorate of Enforcement, examining cargo at a customs port, drafting policy files in the Central Secretariat, or running statistical surveys for MoSPI. The pay, the daily work, the transfer policy and the promotion ladder are completely different in each case.

That is exactly why post preference matters so much. Candidates routinely fill their preference form in the last ten minutes before the deadline, based on a friend's advice or a YouTube ranking, and then spend the next decade in a role that never suited them. A post that is perfect for someone who wants field work and investigative authority is miserable for someone who wants fixed hours and a home-state posting.

This guide is written to fix that. It covers every major SSC CGL post in detail — department, classification, pay level, basic pay, approximate in-hand salary, age limit, day-to-day job profile, allowances and perks, and the full promotion chain — so you can build your preference list on facts rather than folklore.

Understanding Pay Levels and Grade Pay

Before the salary tables make sense, it helps to understand what a "pay level" actually is. Under the 7th Central Pay Commission, the old system of pay band plus grade pay was replaced by a single pay matrix. Each post is assigned a level in that matrix, and each level is a column of progressively higher cells. You enter at the first cell and move one cell up with each annual increment.

Older articles and departmental orders still refer to the pre-7th CPC grade pay, and many candidates use both terms interchangeably. The rough correspondence is:

Pay LevelOld Grade PayEntry Basic PayMatrix Range
Level 8₹4,800₹47,600₹47,600 – ₹1,51,100
Level 7₹4,600₹44,900₹44,900 – ₹1,42,400
Level 6₹4,200₹35,400₹35,400 – ₹1,12,400
Level 5₹2,800₹29,200₹29,200 – ₹92,300
Level 4₹2,400₹25,500₹25,500 – ₹81,100

Two practical points follow from this. First, the gap between levels widens over a career — a Level 8 officer and a Level 4 employee start about ₹22,000 apart in basic pay, but that difference roughly triples by the top of each matrix. Second, your pay level determines far more than salary: it sets your seniority, the promotion posts you are eligible for, your HRA and TA slabs, and in several departments your entitlement to government accommodation and official vehicles.

SSC CGL Salary Structure (7th CPC)

Every SSC CGL employee receives a fixed basic pay according to the assigned pay level, and on top of that a set of allowances that together add a substantial amount to the monthly figure. The basic pay is uniform across the country; the allowances are what vary by posting.

Pay LevelBasic PayTypical Posts
Level 8₹47,600Assistant Audit Officer, Assistant Accounts Officer
Level 7₹44,900Income Tax Inspector, Assistant Section Officer, CBI SI, AEO, Preventive Officer, Examiner
Level 6₹35,400JSO, Divisional Accountant, Executive Assistant, NIA SI
Level 5₹29,200Auditor, Accountant
Level 4₹25,500Tax Assistant, Postal Assistant, Senior Secretariat Assistant

What makes up your monthly salary

  • Basic Pay — the pay matrix cell for your level and stage.
  • Dearness Allowance (DA) — a percentage of basic pay, revised twice a year to offset inflation. This is the largest add-on and it grows every cycle.
  • House Rent Allowance (HRA) — depends on whether your posting city is classified X (metro), Y or Z. The difference between an X-city and a Z-city posting can be several thousand rupees a month.
  • Transport Allowance (TA) — a fixed amount by pay level and city class, with DA applied on top of it.
  • Post-specific allowances — some departments carry additional entitlements, for example special security allowance in certain intelligence and enforcement organisations, subject to departmental rules.

What gets deducted

  • National Pension System (NPS) — employee contribution deducted monthly, with a matching government contribution credited to your pension account.
  • Income tax (TDS) — deducted at source based on your total annual income and chosen tax regime.
  • CGHS contribution — a modest monthly amount where the Central Government Health Scheme is applicable at your station.
  • Group insurance and welfare fund deductions — small, department-specific amounts.

The number that reaches your bank account is basic pay plus allowances minus these deductions. That is why two people at the same pay level, appointed on the same day, can see noticeably different credits — one posted in Delhi and one in a Z-class town are on the same basic pay but very different HRA.

Allowances and Perks of SSC CGL Posts

Salary is only part of what an SSC CGL post is worth. The non-salary benefits — medical cover for your family, pension, leave, travel concession and a promotion ladder that does not depend on market conditions — are a large share of the real value of the job, and they apply across all pay levels.

Financial benefits

  • Dearness Allowance, revised periodically as DA rates are announced
  • House Rent Allowance according to city classification, or government accommodation where available
  • Transport Allowance, with DA applied on it
  • Annual increment in the pay matrix, on the fixed increment date
  • Performance and department-specific benefits where applicable under rules

Retirement benefits

  • National Pension System with matching government contribution
  • Gratuity as per applicable rules
  • Retirement and terminal benefits under Government norms

Medical benefits

  • CGHS or the approved government medical facility applicable at your station, subject to eligibility
  • Cover extends to dependent family members as per scheme rules
  • Medical reimbursement in accordance with applicable rules

Leave benefits

  • Earned Leave, accumulable and encashable as per rules
  • Casual Leave and Half Pay Leave
  • Child Care Leave, where applicable
  • Maternity and Paternity Leave under Government rules
  • Gazetted holidays and Restricted Holidays of your choice

Travel benefits

  • Leave Travel Concession for self and family, in the prescribed blocks
  • Travelling Allowance for official duty and transfers
  • Daily Allowance during official tours, where applicable

Career benefits

  • Regular departmental promotions on a defined seniority and DPC cycle
  • Departmental training programmes, including induction and refresher courses
  • A posting in a nationally recognised ministry or organisation
  • Employment security that does not depend on economic cycles
  • Reasonable work-life balance in most administrative posts
  • Room to specialise in taxation, audit, accounts, investigation, statistics, foreign trade or policy implementation

Pay Level 8 Posts — Highest Entry-Level Pay Through SSC CGL

Pay Level 8 sits at the top of the SSC CGL structure. Three posts fall in this level, all under the Indian Audit & Accounts Department in the office of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India, and all classified as Group "B" Gazetted (Non-Ministerial).

Two features set these apart from every other SSC CGL post. The first is gazetted status from day one — your appointment is notified in the Gazette of India, and you can attest documents and issue certificates in an official capacity, which most SSC CGL entrants only reach after a promotion or two. The second is the nature of the work: auditing the accounts of government bodies means you are examining how public money was actually spent, and your observations feed into reports that ultimately reach Parliament or a State Legislature.

There is a trade-off worth knowing before you rank these first. AAO and AAO work is analytical, document-heavy and deadline-driven, with field visits for audit inspections that can take you away from your station for stretches at a time. Candidates who want field authority, uniform, or investigative powers usually find Level 7 enforcement posts a better fit despite the lower pay level.

Note on eligibility: Level 8 posts carry an additional Tier 2 paper requirement in most cycles, and AAO/AAO candidates are assessed on a separate merit list. Check the eligibility and paper structure specified for these posts in the current notification before opting for them.

Job profile

The Assistant Audit Officer in the Central Cadre audits the accounts, expenditure and financial operations of Central Government Ministries, Departments, Autonomous Bodies and Public Sector Undertakings. The core question the role asks, over and over, is whether public money was spent in accordance with financial rules and whether the programme it funded actually achieved what it was meant to.

Day-to-day responsibilities include:

  • Conducting financial, compliance and performance audits of auditee organisations
  • Examining government expenditure, receipts, sanctions and procurement records
  • Drafting audit observations, paragraphs and inspection reports
  • Reviewing accounting records, vouchers and financial statements
  • Participating in audit inspections at offices across India
  • Assisting senior audit officers in preparing reports that are laid before Parliament

The work is a mix of office-based analysis and official tours for inspection. Tour frequency depends on the wing you are posted to — commercial audit, defence audit, railway audit and civil audit all have different travel patterns.

Why choose this post

Take this post if you are genuinely interested in finance, audit and analytical work rather than just the salary figure. It offers the highest entry pay available through SSC CGL, gazetted rank from day one, structured career growth within a single well-defined department, and work that has real consequences for how public funds are handled. The Central Cadre also gives you exposure to auditing large national organisations rather than state-level bodies.

Salary & perks

  • Basic Pay ₹47,600 (Level 8) with annual increments
  • Dearness Allowance, House Rent Allowance, Transport Allowance
  • National Pension System with government contribution
  • Government medical facilities and CGHS where applicable
  • Leave Travel Concession and full leave entitlement
  • Travelling and daily allowance during audit tours
  • Departmental training, including specialised audit courses
  • Gazetted status and the job security that comes with a permanent Central Government post

Promotion path

  1. Assistant Audit Officer
  2. Audit Officer
  3. Senior Audit Officer
  4. Deputy Accountant General
  5. Senior administrative positions, subject to departmental rules

Job profile

The State Cadre Assistant Audit Officer performs the same audit function, but the auditee universe is different: State Government departments, local bodies, panchayati raj institutions and state-funded organisations. You are checking whether state expenditure has been properly accounted for and whether financial rules were followed while implementing government programmes.

Responsibilities include:

  • Conducting financial and compliance audits of state departments and bodies
  • Reviewing departmental expenditure and sanctions
  • Examining utilisation of grants and public funds
  • Preparing audit reports and inspection observations
  • Visiting government offices and field units for inspections
  • Coordinating with senior audit authorities in the Accountant General's office

The role gives you unusually wide exposure to public finance and state governance — over a few years you see how schemes are actually implemented on the ground, which is a perspective very few desk jobs provide.

Why choose this post

The main practical advantage over the Central Cadre is location stability. State Cadre postings are within an allotted state, which means less uncertainty about where you will live and, for many candidates, the possibility of serving closer to home. Combine that with Level 8 pay, gazetted status and a clear promotion ladder, and it becomes one of the most sought-after SSC CGL positions — particularly for candidates who value predictability over the wider canvas of central audit.

Salary & perks

  • Basic Pay ₹47,600 (Level 8) with annual increments
  • Dearness Allowance, House Rent Allowance, Transport Allowance
  • Government medical facilities

  • Official tour benefits during audit inspections
  • Regular promotions within the audit hierarchy

Job profile

Where the AAO audits accounts after the fact, the Assistant Accounts Officer maintains and compiles them. The role covers preparation of government accounts, verification of financial transactions, monitoring of expenditure against budget, reconciliation, and compliance with financial regulations — the machinery that produces the accounts everyone else relies on.

Typical responsibilities include:

  • Preparation and maintenance of government accounts
  • Verification and authorisation of financial transactions
  • Budget monitoring and expenditure control
  • Compilation of monthly and annual financial statements
  • Reconciliation with treasuries and departmental figures
  • Coordination with various government departments on accounting matters
  • Supporting senior officers in financial reporting

This is the most office-bound of the three Level 8 posts. Tours are limited, hours are regular, and the work follows a predictable monthly and annual accounting cycle with pressure concentrated around closing periods.

Why choose this post

Assistant Accounts Officer suits candidates who want the pay and status of Level 8 without the travel that audit work involves. If you have a commerce or accounting background, the work maps directly onto what you already know, and the specialisation you build — government accounting, budgeting, financial reporting — is genuinely valuable for the rest of your career. For anyone prioritising fixed hours and a settled family life, this is arguably the most comfortable high-pay post in the entire SSC CGL list.

Salary & perks

  • Basic Pay ₹47,600 (Level 8) with annual increments
  • Dearness Allowance, House Rent Allowance, Transport Allowance
  • National Pension System with government contribution
  • Government medical facilities
  • Leave Travel Concession, paid holidays and full leave entitlement
  • Professional training programmes in government accounting
  • Strong long-term career growth within the accounts hierarchy

Promotion path

  1. Assistant Accounts Officer
  2. Accounts Officer
  3. Senior Accounts Officer
  4. Deputy Accountant General or equivalent senior positions, as per departmental rules

Which Level 8 Post Should You Prefer?

All three carry identical pay, identical classification and comparable promotion ladders, so the choice comes down to the shape of your working life rather than the money.

One caution worth repeating: candidates often place all three Level 8 posts at the top of the preference list purely because they pay the most, without asking whether audit work suits them. The pay difference over a Level 7 Inspector post is real but not dramatic once DA and allowances are counted, while the difference in daily work is enormous. Rank honestly.
