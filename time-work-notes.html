Treat the total work as the LCM of the days given. Each person's daily work then becomes a whole number, and every question turns into simple division.

1. Key formulas

SituationFormula
Work per day1 ÷ days to finish
A and B togetherxy/(x + y) days
Efficiency ∝ 1/timeA : B = y : x if A takes x and B takes y days
Men–days ruleM₁D₁H₁ / W₁ = M₂D₂H₂ / W₂
Wages ∝work done (efficiency × days)
A leaves after d dayswork done by A = d × (A's daily work); rest by others

2. Shortcuts that save time

LCM method A takes 12 days, B takes 18 days. Total work = LCM = 36 units. A does 3 units/day, B does 2 units/day. Together 5 units/day → 36/5 = 7.2 days.
  • Alternate-day work: add up one full cycle (A + B), then handle the remaining units.
  • Wages are split in the ratio of work done, not of days worked.
  • If A is twice as efficient as B, A takes half the days B takes.

3. Solved examples

Example 1 A can do a job in 10 days and B in 15 days. Together: (10 × 15)/25 = 6 days.
Example 2 A and B finish a job in 12 days; A alone takes 20 days. B alone: LCM(12, 20) = 60 units. A = 3/day, A + B = 5/day, so B = 2/day → 60/2 = 30 days.
Example 3 15 men can build a wall in 20 days. How many days for 25 men? 15 × 20 = 25 × D → 12 days.
Example 4 A and B work alternately, A first. A takes 10 days and B takes 15 days. Work = 30 units; A = 3/day, B = 2/day. Two-day cycle = 5 units. 6 cycles = 30 units in 12 days.

4. Traps to avoid

  1. Do not add days — add daily work.
  2. If a worker leaves midway, count the units already done before dividing the rest.
  3. In wage problems, the share is by units of work done, not by hours at the site.
  4. Check whether the last day is partial before rounding.
