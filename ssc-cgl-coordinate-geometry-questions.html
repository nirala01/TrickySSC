A coordinate geometry question is a formula waiting to be applied to numbers. Once you know which object the question is about — a point, a segment, a line, a triangle or a circle — the working is usually two or three lines. The skill is turning the words into the right formula: equidistant means the perpendicular bisector, dividing a segment means the section formula, never meeting means equal slopes, and a line touching a circle means its distance from the centre equals the radius.

The marks are lost in three places. The first is sign slips — subtracting a negative coordinate, or reading the y-intercept of 4x − 3y = 24 as 8 instead of −8. The second is mixing up the centres: the centroid comes from the sum of the vertices divided by 3, the circumcentre is the same distance from all three, and in a right triangle the orthocentre is the right-angle vertex. The third is keeping one answer when the condition gives two — a given distance or area usually produces two values of k, and the question’s condition decides which one stays. Each test puts twenty-five coordinate geometry questions in front of you in one sitting, which is what makes those checks automatic.

What You Will Practice in the Coordinate Geometry Chapter

Points, distance and division

  • Quadrants, signs and the distance of a point from each axis
  • Reflections in the axes, the origin, the lines x = a and y = b, and the lines y = x and y = −x
  • The distance formula — perimeters, equidistant points, and telling right, isosceles and equilateral triangles or squares and rhombuses apart
  • The midpoint formula and the fourth vertex of a parallelogram
  • Internal and external division, trisection points, and the ratio in which an axis or a line cuts a segment

Triangles and areas

  • The centroid, circumcentre, orthocentre and incentre, and the Euler-line link H = 3G − 2O
  • Collinearity by equal slopes or zero area
  • The area of a triangle from its vertices, and the triangle joining the midpoints of the sides
  • Quadrilaterals and polygons by the shoelace method, and rhombuses from their diagonals
  • Areas bounded by a line and the axes, and by two or three lines

Lines, pairs of lines and circles

  • Slope, inclination and the angle between two lines
  • Point-slope, two-point, slope-intercept and intercept forms of a line
  • Lines parallel or perpendicular to a given line, and the altitudes, medians and perpendicular bisectors of a triangle
  • Unique, no and infinitely many solutions of a pair of linear equations, and three concurrent lines
  • Perpendicular distance, the gap between parallel lines, the foot of a perpendicular and the image of a point in a line
  • Circles — the equation from a centre and radius, from a diameter, and the radius from a tangent line

The distance, section and area rows cover most Tier I questions. For lines, the slope row and the pair-of-equations row answer almost everything else.

The three mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Losing a minus sign. The y-intercept of 4x − 3y = 24 is −8, and subtracting A = (−3, 5) means adding 3. Write every substitution in brackets before simplifying.
  • Using the wrong centre. The centroid comes from the sum of the vertices divided by 3; the circumcentre is the same distance from all three; the incentre weights each vertex by the opposite side. In a right triangle the circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse.
  • Keeping one root when there are two. A distance, an area or a squared ratio usually gives two values. Check both against the question — “positive”, “third quadrant” or the constants’ ratio decides which one stays.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Coordinate Geometry Questions

Q1. Find the point on the x-axis that is equidistant from A(3, 4) and B(7, 2).
Let the point be P(x, 0). PA² = PB² gives (x − 3)² + 16 = (x − 7)² + 4; the x² terms cancel, leaving 8x = 28, so x = 3.5. Check: PA² = 0.25 + 16 = 16.25 and PB² = 12.25 + 4 = 16.25. Answer: (3.5, 0)
Q2. Find the area of the triangle whose vertices are (1, 1), (4, 5) and (7, 2).
Area = ½|x1(y2 − y3) + x2(y3 − y1) + x3(y1 − y2)| = ½|1(3) + 4(1) + 7(−4)| = ½|−21|. The modulus removes the sign. Answer: 10.5 square units
Q3. For what value of k are the lines 2x + 3y = 7 and 6x + ky = 5 parallel?
Parallel lines need a1/a2 = b1/b2: 2/6 = 3/k, so k = 9. The constants give 7/5, not 1/3, so the lines are distinct rather than the same line. Answer: 9
Q4. How far is the point (1, 2) from the line 5x + 12y − 3 = 0?
Perpendicular distance = |ax1 + by1 + c| / √(a² + b²) = |5 + 24 − 3| / 13 = 26/13. Answer: 2 units

How to Prepare Coordinate Geometry for SSC CGL

  1. Learn the formulas as a chain. Distance leads to equidistant points and circles; the section formula leads to midpoints, centroids and trisection points; slope leads to parallel, perpendicular and every line equation.
  2. Sketch the points first. A ten-second sketch on rough axes shows the quadrant, any right angle and the rough size of the answer before any formula.
  3. Bracket every negative coordinate. Most wrong options in this chapter come from a single sign slip, and brackets make that slip visible.
  4. Look for a right angle. If two sides are perpendicular, the circumcentre, orthocentre and area each come in one line.
  5. Use the intercept form for axis questions. x/a + y/b = 1 answers intercept, area-with-axes and equal-intercept questions directly.
  6. Compare ratios for pairs of lines. Write a1/a2, b1/b2 and c1/c2 side by side; the pattern tells you unique, none or infinitely many.
  7. Keep both roots until the end. Then apply the question’s condition — quadrant, sign or position — to choose between them.
  8. Check with a second method. An area from the vertex formula can be checked with the box method, and the foot of a perpendicular must satisfy the line.
  9. Do Level 1 untimed, the same day you finish the theory. It is set at real SSC CGL difficulty; the aim is picking the right formula, not speed.
  10. Repeat Level 1 with the 15-minute clock on, then move to Level 2. After that, verify against a previous year paper and a full mock test under locked sectional timing.

SSC CGL निर्देशांक ज्यामिति (Coordinate Geometry) चैप्टर टेस्ट — हिंदी में

निर्देशांक ज्यामिति का हर प्रश्न शब्दों को सही सूत्र में बदलने का खेल है। पहले पहचानें कि प्रश्न किसके बारे में है — बिंदु, रेखाखंड, रेखा, त्रिभुज या वृत्त — फिर हल अक्सर दो-तीन पंक्तियों का होता है। जैसे बराबर दूरी का अर्थ है लम्ब समद्विभाजक, रेखाखंड को बाँटने का अर्थ है विभाजन सूत्र (SECTION FORMULA), कभी न मिलने का अर्थ है समान ढाल (SLOPE), और वृत्त को छूने वाली रेखा का अर्थ है केन्द्र से उसकी दूरी = त्रिज्या।

अंक तीन जगह कटते हैं। पहली: ऋण चिह्न की गलती — ऋणात्मक निर्देशांक घटाना या अंतःखंड (INTERCEPT) का चिह्न भूल जाना। दूसरी: केंद्रों को आपस में मिला देना — केन्द्रक (CENTROID) तीनों शीर्षों के निर्देशांकों के योग का एक-तिहाई है, परिकेन्द्र (CIRCUMCENTRE) तीनों शीर्षों से समान दूरी पर है, और समकोण त्रिभुज में लम्बकेन्द्र (ORTHOCENTRE) समकोण वाला शीर्ष है। तीसरी: दो में से एक मान छोड़ देना — दी गई दूरी या क्षेत्रफल से k के प्रायः दो मान आते हैं, और प्रश्न की शर्त तय करती है कि कौन-सा सही है।

SSC CGL Coordinate Geometry Questions — Frequently Asked Questions

How many coordinate geometry questions are asked in SSC CGL?
Usually none or one in Tier I out of the 25 maths questions, and one or two in Tier II Paper I. Most are intercepts, areas from vertices, slopes or the consistency of two linear equations, and each one should take under a minute once the formulas are familiar.
What topics come under coordinate geometry for SSC CGL?
Quadrants and reflections, the distance, midpoint and section formulas, the centroid, circumcentre, orthocentre and incentre, collinearity, areas of triangles and quadrilaterals from their vertices, intercepts, slopes and the angle between lines, every form of the equation of a line, parallel and perpendicular lines, pairs of linear equations, perpendicular distance, the image of a point in a line, and the equation of a circle.
Which coordinate geometry formulas should I memorise first?
The distance formula, the section formula, the centroid, the area of a triangle from its vertices, the slope from two points and from ax + by + c = 0, the conditions m₁ = m₂ for parallel and m₁m₂ = −1 for perpendicular lines, the intercept form x/a + y/b = 1, the perpendicular distance |ax₁ + by₁ + c| / √(a² + b²), and the circle (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r².
How do I know if two linear equations have a unique solution, no solution or infinitely many?
Compare the ratios of the coefficients. If a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂ the lines cross once. If a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ but the constants’ ratio c₁/c₂ is different, the lines are parallel and there is no solution. If all three ratios are equal, the two equations describe the same line and there are infinitely many solutions.
How do I find the area of a triangle when its vertices are given?
Use ½|x₁(y₂ − y₃) + x₂(y₃ − y₁) + x₃(y₁ − y₂)|. If one vertex is the origin this becomes ½|x₁y₂ − x₂y₁|. An area of zero means the three points are collinear, which is the quickest collinearity test.
Are there figure-based questions in the coordinate geometry tests?
Yes. Five questions in every test come with an original diagram — points and their reflections on a grid, plotted triangles and quadrilaterals, regions bounded by lines, and circles with a marked centre or diameter. Each figure is drawn for TrickySSC and shown alongside the question in both English and Hindi.
How many questions are in each coordinate geometry practice test?
Twenty-five questions per test, with an optional 15-minute timer and the real exam marking scheme of +2 for a correct answer, −0.5 for a wrong one and 0 for an unattempted question.
Are the coordinate geometry tests available in Hindi?
Yes. Questions, options, figures, detailed solutions and shortcut tricks are available in Hindi, and you choose English or Hindi before the test starts.
Which chapters should I study along with coordinate geometry?
Geometry supplies the centres, similar triangles and circle facts that coordinate geometry puts on a grid, quadratic equations handle the two roots that distance and area conditions produce, ratio and proportion underlies the section formula, and trigonometry gives the slope as tan θ.
