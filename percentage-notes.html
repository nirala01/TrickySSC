Percentage is the base of profit and loss, discount, interest and data interpretation. The single biggest speed-up is to think in fractions instead of percents.

1. Concepts in brief

What a percentage is

"Per cent" means "out of 100". 35% is 35 out of every 100, which is the fraction 35/100 or the decimal 0.35. Moving between these three forms quickly is the whole skill: to turn a fraction into a percentage multiply by 100, and to turn a percentage into a fraction divide by 100.

The base decides everything

A percentage is always a percentage of something. "Increase by 20%" means 20% of the original value; "20% of the new value" would be a different number. Before you calculate, ask: what is the 100% here?

Increase and decrease as multipliers

A rise of r% multiplies a value by (100 + r)/100; a fall of r% multiplies it by (100 − r)/100. Several changes in a row simply multiply together — which is why a 20% rise and a 20% fall give 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, a 4% loss.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 Sugar price rises 25%. By how much must consumption fall so spending is unchanged?
25/125 × 100 = 20%. (Fraction view: price × 5/4, so consumption × 4/5.)
Example 2 A salary is raised 20% and then cut 20%. Net change = 20 − 20 − 400/100 = −4%.
Example 3 40% of a number is 72 more than 25% of it. 15% ↔ 72, so the number is 72 × 100/15 = 480.
Example 4 In a two-person election the winner gets 55% of valid votes and wins by 1500. The gap is 55 − 45 = 10% of the votes, so valid votes = 15,000.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 5 A town of 50,000 grows by 10% a year. After 2 years: 50000 × 1.1 × 1.1 = 60,500.
Example 6 A's income is 20% less than B's. By what percentage is B's income more than A's? 20/80 × 100 = 25%.
Example 7 A student needs 40% to pass. He scores 180 and fails by 20 marks, so the pass mark is 200 = 40% of the maximum. Maximum marks = 500.
Example 8 The price of petrol rises 20% and then 10%. Total rise = 20 + 10 + 200/100 = 32%.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. Find 37.5% of 640.

Answer: 240 (37.5% = 3/8).

Q2. A number increased by 30% becomes 390. Find the number.

Answer: 300 (390 × 10/13).

Q3. A price is raised 10% and then lowered 10%. Net change?

Answer: 1% decrease (−10²/100).

Q4. Price falls 20%. By how much can consumption rise with the same spending?

Answer: 25% (20/80 × 100).

Q5. A machine worth ₹20,000 depreciates 10% a year. Value after 2 years?

Answer: ₹16,200 (20000 × 0.9 × 0.9).

6. Traps to avoid

  1. A 20% rise followed by a 20% fall is not zero change — the base changed.
  2. "A is 25% more than B" means B is 20% less than A, not 25%.
  3. Reverse questions: if ₹600 is the price after a 20% rise, the original is 600 × 5/6 = ₹500, not 600 − 20% of 600.
  4. Percentage points ≠ percent: a rate going from 10% to 12% is a 2-point rise but a 20% rise.
  5. In elections, check whether the percentage is of total votes or of valid votes.
