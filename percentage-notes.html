Percentage is the base of profit and loss, discount, interest and data interpretation. The single biggest speed-up is to think in fractions instead of percents.

1. Fraction equivalents to memorise

Use multipliers A 25% increase means × 5/4; a 20% decrease means × 4/5; a 12½% increase means × 9/8. Chains of changes become one multiplication.

3. Solved examples

Example 1 Sugar price rises 25%. By how much must consumption fall so spending is unchanged?
25/125 × 100 = 20%. (Fraction view: price × 5/4, so consumption × 4/5.)
Example 2 A salary is raised 20% and then cut 20%. Net change = 20 − 20 − 400/100 = −4%.
Example 3 40% of a number is 72 more than 25% of it. 15% ↔ 72, so the number is 72 × 100/15 = 480.
Example 4 In a two-person election the winner gets 55% of valid votes and wins by 1500. The gap is 55 − 45 = 10% of the votes, so valid votes = 15,000.

4. Traps to avoid

  1. A 20% rise followed by a 20% fall is not zero change — the base changed.
  2. "A is 25% more than B" means B is 20% less than A, not 25%.
  3. Reverse questions: if ₹600 is the price after a 20% rise, the original is 600 × 5/6 = ₹500, not 600 − 20% of 600.
  4. Percentage points ≠ percent: a rate going from 10% to 12% is a 2-point rise but a 20% rise.
  5. In elections, check whether the percentage is of total votes or of valid votes.
