Data Interpretation tests reading speed and percentage sense, not heavy maths. Read the title, units and legend first, then compute only what each question needs.

1. Concepts in brief

The chart types you will see

  • Table — exact numbers in rows and columns; questions test calculation.
  • Bar graph — compares categories; read values against the scale.
  • Line graph — shows change over time; slopes show growth or fall.
  • Pie chart — shows parts of a whole, given as percentages or degrees.
  • Mixed charts — two of these together; match the right one to each question.

Approximate, then check

Round the numbers to friendly values, estimate, and compare with the options. If two options are close, go back and calculate exactly. This saves more time in DI than any formula.

A worked set

Cars produced by a company (in thousands): 2021 – 40, 2022 – 50, 2023 – 45, 2024 – 60, 2025 – 75.

2. How to approach every DI set

  1. Read the title, axes and units — thousands, lakhs, crores, per cent?
  2. Read the question before reading the data — it tells you what to look for.
  3. Estimate when options are far apart; calculate only when they are close.
  4. Use fractions from the percentage table (33⅓ = 1/3, 12½ = 1/8 and so on).

4. Shortcuts and examples

Compare fractions, don't compute them Is 18/45 greater than 22/55? Both reduce to 2/5 — equal. Cross-multiply (18 × 55 vs 22 × 45) only when reducing doesn't help.
Example 1 In a pie chart, a sector of 20% has angle 20 × 3.6 = 72°. A 90° sector is 25%.
Example 2 Sales rise from 400 to 500: change = 100/400 × 100 = 25%. If they then fall back from 500 to 400, the fall is 100/500 × 100 = 20% — a different base.
Example 3 A pie chart for total expenditure ₹60,000 has Rent = 30%. Rent = 0.30 × 60000 = ₹18,000.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 4 Growth from 2024 to 2025 = 15/60 × 100 = 25%.
Example 5 Average yearly production = 270/5 = 54 thousand.
Example 6 Ratio of 2021 to 2025 production = 40 : 75 = 8 : 15.
Example 7 Years above the average of 54: 2024 and 2025 → 2 years.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Use this pie chart: a family's monthly budget of ₹40,000 is split as Food 30%, Rent 25%, Education 15%, Transport 10%, Savings 20%.

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. How much is spent on rent?

Answer: ₹10,000.

Q2. What is the central angle for education?

Answer: 54° (15 × 3.6).

Q3. By how much does food exceed transport?

Answer: ₹8,000 (20% of 40,000).

Q4. Ratio of savings to rent?

Answer: 4 : 5.

Q5. If income rises 10% and the shares stay the same, food spending becomes?

Answer: ₹13,200 (30% of 44,000).

6. Traps to avoid

  1. Percentage points and percent are different: 10% → 15% is a 5-point rise but a 50% rise.
  2. Check the unit in the graph — "in thousands" changes every answer.
  3. Use the right base: "percentage of A over B" divides by B, not A.
  4. In multiple-chart sets, match the right chart, year and category before calculating.
  5. Don't read approximate values from a graph as exact — use the figures given, or the nearest gridline.
