Every puzzle in this chapter is solved the same way: draw the frame, fix the definite clues, then hang the linked clues on them. The frame is a column of floors or boxes, a row of days or months, a chain from tallest to shortest, or a small grid of names against cities, colours or professions. Once the frame is on paper, each clue either fixes a place or cuts the choices down to two — and two choices are settled by trying one and seeing whether a clue breaks.

Marks are lost in three places. The first is counting “between” wrongly — “two floors between X and Y” means their floor numbers differ by 3, not by 2. The second is reading “somewhere above” as “immediately above”. The third is stopping too early: answering from a half-built arrangement when one more clue would have moved a person.

Puzzles are a regular part of General Intelligence — usually one or two questions in a Tier I shift, and the same small puzzles appear in CHSL. Each is a short set of clues with a single question. The forms it takes are listed below, most common first.

What You Will Practice in the Puzzle Chapter

Floors, boxes and days

  • Five to eight people on the floors of a building, with “immediately above”, “two floors below”, “floors between”, odd and even floors
  • A building with one vacant floor, and floors where each person also likes a colour or comes from a city
  • Boxes in a single stack, with positions counted from the top or from the bottom
  • One person per day from Monday onward, with “the day immediately after” and “persons between”

Comparisons and months

  • Height, weight, age, marks, speed and salary chains — “A is taller than B but shorter than C”
  • “Only two persons are taller than X” and “X is heavier than only one person”
  • Birthdays in six or seven given months, with 30- and 31-day months and “born between”

Data arrangement (matching grids)

  • Four or five names matched with one item each — city, colour, game, fruit, profession or car
  • Two items per person in Level 2, with clues such as “the person from Delhi likes Red”
  • “Neither A nor B is the doctor” and other elimination clues

Rules worth knowing instantly

ClueWhat it means
X lives immediately above YX’s floor = Y’s floor + 1
X lives two floors above YX’s floor = Y’s floor + 2
Two floors (or persons) between X and YThe floor numbers differ by 3, in either direction
X lives somewhere above YAny higher floor — not necessarily the next one
k-th box from the top, n boxes in all(n − k + 1)-th from the bottom
Only two persons are taller than XX is the 3rd tallest
X is taller than only one personX is the 2nd shortest
One floor is vacantIt counts as a floor, never as a person

Write every clue as a number relation (+1, +2, a gap of 3) before placing anyone — most wrong answers come from placing first and translating later.

The three mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Counting the end people. “Only one person lives between X and Y” puts them two floors apart, not one.
  • Turning “above” into “immediately above”. “Somewhere above” only removes the floors at and below Y.
  • Answering from a half-built arrangement. Finish placing everyone before you read the answer — the last clue often moves someone.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Puzzles

Q1. C, E, T, U and Y are five students who stay in a 5-storey building, one per floor; the floors are numbered 1 to 5 from the bottom. Y stays on the top floor. Only two persons stay between Y and U. E stays somewhere below the floor of T. E stays on some floor above U. Who lives on floor 2?
Y is on floor 5. Two persons between Y and U puts U on floor 2. E is above U but below T, and only floors 1, 3 and 4 are free, so E is on 3 and T on 4; C takes floor 1. Answer: U
Q2. Exactly one of N, O, P, Q and R visits the bank on each day from Monday to Friday. N goes just one day before P. O goes on the day immediately after P. O goes on some day before Thursday. R’s day comes just after O’s. Who visits the bank on Friday?
N, P, O and R come on four consecutive days in that order. O is before Thursday, so the block must start on Monday: N Mon, P Tue, O Wed, R Thu. Friday is left for Q. Answer: Q
Q3. Of the five colleagues J, K, L, M and N, no two have the same weight. K is the fourth heaviest. M is heavier than J, who in turn is heavier than N. L is the lightest. Who is the third heaviest?
L is 5th and K is 4th, so the top three places go to M > J > N in that order. Chain: M > J > N > K > L. Answer: N
Q4. F, S, T, Y and Z live on floors 1 to 5 (floor 1 at the bottom), one per floor, and like different colours — Orange, Red, Black, Blue and Pink. The person who likes Blue lives three floors below Z. The person who likes Red and Y have exactly two floors between them. The person who likes Pink lives below Y. The person who likes Orange lives three floors above F. The person who likes Blue lives above T. Who likes Red?
Blue is three floors below Z and above T, so Blue is on floor 2, Z on 5 and T on 1. Orange is three floors above F: F must be on 2 (floor 1 is T), so Orange is on 5. Pink is below Y and Red has two floors between it and Y; the only fit is Y on 4, Red on 1, Pink on 3, and S takes floor 3. Floor 1 is T, so T likes Red. Answer: T

How to Prepare Puzzles for SSC CGL

  1. Draw the frame before reading the clues twice. Floors bottom to top, days left to right, a chain for comparisons, a grid for matching.
  2. Place the definite clues first. An exact floor, the top or bottom, a named day — then attach everything linked to those people.
  3. Translate each clue into numbers. “Immediately above” is +1, “two floors above” is +2, “two between” is a gap of 3.
  4. When two places remain, test one. Put the person there and follow the clues; if one breaks, the other place is the answer.
  5. In matching grids, cross out first. Every “not” clue is a cross; a row or column with one blank left is solved.
  6. Do Level 1 untimed first, then with the 15-minute clock. Move to Level 2 once Level 1 is steady, and then check yourself on a previous year paper.

SSC CGL Puzzle & Data Arrangement — Frequently Asked Questions

How many puzzle questions are asked in SSC CGL?
Usually one or two questions in a Tier I reasoning section, and the same small puzzles appear in SSC CHSL. Each one is a short puzzle of four to eight clues with a single question, so it is quick once the method is fixed.
What is the fastest way to solve a floor puzzle?
Draw the floors as a column numbered from 1 at the bottom. Place the definite clues first (an exact floor, top or bottom), then hang the ‘immediately above’ and ‘floors between’ clues on them. If two places remain for someone, try one and see whether a clue breaks.
What does ‘two floors between X and Y’ mean?
Two floors lie strictly between them, so their floor numbers differ by 3 — for example floors 2 and 5. ‘Two floors above Y’ is different: it means Y’s floor number plus 2.
How do I handle a vacant floor?
Treat the vacant floor like an extra person called ‘vacant’. It counts when a clue talks about floors between two people, but not when a clue or question counts persons.
How do I solve comparison puzzles like ‘A is taller than B but shorter than C’?
Turn every clue into a piece of one chain, C > A > B, and join the pieces. ‘Only two are taller than X’ places X third from the top, and ‘X is taller than only one person’ places X second from the bottom.
How many questions are in each test?
Twenty-five questions per test, with an optional 15-minute timer and the real exam marking of +2 for a correct answer, −0.5 for a wrong one and 0 for an unattempted question.
Are the tests available in Hindi?
Yes. Questions, options, detailed solutions and shortcut tricks are available in Hindi, and you choose English or Hindi before the test starts.
What is the difference between Level 1 and Level 2?
Level 1 is set at real SSC CGL difficulty — five to seven people, one set of names and clues you can follow straight through. Level 2 is harder: a second attribute such as colour or city on every floor or day, a vacant floor, birth months, two-attribute matching grids and longer comparison chains, with some puzzles that need a case to be tested and ruled out.
