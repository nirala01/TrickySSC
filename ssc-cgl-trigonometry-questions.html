Trigonometry looks like a long formula list and behaves like a short one. Almost every question in the SSC CGL paper is an algebraic identity wearing trigonometric clothes: a difference of squares, a perfect square, a sum of cubes, or a quantity added to its own reciprocal. Once you see which algebraic shape is in front of you, the trigonometry is only the last line.

Three facts carry most of the chapter. sin² θ + cos² θ = 1 and its two cousins turn every squared expression into something smaller. Complementary angles swap the ratio for its co-ratio, which is why sin 21° and cos 69° are the same number and why long products of tangents collapse to 1. And the conjugate pairs — sec θ ± tan θ, cosec θ ± cot θ — multiply to 1, so knowing one member hands you the other. Each test puts twenty-five questions in front of you in one sitting, which is what turns those three into reflexes.

How Many Trigonometry Questions Are Asked in SSC CGL?

Trigonometry is one of the reliable scoring blocks of advanced maths. Counting height and distance separately, it is worth two to three questions in Tier I and three to four in Tier II Paper I — and the identity questions among them are usually the quickest marks on the page.

What You Will Practice in the Trigonometry Chapter

Ratios, identities and angles

  • One ratio given and another asked, through the right triangle and the Pythagorean triples 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25 and 9-40-41
  • A ratio with the quadrant named — getting the sign right before the value
  • Expressions built from the standard angles 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°, with coefficients and squares
  • Complementary angles: sin(90° − θ) = cos θ, tangent products that collapse to 1, and squared pairs that add to 1
  • Allied angles and reduction of large or negative angles — 1230°, −870°, 600° — to the first turn
  • Homogeneous fractions in sine and cosine, cleared by dividing through by cos θ (or cos² θ)
  • Identities to simplify, and identities to recognise among four options
  • Conjugate pairs sec θ ± tan θ and cosec θ ± cot θ, split, squared and inverted
  • sin θ ± cos θ and sin θ cos θ converted into each other, and into cubes
  • Power sums: sin⁴ θ + cos⁴ θ = 1 − 2p and sin⁶ θ + cos⁶ θ = 1 − 3p
  • A ratio plus its reciprocal, squared and cubed — tan θ + cot θ, sin θ + cosec θ
  • Conditions that force a single angle, such as sin θ + cos θ = √2 or sin θ cos θ = ½

Angles, ranges and figures

  • Maximum and minimum of a sin θ + b cos θ, of weighted squares, and AM–GM bounds with the equality point checked
  • Trigonometric equations solved for an acute angle, including quadratics with a root to reject
  • Compound angles: sin(A ± B), cos(A ± B), tan(A ± B), and exact values such as 15°, 75° and 22½°
  • Double, triple and half angles, run forwards and backwards
  • Sum-to-product and product-to-sum, and chained products such as cos 20° cos 40° cos 80°
  • Elimination of the angle from a pair of expressions — the ellipse and hyperbola forms
  • Ratios of the angles of a triangle, where A + B + C = 180°
  • Radian and degree measure, complements and supplements, arcs and sector areas
  • Where a ratio is undefined, which values are impossible, and how many angles satisfy a condition

The first three rows answer the majority of Tier I trigonometry questions on their own. The power-sum row is the one that turns a frightening sixth-power expression into a single subtraction.

The three mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Reading the sign off the ratio instead of the quadrant. A sine of ½ is not enough to place an angle — 30° and 150° share it. Fix the quadrant first, then the value; in the third quadrant a negative sine and a negative cosine give a positive tangent.
  • Stopping one step short. Squared answers are the commonest wrong tick: tan² θ = 2 is not the tangent, and sin²(θ÷2) = ⅛ is not the sine. Read whether the question wants the square, the root, or the reciprocal.
  • Using AM–GM without checking the equality point. sec² θ + 4cos² θ really does bottom out at 4, but 4cosec² θ + sin² θ never reaches the bound of 4 the same argument suggests, because it would need sin² θ = 2. When the equality point is out of range, the minimum sits at an endpoint instead.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Trigonometry Questions

Q1. If sec θ + tan θ = 5, what is sin θ?
The pair is conjugate, so secθ − tanθ = 1÷5. Adding the two gives 2secθ = 5 + 0.2 = 5.2, so secθ = 13÷5; subtracting gives tanθ = 12÷5. Then sinθ = tanθ ÷ secθ = 12÷13 — the 5-12-13 triangle. Answer: 12/13
Q2. If sin θ + cos θ = &frac54;, find sin⁶ θ + cos⁶ θ.
Square the sum: 25÷16 = 1 + 2sinθcosθ, so sinθcosθ = 9÷32 and p = sin²θcos²θ = 81÷1024. Then sin⁶θ + cos⁶θ = 1 − 3p = 1 − 243÷1024 = 781÷1024. The whole question is one squaring and one substitution. Answer: 781/1024
Q3. Evaluate tan 1° × tan 2° × … × tan 89°.
Pair each angle with its complement: tan 1° tan 89° = 1, tan 2° tan 88° = 1, and so on up to tan 44° tan 46° = 1 — forty-four pairs. The unpaired middle term is tan 45° = 1. There is no tan 90° in the list, so nothing blows up. Answer: 1
Q4. What is the greatest value of 12 sin θ − 5 cos θ + 2?
A linear combination runs between −√(a²+b²) and +√(a²+b²), and the sign of b does not matter because it is squared: √(144 + 25) = 13. The constant lifts the whole range, so the maximum is 13 + 2 = 15, reached at sinθ = 12÷13, cosθ = −5÷13. Answer: 15

How to Prepare Trigonometry for SSC CGL

  1. Learn the standard-angle table cold. 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90° for all six ratios, written from memory in under a minute. Everything else in the chapter assumes it.
  2. Name the algebraic shape first. Before touching an identity, ask whether you are looking at a difference of squares, a perfect square, a sum of cubes, or a quantity plus its reciprocal. The trigonometry is the last line, not the first.
  3. Keep the three Pythagorean identities in both directions. You need 1 − sin²θ = cos²θ as often as the forward form, and tan²θ − sec²θ = −1 catches people out.
  4. Turn every conjugate pair into its partner immediately. Given sec θ + tan θ, write the reciprocal underneath before doing anything else.
  5. Reduce p first. When fourth or sixth powers appear, find p = sin²θcos²θ and both answers follow without ever finding the angle.
  6. Divide homogeneous fractions through. Every term carrying exactly one sine or cosine means dividing by cos θ leaves only tangents; if every term is a square, divide by cos² θ.
  7. Fix the quadrant before the value. Write down which ratios are positive there, then read the size off the reference angle.
  8. Check what is actually asked. Square or root, ratio or reciprocal, θ or 2θ — most wrong options are the correct working stopped one step early.
  9. Do Level 1 untimed, the same day you finish the identities. It is set at real SSC CGL difficulty; the aim is picking the right identity, not speed.
  10. Repeat Level 1 with the 15-minute clock on, then move to Level 2. After that, verify against a previous year paper and a full mock test under locked sectional timing.

त्रिकोणमिति देखने में सूत्रों की लंबी सूची लगती है, पर काम करती है छोटी सूची की तरह। परीक्षा का लगभग हर प्रश्न असल में बीजगणित की कोई पहचान (IDENTITY) है, बस त्रिकोणमिति के कपड़ों में — वर्गों का अंतर, पूर्ण वर्ग, घनों का योग, या कोई राशि अपने ही व्युत्क्रम के साथ। सामने का बीजीय ढाँचा पहचान लेने पर त्रिकोणमिति केवल आखिरी पंक्ति रह जाती है।

तीन बातें पूरा अध्याय सँभालती हैं। पहली: sin² θ + cos² θ = 1 और उसके दो साथी, जो हर वर्गित व्यंजक को छोटा कर देते हैं। दूसरी: पूरक कोण — sin(90° − θ) = cos θ, इसीलिए sin 21° और cos 69° एक ही संख्या हैं और tangent के लंबे गुणनफल 1 में सिमट जाते हैं। तीसरी: संयुग्मी जोड़े sec θ ± tan θ और cosec θ ± cot θ, जिनका गुणनफल 1 होता है — एक पता हो तो दूसरा अपने आप मिल जाता है।

अंक तीन जगह कटते हैं: चिह्न quadrant से तय करने के बजाय ratio से मान लेना; एक कदम पहले रुक जाना (वर्ग को उत्तर समझ लेना, या θ की जगह 2θ पढ़ना); और AM–GM की सीमा बिना यह जाँचे लगा देना कि समानता का बिंदु परास में है भी या नहीं।

SSC CGL Trigonometry Questions — Frequently Asked Questions

How many trigonometry questions are asked in SSC CGL?
Two to three in Tier I out of the 25 maths questions, and three to four in Tier II Paper I, counting height and distance separately. Most are a single identity applied once — simplify an expression, evaluate a chain of standard angles, or find a maximum value — and are among the fastest marks in the paper.
Which trigonometry formulas must be memorised for SSC CGL?
Three Pythagorean identities (sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, sec²θ − tan²θ = 1, cosec²θ − cot²θ = 1), the standard-angle table for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°, the complementary rule sin(90° − θ) = cosθ, the compound-angle formulas for sin(A ± B), cos(A ± B) and tan(A ± B), and the double-angle forms. Everything else in the chapter is built from these.
What is the trick for secθ + tanθ questions?
They are a conjugate pair: (secθ + tanθ)(secθ − tanθ) = 1, so each is the reciprocal of the other. If secθ + tanθ = k then secθ − tanθ = 1/k, and adding or subtracting the two gives secθ and tanθ separately. The same works for cosecθ ± cotθ.
How do I find the maximum and minimum of a sinθ + b cosθ?
It always lies between −√(a² + b²) and +√(a² + b²), and both ends are actually reached. So 3sinθ + 4cosθ runs from −5 to 5. The sign of b does not matter, because it is squared.
How are sin⁴θ + cos⁴θ and sin⁶θ + cos⁶θ simplified?
Both reduce to the single quantity p = sin²θcos²θ: the fourth-power sum is 1 − 2p and the sixth-power sum is 1 − 3p. Find p from whatever the question gives — usually sinθ + cosθ or sinθcosθ — and both answers follow in one line.
Is height and distance part of the trigonometry chapter here?
No. Angle of elevation and depression questions are kept in a separate Heights & Distances chapter so that the practice stays on identities and angle work. Both appear in the exam, and both are worth preparing.
How many questions are in each trigonometry practice test?
Twenty-five questions per test, with an optional 15-minute timer and the real exam marking scheme of +2 for a correct answer, −0.5 for a wrong one and 0 for an unattempted question.
Are the trigonometry tests available in Hindi?
Yes. Questions, options, detailed solutions and shortcut tricks are available in Hindi, and you choose English or Hindi before the test starts.
Which chapters should I study along with trigonometry?
Algebra first — almost every identity question is an algebraic identity in disguise, so a² ± b², (a ± b)², a³ ± b³ and the difference of squares do most of the work. Ratio and proportion covers the right-triangle triples, quadratic equations cover the Level 2 questions that end in a quadratic, and heights and distances applies the same ratios to a figure.
