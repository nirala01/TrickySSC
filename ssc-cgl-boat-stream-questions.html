The whole chapter is two additions. Going with the current, the boat’s speed over the ground is boat speed + stream speed; going against it, boat speed − stream speed. Run those backwards and you get the other pair: boat speed = half the sum of the two ground speeds, stream speed = half their difference. Every question in the chapter is one of these four lines, plus distance = speed × time.

The marks are lost in three places. The first is mixing up which speed is which — a boat at 8 km/h in a 2 km/h stream goes 10 km/h downstream and 6 km/h upstream, and the question will offer 10 and 6 the wrong way round. The second is averaging the two ground speeds for a round trip: the average speed of the trip is not the boat speed, because the upstream leg takes longer. The third is reading a time ratio as a speed ratio — if the upstream leg takes twice as long as the downstream leg, the ground speeds are in the ratio 1 : 2, and the boat is three times the stream, not two. Each test puts twenty-five boat and stream questions in front of you in one sitting, which is what makes those checks automatic.

Rules worth memorising

The first two rows carry the chapter. Everything else is those two applied with the right distance, or the raft rule — anything that only floats moves at the speed of the stream.

The three mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Swapping the two speeds. Downstream is with the current and is the larger speed. If the upstream figure comes out larger than the downstream one, a sign has been flipped somewhere.
  • Averaging speeds for a round trip. The two legs are equal distances at different speeds, so the trip average is 2DU ÷ (D + U), and it is always less than the boat’s still-water speed. Use total distance over total time.
  • Reading time ratios as speed ratios. Over the same distance, times are inverse to speeds. Upstream taking twice as long means the ground speeds are 1 : 2, which makes the boat 3 : 1 to the stream.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Boat and Stream

Q1. A boat goes 24 km downstream in 2 hours and returns the same distance upstream in 4 hours. Find the speed of the boat in still water and the speed of the stream.
Downstream speed = 24 ÷ 2 = 12 km/h; upstream speed = 24 ÷ 4 = 6 km/h. Boat = (12 + 6) ÷ 2 = 9 km/h; stream = (12 − 6) ÷ 2 = 3 km/h. Check: 9 + 3 = 12 and 9 − 3 = 6. Answer: 9 km/h and 3 km/h
Q2. A boat takes twice as long to go a certain distance upstream as it takes to come back downstream. What is the ratio of the speed of the boat in still water to the speed of the stream?
Same distance, so speeds are inverse to times: upstream : downstream = 1 : 2. Let them be x and 2x. Boat = (2x + x) ÷ 2 = 1.5x, stream = (2x − x) ÷ 2 = 0.5x. Ratio 1.5 : 0.5 = 3 : 1. Reading “twice” as 2 : 1 for the boat and stream is the trap. Answer: 3 : 1
Q3. A man rows at 5 km/h in still water in a river flowing at 1 km/h. He rows to a place and back in 5 hours. How far is the place?
Downstream 6 km/h, upstream 4 km/h. For distance d: d/6 + d/4 = 5, so 5d/12 = 5 and d = 12 km. Shortcut: d = T(b² − s²) ÷ 2b = 5 × 24 ÷ 10 = 12. Halving the time and using the boat speed (2.5 × 5 = 12.5) is the wrong option. Answer: 12 km
Q4. A raft drifts 6 km down a river in 3 hours. A boat that rows at 8 km/h in still water starts from the same point at the same time. How far apart are they after 3 hours if the boat goes downstream?
The raft moves only with the current, so the stream speed is 6 ÷ 3 = 2 km/h. The boat goes downstream at 8 + 2 = 10 km/h and covers 30 km in 3 hours; the raft covers 6 km. Gap = 30 − 6 = 24 km — which is simply the boat’s still-water speed times the time, 8 × 3, because the current carries both alike. Answer: 24 km

How to Prepare Boat and Stream for SSC CGL

  1. Write the four speeds before reading the question twice. b, s, b + s and b − s. Half the chapter is knowing which of the four the question is using.
  2. Downstream is the bigger number. If your upstream speed comes out larger, stop and check the sign.
  3. Convert units once, at the start. Metres per minute and km/h do not mix; settle on one before adding or subtracting anything.
  4. Over equal distances, invert the times to get the speeds. Then take half the sum and half the difference.
  5. For a round trip, add the two times, never average the two speeds. The 2bd ÷ (b² − s²) line saves a minute when the total time is given.
  6. Anything that only floats moves with the stream. A raft, a hat, a log: its speed is s, and the boat separates from it at speed b whatever the current.
  7. Between two boats the current cancels. Add or subtract their still-water speeds as in an ordinary meeting question.
  8. Underline what is asked. Boat speed, stream speed, a distance, a total time and the time for one leg are all different answers.
  9. Do Level 1 untimed, the same day you finish the theory. It is set at real SSC CGL difficulty; the aim is picking the right rule, not speed.
  10. Repeat Level 1 with the 15-minute clock on, then move to Level 2. After that, verify against a previous year paper and a full mock test under locked sectional timing.

SSC CGL Boat and Stream — Frequently Asked Questions

How many boat and stream questions are asked in SSC CGL?
Usually none or one in Tier I out of the 25 maths questions, and none or one in Tier II Paper I. It is a single-rule question in most shifts — the two speeds, a round-trip distance or a speed ratio — and should take well under a minute.
What topics come under boat and stream for SSC CGL?
Downstream and upstream speeds; boat and stream speeds recovered from them; distances and times for one leg or a round trip; the one-way distance from a total round-trip time; ratios of times and speeds; a boat taking a stated multiple of the downstream time to return; floating objects, rafts and logs that move with the current; boats meeting on a river; and Level 2 chains such as two round trips at two speeds, a quadratic from a round-trip time, and a boat turning back for something that fell overboard.
What are downstream and upstream?
Downstream means moving in the direction of the current, so the boat's ground speed is its still-water speed plus the stream speed. Upstream means moving against the current, so the ground speed is the still-water speed minus the stream speed. Downstream is always the faster of the two.
How do I find the speed of the boat and the speed of the stream?
From the downstream speed D and the upstream speed U: boat speed = (D + U) ÷ 2 and stream speed = (D − U) ÷ 2. If a boat does 12 km/h downstream and 6 km/h upstream, the boat is 9 km/h and the stream 3 km/h.
How do round-trip questions work?
Add the two leg times: d ÷ (b + s) + d ÷ (b − s), which simplifies to 2bd ÷ (b² − s²). If the total time T is given and the distance is asked, d = T(b² − s²) ÷ 2b. Never take the boat speed times half the total time — the upstream leg is the slower one and takes more than half.
How fast does a raft or a floating object move?
At the speed of the stream alone. A raft that drifts 6 km in 3 hours tells you the current is 2 km/h. Because the current carries the raft and the boat alike, a boat moves away from or towards a raft at its own still-water speed, whichever way it rows.
How many questions are in each boat and stream practice test?
Twenty-five questions per test, with an optional 15-minute timer and the real exam marking scheme of +2 for a correct answer, −0.5 for a wrong one and 0 for an unattempted question.
Are the boat and stream tests available in Hindi?
Yes. Questions, options, detailed solutions and shortcut tricks are available in Hindi, and you choose English or Hindi before the test starts.
Which chapters should I study along with boat and stream?
Time, speed and distance first, because boat and stream is the same chapter with the speeds added or subtracted before you start. Ratio and proportion covers the time and speed ratios, and quadratic equations help with the Level 2 round-trip questions where the total time is given and a speed is unknown.
