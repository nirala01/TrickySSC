Probability is favourable outcomes divided by total outcomes — but only when all outcomes are equally likely. Count carefully and the rest is arithmetic.

1. Concepts in brief

The words you need

An experiment is something with uncertain results, like tossing a coin. Each possible result is an outcome, and the list of all outcomes is the sample space. An event is any group of outcomes you care about, such as "getting an even number".

Equally likely outcomes

The basic formula only works when every outcome has the same chance — a fair coin, a fair die, a well-shuffled pack. Then probability is simply favourable outcomes ÷ total outcomes.

Sure, impossible and complementary events

An event that must happen has probability 1; one that cannot happen has probability 0. An event and its complement ("not E") always add to 1, which is why "at least one" questions are easier through the complement.

3. Shortcuts that save time

"At least one" → use the complement P(at least one head in 3 tosses) = 1 − P(no head) = 1 − 1/8 = 7/8.
  • Choosing r from n: use ⁿCr = n!/(r!(n − r)!) for both favourable and total.
  • Replacement keeps the probabilities the same each time; without replacement they change.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 Probability of getting a prime on one die: primes 2, 3, 5 → 3/6 = 1/2.
Example 2 Two dice thrown, probability of sum 8: (2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3), (6,2) = 5/36.
Example 3 A bag has 4 red and 6 blue balls. Two are drawn without replacement. P(both red) = (4/10)(3/9) = 2/15.
Example 4 Probability of drawing a king or a heart from a pack = 4/52 + 13/52 − 1/52 = 16/52 = 4/13.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 5 Three coins are tossed. P(exactly two heads) = HHT, HTH, THH out of 8 = 3/8.
Example 6 A card is drawn from a pack. P(face card) = 12/52 = 3/13.
Example 7 Two dice are thrown. P(a doublet) = 6/36 = 1/6.
Example 8 P(a leap year has 53 Sundays): 52 full weeks plus 2 extra days, and 2 of the 7 possible pairs contain a Sunday → 2/7.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. A die is thrown. P(even number)?

Answer: 1/2.

Q2. Two coins are tossed. P(at least one tail)?

Answer: 3/4 (1 − 1/4).

Q3. A card is drawn. P(red ace)?

Answer: 1/26 (2/52).

Q4. A bag has 5 red and 3 green balls. P(green)?

Answer: 3/8.

Q5. Two dice are thrown. P(sum 10 or more)?

Answer: 1/6 (6 outcomes out of 36).

6. Traps to avoid

  1. Subtract the overlap when events are not mutually exclusive.
  2. Two dice have 36 outcomes, not 12.
  3. Without replacement, reduce both the favourable and the total on the second draw.
  4. Probability is never more than 1 — if you get it, check the count.
