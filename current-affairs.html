About this page

Daily Current Affairs 2026 for SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO and GD

This page is the complete date-wise archive of daily current affairs on TrickySSC. Every day gets its own page, and each page is written specifically for candidates preparing for SSC CGL, SSC CHSL, SSC MTS, SSC CPO, SSC GD Constable and other competitive examinations — not as news reporting, but as the short factual one-liners that the General Awareness section actually tests.

Most current affairs sources give you long articles. SSC does not ask long articles. It asks which ministry implements a scheme, where a summit was held, what a programme's full form is, who launched a mission and on which date. So every topic here is stripped down to those facts, and every topic ends with an Important Facts for Exams box that works as a ready-made revision sheet in the final weeks before the exam.

Why current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each — 50 marks out of 200 — and under the 2026 pattern it must be finished inside a locked 15-minute sectional window. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, this section rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can clear it in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a cushion into the rest of the paper.

Current affairs form a meaningful share of that section, and the same items reappear across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO and GD within the same exam cycle. Ten focused minutes a day is far more effective than trying to absorb a 300-page compilation in the last week.

Categories SSC asks current affairs from

Across recent exam cycles, current affairs questions have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes notes much easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body

How to use this current affairs archive

  1. Read the newest page every morning. Start with the “Today at a glance” box — seven lines, under two minutes.
  2. Read the full topics once, slowly. Understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Attempt the 10 MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something beats several passive re-readings later.
  4. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven dates and read only the fact boxes — about fifteen minutes for a full week.
  5. Link every current affair to static GK. A summit item should trigger a revision of headquarters and capitals; a scheme item should trigger the ministry. SSC frequently mixes the two.
  6. Revise the fact boxes in the last ten days. They are written in question–answer shape precisely for that final pass.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often are current affairs updated on TrickySSC?
A new page is published every day, and it appears in the date list on this page automatically. The newest date is always shown in the highlighted card at the top, so you never have to hunt for the latest one.
Are the daily current affairs pages free?
Yes. Every daily page and the complete date-wise archive are free to read, and no login or registration is required. SSC CGL and SSC CHSL previous year papers are also free; the full 100-mock test series is on the paid plan, with Mocks 1–4 free for everyone.
Which exams are these current affairs useful for?
SSC CGL, SSC CHSL, SSC MTS, SSC CPO, SSC GD Constable, SSC JE and SSC JHT, and they also work well for state-level PSC prelims, railway exams and banking exams, since the General Awareness syllabus overlaps heavily.
How many months of current affairs should I read before the exam?
As a rule of thumb, cover the last 8 to 12 months before the exam date, with the most recent 6 months revised more thoroughly. Reading daily is easier than compressing a year into the last fortnight, which is why this archive is organised date-wise.
Does each daily page include practice questions?
Yes. Every page ends with 10 exam-style multiple choice questions drawn from that day's topics, with the correct answer and a short explanation shown as soon as you tap an option.
What is the best way to make current affairs notes?
Do not rewrite the news. Copy only the fact box from each topic — the date, full form, ministry, venue, number and person involved. That is the form in which SSC asks the question, and it is the only form worth revising in the last week.
Are current affairs enough to clear the General Awareness section?
No. Static GK still carries the larger share of the section. Read current affairs for about ten minutes daily and revise static GK chapter-wise alongside, using chapter-wise tests to check retention.
Are these current affairs available in Hindi?
The daily pages are currently published in English. Hindi versions of practice content are available across previous year papers, mock tests and chapter-wise tests, where questions and solutions are provided in both languages.
