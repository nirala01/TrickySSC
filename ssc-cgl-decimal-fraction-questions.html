Terminating or recurring — read the denominator

Reduce first: 9/45 looks like it should recur, but it is 1/5 = 0.2. The number of decimal places of a terminating decimal is the larger of the powers of 2 and 5 in the denominator — 7/320 has 320 = 26 × 5, so six places.

Continued fractions

  • Evaluating: start at the innermost layer, and at each layer add the whole number to the reciprocal of the value below it.
  • Unknown inside: peel from the outside in — flip both sides, subtract the layer's whole number, flip again.
  • Fraction to continued fraction: flip, split off the whole part, flip the remainder, repeat. 7/24 → 24/7 = 3 + 3/7 → 7/3 = 2 + 1/3, so 7/24 = 1/(3 + 1/(2 + 1/3)).
  • All-ones ladder: 1 + 1/(1 + 1/(1 + …)) gives the fractions 3/2, 5/3, 8/5, 13/8 — each numerator is the sum of the previous two.

Also worth memorising

  • 99 k−1⁄k × m = 100m − m/k. So 99 5/6 × 36 = 3600 − 6 = 3594, and 999 7/8 × 16 + 2 = 16000
  • Least fraction to add to a sum of mixed numbers = 1 − its fractional part; least fraction to subtract = the fractional part itself
  • Adding the same number to both parts of a proper fraction makes it larger; of an improper fraction, smaller
  • Multiplied by p/q instead of dividing: the error is the number × (q/p − p/q)
  • Two fractions from sum S and product P: (x − y)² = S² − 4P
  • 0.125 = 1/8, 0.375 = 3/8, 0.625 = 5/8, 0.875 = 7/8, 0.0625 = 1/16 — convert on sight

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Decimal & Fraction

Q1. Express 0.46 as a fraction in its lowest terms.
Mixed recurring: all digits minus the non-repeating digits, over one 9 and one 0. (46 − 4)/90 = 42/90 = 7/15. Answer: 7/15
Q2. Which of 9/64, 5/12, 7/15, 11/18 is a terminating decimal?
Only a denominator made of 2s and 5s terminates. 64 = 26; the other three denominators each contain a 3. 9/64 = 0.140625. Answer: 9/64
Q3. Evaluate 3 + 1/(2 + 1/(1 + 1/4)).
Inside-out: 1 + 1/4 = 5/4, so the middle layer is 2 + 4/5 = 14/5, and the outer layer is 3 + 5/14 = 47/14. Answer: 47/14
Q4. A fraction becomes 2/3 when 1 is added to its numerator, and 1/2 when 1 is added to its denominator. Find the fraction.
Let it be n/d. (n + 1)/d = 2/3 gives 3n + 3 = 2d; n/(d + 1) = 1/2 gives d = 2n − 1. Substituting, 3n + 3 = 4n − 2, so n = 5 and d = 9. Answer: 5/9
Q5. What is the 40th digit after the decimal point in 2/7?
2/7 = 0.285714, a six-digit block. 40 = 6 × 6 + 4, so the 40th digit is the 4th digit of the block. Answer: 7

How to Prepare Decimal & Fraction for SSC CGL

  1. Learn the conversion table first and test it on random decimals. 9s for repeating digits, 0s for non-repeating digits — once this is automatic, half the chapter is done.
  2. Always reduce before you classify. Whether a fraction terminates depends on the lowest-terms denominator; 9/45 is 1/5.
  3. Treat continued fractions as one method. Inside-out to evaluate, outside-in to solve for an unknown — there is nothing else to learn.
  4. Never compare recurring decimals by their digits. Convert to fractions and cross-multiply, or expand both to the same number of places; 0.54 and 0.54 differ from the third digit.
  5. Do Level 1 untimed, the same day you finish the rules. The aim is correct recall, not speed.
  6. Move to Level 2 with the 30-minute clock on. A conversion that takes more than twenty seconds means you are still deriving the rule instead of applying it.
  7. Study it with simplification. Do number system and divisibility before, and simplification right after — the four chapters share their tricks.
  8. Verify against a real paper. Once Level 2 is consistent, attempt a previous year paper and then a full mock test under locked sectional timing.

SSC CGL Decimal & Fraction — Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert a recurring decimal to a fraction?
For a pure recurring decimal, write the repeating block over as many 9s as it has digits: 0.36 = 36/99 = 4/11. For a mixed recurring decimal, subtract the non-repeating digits from all the digits and write the result over 9s for the repeating digits followed by 0s for the non-repeating digits: 0.46 = (46 − 4)/90 = 7/15.
When does a fraction give a terminating decimal?
Reduce to lowest terms first. It terminates only when the denominator has no prime factor other than 2 and 5. So 7/40 terminates (40 = 8 × 5) while 7/30 recurs (30 has a 3). The number of decimal places is the larger of the powers of 2 and 5 in the denominator.
What is the fastest way to compare fractions?
Cross-multiply for a pair: a/b > c/d exactly when ad > bc. For a list, compare each fraction with 1 when they are all close to 1, remember that same-numerator fractions shrink as the denominator grows, and that fractions whose numerator and denominator both rise by the same amounts move steadily towards the ratio of those increases.
How do I solve a continued fraction quickly?
Work from the innermost layer outwards, adding the whole number to the reciprocal of the value below at each step. If the unknown is inside the fraction, peel from the outside in: flip both sides, subtract that layer's whole number, flip again, and repeat until only the unknown is left.
How many decimal and fraction questions are asked in SSC CGL?
One to two in a typical Tier I shift — usually a recurring decimal conversion, a continued fraction, or a fraction word problem. The chapter also feeds simplification and comparison questions, so its skills are worth more than the direct count.
What is the trick for 99 and 999 type products?
Write the mixed number as a round number minus a small fraction. 99 5/6 = 100 − 1/6, so 99 5/6 × 36 = 3600 − 6 = 3594. The same idea works for 999 and 9999, and for questions that add a small fraction afterwards to bring the result back to a whole number.
