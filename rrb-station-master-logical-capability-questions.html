RRB Station Master Logical Capability — Reasoning & Reading Comprehension

20 marks · CEN 08/2026 pattern

Logical Capability is the second section of the RRB Station Master CBT. It mixes logical reasoning with English reading comprehension; in the 2026 RRB Section Controller papers on the same pattern, the passages made up most of the section. Here is the syllabus, the pattern and seven solved questions.

What Logical Capability contains

Logical Capability carries 20 marks in the RRB Station Master CBT. The notification lists two parts: logical reasoning (binary logic, syllogism, clocks and calendars, assumptions, blood relations, family tree and logic-based puzzles) and reading comprehension in English, testing the main idea, supporting ideas, application, logical structure and style.

In the 2026 RRB Section Controller papers on the same pattern, reading comprehension was most of the section: three English passages of about 190 words with five questions each, and only four or five logical reasoning questions. The passages stay in English even in the Hindi paper.

Solved logical reasoning questions

Q1.

1 January 2027 is a Friday. What day of the week is 1 March 2027?

  1. Sunday
  2. Monday
  3. Tuesday
  4. Wednesday
Show answer & solution

Answer: (b) Monday

Days from 1 January to 1 March 2027 = 31 (January) + 28 (February, 2027 is not a leap year) = 59. 59 = 8 weeks + 3 days. Friday + 3 days = Monday.

Q2.

What is the angle between the hour hand and the minute hand of a clock at 3:40?

  1. 120°
  2. 130°
  3. 140°
  4. 150°
Show answer & solution

Answer: (b) 130°

Angle = |30H − 5.5M| = |30 × 3 − 5.5 × 40| = |90 − 220| = 130°.

Q3.

Pointing to a man, Ritu says, “His mother is the only daughter of my mother.” How is the man related to Ritu?

  1. Brother
  2. Nephew
  3. Son
  4. Cousin
Show answer & solution

Answer: (c) Son

The only daughter of Ritu’s mother is Ritu herself. So the man’s mother is Ritu, and he is her son.

Q4.

Statements: All signals are lights. Some lights are red.
Conclusions: I. Some signals are red. II. Some red things are lights.

  1. Only I follows
  2. Only II follows
  3. Both follow
  4. Neither follows
Show answer & solution

Answer: (b) Only II follows

‘Some lights are red’ converts to ‘some red things are lights’, so II follows. The red lights need not be signals, so I does not follow.

Q5.

Of A and B, one always tells the truth and the other always lies. A says, “We are both liars.” Who is the truth-teller?

  1. A
  2. B
  3. Both
  4. Cannot be decided
Show answer & solution

Answer: (b) B

If A told the truth, A would be a liar — a contradiction. So A is the liar and the statement is false: they are not both liars. Hence B tells the truth.

Reading comprehension: a short practice passage

Railway signalling began with men standing beside the track and waving flags. As trains grew faster, a driver could no longer see a flag in time to stop, and fixed signals on tall posts took over. Even these depended on a signalman pulling levers in a cabin, and an error by one tired person could put two trains on the same line. The answer was interlocking: a mechanical, and later electrical, arrangement in which a signal cannot show ‘clear’ unless the points are set and locked for that route. Interlocking did not remove people from signalling, but it made the most dangerous mistakes physically impossible.

Q6.

Which of the following is the most suitable title for the passage?

  1. The History of Railway Flags
  2. How Interlocking Made Signalling Safer
  3. Why Signalmen Get Tired
  4. Electric Trains in India
Show answer & solution

Answer: (b) How Interlocking Made Signalling Safer

The passage moves from flags to fixed signals to interlocking, and ends on how interlocking prevents the most dangerous mistakes. Option (b) covers the whole passage; the others are details or not mentioned.

Q7.

According to the passage, what does interlocking do?

  1. It removes signalmen from cabins
  2. It stops a signal showing clear unless the route is set and locked
  3. It lets drivers see flags from further away
  4. It makes trains run faster
Show answer & solution

Answer: (b)

The passage says a signal cannot show ‘clear’ unless the points are set and locked for that route, and that interlocking did not remove people from signalling.

How to prepare Logical Capability

  • Read one English editorial or article a day. With 15 of 20 marks coming from passages in the 2026 papers, reading speed and vocabulary matter more than reasoning shortcuts.
  • For each passage, note the main idea in one line before you look at the questions. Title and central-theme questions then take seconds.
  • Answer from the passage, not your own knowledge. Inference options that sound true but are not stated are the usual traps.
  • Keep formulas ready for calendars (odd days) and clocks (30H − 5.5M); these are sure marks.
