SSC CGL English Syllabus 2026 — Chapter by Chapter

Vocabulary — the repeat-heavy block

Synonyms, antonyms, idioms and phrases, one word substitution and spelling together account for eight to ten questions in every Tier I shift, and SSC repeats these words heavily across years. This is the block where chapter wise practice converts directly into marks: every test you attempt adds words you have actually seen in an SSC-style question rather than a random list.

Grammar — rule-based and drillable

Spot the error, sentence improvement, active and passive voice, and direct and indirect narration are governed by a finite set of rules. Once subject–verb agreement, article use, preposition pairing and tense sequence are solid, these questions become near-automatic. Level 2 tests in these chapters are the fastest way to find which specific rule keeps costing you marks.

Comprehension — cloze test, para jumbles and RC

The cloze passage is worth five marks in a single block, which makes it the highest-value item in the section. Para jumbles reward a fixed approach — find the opening sentence, then link pronouns and connectors. Reading comprehension is limited in Tier I but becomes central in Tier II Paper I, where English carries 45 questions.

How to Use Chapter Wise Practice for SSC CGL English

  1. Run vocabulary daily, grammar in blocks. Ten minutes a day on synonyms, antonyms, idioms and one word substitution beats one long session a week — this is memory work.
  2. Do spot the error and sentence improvement together. They test the same rules from two directions, so practising them back to back doubles the return on each rule you learn.
  3. Treat the cloze test as five marks, not five questions. Read the whole passage once before filling any blank; the later blanks often decide the earlier ones.
  4. Write down every unknown word from the solution screen. SSC repeats vocabulary heavily, so a word missed today is very likely a word asked again.
  5. Target 13 minutes for 25 questions. English is where you save time for the maths section — practise with the timer on once a chapter reaches Level 2.
  6. Cross-check against real papers. After four or five chapters are solid, attempt a previous year paper and then a full mock test to confirm the speed holds under sectional timing.

SSC CGL English Chapter Wise Test — FAQs

How many English chapters are covered in SSC CGL chapter wise practice?
Fourteen chapters, covering the full SSC CGL English Language syllabus — spot the error, sentence improvement, fill in the blanks, voice, narration, para jumbles, synonyms, antonyms, spelling, idioms and phrases, one word substitution, homonyms, cloze test and reading comprehension.
How many English questions come in SSC CGL Tier I?
Twenty-five questions worth 50 marks with a locked 15-minute sectional clock. In Tier II Paper I, English carries 45 questions worth 135 marks — the largest module in the exam.
Which English topics repeat most in SSC CGL?
Vocabulary repeats the most. Synonyms, antonyms, idioms and phrases and one word substitution recur across years, which is why solving them chapter wise from a previous-year-based bank works better than memorising unrelated word lists.
Are the SSC CGL English chapter tests free?
Yes — chapter wise practice is open to everyone right now, and no login is required to attempt a chapter test.
How many questions are in each English chapter test?
Fifty questions, with an optional 30-minute timer and the real exam marking scheme of +2, −0.5 and 0.
Are solutions provided in Hindi?
Yes. Wherever the Hindi set has been uploaded for a chapter, the questions, options and detailed solutions are available in हिंदी, and you choose the language before the test starts.
How much time should I spend on English in the exam?
Aim to finish the 25 Tier I questions in about 12–13 minutes so the saved time goes to Quantitative Aptitude. Practise with the timer on once a chapter reaches Level 2.
What is the difference between the three levels?
Level 1 is basic, Level 2 matches real SSC CGL Tier I difficulty, and Level 3 is harder — Tier II standard, including longer comprehension and tougher vocabulary.
