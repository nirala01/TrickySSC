Profit & Loss

Concepts, formulas, solved examples and practice · Quantitative Aptitude

Profit and loss percentages are always calculated on the cost price unless the question says otherwise. Hold on to that, and most questions become one-line ratio work.

1. Concepts in brief

The three prices

Cost price (CP) is what the seller paid. Selling price (SP) is what the buyer pays. Marked price (MP) is the price on the tag, before any discount (that belongs to the Discount chapter). If SP is above CP the seller makes a profit; if below, a loss.

Why percentages are on CP

Profit is measured against the money the seller risked, which is the cost price. So a ₹20 profit on an item bought for ₹100 is 20%, not 20/120. Only when a question clearly says "profit on selling price" do you divide by SP.

Think in ratios

A 25% profit means SP is 125 when CP is 100, so CP : SP = 4 : 5. Once you write that ratio, the question becomes "what is one part worth?". This avoids most equations.

Overheads

Transport, repair or tax paid by the seller before selling are added to the cost price. Profit is always worked out on this total cost.

3. Shortcuts that save time

Fraction method Profit 20% → CP : SP = 5 : 6. Profit 25% → 4 : 5. Loss 12½% → 8 : 7. Work with these ratios instead of percentages; the answer usually falls out in one step.
  • If the question gives two selling prices with two profit/loss figures, the difference in SP equals the difference in percentages of CP.
  • A dealer who cheats on both buying and selling: multiply the two gain factors.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 An article sold for ₹540 makes a 10% loss. To gain 10%, sell it at?
CP = 540 × 100/90 = 600. New SP = 600 × 1.1 = ₹660.
Example 2 CP of 20 articles = SP of 16. Profit = (20 − 16)/16 × 100 = 25%.
Example 3 Two cycles sold at ₹1980 each, one at 10% gain and one at 10% loss. CPs are 1800 and 2200 (total 4000); total SP 3960. Loss = 1%, matching 10²/100.
Example 4 A shopkeeper uses a 900 g weight for 1 kg and sells at cost price. Gain = 100/900 × 100 = 11⅑%.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 5 Selling an article for ₹720 gives a 20% profit. At what price should it be sold for a 25% profit? CP = 720 × 5/6 = 600; new SP = 600 × 5/4 = ₹750.
Example 6 Selling at ₹1440 causes a 10% loss. Price for a 15% gain? CP = 1440 × 10/9 = 1600; SP = 1600 × 1.15 = ₹1840.
Example 7 A man buys 12 pens for ₹10 and sells 10 pens for ₹12. Profit = (12 × 12)/(10 × 10) − 1 = 1.44 − 1 = 44%.
Example 8 Selling at 15% profit instead of 10% profit brings ₹40 more. So 5% of CP = 40 and CP = ₹800.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. CP ₹450, SP ₹540. Profit %?

Answer: 20% (90/450 × 100).

Q2. An article is sold for ₹960 at a 20% loss. Find the CP.

Answer: ₹1200 (960 × 100/80).

Q3. CP of 15 articles = SP of 12 articles. Profit or loss %?

Answer: 25% profit ((15 − 12)/12 × 100).

Q4. A dealer uses an 800 g weight for 1 kg and sells at cost price. Gain %?

Answer: 25% (200/800 × 100).

Q5. A profit of 25% on CP is what percentage on SP?

Answer: 20% (25/125 × 100).

6. Traps to avoid

  1. Profit % is on CP, not SP — unless the question says "profit on selling price".
  2. False weight: divide by the false weight (900), not the true weight (1000).
  3. Same SP with x% gain and x% loss → always a loss. Same CP with x% gain and x% loss → no profit, no loss.
  4. Overheads (repair, transport) are added to the CP before finding profit.
  5. Read whether the question asks for profit amount or profit percentage.
