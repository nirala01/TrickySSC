President Assents to Frontier Nagaland Authority Bill

  • President Droupadi Murmu gave assent to the Frontier Nagaland Territorial Authority (FNTA) Bill, 2026.
  • The Nagaland Legislative Assembly had passed the Bill unanimously on 3 September 2026.
  • Chief Minister Neiphiu Rio announced the assent on 26 October 2026.
  • Rio shared the announcement on X, the social media platform.
  • The Bill concerns the proposed Frontier Nagaland Territorial Authority.
  • Rio thanked the Union Home Ministry for coordinating the process.
  • The Chief Minister described the development as a step towards the aspirations of Eastern Nagaland’s people.
  • Nagaland is a state in northeastern India; its capital is Kohima.

International Relations Parliamentary Forum 26 October 2026

New Delhi to Host 12th BRICS Parliamentary Forum

  • India is hosting the 12th BRICS Parliamentary Forum in New Delhi.
  • The two-day forum is scheduled to begin on 27 October 2026.
  • Its theme is “Fostering Parliaments for People-Centric Development”.
  • Prime Minister Narendra Modi is scheduled to attend and meet visiting delegation leaders.
  • Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla is the forum’s Chairperson and will preside over proceedings.
  • Delegations from 11 BRICS member countries and 10 partner countries have been invited.
  • The programme includes four high-level sessions focused on Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability.
  • A separate meeting will bring together chairs of international affairs committees from BRICS member and partner parliaments.
  • That meeting will discuss parliamentary diplomacy, capacity-building and sharing best practices.
  • The forum is intended to advance parliamentary diplomacy, democratic institutions and cooperation among participating countries.

Sports, Culture & Days Weightlifting 26 October 2026

Ahmedabad to Host Senior World Weightlifting Championships

  • The International Weightlifting Federation (IWF) awarded Ahmedabad the 2029 Senior World Weightlifting Championships.
  • Ahmedabad will become the first Indian city to host the senior edition of the global event.
  • The hosting decision was made at an IWF Executive Board meeting in Ningbo, China.
  • The 2030 Senior World Weightlifting Championships were awarded to Moscow, Russia.
  • The 2028 World Youth Championships will be held in Prishtina, Kosovo.
  • The World Junior Championships were awarded to Caracas, Venezuela.
  • The 2029 event is the senior-level championship, distinct from the youth and junior competitions.
  • The host announcements cover senior editions in 2029 and 2030, and youth and junior events in 2028.

Economy & Banking Ports 26 October 2026

India Targets Fourfold Port Capacity by 2047

  • India aims to quadruple its port capacity by 2047 to support economic growth and expanding international trade.
  • Union Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal outlined the target during his visit to the Netherlands.
  • Goyal made the remarks in Rotterdam, home to the Port of Rotterdam.
  • He said India had doubled port capacity over the preceding decade.
  • India is exploring cooperation with the Port of Rotterdam on maritime infrastructure and sustainable trade.
  • The areas identified for cooperation include green energy and carbon capture.
  • Goyal highlighted modernising existing ports, building world-class infrastructure and improving operational efficiency.
  • The government’s Sagarmala initiative was cited in connection with port infrastructure expansion and maritime connectivity.
  • Stronger links with international maritime hubs such as Rotterdam are intended to support India’s maritime capabilities and global trade role.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
