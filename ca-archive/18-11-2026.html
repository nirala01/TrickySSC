Quad Announces Maritime Surveillance and Cybersecurity Initiatives

  • The Quad leaders reaffirmed their commitment to a free, open and stable Indo-Pacific.
  • The four participating countries are India, the United States, Australia and Japan.
  • The meeting was attended by Narendra Modi, Donald Trump, Anthony Albanese and Sanae Takaichi.
  • The leaders opposed unilateral attempts to change the status quo through force or coercion.
  • They raised concern over economic coercion, including arbitrary export restrictions.
  • The Indo-Pacific Maritime Surveillance Collaboration will strengthen maritime domain awareness.
  • Quad countries plan their first maritime exercise in 2027, involving P-8 and P-3C patrol aircraft.
  • A first Quad Business Leaders’ Forum is planned for early 2027.
  • The announced AI Accelerator is intended to promote trusted artificial intelligence.
  • The leaders also agreed to increase cooperation on undersea cables, energy security and cybersecurity.
  • They condemned terrorism, including cross-border terrorism, and backed ASEAN centrality.

Economy & Banking Exports 18 November 2026

India’s Electronics Exports Rise 44% in April–September

  • India’s electronic goods exports grew 44% in the first half of the fiscal year, April–September.
  • Exports rose from over $22 billion in April–September 2025–26 to around $32 billion in the corresponding current-year period.
  • Smartphones remained the largest component of India’s electronics export basket.
  • Smartphone exports increased by over 35%, from $13 billion to more than $18 billion.
  • Smartphones’ share of electronics exports fell from 60.3% to 56.8% as other segments grew faster.
  • Non-smartphone electronics exports grew by over 56%, from around $9 billion to more than $13 billion.
  • The non-smartphone share of total electronics exports increased from 39.7% to 43.2%.
  • The broader export basket included components, printed circuit boards, communication equipment, memory products and storage devices.
  • Exports of parts and accessories comprising mounted printed circuit boards rose from over $63 million to more than $2 billion.
  • Mounted printed circuit board parts and accessories recorded reported growth of 3,219%.
  • Singapore was the leading destination for these parts and accessories, with exports of $1.87 billion.
  • China was the next reported destination, with exports of $111.5 million.

International Relations MoU 18 November 2026

ICAR and RIS Sign MoU on Global South Agriculture Cooperation

  • The Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) and the Research and Information System for Developing Countries (RIS) signed an MoU on 17 November 2026.
  • The agreement was signed in New Delhi to strengthen agricultural cooperation and knowledge exchange among developing countries.
  • The partnership will be facilitated through ‘दक्षिण’ (South) – Global South Centre of Excellence.
  • Its focus areas include sharing agricultural technologies, documenting best practices, knowledge exchange and capacity development.
  • The MoU was exchanged by M. L. Jat, Secretary of DARE and Director General of ICAR, and Sachin Kumar Sharma, Director General of RIS.
  • The signing took place during a two-day high-level policy dialogue held on 17–18 November 2026 at the India Habitat Centre.
  • The dialogue was organised by RIS–‘दक्षिण’ in partnership with India’s Ministry of External Affairs.
  • ICAR has 114 research institutes, 152 regional research centres and 731 Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs), and coordinates with about 80 agricultural universities.
  • ICAR reported that it has signed 74 MoUs, about 60% of them with institutions in developing countries.
  • The partnership aims to share suitable technologies and practices between countries, including those with similar agro-climatic conditions.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
