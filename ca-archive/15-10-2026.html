Government Schemes Social Security 15 October 2026

NSAP Continues as Pradhan Mantri Samajik Sahayata Yojana

  • Union Cabinet approved continuation of the National Social Assistance Programme (NSAP) for five years, from 2026–27 to 2030–31.
  • The approved programme has a total outlay of ₹53,428 crore for the five-year period.
  • NSAP has been renamed Pradhan Mantri Samajik Sahayata Yojana.
  • The Indira Gandhi National Old Age Pension Scheme is renamed Pradhan Mantri Vridhavastha Pension Yojana.
  • The Indira Gandhi National Widow Pension Scheme is renamed Pradhan Mantri Vidhawa Pension Yojana.
  • The disability pension scheme becomes Pradhan Mantri Divyangjan Pension Yojana; the National Family Benefit Scheme becomes Pradhan Mantri Parivar Labh Yojana.
  • Under the disability pension scheme, assistance is ₹375 for beneficiaries up to 79 years and ₹625 for those aged 80 years and above.
  • The benchmark disability threshold for the pension changes from 80% to 40%, and the minimum age criterion is removed—the pension eligibility can begin from birth.
  • Old-age and widow pensions and the family benefit scheme continue in their existing form; revised disability-pension guidelines are to be implemented within three months.
  • The approval is expected to benefit 3.2 crore people across all 36 States and Union Territories.

  • Q: What is the new name of the disability pension scheme? A: Pradhan Mantri Divyangjan Pension Yojana.
  • Q: What is the revised benchmark disability threshold? A: 40%.
  • Q: How many beneficiaries are expected across States and UTs? A: 3.2 crore.

Economy & Banking Banking 15 October 2026

CCEA Approves IDBI Bank Strategic Restructuring

  • The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) approved the strategic restructuring of IDBI Bank.
  • Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman described the approval as a milestone in India’s reform trajectory.
  • The restructuring is intended to enable the bank’s next phase of growth through an infusion of foreign direct investment (FDI).
  • The Government plans to retain a minority stake in IDBI Bank as part of the restructuring.
  • The stated objectives include strengthening the banking ecosystem and helping banking services reach global benchmarks.
  • The announcement was made by the Finance Minister in a social media post following the CCEA approval.
  • The report describes the move as part of broader financial-sector reform and a future-ready banking sector.
  • The source does not specify the size of the stake, the proposed investor, or the amount of FDI.

International Relations Tax Treaty 15 October 2026

India–Mauritius Tax Treaty Adds Principal Purpose Test

  • The Central Government notified amendments to the India–Mauritius Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA).
  • The amendment introduces the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) to curb treaty misuse for tax avoidance and evasion.
  • Treaty benefits may be denied when obtaining those benefits is found to be one of the principal purposes of a transaction or arrangement.
  • The measure aims to prevent unintended tax advantages while retaining the treaty’s purpose of eliminating double taxation.
  • India and Mauritius signed the amending protocol in March 2024.
  • The protocol was signed in Port Louis, the capital of Mauritius.
  • It came into force on the 10th of the preceding month, after both countries completed the required legal formalities.
  • The amendment also seeks to strengthen safeguards while facilitating bilateral trade and investment.

Science & Technology Research Funding 15 October 2026

RDI Scheme Opens Funding to Advanced Chemicals and Materials

  • Advanced chemicals, petrochemicals and material technologies were added as an eligible technology area under the Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) Scheme.
  • The inclusion is effective from 8 October 2026.
  • The decision followed a proposal from the Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals (DCPC) under the Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers.
  • The Department of Science and Technology (DST) made the decision to add the sector to the scheme.
  • The expansion is intended to strengthen research, development and innovation in the chemicals and petrochemicals sector.
  • It is expected to encourage home-grown technologies for advanced industrial applications and manufacturing.
  • The RDI Scheme was launched in November 2025 with a corpus of ₹1 lakh crore to encourage private-sector investment in research and innovation.
  • Its aims include long-term financing and refinancing on favourable terms for transformative projects and faster adoption of advanced technologies.
  • The scheme also seeks to improve industrial competitiveness and promote self-reliance in critical and emerging technology areas.

National Saras Mela 2026 Moves Outside Delhi-NCR

  • The National Saras Aajeevika Mela 2026 is being held at Punnami Ghat in Vijayawada, Andhra Pradesh.
  • For the first time, the national-level Saras Mela is being organised outside Delhi-NCR.
  • President Droupadi Murmu is scheduled to inaugurate the fair on 15 October 2026.
  • The event is organised by the Ministry of Rural Development, with DAY-NRLM and Andhra Pradesh’s SERP as partners.
  • Around 1,000 women entrepreneurs and artisans from 34 States and Union Territories are taking part.
  • More than 650 stalls showcase traditional handloom, handicrafts, organic products and household goods.
  • The fair is designed to promote rural women’s entrepreneurship, livelihoods and local products.
  • A Lakhpati Didi Compendium and the Saras Shakti application are scheduled to be launched at the event.
  • The programme highlights the national goal of developing 6 crore Lakhpati Didis.
  • An India Food Court features more than 200 women chefs from 28 States, preparing regional dishes; themed pavilions and live demonstrations are also planned.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.
