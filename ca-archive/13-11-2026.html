International Relations Summits 13 November 2026

PM Modi to Attend ASEAN-India and East Asia Summits

  • Prime Minister Narendra Modi is scheduled to visit the Philippines for two days from 16 November 2026.
  • The visit includes the 23rd ASEAN-India Summit and the 21st East Asia Summit in Manila.
  • The programme is to begin with a dinner hosted by the President of the Philippines.
  • The Ministry of External Affairs outlined the programme through Secretary (East) Rudrendra Tandon in New Delhi.
  • India engages with ASEAN collectively and through bilateral relations with its 11 member states, according to the release.
  • India’s engagement with the ASEAN regional platform is an important element of its Act East Policy.
  • The Prime Minister’s participation reflects India’s engagement with ASEAN at the Heads of Government level.
  • India’s trade with the ASEAN region is approximately US$122 billion, as stated in the report.
  • The East Asia Summit process is also part of the Prime Minister’s programme in Manila.
  • India regards a strong strategic relationship with ASEAN, collectively and bilaterally, as beneficial to both sides.

Economy & Banking Banking 13 November 2026

RBI Consolidates Rules for Domestic Money Transfers

  • The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) issued consolidated directions for domestic money transfers.
  • The directions retain existing transaction limits and add safeguards for cash-based remittances.
  • A person without a bank account can remit up to ₹50,000 to a beneficiary’s bank account through NEFT at a bank branch.
  • For transfers through business correspondents, ATMs and other permitted channels, the limit is ₹5,000 per transaction.
  • The monthly limit through those channels is ₹25,000 per remitter.
  • Cash payouts to beneficiaries without bank accounts are capped at ₹10,000 per transaction.
  • The monthly cash-payout cap is ₹25,000 per beneficiary.
  • Remitters must register using a verified mobile number and a self-certified valid document under KYC norms.
  • Banks must include remitter details and cash-transfer identifiers in IMPS/NEFT messages.
  • Banks are required to use transaction checks and customer alerts, examine unusual credit patterns and report suspicious transactions.
  • Transfers must be real-time or near real-time, with inter-bank settlement through RBI-approved systems.
  • The directions cover banks, authorised payment operators and participants, but exclude card-to-card transfers.

Safeguards and Coverage

  • KYC is Know Your Customer; remitter registration requires a verified mobile number and a self-certified valid document.
  • The directions apply to banks and authorised payment operators and participants; card-to-card transfers are excluded.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: Which institution issued the consolidated domestic money-transfer directions? A: The Reserve Bank of India.
  • Q: What is the branch NEFT limit for a remitter without a bank account? A: ₹50,000.
  • Q: What is the monthly permitted-channel limit per remitter? A: ₹25,000.
  • Q: What is the per-transaction cap for cash payouts to unbanked beneficiaries? A: ₹10,000.
  • Q: Which two transfer systems are named for message details? A: IMPS and NEFT.
  • Q: Which type of transfer is excluded? A: Card-to-card transfers.

Defence Digital Portal 13 November 2026

Sanjay Seth Launches Revised Gallantry Awards Portal

  • Minister of State for Defence Sanjay Seth launched the revised Gallantry Awards Portal in New Delhi on 12 November 2026.
  • The portal is available at gallantryawards.gov.in and was developed to honour award recipients and preserve their legacy.
  • The redesigned portal includes an improved user interface, easier navigation, device-friendly design and improved accessibility.
  • Its sections include events, memorials and museums, WARPEDIA, award recipients, Gallantrypedia articles, media and interactive tributes.
  • A dedicated Veer Gatha digital section provides a separate page for each edition.
  • Veer Gatha pages bring together edition introductions, themes, event timelines, assessment criteria, awards, resources and results.
  • The section also provides information on award ceremonies, media galleries and student activities.
  • Veer Gatha connects students with the stories of gallantry award recipients and encourages them to express lessons from those stories through their work.
  • Participation grew from about 8 lakh students and 4,788 schools in the first edition to over 1.92 crore students and 1.9 lakh schools in the fifth.
  • The fifth edition included 28,005 students from 91 CBSE-affiliated schools in 18 countries.
  • The initiative aims to create an authentic digital collection on gallantry awards, recipients, wars and post-Independence military operations.
  • The launch took place under the Ministry of Defence; Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh was among the officials present.

Portal Features

  • The portal’s subject areas include award recipients, military history resources, media and interactive tributes.
  • Each Veer Gatha edition has a dedicated page with its theme, timeline, criteria, outcomes and student-related material.

Environment & Ecology Tribal Livelihoods 13 November 2026

Initiative Targets Medicinal Plants in Tribal Hotspots

  • The Ministry of Tribal Affairs, with CSIR–National Botanical Research Institute (CSIR-NBRI), launched a three-year project on 12 November 2026.
  • The project was formally launched at Central University of Odisha, Koraput, Odisha.
  • Its title is “Integrated Conservation, Agrotechnology and Bio-resource Enterprise Development for Sustainable Tribal Livelihoods in Tribal Hotspots of India.”
  • It covers 16 selected tribal hotspots across 13 states and Union Territories.
  • The project aims to conserve medicinal-plant biodiversity and create sustainable livelihoods and enterprise opportunities using traditional knowledge and local bio-resources.
  • It plans to document approximately 200–300 medicinal plant species and scientifically propagate 30–40 rare, endangered and threatened (RET) species.
  • The project will establish 16 community nurseries and 16 seed banks.
  • It plans to distribute 40,000–50,000 quality planting materials and build community capacity for cultivation and bio-resource enterprises.
  • The hotspots named include Alluri Sitarama Raju, Kokrajhar, Narayanpur, Navsari, East Singhbhum, Kodagu, Wayanad, Kargil and Anuppur.
  • Other named hotspots are Gadchiroli and Nashik; Kandhamal, Balangir and Koraput; Sonbhadra; and Dharchula.
  • In October 2026, CSIR-NBRI fieldwork in Sonbhadra documented traditional medicinal practices through interactions with knowledgeable tribal residents in 12 villages.
  • The project seeks to connect scientific institutions, Krishi Vigyan Kendras, Van Dhan Vikas Kendras, state departments and local communities.

Project Profile

Implementation

  • The project brings together traditional knowledge, scientific research and community-based enterprise development.
  • The release identifies tribal farmers, traditional practitioners, women and rural youth as participants in conservation, capacity-building, cultivation and value-chain development.
  • The Sonbhadra field activity also collected plant specimens, while orchid samples were gathered for germplasm conservation at the CSIR-NBRI Botanical Garden.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: Which ministry launched the tribal medicinal-plants initiative? A: The Ministry of Tribal Affairs.
  • Q: Which research institute is the project partner? A: CSIR-NBRI, the National Botanical Research Institute.
  • Q: Where was the project formally launched? A: Central University of Odisha, Koraput.
  • Q: How long is the project planned to run? A: Three years.
  • Q: How many tribal hotspots does it cover? A: 16 hotspots across 13 states/UTs.
  • Q: How many community nurseries and seed banks are planned? A: 16 of each.
  • Q: What does RET stand for in the project release? A: Rare, endangered and threatened.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.
