International Relations MoU 26 November 2026

Cabinet Clears India–Singapore Aviation MRO MoU

  • The Union Cabinet approved signing an India–Singapore MoU on cooperation in aviation MRO.
  • MRO stands for Maintenance, Repair and Overhaul.
  • The MoU covers skills and industry development in the aviation MRO sector.
  • It will provide a framework for exchanging knowledge, expertise and best practices between the two countries.
  • The cooperation is intended to help Indian training institutions strengthen their curricula and training capabilities.
  • Training institutions are expected to benefit from knowledge exchange and industry exposure.
  • The initiative aims to support domestic MRO infrastructure through greater industry participation.
  • Developing domestic MRO capacity is expected over time to reduce reliance on overseas maintenance services.
  • The Union Minister of Civil Aviation, Ram Mohan Naidu, welcomed the decision.
  • The MoU is aligned with the Atmanirbhar Bharat vision and is intended to support skilled employment opportunities for young people.

  • Q: Which Union Minister of Civil Aviation welcomed the decision? A: Ram Mohan Naidu.
  • Q: What is the MoU’s broad purpose? A: Cooperation in skills and industry development in aviation MRO.
  • Q: Which national vision did the minister link the MoU with? A: Atmanirbhar Bharat.

  • Visitors from across the world decided the honour through their votes.
  • The Biennale is described in the report as the Olympics of the art world.
  • India’s pavilion was housed at the historic Arsenale in Venice.
  • The National Gallery of Modern Art (NGMA) commissioned the India Pavilion.
  • NGMA is under the Ministry of Culture.
  • The Indira Gandhi National Centre for the Arts (IGNCA) served as the executing agency.
  • Prime Minister Narendra Modi welcomed the award as a proud moment for India’s art and cultural world.
  • The Prime Minister noted that Indian artists draw on the country’s rich and diverse cultural heritage while bringing fresh perspectives to art.
  • Culture and Tourism Minister Gajendra Singh Shekhawat linked the recognition to the talent of new-age Indian artists and the country’s contemporary art scene.

Constitution Day Observed on 26 November

  • Constitution Day, also called Samvidhan Diwas, is observed on 26 November.
  • The day commemorates the Constituent Assembly’s adoption of the Constitution of India on 26 November 1949.
  • India has observed Constitution Day since 2015.
  • The Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs is facilitating an online reading of the Preamble through the MyGov portal.
  • The online Preamble reading is available in 23 languages: 22 Scheduled Languages and English.
  • The initiative gives citizens an opportunity to read the Preamble and reaffirm its ideals and values.
  • Activities are being organised by Union ministries and departments, State and Union Territory governments, autonomous bodies and institutions.
  • Planned activities include collective Preamble readings, seminars, webinars and quizzes.
  • Essay and debate competitions, poster-making, cultural programmes and awareness campaigns are also among the activities.
  • Walks, runs, digital outreach and community-based initiatives are being used to promote awareness of the Constitution.

  • Q: Through which portal is the online Preamble reading being facilitated? A: MyGov.

Economy & Banking Capital Markets 26 November 2026

SEBI Simplifies KYC and Investor Onboarding

  • SEBI introduced measures to simplify client onboarding and streamline risk management at KYC Registration Agencies.
  • Common client details will be centralised at the KRA level to avoid duplication across intermediaries.
  • The centralised details include income range, occupation, CKYC ID and DIGIPIN.
  • Separate mobile verification will be optional when the number is Aadhaar-seeded and verified in the KRA database.
  • Investors will not need separate name-change proof if the change is already updated in their PAN and Aadhaar records.
  • Overseas address proof will be optional for resident OCI cardholders who provide verified Indian address proof.
  • The same overseas-address-proof relaxation applies to foreign nationals providing verified Indian address proof.
  • Intermediaries must report account closures to KRAs within five working days.
  • The revised provisions take effect from 24 December 2026.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
