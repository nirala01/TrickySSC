Science & Technology Railways 2 November 2026

India’s First Swadeshi Bullet Train Design Ready

  • Railway Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said India’s first Swadeshi bullet train design is ready during a visit to BEML in Bengaluru.
  • The minister inspected models and systems of the high-speed trainset at BEML on 2 November 2026.
  • The Swadeshi high-speed train is identified as B28 and is being developed by a BEML-led consortium.
  • The first prototype may roll out in early 2027, according to the minister.
  • The B28 is intended for the Mumbai–Ahmedabad High-Speed Rail Corridor and potentially other corridors in the future.
  • Its propulsion, electronics and bogie are being manufactured to international standards.
  • Components and systems are undergoing tests to check efficiency and safety before manufacturing progresses further.
  • The train is designed for operating speeds in the range of 280–350 kmph.
  • The trainset is undergoing a squeeze test to check whether coaches can withstand atmospheric pressure without deformation.
  • The minister expressed confidence that the BEML-led consortium’s train would meet international standards.

  • Testing covers the train’s components and systems, including propulsion, electronics and the bogie.

  • Q: What speed range was mentioned for the train? A: 280–350 kmph.
  • Q: When may the first prototype roll out? A: Early 2027.
  • Q: What does the squeeze test assess? A: Whether the coaches can withstand atmospheric pressure without deformation.

Science & Technology Digital Aviation 2 November 2026

Digi Yatra Starts International Passenger Trials at Seven Airports

  • Digi Yatra has begun international passenger trials at seven Indian airports.
  • The trials are scheduled to run from November to December 2026.
  • The facility is for eligible Indian nationals holding e-passports who are travelling on selected international flights.
  • The selected flights for the trial are operated by Air India and IndiGo.
  • The seven airports are Delhi, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Cochin, Mumbai, Chennai and Amritsar.
  • Trial access is planned at terminal entry points, Security Hold Areas and boarding gates where the required infrastructure is available.
  • Passengers must create an e-passport-based Verifiable Credential using the Digi Yatra mobile application.
  • Credential creation involves scanning the passport’s Machine Readable Zone and reading its embedded chip.
  • The embedded chip is read using the Near Field Communication (NFC) feature of a compatible smartphone.
  • The credential is stored in the passenger’s Digi Yatra wallet on their device for use at designated trial touchpoints.

Who Can Use the Trial?

  • Eligible Indian nationals with e-passports can use the facility on selected Air India and IndiGo international flights.
  • Access is available only at designated airport touchpoints enabled for the trial.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: Which ministry is responsible for the Digi Yatra international trials? A: Ministry of Civil Aviation.
  • Q: How many airports are included in the trials? A: Seven.
  • Q: Which months are covered by the trial schedule? A: November and December 2026.
  • Q: Which passengers are eligible? A: Eligible Indian nationals holding e-passports.
  • Q: Which airlines’ selected international flights are included? A: Air India and IndiGo.
  • Q: What does NFC stand for? A: Near Field Communication.
  • Q: Where is the Digi Yatra Verifiable Credential stored? A: In the passenger’s Digi Yatra wallet on their device.

Economy & Banking Energy 2 November 2026

Seven OPEC+ Countries Keep December Oil Quotas Unchanged

  • Seven OPEC+ countries decided to keep their December 2026 oil production quotas unchanged.
  • The decision preserves the production quotas set for September 2026.
  • The countries reached the decision at a virtual meeting on 2 November 2026.
  • The meeting reviewed global oil market conditions and the market outlook.
  • The seven countries named were Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq and Kuwait.
  • The other countries named were Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman.
  • The decision concerned the seven countries’ production quotas for December; the report did not state a quota quantity.
  • OPEC stands for the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries; OPEC+ includes oil-producing countries cooperating with OPEC.

Appointments Geology 2 November 2026

Dinesh V. Ganvir Takes Charge as 55th GSI Director General

  • Dinesh Vitthalrao Ganvir took charge as the 55th Director General of the Geological Survey of India (GSI).
  • GSI is a subordinate office under the Ministry of Mines; the appointment was announced by the ministry.
  • Ganvir joined GSI on 1 July 1994, after completing a postgraduate degree in geology at RTM Nagpur University.
  • He has served in GSI’s Central, North Eastern and Northern regions, with more than 32 years of service stated in the release.
  • Before becoming Director General, he was Additional Director General and Head of Department in GSI’s Central Region at Nagpur and also held additional charge as National Mission Head-II.
  • His work has included geological mapping, mineral exploration, engineering geology and multidisciplinary geoscience studies.
  • His cited assignments included thematic mapping in the Western Bastar Craton, gold and diamond exploration in Maharashtra’s Sakoli Fold Belt, and geological studies related to Bhimbetka and Ellora conservation.
  • During his leadership work, GSI submitted 359 geological reports and 377 geological memoranda; 202 blocks related to critical minerals, of which 40 had been auctioned.
  • Under the Exploration Licence system, 56 blocks were submitted and 11 had been auctioned under Phase I and Phase II.
  • His priorities include critical and strategic mineral exploration, improved mineral-prospectivity mapping, stronger laboratory and analytical capacity, offshore exploration and greater use of advanced data analysis.

  • Q: Which two UNESCO World Heritage sites were named in connection with his geological studies? A: Bhimbetka and Ellora.
  • Q: What was a stated priority for his tenure? A: Accelerating exploration for critical and strategic minerals.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
