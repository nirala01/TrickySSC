Economy & Banking RBI Regulations 12 October 2026

RBI Tightens Rules for Rupee Derivative Transactions

  • RBI reduced the limit for foreign-exchange derivative transactions without underlying exposure from $100 million to $5 million.
  • The revised threshold applies across authorised dealers and recognised stock exchanges.
  • Authorised dealers cannot rebook rupee-involving foreign-exchange derivative contracts cancelled after the directions were issued.
  • Rollover of these contracts at maturity remains permitted.
  • A user hedging a contracted exposure must confirm that the same exposure has not been hedged through another authorised dealer.
  • RBI introduced the Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve (FERR) for specified derivative contracts.
  • An authorised dealer must hold a cash reserve equal to 20% of the rupee value of qualifying contracts exceeding $2 million.
  • FERR applies to contracts hedging current-account exposures involving the purchase of foreign currency against the rupee.
  • The FERR cash reserve must be maintained daily until the relevant contract is terminated.
  • Authorised dealers must report FERR details daily through the Centralised Information Management System (CIMS).
  • RBI warned that splitting activity across multiple transactions to bypass the rules would count as a violation.
  • Circular No. 25 took effect immediately; FERR requirements under Circular No. 26 apply to contracts entered into after its directions were issued.

Key Regulatory Details

RegulatorReserve Bank of India (RBI)

Purpose and Application

  • The restrictions aim to curb currency speculation and support an orderly foreign-exchange market.
  • The reserve requirement concerns qualifying current-account hedges involving purchase of foreign currency against the rupee.
  • Circular No. 25 is effective immediately, while Circular No. 26's FERR provisions cover contracts undertaken after issuance.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: Which institution issued the rupee-derivatives directions? A: Reserve Bank of India.
  • Q: To what amount was the transaction limit without underlying exposure reduced? A: $5 million.
  • Q: What is the full form of FERR? A: Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve.
  • Q: What proportion of the qualifying contract's rupee value is the FERR reserve? A: 20%.
  • Q: What is the qualifying FERR contract threshold? A: More than $2 million.
  • Q: Through which system are FERR details reported? A: Centralised Information Management System (CIMS).
  • Q: Can cancelled rupee-involving derivative contracts be rebooked under the new directions? A: No; rollovers at maturity remain permitted.

International Relations Russia-Ukraine 12 October 2026

Trump Announces Reported Russia–Ukraine Energy Ceasefire

  • US President Donald Trump announced that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an energy ceasefire.
  • Trump said in a social media post that the ceasefire would take effect immediately.
  • He called on both countries to honour the reported agreement.
  • At the time of the report, neither Russia nor Ukraine had immediately commented on the announcement.
  • The announcement followed Trump's criticism of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy over attacks on diesel facilities in Russia.
  • Trump said those attacks had contributed to a global diesel shortage.
  • Trump had also called for a change in Ukraine's leadership and criticised Zelenskyy over the lack of a peace agreement with Russia.
  • He also connected rising diesel prices in the United States with Zelenskyy's actions.
  • Washington had announced an agreement with Russian President Vladimir Putin for Moscow to supply millions of tonnes of diesel to the United States.
  • Zelenskyy criticised the proposed petroleum-product sales, arguing they could help finance Russia's war.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.
