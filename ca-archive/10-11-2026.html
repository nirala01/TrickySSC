International Relations Strategic Partnership 10 November 2026

India and EU Review Strategic Partnership and FTA Progress

  • India and the European Union held the 7th India–EU Strategic Partnership Review Meeting in New Delhi.
  • The meeting reviewed the full range of bilateral ties and progress under the Joint Comprehensive Strategic Agenda.
  • It was co-chaired by Sibi George, Secretary (West) in India’s Ministry of External Affairs, and Matti Maasikas, the EU’s Deputy Secretary-General for Geoeconomics and Interinstitutional Issues.
  • Both sides discussed stronger trade and investment cooperation, including timely signing and implementation of the India–EU Free Trade Agreement.
  • The review also covered supply-chain resilience and economic security.
  • Connectivity discussions included the Global Gateway and the India–Middle East–Europe Economic Corridor.
  • The sides discussed trilateral cooperation in clean energy, climate resilience, green mobility and digitalisation.
  • They agreed to strengthen coordination on effective global governance and exchanged views on talent mobility, education and skills development.
  • Both sides looked ahead to the next edition of the India–EU Human Rights Dialogue.

Defence Joint Exercise 10 November 2026

India and Myanmar Complete First Tri-Service HADR Exercise

  • Rahat Mitra, the first bilateral tri-service Humanitarian Assistance and Disaster Relief exercise between India and Myanmar, concluded in Chennai.
  • The two-day exercise was held on 5 and 6 November 2026.
  • Participants included personnel from the Indian Army, Indian Navy and Indian Air Force, alongside a Myanmar Defence Forces contingent.
  • Its aims included improving operational interoperability and coordination, and exchanging good practices for disaster-response operations.
  • The opening activities included joint planning and familiarisation with HADR capabilities; a tabletop scenario considered a severe cyclone.
  • The Indian Army demonstrated its capabilities in obstacle clearance and bridge construction, while participants visited INS Sukanya to learn about ship-based HADR.
  • The second day showcased coordinated responses across maritime, land and air domains, including coastal disaster-response arrangements and communications infrastructure.
  • The demonstrations covered field medical support, disaster-relief camp coordination, Navy flood-relief teams, maritime search and rescue, and casualty evacuation.
  • A Chetak helicopter and INS Bitra took part in a coordinated maritime search-and-rescue demonstration; the Indian Air Force also demonstrated casualty evacuation using an ALH helicopter.
  • The exercise provided a platform for sharing operational experience and strengthening mutual understanding for future disaster-response operations.

Government Schemes Urban Cleanliness 10 November 2026

10th Swachh Survekshan Awards to Be Presented in New Delhi

  • President Droupadi Murmu is scheduled to present the Swachh Survekshan 2025–26 awards on 11 November 2026 at Vigyan Bhawan, New Delhi.
  • The 2025–26 survey is the 10th edition of India’s urban cleanliness assessment.
  • The awards recognise cities for contributions to the Swachh Bharat Mission–Urban; award-winning cities were to be announced at the ceremony.
  • The survey is conducted under the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, through its Department of Urban Development.
  • The edition’s theme is “Swachhata ki Nai Pehal – Badhayein Haath, Karein Safai Saath”.
  • The survey’s coverage grew from 73 urban local bodies in 2016 to more than 4,751 cities in the latest edition.
  • Urban local bodies were assessed using 43 indicators across 10 assessment areas, including waste management, sanitation access, used-water management and citizen feedback.
  • More than 3,000 trained assessors conducted field assessments, supported by real-time GPS monitoring; the assessment process was digital and transparent.
  • This edition gave greater weight to citizen feedback and expanded assessment of urban riverfronts, beaches and water bodies, alongside attention to sanitation and heritage or tourist sites.
  • Awards were also to recognise cities performing well under Swachh Shahar Jodi, a city-to-city mentoring and peer-learning initiative launched on 27 September 2025.

Economy & Banking Telecom Regulation 10 November 2026

TRAI Sets Framework for IP-Based Interconnection Port Charges

  • The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) issued the Telecom Interconnection (Port Charges) (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2026.
  • The amendment establishes a framework for charges on IP-based interconnection ports, supplementing existing provisions for E1/Time Division Multiplexing ports.
  • The amended regulations are scheduled to take effect on 1 January 2027.
  • Interconnection links two or more telecom networks so that traffic can pass between them and users on different networks can communicate.
  • TRAI’s authority to prescribe interconnection terms and conditions comes under Section 11(1)(b) of the TRAI Act, 1997.
  • The amended definition of a port recognises IP ports alongside the existing E1 port technology; IP-port bandwidth is expressed in Mbps or Gbps.
  • The rules set annual maximum-charge ceilings for 1 Gbps and 10 Gbps IP ports; providers may mutually agree on lower charges.
  • The framework applies IP-port charges uniformly to fixed-line, mobile, national long-distance and international long-distance services.
  • TRAI also provided for phased financial disincentives for specified violations, capped at ₹15 lakh per service area for each violation.
  • The amendment followed a review process that included stakeholder consultation and an open discussion held on 28 January 2026.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
