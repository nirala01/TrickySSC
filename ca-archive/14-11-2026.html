Government Schemes Urban Sanitation 14 November 2026

Swachh Survey 2025–26: Top Swachh City Pairs Announced

  • Swachh Survey 2025–26 was the 10th edition of India’s urban cleanliness survey; its awards ceremony took place in New Delhi on 11 November 2026.
  • President Droupadi Murmu presented awards to the country’s cleanest cities at the ceremony.
  • The survey theme was “Swachhata Ki Nai Pehal—Badhayein Haath, Karein Safai Saath” (New initiatives in cleanliness—join hands and clean together).
  • The Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA) announced the Swachh City Pair results under Swachh Bharat Mission–Urban 2.0.
  • Ahmedabad and Sanand won first place; the mentor city Ahmedabad is in the over-10-lakh population category and Sanand in the 20,000–50,000 category.
  • Indore and Depalpur secured second place; Indore is in the over-10-lakh category and Depalpur in the under-20,000 category.
  • Tirupati and Kuppam ranked third; Tirupati is in the 50,000–3-lakh category and Kuppam in the 20,000–50,000 category.
  • The top three pairs were selected on their combined scores, reflecting the joint performance and collaborative efforts of mentor and mentee cities.
  • The initiative began on 27 September 2025 and promotes peer learning in urban waste management through the principle of “one teaches one.”
  • The programme included 70 high-performing mentor cities and about 191 partner cities across 33 States and Union Territories, based on Swachh Survey 2024–25 rankings.
  • For this survey edition, 4,751 urban local bodies were assessed using 176 sub-indicators across 10 segments, with more than 3,000 trained field assessors.
  • The survey announced 60 national awards across seven categories; field assessment used GPS-based monitoring and a digital evidence-verification process.

Economy & Banking Foreign Exchange 14 November 2026

India’s Forex Reserves Rise to $719.547 Billion

  • India’s foreign exchange reserves rose by about $2.49 billion to $719.547 billion in the week ended 6 November 2026.
  • The latest figures were reported from a Reserve Bank of India (RBI) statement.
  • Foreign currency assets, the largest component, increased by $232 million to $591.350 billion.
  • Gold reserves rose by $2.205 billion to $104.796 billion.
  • The Special Drawing Rights (SDR) component increased by $52 million to $18.610 billion.
  • India’s reserve position with the International Monetary Fund (IMF) rose by $1 million to $4.791 billion.
  • In the previous reporting week, reserves had fallen by $2.691 billion to $717.056 billion.
  • The four reported components—foreign currency assets, gold, SDRs and the IMF reserve position—make up the stated reserve total.

Economy & Banking Trade 14 November 2026

India’s Total Exports Grow 16.66% in April–October

  • India’s estimated total exports of goods and services reached $568.84 billion in April–October 2026–27, up 16.66% from the same period a year earlier.
  • Estimated total imports for April–October 2026–27 were $662.19 billion, a rise of 16.00% year on year.
  • For October 2026, total goods-and-services exports were estimated at $89.99 billion, 29.90% above October 2025.
  • October 2026 total imports were estimated at $110.07 billion, up 16.58% from October 2025.
  • Merchandise exports in April–October 2026–27 were $309.06 billion, compared with $253.40 billion in the corresponding period of 2025–26—a 21.96% increase.
  • Estimated services exports for April–October 2026–27 stood at $259.78 billion; October services exports were estimated at $40.51 billion.
  • Non-petroleum exports reached $259.22 billion in April–October 2026–27, 18.01% higher than the corresponding previous-year period.
  • In October, petroleum product exports rose 140.21% year on year to $8.89 billion.
  • October exports of electronic goods increased 62.70% to $6.64 billion, while gems and jewellery exports rose 40.06% to $3.21 billion.
  • Engineering goods exports grew 36.01% to $12.74 billion in October 2026; organic and inorganic chemical exports rose 36.20% to $2.89 billion.
  • The figures were released by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry; October services trade figures were estimates based on the latest available RBI data for September.

Science & Technology Astronomy 14 November 2026

India’s First LED Dome Planetarium Opens in Mysuru

  • Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated COSMOS–Mysuru Planetarium in Mysuru, Karnataka, on 13 November 2026.
  • The facility is described in the release as India’s first LED dome planetarium.
  • Its LED dome presents astronomy and the universe through immersive planetarium shows, bringing scientific learning closer to young people.
  • The Indian Institute of Astrophysics and the University of Mysore were among the institutions acknowledged for their role in the project.
  • The inauguration highlighted Mysuru’s tradition of supporting educational institutions, including the establishment of the University of Mysore under Maharaja Nalwadi Krishnaraja Wadiyar.
  • The Prime Minister also cited Indian astronomers Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Bhaskaracharya and Nilakantha Somayaji as part of India’s scientific heritage.
  • The address connected the planetarium with India’s space achievements, including Chandrayaan-3’s landing near the Moon’s south pole and the Aditya-L1 solar mission.
  • The release noted that India had demonstrated space docking and undocking capability, becoming the fourth country to do so.
  • It also highlighted private participation in the space sector, including opportunities for Indian start-ups to develop rockets and satellites.
  • The programme’s wider message linked science learning and research with practical applications for public welfare.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
