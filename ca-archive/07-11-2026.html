Science & Technology Semiconductors 7 November 2026

SEMICON 2.0 Portal Opens; 12 More ECMS Applicants Approved

  • SEMICON 2.0 portal was launched by Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw on 6 November 2026 under the SEMICON India Programme.
  • Applications opened for pillars 2, 3 and 4: machines and materials, additional fabrication units, and a stronger ATMP/OSAT industry.
  • SEMICON 2.0 has a Union Cabinet-approved financial outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore under the India Semiconductor Mission (ISM).
  • Its six pillars cover chip design; machines and materials; fabrication units; ATMP/OSAT; research and development; and talent development.
  • ECMS received approval for 12 additional applications involving estimated investment of ₹7,503 crore.
  • The 12 newly approved applications are expected to generate ₹39,045 crore in production and 7,127 direct jobs.
  • The new approvals cover components, supply-chain products and capital goods, including multilayer PCBs, relays, heat sinks, copper foil and automation equipment.
  • The new projects are located across seven states: Gujarat, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Punjab, Tamil Nadu and Uttar Pradesh.
  • ECMS approvals include its first approvals for copper foil, cathode active material and specialty ceramics.
  • With the latest approvals, ECMS has approved 118 applications across 16 states, covering 34 products and potential investment of ₹77,051 crore.
  • The 118 approved ECMS applications are projected to generate ₹5,73,146 crore in production and 81,755 direct jobs.
  • Under SEMICON 1.0, 12 projects across six states had approvals involving over ₹1.64 lakh crore; five had begun commercial production by October 2026.

Economy & Banking Food Security 7 November 2026

Rice Sale Target under OMSS(D) Doubled to 50 LMT

  • The Department of Food and Public Distribution, under the Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution, announced measures to improve rice availability and check prices.
  • For 2026–27, the rice sale target under Open Market Sale Scheme (Domestic), or OMSS(D), was raised from 25 LMT to 50 LMT.
  • By 31 October 2026, 25 LMT of rice had been sold to private parties through OMSS(D) e-auctions.
  • The comparable OMSS(D) e-auction sales for the same period in the previous financial year were 11 LMT.
  • Rice and paddy stock disclosure was made compulsory nationwide for traders, wholesalers, retailers, large-chain retailers, processors and mill owners.
  • Stock holders must declare their holdings in six categories and update the Food Stock portal every Friday.
  • Failure to register or violation of stock limits can attract action under Sections 6 and 7 of the Essential Commodities Act, 1955.
  • The Centre agreed that Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Telangana may impose rice and paddy stock limits as needed.
  • Any such state stock limits are permitted to remain in force until 31 January 2027.
  • Additional Bharat Rice stock was allocated to NAFED, NCCF and Kendriya Bhandar to widen availability, particularly in southern states.

Defence Joint Exercises 7 November 2026

India and Myanmar Begin First Tri-Service HADR Exercise

  • India and Myanmar began their first bilateral tri-service Humanitarian Assistance and Disaster Relief (HADR) exercise, Rahat Mitra, on 5 November 2026.
  • Rahat Mitra was held in Chennai from 5 to 6 November 2026.
  • Its theme was Together for Humanity, Stronger Through Partnership.
  • The exercise aimed to strengthen bilateral cooperation, mutual trust and coordination between the two countries’ defence forces.
  • More than 300 personnel from the armed forces of both countries took part.
  • The exercise provided a forum to exchange professional experience and improve coordination and operational capability in HADR missions.
  • The Indian Navy coordinated the exercise.
  • Rahat Mitra supports India’s Neighbourhood First policy and its MAHASAGAR strategic approach.
  • MAHASAGAR expands to Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions.

Defence Maritime Exercise 7 November 2026

First BIMSTEC Maritime Security Exercise Concludes

  • The first BIMSTEC Maritime Security Exercise concluded on 3 November 2026 at Sri Vijaya Puram.
  • The exercise ran from 30 October to 3 November 2026 under the supervision of the Andaman and Nicobar Command (ANC).
  • The ANC is India’s only operational tri-service command.
  • The exercise included coordinated maritime activities and naval participation by BIMSTEC member countries.
  • Indian Navy ships INS Taragiri, INS Karmuk and INS Saryu took part.
  • The participating foreign naval ships named in the release were Myanmar Navy’s UMS King Ang Zeya and Sri Lanka Navy’s SLNS Gajabahu.
  • The port phase at Sri Vijaya Puram included senior-level meetings, professional discussions and cultural exchanges.
  • A sea phase near Sri Vijaya Puram took place on 2 and 3 November, featuring coordinated deployments and joint operational activities.
  • The exercise advanced the Delhi Declaration and the outcomes of the fourth BIMSTEC Expert Group meeting on maritime security, held in November 2025.
  • BIMSTEC expands to Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
