Aditya Wins India’s First Gold at U-19 Boxing Championships

  • The reported result established Aditya as the champion of the men’s 55 kg division; no final score for other bouts was reported.

Defence Naval Exercises 28 October 2026

33rd India–Singapore SIMBEX Concludes at Visakhapatnam

  • The 33rd edition of SIMBEX-2026 concluded on 26 October 2026 at Visakhapatnam.
  • The Indian Navy hosted the bilateral maritime exercise with the Republic of Singapore Navy.
  • The exercise aimed to strengthen maritime cooperation and operational coordination between the two navies.
  • SIMBEX was conducted in two stages: a harbour phase and a sea phase in the Bay of Bengal.
  • Harbour activities included expert exchanges, ship visits, professional discussions, sports events and planning activities.
  • The sea phase included maritime interdiction, anti-submarine warfare (ASW) and drills against air and surface threats.
  • The sea phase also included seamanship activities and aircraft operations from each other’s ships.
  • Singapore participated with RSS Supreme, a Formidable-class stealth frigate carrying an SH-70B helicopter.
  • Indian Navy assets included indigenous stealth frigate INS Nilgiri, ASW-capable corvette INS Kamorta, P-8I and Dornier patrol aircraft, Hawk advanced jet trainers and MH-60R helicopters.
  • The exercise supported cooperation on maritime security in the Indian Ocean Region and regional stability.

Economy & Banking Highways 28 October 2026

Concessional Local Pass Introduced for Nearby Highway Users

  • The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways introduced a Concessional Local Pass for eligible non-commercial vehicle owners.
  • Eligibility covers residents living within 20 kilometres of a National Highway fee plaza.
  • Applicants must have a valid and functional FASTag.
  • Eligible users may choose between the existing monthly pass and the new concessional Local Pass for a particular fee plaza.
  • The annual pass fee is ₹1,500 at plazas outside municipal or local town limits.
  • The annual fee is ₹3,000 at plazas within municipal or local town limits.
  • The pass is valid only for the fee plaza for which it is issued and cannot be transferred to another plaza.
  • The provision does not apply to National Highways or expressways using a closed user-fee collection system.
  • The rule is to take effect from 1 November 2026, the first day of the month following the 28 October report.

International Relations MoU 28 October 2026

India and France Partner to Preserve Manuscript Heritage

  • India’s Ministry of Culture and the Bibliothèque nationale de France (BnF) signed and exchanged an MoU in Paris on 26 October 2026.
  • The agreement was made under the Gyan Bharatam Mission to strengthen institutional cooperation on manuscripts and documentary heritage.
  • The MoU was signed by Ministry of Culture Secretary Vivek Aggarwal and BnF President Gilles Pécout.
  • The partnership also involves the National Archives of India in New Delhi and the National Library in Kolkata.
  • Its five cooperation areas are digitisation, sharing through the national digital repository, technology exchange, conservation, and research and development.
  • India and BnF will identify and digitise Indian-origin manuscripts and collections held by BnF.
  • The agreement provides for sharing expertise in digitisation and conservation methods, cataloguing standards, OCR/HTR tools for Indian scripts and digital humanities.
  • Planned activities include joint workshops, capacity-building, research, publications, exhibitions and exchanges of scholars and experts.
  • The Gyan Bharatam Mission focuses on identifying, documenting, conserving, digitising, translating, researching and disseminating India’s manuscript heritage.
  • The mission’s national digital repository is envisaged as a secure collection of Indian-origin manuscripts and Indian knowledge systems, with permission-based access for research and study.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
