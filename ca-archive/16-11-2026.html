International Relations Trade Agreement 16 November 2026

Indian Goods to Get Preferential US Market Access

  • Piyush Goyal, Union Minister for Commerce and Industry, said Indian goods would receive preferential access to the US market with lower tariffs than goods from competing countries.
  • The understanding was reached between Prime Minister Narendra Modi and US President Donald Trump when they announced a broad framework for the India–US Bilateral Trade Agreement (BTA).
  • The reported tariff advantage concerns Indian goods compared with those of competitor countries; the report did not specify tariff rates or product lists.
  • India and the United States were working to conclude the first tranche of the trade agreement by the end of the then-current financial year.
  • Goyal made the remarks in Cairo, Egypt, while responding to a reporter’s question.
  • The Commerce Minister was on a two-day visit to Egypt to discuss ways to expand trade and investment between India and Egypt.
  • The BTA framework is the broader arrangement within which the two countries are working towards the first tranche.
  • The report identified market access and relative tariff treatment as key features of the understanding, without giving details of the agreement’s other provisions.

  • A bilateral agreement is an arrangement between two parties; in this case, the parties are India and the United States.

  • Q: Who is India’s Commerce and Industry Minister? A: Piyush Goyal.
  • Q: Which two countries are negotiating the BTA? A: India and the United States.
  • Q: What does BTA stand for? A: Bilateral Trade Agreement.
  • Q: Where did Piyush Goyal make the reported remarks? A: Cairo, Egypt.
  • Q: What market-access treatment was indicated for Indian goods in the US? A: Preferential access with lower tariffs than those imposed on competitor countries.
  • Q: By when were the sides working to conclude the first tranche? A: By the end of the current financial year, as stated in the report.
  • Q: What was the purpose of Goyal’s visit to Egypt? A: To discuss ways to enhance trade and investment between India and Egypt.

Economy & Banking Health Accounts 16 November 2026

National Health Accounts Show Higher Public Spending

  • The National Health Accounts (NHA) 2023–24 estimates were released by the Union Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.
  • Government health expenditure rose from ₹1,29,778 crore in 2013–14 to ₹4,14,196 crore in 2023–24—more than three times the earlier amount.
  • Out-of-pocket expenditure (OOPE) declined from 64.2% of total health expenditure in 2013–14 to 42.7% in 2023–24, a fall of 21.5 percentage points.
  • OOPE also fell year-on-year, from 43.4% in 2022–23 to 42.7% in 2023–24.
  • Per-person government health expenditure increased from ₹1,042 in 2013–14 to ₹2,959 in 2023–24.
  • Government spending on primary healthcare reached about ₹1.53 lakh crore in 2023–24.
  • Health-related social security expenditure grew from 6% of total health expenditure in 2013–14 to 11% in 2023–24.
  • Private health insurance expenditure increased from 3.4% to 10.2% of total health expenditure between 2013–14 and 2023–24.
  • The release links financial protection to arrangements including government-funded health insurance, state health insurance, social health insurance and medical benefits for government employees.
  • The release cites Ayushman Bharat, free medicines and diagnostics, and Jan Aushadhi among initiatives supporting broader access to healthcare.

  • OOPE means out-of-pocket expenditure: healthcare payments made directly by individuals or households.
  • NHA estimates describe the sources and uses of health financing; the release compares the 2013–14 and 2023–24 estimates.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: Which ministry released the NHA 2023–24 estimates? A: Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.
  • Q: What was India’s OOPE share of total health expenditure in 2023–24? A: 42.7%.
  • Q: From what level did the OOPE share fall between 2013–14 and 2023–24? A: From 64.2%.
  • Q: What was government health expenditure in 2023–24? A: ₹4,14,196 crore.
  • Q: What was per-person government health expenditure in 2023–24? A: ₹2,959.
  • Q: How much government spending on primary healthcare was reported for 2023–24? A: About ₹1.53 lakh crore.
  • Q: What share of total health expenditure was health-related social security in 2023–24? A: 11%.

Environment & Ecology Cyclone Watch 16 November 2026

Bay of Bengal Depression Forecast to Intensify

  • The India Meteorological Department (IMD) reported a depression over the southeast and adjoining southwest Bay of Bengal.
  • The system had moved nearly northwards and was forecast to travel north-northwestwards.
  • The IMD expected it to become a cyclonic storm over the central parts of the south and adjoining west-central Bay of Bengal the following day.
  • It was forecast to intensify further into a severe cyclonic storm over the west-central Bay on Tuesday.
  • After that, the system was expected to strengthen further and move northwest towards the Andhra Pradesh coast over the following two days.
  • The forecast was issued on 15 November; the stated next-day and Tuesday stages therefore referred to 16 and 17 November 2026.
  • The IMD forecast heavy rain, thunderstorms with lightning and gusty winds over the Andaman and Nicobar Islands through the following day.
  • Dense fog was forecast at isolated places in Uttar Pradesh the following day.
  • The report presented these developments as forecasts and did not state that the predicted intensification or landfall had already occurred.

Economy & Banking Cooperatives 16 November 2026

NHEC Revival and Bharat Taxi Begin in Pune

  • Union Home and Cooperation Minister Amit Shah performed the groundbreaking ceremony for the revival of the National Heavy Engineering Cooperative (NHEC) at Talegaon Dabhade in Pune district, Maharashtra.
  • Shah also digitally inaugurated the Western Regional Office of the Central Registrar of Cooperative Societies (CRCS).
  • The CRCS regional office is in Pune and handles regulatory work for multi-state cooperative societies in Maharashtra, Gujarat, Goa, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, and Daman and Diu.
  • Bharat Taxi, described in the release as a cooperative-based taxi service, was launched in Pune.
  • The release said the service would expand across the country and that drivers—called ‘sarathis’—would receive their full earnings without commission deductions.
  • The NHEC revival is a cooperative-sector initiative at an industrial site that had previously housed a factory making plants for sugar mills.
  • The release described NHEC as a unit for making sugar mills and centrifugal machinery, and called it the world’s first such sugar-mill and centrifugal-machinery unit.
  • The planned facility is to make equipment or products for sugar mills, power generation, ethanol, compressed biogas (CBG), dairy plants and containers.
  • The release stated that the earnings from the facility would go to cooperative institutions.
  • The ceremony and launches were presented as part of efforts to strengthen and modernise the cooperative movement under the ‘Sahkar se Samriddhi’ vision.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
