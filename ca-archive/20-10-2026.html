Economy & Banking Trade Agreement 20 October 2026

India–New Zealand FTA to Take Effect on 20 October

  • The India–New Zealand Free Trade Agreement (FTA) takes effect on 20 October 2026, coinciding with Vijayadashami.
  • The implementation date was jointly announced by Union Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal and New Zealand Trade and Investment Minister Todd McClay.
  • The agreement provides 100% tariff-free access in New Zealand for Indian goods, removing duties of up to 10% across tariff categories.
  • Indian sectors expected to benefit include textiles, footwear, leather, gems and jewellery, engineering goods, automobiles, pharmaceuticals, chemicals and processed food.
  • New Zealand committed to facilitate US$20 billion in investment in India under the agreement.
  • New Zealand’s services offer provides market access in about 118 sectors and most-favoured-nation commitments in about 139 sub-sectors.
  • Indian professionals are eligible for an annual quota of 5,000 temporary employment-entry visas and 1,000 working-holiday visas.
  • There is no cap on the number of Indian students in New Zealand; eligible STEM graduates can work for up to three years after study, and doctoral researchers for up to four years.
  • The agreement includes an Agricultural Productivity Partnership, with action plans for kiwifruit, apples and honey and technical support for Centres of Excellence.
  • The first-ever JCB machine export from India to New Zealand was flagged off at the implementation event.

Agriculture and Services

  • The Agricultural Productivity Partnership covers kiwifruit, apples and honey, as well as cooperation in horticulture, forestry, livestock and fisheries.

  • Apple Centres of Excellence are proposed in Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand; honey centres are envisaged in Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal.
  • The services offer includes dedicated access for health and traditional medicine services, including Ayurveda and yoga.

  • Q: What is the annual temporary employment-entry visa quota for Indian professionals? A: 5,000.

Economy & Banking Foreign Investment 20 October 2026

India Receives Over US$748 Billion in FDI During 2014–2025

  • India received over US$748 billion in foreign direct investment (FDI) between 2014 and 2025, a record inflow for the period reported.
  • The number of countries investing in India increased from 89 to 112.
  • The reported policy direction involved liberalising sectoral rules to attract more foreign investment.
  • FDI rules were liberalised in sectors including defence, telecommunications, insurance and space.
  • Foreign investment of up to 74% is permitted in defence under the figures in the report.
  • The report gives a 100% foreign investment limit for telecommunications, insurance and the space sector.
  • Prime Minister Narendra Modi highlighted the investment figures while addressing a Rozgar Mela in New Delhi.
  • Chief Economic Advisor V. Anantha Nageswaran noted that sectors including space, drones, defence and insurance had been opened to foreign investment.
  • The reported aims of the policy reforms include simpler investment procedures and a more investor-friendly environment.

Defence Military Exercise 20 October 2026

Fifth Austrahind Exercise Begins in Thiruvananthapuram

  • The fifth edition of Austrahind began on 19 October 2026 at the Foreign Training Centre in Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala.
  • The India–Australia army exercise is scheduled to run until 1 November 2026.
  • About 120 personnel from each side are taking part.
  • The Indian contingent includes Garhwal Rifles personnel and members of other regiments and services; Australia’s contingent includes soldiers from its 13th Brigade.
  • The exercise focuses on joint sub-conventional operations in island and coastal areas under Chapter VII of the UN Charter.
  • Training covers joint planning and tactical drills in realistic operational conditions, including air and maritime aspects.
  • Key training areas include coordinated coastal and island operations, surveillance and reconnaissance, protection of vital installations, and humanitarian assistance and disaster relief.
  • A New Zealand observer group is participating for the first time in the exercise.
  • The fourth edition was held at Irwin Barracks in Perth, Australia, from 13 to 26 October 2025.

Government Schemes Digital Agriculture 20 October 2026

Shivraj Singh Chouhan Launches Upgraded Agri Welfare Portal

  • Union Agriculture and Farmers Welfare Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan launched the upgraded Agri Welfare Portal for farmers.
  • The portal is designed to give farmers direct mobile access to government schemes, financial assistance and agricultural information.
  • It is intended to reduce procedural barriers and the need for intermediaries when farmers seek information or apply for services.
  • The platform brings information and applications together so farmers need not visit multiple government offices or navigate separate websites.
  • It is designed as a common digital platform for Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs), Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS), scientists, researchers and startups.
  • The portal provides a single-window gateway to major initiatives including PM-KISAN, AgriStack and the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana.
  • A training campaign through Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs) will help farmers use the portal and other digital tools.
  • Officials were asked to keep information on the portal accurate and regularly updated.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
