Environment & Ecology Ramsar Sites 16 October 2026

India’s Ramsar Site Count Rises to 103

  • Two wetlands—Sukhna Wetland and Sauj Jheel—were added to India’s Ramsar list.
  • Sukhna Wetland is in Chandigarh.
  • Sauj Jheel is in Uttar Pradesh.
  • India’s Ramsar tally reached 103 after these additions.
  • Bhupender Yadav, Union Minister for Environment, Forest and Climate Change, announced the additions on 15 October 2026.
  • The ministry responsible for the announcement is the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change.
  • Wetland conservation supports biodiversity, water security and climate security, according to the announcement.
  • Local livelihoods and the ecological and climate benefits of wetlands were also highlighted.

  • Q: Where was the Ramsar Convention adopted? A: Ramsar, Iran.

Defence Defence Contracts 16 October 2026

Defence Ministry Signs GSAT-7C and Varunastra Contracts

  • The Ministry of Defence signed contracts worth over ₹4,000 crore for GSAT-7C and Varunastra Heavy Weight Torpedoes.
  • GSAT-7C is a dedicated communication satellite capability for the Indian Air Force.
  • NewSpace India Limited, based in Bengaluru, received the GSAT-7C contract valued at over ₹2,500 crore.
  • The satellite is to be indigenously designed, developed and manufactured.
  • GSAT-7C is intended to improve secure, resilient and seamless communications for Indian Air Force assets.
  • Varunastra is an indigenous ship-launched anti-submarine torpedo for the Indian Navy.
  • Bharat Dynamics Limited, Hyderabad, received the Varunastra contract valued at over ₹1,500 crore.
  • The Naval Science and Technological Laboratory developed Varunastra.
  • Varunastra can engage enemy submarines at long ranges and is equipped with advanced manoeuvring capabilities.
  • Both contracts support domestic defence manufacturing and self-reliance in critical technologies.

Economy & Banking Trade Data 16 October 2026

India’s Total Exports Estimated at $476.20 Billion

  • Total exports of goods and services for April–September 2026–27 were estimated at US$476.20 billion, up 13.84% year on year.
  • Merchandise exports reached US$259.89 billion, a rise of 18.51% from the corresponding period of 2025–26.
  • Services exports were estimated at US$216.31 billion, compared with US$199.02 billion a year earlier.
  • Total imports for April–September 2026–27 were estimated at US$551.13 billion, up 15.68%.
  • The total trade deficit for the six-month period was estimated at US$74.93 billion.
  • Non-petroleum exports reached US$218.70 billion, a 15.56% increase year on year.
  • September 2026 total exports were estimated at US$80.75 billion, up 10.99% from September 2025.
  • September 2026 total imports were estimated at US$94.05 billion, an 8.19% increase year on year.
  • Electronic goods were a leading merchandise export growth driver in September; exports rose 70.44% to US$5.31 billion.
  • The Commerce and Industry Ministry released the trade figures; September services figures were estimates.

Economy & Banking Digital Governance 16 October 2026

Piyush Goyal Launches National Strategic Trade Command Centre

  • Piyush Goyal, Union Minister of Commerce and Industry, launched the National Strategic Trade Command Centre (NSTCC).
  • The launch venue was Vanijya Bhawan in New Delhi.
  • The Department of Commerce, under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, developed the centre.
  • NSTCC’s purpose is to strengthen monitoring, analysis and governance of India’s international trade using integrated data.
  • The platform is designed to support data-driven monitoring and evidence-based trade decisions.
  • Product-wise and market-wise analysis can help identify export opportunities and assess India’s trade position.
  • Its dashboards bring together trade performance, export-promotion schemes, regulatory approvals, application processing and operational indicators.
  • Initial coverage includes IEC, Advance Authorisation, EPCG, Certificates of Origin, SCOMET and restricted import-export processes.
  • The centre uses DGFT systems and the Trade Connect e-Platform, with provision for additional data integration.
  • SCOMET means Special Chemicals, Organisms, Materials, Equipment and Technologies.

Government Schemes Telecom Service 16 October 2026

Caller Name Presentation to Begin from 1 November

  • The Department of Telecommunications (DoT) directed telecom providers to introduce Calling Name Presentation (CNAP) across service areas from 1 November 2026.
  • CNAP displays the caller’s name alongside the caller’s number on the recipient’s phone.
  • The displayed name is taken from the originating telecom provider’s subscriber database.
  • The service is to be available to all users without a separate request.
  • Unsaved international calls will be labelled “International Call”.
  • Trials have been conducted on wireless terrestrial networks using 4G and higher-generation technologies.
  • CNAP on 2G and 3G wireless networks is to be considered separately.
  • Public switched telephone networks are also outside the initial technology coverage and will be considered separately.

  • User action is not required to receive the service.
  • 2G, 3G and public switched telephone network coverage will be considered separately.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: What is the full form of CNAP? A: Calling Name Presentation.
  • Q: Which department directed telecom providers to introduce CNAP? A: Department of Telecommunications.
  • Q: From when is CNAP to be introduced? A: 1 November 2026.
  • Q: Where does the caller name displayed by CNAP come from? A: The originating provider’s subscriber database.
  • Q: What appears for an unsaved international call? A: “International Call”.
  • Q: Which technology generations have been used in trials? A: 4G and higher-generation wireless terrestrial networks.
  • Q: Will users need to make a separate request? A: No; the service is to be provided to all users.

Economy & Banking Employment Statistics 16 October 2026

September Unemployment Rate Declines to 5 Percent

  • The National Statistics Office (NSO) released the September labour-force indicators.
  • Overall Labour Force Participation Rate (LFPR) rose to 55.5%.
  • Overall Worker Population Ratio (WPR) reached 52.7% year on year.
  • The overall unemployment rate fell by 0.2 percentage points from September last year to 5%.
  • Rural unemployment was 4.2% in September.
  • Urban unemployment remained broadly stable at 6.8%.
  • Rural participation and employment conditions improved, with stronger growth in rural LFPR.
  • Urban female participation rose month on month to its highest level since October 2025.
  • Urban WPR also reached its highest level since October 2025, with improvement among men and women.
  • Urban LFPR recorded a marginal year-on-year decline, while female participation improved over the year, particularly in rural areas.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
