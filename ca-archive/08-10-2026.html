Awards & Honours Nobel Prize 8 October 2026

Kagan and Soai Win 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry

  • The 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry went to French chemist Henri B. Kagan and Japanese chemist Kenso Soai.
  • They were recognised for discoveries on non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis.
  • Their work helps explain how molecular asymmetry, or homochirality, can emerge spontaneously in nature.
  • Many molecules, including amino acids, occur in two mirror-image forms, but living organisms overwhelmingly use one form.
  • Traditional laboratory synthesis commonly produced equal amounts of both mirror-image forms.
  • Kagan developed a method in 1986 to increase the excess of one molecular mirror image in a reaction.
  • Soai built on this work with a reaction in 1995 and demonstrated a reaction yielding only one mirror-image form in 2003.
  • The discoveries showed that chemical reactions can be steered to produce predominantly, or exclusively, one mirror-image form.
  • The work has implications for pharmaceutical development, where the molecular form can affect therapeutic effectiveness.
  • Kagan and Soai will share a prize of 12 million Swedish kronor.

Economy & Banking Banking 8 October 2026

RBI Removes Paytm Payments Bank from Scheduled Banks

  • The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) removed Paytm Payments Bank Ltd (PPBL) from the list of scheduled banks.
  • PPBL was excluded from the Second Schedule to the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934.
  • The RBI action followed cancellation of PPBL’s banking licence.
  • The RBI cancelled the licence in April, citing non-compliance with regulatory norms.
  • The RBI said the bank’s affairs were being conducted in a manner detrimental to depositors’ interests.
  • The exclusion notification was published on 31 July and later notified in the Gazette of India.
  • The Delhi High Court subsequently ordered the winding up of Paytm Payments Bank.
  • In March 2022, the RBI barred PPBL from onboarding new customers following supervisory concerns.
  • Further restrictions in January and February 2024 barred fresh deposits, credits and top-ups in customer accounts, prepaid instruments and wallets.

Economy & Banking Monetary Policy 8 October 2026

RBI Governor Assesses Impact of 25-Basis-Point Repo Hike

  • RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra said a recent 25-basis-point repo rate hike was unlikely to significantly affect overall economic expansion.
  • The rate increase could moderate bank credit growth, which was above 18 per cent.
  • The Governor expected credit growth to remain strong and support economic activity.
  • He said minor fluctuations in the pace of credit growth would not materially affect the economy.
  • The RBI shifted its policy stance to calibrated tightening and indicated rate cuts were unlikely in the near term.
  • Monetary policy decisions would be guided mainly by domestic growth and inflation conditions, while also considering global developments.
  • The Governor cited renewed escalation of the West Asia conflict and volatile crude oil prices as risks to global sentiment.
  • Rising bond yields in advanced economies and an appreciating US dollar were also linked to greater financial-market volatility.
  • Global growth was expected to slow in 2026, although it was described as resilient.
  • Higher energy and food prices were expected to raise inflation and could prompt further monetary tightening by major central banks.

Government Schemes Healthcare Regulation 8 October 2026

NCAHP Notifies New Rules for Allied Healthcare Education

  • The National Commission for Allied and Healthcare Professions (NCAHP), under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, notified measures under the NCAHP Act, 2021.
  • The measures cover autonomous boards and a Central Assessment Committee (CAC), practitioner registration, and a competency-based diploma curriculum.
  • Autonomous boards under state allied and healthcare councils will handle responsibilities relating to education, institutional assessment, professional ethics and registration.
  • Each autonomous board will have 10 to 20 members, including a chairperson and representation from recognised professions.
  • An assessment and rating board will evaluate applications, inspect institutions and publish assessment and rating information through the state council’s website.
  • Ethics and registration boards will verify practitioner documents, support state registers and consider matters involving professional conduct and registration.
  • Where a state has no functioning council, the CAC may carry out autonomous-board functions under NCAHP oversight.
  • The CAC will have 10 to 30 members, including a chairperson, with professional and relevant state or Union Territory representation.
  • NCAHP introduced digital practitioner registration with a unique identification number and QR-code-enabled credentials, allowing public verification through state and central registers.
  • The competency-based Diploma in Medical Radiology and Imaging Technology (D.MRIT) is a two-year course with a six-month clinical internship.
  • The D.MRIT curriculum is mandatory for relevant institutions from academic year 2027–28; institutions may adopt it from 2026–27.
  • The course is intended for Class 12 science students with Physics, Chemistry and Biology and addresses demand for trained imaging professionals.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

Why is the work of Kagan and Soai important?
Their discoveries showed how reactions can favour one of two mirror-image molecular forms. This matters in areas such as pharmaceutical development, where the molecular form can affect therapeutic effectiveness.
Why was Paytm Payments Bank removed from the scheduled banks list?

What is the RBI’s policy stance after the repo rate hike?

What are the main features of the new NCAHP measures?
