Government Schemes Postal Modernisation 27 November 2026

India Post Gets ₹2,632 Crore for Transformation

  • The Union Cabinet approved a combined ₹2,632 crore outlay for India Post’s Postal Operations Plan and Estate Management Plan.
  • The funding covers the 16th Finance Commission cycle, from financial year 2026–27 to 2030–31.
  • The Postal Operations Plan has an allocation of ₹1,141 crore; the Estate Management Plan has ₹1,491 crore.
  • The plans aim to modernise postal infrastructure, strengthen logistics and improve citizen-facing services across India.
  • The Postal Operations Plan covers rural postal access, postal-network and parcel-network optimisation, international postal infrastructure, marketing, service quality and philately.
  • The Estate Management Plan focuses on modernising post office buildings and improving the infrastructure used to deliver services.
  • Planned estate works include 190 new departmental post office buildings, reconstruction of 50 buildings and renovation and branding of 2,550 departmental sub-post offices.
  • The plan also includes upgrading 2,500 rented post offices, conserving 20 historic postal buildings and renovating 150 residential colonies.
  • The estate plan supports a permanent integrated technology centre for CEPT in Bengaluru.
  • The Department of Posts set a target of about ₹19,803 crore in annual revenue for financial year 2026–27, supported by logistics growth, technology adoption and operational improvements.

Technology and Background

  • India Post is the postal department under the Ministry of Communications.
  • CEPT stands for Centre for Excellence in Postal Technology; the release identifies Bengaluru as the location for its planned permanent integrated technology centre.
  • The Department’s Integrated Insurance Management System supports the purchase process for Postal Life Insurance (PLI) policies.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: What is the combined outlay for the two India Post plans? A: ₹2,632 crore.
  • Q: Which plan receives ₹1,141 crore? A: The Postal Operations Plan.
  • Q: How much is allocated to the Estate Management Plan? A: ₹1,491 crore.
  • Q: Which Finance Commission cycle does the funding cover? A: The 16th Finance Commission cycle, FY 2026–27 to FY 2030–31.
  • Q: Which ministry oversees India Post? A: The Ministry of Communications.
  • Q: What is the full form of PLI? A: Postal Life Insurance.
  • Q: Where is the permanent integrated technology centre for CEPT planned? A: Bengaluru.

Awards & Honours Governance Rankings 27 November 2026

DFS Pension Reforms Tops GRAI Group B

  • The Pension Reforms Division of the Department of Financial Services (DFS) ranked first in Group B of the October 2026 Grievance Redressal Assessment and Index.
  • The ranking was issued by the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG).
  • Group B covers ministries and departments that received fewer than 500 complaints.
  • The GRAI framework assesses grievance resolution through CPGRAMS, including the effectiveness and timeliness of disposal.
  • A dedicated grievance redressal cell monitors CPGRAMS every day, and the matter is reviewed regularly in senior-level meetings.
  • Additional Secretary, Joint Secretary and division-head level officials review 20 complaints each month.
  • Since January 2024, the DFS Secretary has personally reviewed 20 randomly selected closed complaints every month.
  • DFS said its monitoring includes pending complaints, timely disposal, coordination with concerned organisations and ongoing improvements to grievance processes.

Defence India–UAE Defence 27 November 2026

India and UAE Hold 14th Joint Defence Meeting

  • India and the United Arab Emirates (UAE) held the 14th Joint Defence Cooperation Committee (JDCC) meeting in Abu Dhabi on 26 November 2026.
  • India’s Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh co-chaired the meeting with UAE Ministry of Defence Under-Secretary Lieutenant General Ibrahim Nasser M. Al Alawi.
  • The meeting gave strategic direction to programmes under the India–UAE Strategic Defence Partnership Framework, signed during the Prime Minister’s UAE visit in May 2026.
  • Both sides agreed to deepen cooperation in defence industry, defence innovation, advanced technology, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, military training and maritime security.
  • They agreed to accelerate partnerships for the joint development and production of next-generation defence equipment.
  • The co-chairs decided to review progress in different defence-cooperation areas regularly before the next JDCC meeting.
  • Representatives from India’s Defence Department, Armed Forces, National Security Council Secretariat and Ministry of External Affairs took part in the delegation.
  • An Indian defence-industry delegation joined the visit to promote business-to-business partnerships and took part in an industry dialogue with UAE counterparts.
  • The Indian industry delegation visited Tawazun Industrial Park and met the UAE’s Tawazun Economic Council to explore cooperation opportunities.
  • The Indian delegation also met UAE Minister of State for Defence Affairs Mohammed bin Mubarak Fadel Al Mazrouei and discussed regional developments and the strategic defence partnership.

Science & Technology Astronomy 27 November 2026

Elias 2-24 b Identified as Youngest Known Planet

  • Astronomers confirmed Elias 2-24 b, a planet less than one million years old and the youngest known planet reported in the item.
  • The planet is approximately Jupiter-sized and has roughly the mass of Jupiter.
  • Elias 2-24 b orbits a star located about 450 light-years from Earth.
  • Astronomers observed it using a coronagraph at the W. M. Keck Observatory in Hawaii.
  • The Keck Observatory collaborates with NASA under a cooperative agreement.
  • The system’s very young age gives researchers an opportunity to study a planet in the early stages of formation.
  • Observations of Elias 2-24 b may offer insight into conditions in the early stages of planetary-system development.
  • The report notes that studying the young system can provide a glimpse of what our Solar System may have been like billions of years ago.

  • Q: What instrument was used to observe the planet? A: A coronagraph.

International Relations Regional Security 27 November 2026

Maldives Hosts Eighth Colombo Security Conclave Meeting

  • The Maldives hosted the eighth National Security Advisor-level meeting of the Colombo Security Conclave (CSC).
  • The meeting reaffirmed the members’ shared commitment to regional peace and security through sustained cooperation.
  • Member states adopted the Roadmap of Cooperation for 2026–2027 and signed the Agreed Minutes.
  • The roadmap sets out key deliverables and planned activities under the CSC’s five cooperation pillars.
  • The pillars cover maritime safety and security; countering terrorism and radicalisation; and combating trafficking and transnational organised crime.
  • The remaining pillars are cybersecurity and protection of critical infrastructure and technology, and humanitarian assistance and disaster relief.
  • The participating CSC member states named in the report were the Maldives, India, Mauritius and Sri Lanka.
  • Australia and Malaysia participated as guests.
  • India’s delegation was led by National Security Advisor Ajit Doval; the other national delegations included representatives from the Maldives, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh.
  • The first CSC Secretary General, Vice Admiral S. N. Ghormade (Retd.), presented a review of action taken on decisions from the previous NSA-level meeting in New Delhi on 20 November 2025.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
