Defence Counter-terrorism 20 November 2026

Amit Shah Outlines Unified Police Strategy Against Terrorism

  • Amit Shah, Union Home Minister, addressed the inaugural session of a two-day counter-terrorism conference in New Delhi.
  • The conference was organised by the National Intelligence Agency, as named in the supplied report.
  • Shah said the government is developing a system to bring police stations across India onto a unified anti-terror strategy.
  • The proposed approach aims to transmit police-station-level intelligence through multiple levels to Directors General of Police (DGPs) for actionable inputs.
  • He called for stronger proactive action and improved structural and operational capabilities in counter-terrorism.
  • The approach was described as an ecosystem-based effort requiring cooperation between the Centre and States.
  • Shah urged every State’s Anti-Terrorism Squad (ATS) to strengthen its cyber-expert units as technology is increasingly used in terror-related crimes.
  • He stressed that dismantling the entire terror-financing network is essential to eliminate terrorism.
  • Shah cited the recent elimination of Lashkar terrorist Latif Bhat and said Kashmir had become free from terrorism after 35 years.

Exam Context

  • The proposed system links local intelligence collection with higher-level police leadership to help generate actionable intelligence.
  • ATS stands for Anti-Terrorism Squad; the report calls for stronger cyber expertise within State ATS units.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Who addressed the counter-terrorism conference? — Amit Shah.
  • Where was the conference held? — New Delhi.
  • How long was the conference? — Two days.
  • Which agency was named as the conference organiser? — National Intelligence Agency.
  • What does ATS stand for? — Anti-Terrorism Squad.
  • To which senior police post was station-level intelligence proposed to be transmitted? — Director General of Police (DGP).
  • Which network did Shah say must be dismantled to eliminate terrorism? — The terror-financing network.

Environment & Ecology Cyclone 20 November 2026

Cyclone Akvan Forecast to Cross Andhra Pradesh–Odisha Coast

  • Akvan was reported as a Severe Cyclonic Storm over the west-central Bay of Bengal.
  • It was moving north-northwestwards at 13 km per hour over the preceding six hours.
  • The forecast placed landfall by noon the following day, between Narsapur in Andhra Pradesh and Puri in Odisha.
  • The expected crossing zone was along the North Andhra Pradesh–Yanam and South Odisha coasts, close east of Gopalpur.
  • The forecast landfall wind speed was 90–100 km per hour, with gusts up to 110 km per hour.
  • The Odisha government had begun evacuations and set up more than 9,000 shelters in vulnerable areas.
  • More than 300 emergency response teams, including the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) and Odisha Disaster Rapid Action Force (ODRAF), were deployed.
  • The report said over one lakh people had been evacuated to safer places.
  • Odisha Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi said preparations prioritised achieving zero casualties.
  • The Shree Jagannath Temple Administration advised devotees to plan visits according to weather advisories; Indian Railways cancelled or rescheduled some services.

Government Schemes Skill Development 20 November 2026

PM-SETU Agreements Cover Four Telangana ITI Clusters

  • PM-SETU expands to Prime Minister Skilling and Employability Transformation through Upgraded ITIs.
  • Shareholders’ Agreements (SHAs) were signed for four Industrial Training Institute (ITI) clusters in Telangana, moving their approved strategic investment plans into implementation.
  • The signatories were the Directorate General of Training (DGT) under the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE), the Telangana government and the relevant industry partners.
  • Apollo Medskills is the lead industry partner for the Old City cluster in healthcare and life sciences; its approved investment is ₹241 crore.
  • Newland Foundation is the partner for the Sangareddy cluster in pharmaceuticals and chemicals, with ₹241 crore approved.
  • Zen Technologies is the partner for the Medchal cluster in defence and electronics, with ₹254 crore approved.
  • Sri Siddhartha Infratech and Services is the partner for the Patancheru cluster in diversified manufacturing, with ₹275 crore approved.
  • The four clusters have a combined approved investment of approximately ₹1,011 crore.
  • A separate Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) will be formed for each cluster to oversee implementation and long-term operations.
  • Under the reported SPV structure, the industry partner holds 51% and the Union and State governments hold 24.5% each; the agreements brought nationwide PM-SETU SHAs to seven.

Purpose and Structure

  • The projects aim to upgrade government ITIs into industry-oriented technical excellence centres and better align training with sectoral skill needs.
  • Planned measures include modernising workshops and laboratories, updating equipment and curricula, training instructors, and improving apprenticeships and employment outcomes.
  • The SPV shareholding is 51% for the industry partner and 24.5% each for the Union government and the State government.

Important Facts for Exams

  • What is the full form of PM-SETU? — Prime Minister Skilling and Employability Transformation through Upgraded ITIs.
  • Which Union ministry is responsible for PM-SETU? — Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship.
  • Which directorate under MSDE signed the agreements? — Directorate General of Training.
  • How many Telangana ITI clusters were covered? — Four.
  • Which partner was named for the Medchal cluster? — Zen Technologies.
  • What share does the industry partner hold in each cluster SPV? — 51%.
  • How many PM-SETU shareholders’ agreements had been signed nationwide after these agreements? — Seven.

Sports, Culture & Days Film Festival 20 November 2026

57th IFFI Opens in Goa with ‘Stories Without Borders’ Theme

  • The 57th International Film Festival of India (IFFI) was scheduled to begin in Goa on 20 November 2026 and run for nine days, concluding on 28 November.
  • Its theme was ‘Stories Without Borders’, bringing films and creative voices from different countries, cultures and cinematic traditions together.
  • The opening ceremony was to be held at Dr. Shyama Prasad Mukherjee Stadium in Goa.
  • The festival programme included more than 150 international films from 86 countries.
  • The festival was to open with *Fatherland*, directed by Polish filmmaker Paweł Pawlikowski, and conclude with *We Are All Strangers*, directed by Singaporean filmmaker Anthony Chen.
  • *I Matter*, directed by Alina Șerban and co-produced by Romania, Germany and Belgium, was named the mid-festival film.
  • Spain was the Country of Honour and Russia the Partner Country; the Indian Panorama featured 76 films in 18 languages.
  • Two new sections were announced: Animation and Films About Films, each featuring five films.
  • The main competition awards include the Golden Peacock for Best Film and Silver Peacock awards for Best Director, Best Actor, Best Actress, Special Jury Award and Best Debut Feature Film by a Director.
  • Other programme highlights included the ICFT-UNESCO Gandhi Medal, a focus on BRICS cinema, the Creatives Minds of Tomorrow programme and the 20th anniversary of WAVES Film Bazaar.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
