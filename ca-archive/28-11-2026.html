Government Schemes Skill Development 28 November 2026

NAVYA 2.0 to expand vocational training for young women

  • NAVYA 2.0 is the second phase of a joint initiative by the Ministry of Women and Child Development (MWCD) and the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE).
  • It is scheduled to launch on 28 November 2026 at the Uttarakhand Sanskrit Academy auditorium in Haridwar.
  • The initiative aims to build vocational skills, confidence, self-reliance and economic independence among adolescent girls and young women.
  • The expanded phase will cover 100 districts across 24 States and Union Territories, with a focus on aspirational and remote areas.
  • The eligible age range has been widened from 16–18 years to 16–25 years.
  • Training will include industry-relevant modern and non-traditional job roles, with pathways to employment, apprenticeships and entrepreneurship.
  • The pilot began on 24 June 2025 in Sonbhadra, Uttar Pradesh, and was planned for 27 aspirational districts in 19 States.
  • Around 1,200 girls received pilot training in roles including drone manufacturing and assembly technician, solar PV installation helper and fashion designer.
  • The initiative also emphasises digital and financial literacy, career readiness and entrepreneurship.
  • The Skill India Digital Hub (SIDH) will support access, registration, attendance, training progress and certification, with real-time monitoring.
  • The launch programme will also inaugurate Saksham Anganwadi centres and new Anganwadi centres built under PM-JANMAN in Uttarakhand, and award certificates to PMKVY-trained candidates.

  • Q: Where is NAVYA 2.0 scheduled to launch? A: Haridwar, Uttarakhand.
  • Q: When was the NAVYA pilot launched? A: 24 June 2025.
  • Q: Where did the NAVYA pilot begin? A: Sonbhadra, Uttar Pradesh.
  • Q: How many districts will NAVYA 2.0 cover? A: 100 districts across 24 States/UTs.
  • Q: What is the expanded eligible age range? A: 16–25 years.
  • Q: Which digital platform will support NAVYA implementation? A: Skill India Digital Hub (SIDH).

Defence Indian Navy 28 November 2026

Vindhyagiri completes Project 17A frigate deliveries

  • Vindhyagiri (Y-3024), the seventh and final Nilgiri-class warship under Project 17A, was handed over to the Indian Navy in Kolkata on 27 November 2023, as stated in the release.
  • The ship was built by Garden Reach Shipbuilders and Engineers Limited (GRSE); its handover completed delivery of all seven Project 17A warships.
  • Project 17A ships are multi-purpose warships designed for a range of maritime missions and security challenges.
  • The Warship Design Bureau designed the Project 17A ships, which were built under the supervision of the Warship Supervisory Team (Kolkata).
  • Compared with Project 17 (Shivalik-class) warships, Project 17A ships have more advanced weapon and sensor systems.
  • Vindhyagiri uses CODOG propulsion—Combined Diesel or Gas turbine—with a diesel engine and a gas turbine driving controllable-pitch propellers.
  • Its Integrated Platform Management System (IPMS) supports control and monitoring of ship systems.
  • The class supports the Navy in surface warfare, anti-air warfare and anti-submarine operations.
  • The ship is named after the Vindhyagiri mountain range in Karnataka and carries forward the name of an earlier Leander-class INS Vindhyagiri.
  • The earlier INS Vindhyagiri served in the Indian Navy from 8 July 1981 to 11 June 2012.
  • The release says Vindhyagiri was built in 68 months, compared with 84 months for the first ship, INS Nilgiri.
  • Project 17A used about 75% indigenous content and involved more than 200 micro, small and medium enterprises.

Economy & Banking Banking 28 November 2026

RBI lowers minimum daily CRR maintenance to 90%

  • The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) reduced the minimum daily maintenance requirement for the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) from 99% to 90%.
  • The change will take effect from the fortnight beginning 1 December 2026.
  • The RBI announced the decision through a notification.
  • The RBI said it assessed the prevailing and evolving macroeconomic situation and liquidity conditions in making the decision.
  • The move came amid a liquidity crunch in the banking system.
  • Banking-system liquidity had moved into deficit for the first time in five months.
  • The reported pressures included Goods and Services Tax (GST) outflows and RBI interventions in the foreign exchange market.
  • CRR is the cash reserve ratio requirement for banks, maintained with the RBI; the reported change concerns its minimum daily maintenance level.

  • Q: What was the reported liquidity condition in the banking system? A: Liquidity had turned to deficit.

Economy & Banking Capital Markets 28 November 2026

SEBI permits FPIs in physically settled commodity derivatives

  • The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has permitted foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) to participate in physically settled non-agricultural commodity derivative contracts.
  • Before the new framework, FPIs could participate in commodity derivatives on recognised stock exchanges only through cash-settled non-agricultural contracts.
  • The revised framework allows non-cash-settled contracts but excludes options in goods.
  • SEBI said the change is intended to deepen institutional participation and improve liquidity in the commodity derivatives segment.
  • FPIs may hold positions in physically settled contracts only until the tender or staggered delivery period begins.
  • They must close or roll over their open positions before the start of the delivery period.
  • SEBI has provided a safeguard mechanism for cases where an FPI does not voluntarily close or roll over its position.
  • The framework is scheduled to come into effect on 1 June 2027.

  • Q: Which regulator issued the new FPI framework? A: SEBI.
  • Q: What does FPI stand for? A: Foreign portfolio investor.
  • Q: Which commodity derivatives can FPIs newly access under the framework? A: Physically settled non-agricultural contracts.
  • Q: Which contract type is excluded? A: Options in goods.
  • Q: When must FPIs close or roll over open positions? A: Before the delivery period begins.
  • Q: When does the framework take effect? A: 1 June 2027.
  • Q: What is the stated purpose of the change? A: To deepen institutional participation and enhance liquidity.

Economy & Banking External Sector 28 November 2026

India’s forex reserves fall to 700.9 billion dollars

  • India’s foreign exchange reserves fell by 4.4 billion US dollars to 700.9 billion US dollars in the week ended 20 November 2026.
  • The figures were reported from data released by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).
  • Gold reserves declined by more than 4 billion US dollars to 94.5 billion US dollars during the week.
  • Foreign currency assets (FCAs), the largest component of the reserves, fell by 266 million US dollars to 583.2 billion US dollars.
  • Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) decreased by 67 million US dollars to 18.45 billion US dollars.
  • India’s reserve position with the International Monetary Fund (IMF) fell by 15 million US dollars to 4.74 billion US dollars.
  • The report gives the weekly movement and end-period values for the principal reserve components.
  • The weekly reserve change does not, by itself, identify a single cause for the decline.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

What is NAVYA 2.0?

Where and when is NAVYA 2.0 scheduled to launch?
The launch is scheduled for 28 November 2026 at the Uttarakhand Sanskrit Academy auditorium in Haridwar. The expanded initiative is planned for 100 districts across 24 States and Union Territories.
What is the age limit for NAVYA 2.0?

What is special about Vindhyagiri?

What CRR change did RBI announce?
