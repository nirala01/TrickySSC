Defence Missiles 6 November 2026

DRDO Successfully Tests Pralay Missile

  • DRDO successfully flight-tested the indigenously developed Pralay quasi-ballistic missile.
  • The test took place from Dr APJ Abdul Kalam Island, off the coast of Odisha.
  • The missile was developed by Research Centre Imarat, Hyderabad, with other DRDO laboratories.
  • The flight test formed part of user evaluation trials.
  • The Defence Ministry reported that all stipulated test objectives were achieved.
  • Test data were captured by range instruments deployed by the Integrated Test Range, Chandipur.
  • Senior DRDO officials, industry representatives and members of the Defence Forces were present.
  • Defence Minister Rajnath Singh described the successful trial as a milestone for indigenous defence capability.
  • Defence Secretary and DRDO Chairman Rajesh Kumar Singh congratulated the teams involved.
  • The successful test brings the system’s induction closer, according to the Defence Ministry.

  • DRDO stands for Defence Research and Development Organisation and functions under the Ministry of Defence.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: Which organisation tested Pralay? A: Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO).
  • Q: What type of missile is Pralay? A: A quasi-ballistic missile.
  • Q: Where was the flight test conducted? A: Dr APJ Abdul Kalam Island, off Odisha.
  • Q: Which DRDO centre led the missile’s development? A: Research Centre Imarat, Hyderabad, with other DRDO laboratories.
  • Q: Which range’s instruments recorded the test data? A: Integrated Test Range, Chandipur.
  • Q: What kind of trials included the test? A: User evaluation trials.
  • Q: Who was the Defence Minister named in the report? A: Rajnath Singh.

Economy & Banking Tax Devolution 6 November 2026

Centre Advances Tax Devolution to States

  • The Union Government released a regular instalment of ₹1,09,019 crore in tax devolution to State Governments.
  • The instalment was released on 5 November, earlier than its usual release date of 10 November.
  • The advance release was made ahead of the festive season.
  • The Ministry of Finance said the funds would help States accelerate capital spending.
  • The release is also intended to support States’ development-related expenditure.
  • States can use the funds for welfare-related expenditure, as noted by the Ministry.
  • The transfer is a regular instalment of tax devolution, not a separately described scheme.
  • Tax devolution is the sharing of a portion of Union tax proceeds with the States.

Background

  • The Finance Commission recommends how the divisible pool of Union taxes is shared between the Union and the States.
  • The Finance Commission is a constitutional body provided for under Article 280 of the Constitution.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: Which ministry reported the advance tax devolution? A: Ministry of Finance.
  • Q: Who received the instalment? A: State Governments.
  • Q: On what date was the instalment released? A: 5 November.
  • Q: When is the instalment normally released? A: 10 November.
  • Q: What was the stated reason for advancing the release? A: The upcoming festive season.
  • Q: Which constitutional article provides for the Finance Commission? A: Article 280.
  • Q: What is tax devolution? A: The sharing of Union tax proceeds with State Governments.

India Retains Men’s Asian Champions Trophy

  • The final was held in Mohali, at Olympian Balbir Singh Senior International Hockey Stadium.

  • India reached the final after defeating South Korea 8–1 in the semi-final.

  • Malaysia and South Korea were scheduled to play for the bronze medal.

Railways Marks Fourth Phase of Vande Mataram Celebrations

  • The Ministry of Railways marked the fourth phase of year-long celebrations commemorating 150 years of Vande Mataram.
  • A cultural programme was held at Rail Bhawan, New Delhi.
  • The celebrations were organised in four phases, with activities highlighting national unity, patriotism and India’s cultural heritage.
  • The fourth phase included mass singing, an essay competition, a quiz and a Tiranga rally.
  • The first phase began with mass singing on 7 November 2025.
  • The second phase included an essay competition at Railway Board on 22 January 2026.
  • The essay competition’s theme was “Vande Mataram: The Spirit of Unity in India’s Diversity”.
  • The third phase held a quiz on Vande Mataram’s 150-year journey on 10 August 2026.
  • The Railway Board Cultural Group was felicitated for its contribution to organising programmes during the year.
  • The group received a prize and a merit certificate.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
