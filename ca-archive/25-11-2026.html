Government Schemes Digital Governance 25 November 2026

SAMAVESH and New e-Anudaan Portal to Be Launched

  • The Department of Social Justice and Empowerment is scheduled to launch SAMAVESH and the new e-Anudaan Portal in New Delhi on 26 November 2026.
  • SAMAVESH stands for Single Access Mechanism for All Verticals of Empowerment & Social Harmony.
  • Union Minister Dr Virendra Kumar and Minister of State B. L. Verma are due to launch the platforms.
  • SAMAVESH will bring the Department’s schemes, services and organisational portals together through one digital gateway.
  • Its Samajik Sahayak chatbot will use AI to provide information and assistance in more than 22 regional languages.
  • The new e-Anudaan Portal will consolidate grant-in-aid processes for four schemes of the Department.
  • The four schemes are NAPDDR, SMILE, AVYAY and SHRESHTA.
  • NAPDDR is the National Action Plan for Drug Demand Reduction; AVYAY is the Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana.
  • An MoU with BHASHINI is planned to support multilingual access and integrate language technology with SAMAVESH.
  • A second MoU with the National Skill Development Corporation (NSDC) will support skilling and employment under PM-AJAY’s Grant-in-Aid component.

  • e-Anudaan covers NAPDDR, SMILE, Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana (AVYAY) and SHRESHTA.

  • The NSDC MoU relates to skilling and employment support under PM-AJAY’s Grant-in-Aid component.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: Which department developed SAMAVESH? A: Department of Social Justice and Empowerment.
  • Q: Where is SAMAVESH scheduled to be launched? A: New Delhi.
  • Q: What is the name of SAMAVESH’s AI chatbot? A: Samajik Sahayak.
  • Q: Which organisation will partner on multilingual access for SAMAVESH? A: BHASHINI.
  • Q: Which organisation is the skilling and employment partner under the PM-AJAY MoU? A: NSDC.
  • Q: Which portal will consolidate grant-in-aid processes for four schemes? A: The new e-Anudaan Portal.

Science & Technology Space 25 November 2026

India’s Space Economy Projected to Reach $40–45 Billion

  • Union Minister for Science and Technology Dr Jitendra Singh addressed the fifth India International Space Conclave 2026 in New Delhi.
  • India’s space economy is currently valued at nearly $9 billion, according to the Minister.
  • The space economy is projected to reach $40–45 billion in less than a decade.
  • India’s space ecosystem has grown from a single-digit startup count to nearly 500 companies in a few years.
  • The Minister cited NSIL, followed by the creation of IN-SPACe and opening the sector to private participation, as key changes in the sector.
  • Space-based applications can support infrastructure planning under the PM Gati Shakti National Master Plan.
  • The Minister said space technology can help with project timelines, costs, wastage and leakages.
  • Applications of space technology span areas including communications, education, healthcare, railways, infrastructure and defence.
  • Dr Jitendra Singh urged domestic private players to increase investment and industry leaders to encourage overseas investment in the sector.
  • ISRO Chairman Dr V. Narayanan said Mission Gaganyaan was in its final phase; the rocket and other components were ready, while work continued on the orbital module.

International Relations Bilateral Relations 25 November 2026

India and Germany Review Their Strategic Partnership

  • Prime Minister Narendra Modi and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz reviewed the India–Germany Strategic Partnership during a telephone conversation.
  • The leaders noted that 2026 marks 75 years of India–Germany diplomatic relations.
  • They expressed satisfaction with the continuing momentum in bilateral engagement.
  • Both leaders reaffirmed cooperation in defence and advanced technologies.
  • They said the India–EU Free Trade Agreement would create further opportunities for India–Germany trade and investment.
  • Modi and Merz exchanged views on regional and global developments of mutual interest.
  • They agreed to work together to support global peace, stability and a rules-based international order.
  • The leaders also highlighted continued cooperation at multilateral forums to address contemporary global challenges.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body

Frequently Asked Questions

What is SAMAVESH?
SAMAVESH is a digital gateway developed by the Department of Social Justice and Empowerment. It brings the Department’s schemes, services and organisational portals together on one platform.
What is the purpose of the new e-Anudaan Portal?
It will unify grant-in-aid processes for NAPDDR, SMILE, AVYAY and SHRESHTA. The portal is part of the Department of Social Justice and Empowerment’s digital service initiative.
What is Samajik Sahayak?
Samajik Sahayak is an AI-powered chatbot on SAMAVESH. It is designed to provide information and assistance in more than 22 regional languages.
What is the projected size of India’s space economy?
It is projected to grow from nearly US$9 billion to US$40–45 billion in less than a decade. The projection was shared by Union Minister Dr Jitendra Singh.
What is the latest update on Mission Gaganyaan?
ISRO Chairman Dr V. Narayanan said the mission was in its final phase. He said the rocket and other components were ready, with work continuing on the orbital module.
What did India and Germany discuss in their leaders’ call?
Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Chancellor Friedrich Merz reviewed their Strategic Partnership and cooperation in defence and advanced technologies. They also discussed trade, investment and global developments.
Is current affairs enough to score well in SSC General Awareness?
No. Current affairs are important but static GK — History, Geography, Polity, Economics and Science — still carries the larger share of the General Awareness section.
