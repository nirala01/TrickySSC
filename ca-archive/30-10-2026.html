Economy & Banking Economic Growth 30 October 2026

India’s Economy Projected to Grow 7.35% in Q2 FY27

  • Projection: India’s economy is projected to grow by 7.35% in the second quarter of FY 2026–27.
  • Source: The estimate appears in the Finance Ministry’s monthly economic review for October, released on 30 October 2026.
  • Q2 period: The second quarter of a financial year covers July to September.
  • Industrial activity: Stronger industrial output was among the factors supporting the growth outlook.
  • Energy and transport: Higher energy consumption and increased port traffic were also cited as indicators of economic momentum.
  • Domestic demand: Robust GST collections and rising automobile sales added to the reported growth support.
  • Exports: Combined merchandise and services exports grew 11% year-on-year in the previous month.
  • Merchandise exports: These rose 23.1% year-on-year and were the main driver of export growth.
  • Key export sectors: Electronics and engineering goods contributed significantly to the increase in merchandise exports.
  • External sector: The review noted resilience in India’s external sector despite global uncertainty.

Economy & Banking Capital Market 30 October 2026

SEBI Puts India’s Corporate Bond Market at ₹61.5 Lakh Crore

  • Market size: SEBI’s latest data puts the value of India’s corporate bond market at ₹61.5 lakh crore.
  • Regulator: SEBI stands for the Securities and Exchange Board of India, India’s securities-market regulator.
  • Fundraising trend: More domestic companies are raising funds directly through capital markets rather than relying only on bank loans.
  • Credit profile: The share of top-rated AAA bonds among newly issued corporate bonds has fallen to 66%.
  • Lower-rated bonds: The volume of lower-rated corporate bonds has increased, indicating a shift in the issuance mix.
  • Investor interest: Large institutional investors are showing greater interest in these bonds in pursuit of higher returns.
  • NBFCs: Bonds issued by non-banking financial companies are drawing particular investor interest.
  • Mutual fund change: Direct mutual fund plans will become the default option on digital platforms from 1 November 2026.
  • Investor benefit: Direct plans are intended to give individual investors access to mutual funds with lower expense ratios.
  • Derivatives trend: Recent data also points to slower individual participation in high-risk equity derivatives trading.

Appointments European Union 30 October 2026

Christine Lagarde to Leave ECB Presidency on 7 February

  • Decision: European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde has decided to step down on 7 February 2027.
  • Timing: Her departure is seven months before the scheduled end of her term.
  • Notification: Lagarde informed the ECB Governing Council and European Council President António Costa.
  • Succession: Costa said that the process to identify her successor would begin immediately.
  • Appointment authority: The successor will be appointed by the European Council.
  • Legal basis: The appointment process follows Article 283 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union.
  • Institution: The ECB is the central bank of the European Union’s euro area.
  • Headquarters: The European Central Bank is headquartered in Frankfurt, Germany.

AIIA Launches National Artificial Intelligence Challenge for Ayurveda

  • Launch: AIIA launched the AyushTech Innovate: National Open Ayurveda AI Challenge on 30 October 2026.
  • Institution: AIIA is the All India Institute of Ayurveda, located in New Delhi.
  • Ministry: The institute and its incubation centre operate under the Union Ministry of Ayush.
  • Finale: The challenge’s grand finale is scheduled for 15–18 December 2026 at IIT Bombay.
  • Occasion: The launch took place during the sixth Foundation Day celebration of AIIA-iCAINE.

  • IPR facility: An Intellectual Property Rights facility for Ayurveda was also launched to help innovators protect their work.
  • Incubator background: Established on 29 October 2021, AIIA-iCAINE is recognised as India’s first public-sector Ayurveda business incubator.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
