Economy & Banking Aviation 21 October 2026

India Sets Monthly Domestic Passenger Record

  • Civil Aviation Minister Ram Mohan Naidu greeted air traffic control personnel gathered in Chennai on the International Day of the Air Traffic Controller.
  • India recorded over 1.5 crore domestic passengers in a single month, according to the minister.
  • India handles more than 5,000 domestic and 1,000 international traffic movements each day.
  • The minister outlined an ambition to increase passenger numbers from 24 crore to 130 crore by 2047.
  • Air Traffic Controllers (ATCOs) manage aircraft movements and help maintain safe and efficient air traffic.
  • The minister said technology can assist aviation safety, while human judgement, composure and responsibility remain essential.
  • Airports Authority of India (AAI) earmarked ₹3,490 crore for infrastructure modernisation.
  • Of AAI’s earmarked amount, ₹1,532 crore is for air traffic management system automation.
  • The minister said ATCOs will have a pivotal role as India’s aviation sector and air traffic expand.

Key Details

RecordOver 1.5 crore domestic passengers in one month

Passenger ambition24 crore to 130 crore by 2047

ATM automation₹1,532 crore earmarked

Exam Background

  • AAI is the Airports Authority of India, the organisation named in the announcement for airport infrastructure modernisation.
  • ATM in this context means air traffic management; its automation is part of the AAI modernisation plan.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Who is India’s Civil Aviation Minister named in the report? — Ram Mohan Naidu.
  • Where were the air traffic control personnel gathered? — Chennai.
  • What monthly record did India achieve? — Over 1.5 crore domestic passengers in one month.
  • What is the passenger ambition for 2047? — Increase from 24 crore to 130 crore.
  • Which organisation earmarked funds for infrastructure modernisation? — Airports Authority of India.
  • What does ATM stand for in the modernisation announcement? — Air traffic management.

Source: News On AIR report

Government Schemes Crafts Fair 21 October 2026

Shilp Samagam Mela 2026 Opens in Guwahati

  • Shilp Samagam Mela 2026 is an eight-day fair organised by the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment in Guwahati.
  • The Guwahati edition is scheduled to run from 21 to 28 October 2026.
  • The venue is Farm Gate, Khanapara, in Assam’s capital, Guwahati.
  • The fair is open to the public from 11 a.m. to 9 p.m.
  • 75 artisans, including 31 women, are exhibiting around 40 varieties of handloom and handicraft products.
  • Participating artisans include makers from across India, including the North-Eastern states.
  • The fair aims to improve market access, encourage entrepreneurship and support sustainable livelihoods for artisans from SC, OBC, Safai Mitra and DNT communities.
  • It provides artisans a platform to display and sell directly to visitors and connect with potential customers.
  • Bharat-TULIP is promoted at the fair to help take products to markets through shops and e-commerce platforms.
  • The fair supports artisans and beneficiaries associated with NSFDC, NSKFDC and NBCFDC, three national corporations under the ministry.

Purpose and Support

  • Bharat-TULIP stands for Traditional Artisans’ Upliftment Livelihood Programme and supports market access, including online visibility and sales.
  • NSFDC, NSKFDC and NBCFDC are the National Scheduled Castes Finance and Development Corporation, National Safai Karamcharis Finance and Development Corporation, and National Backward Classes Finance and Development Corporation.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Which ministry is organising the Guwahati Shilp Samagam Mela? — Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment.
  • Where is the Guwahati fair being held? — Farm Gate, Khanapara.
  • How long is the Guwahati edition? — Eight days.
  • How many artisans are taking part in Guwahati? — 75, including 31 women.
  • What does Bharat-TULIP expand to? — Traditional Artisans’ Upliftment Livelihood Programme.
  • Name the three national corporations associated with the fair. — NSFDC, NSKFDC and NBCFDC.

Source: PIB release

Government Schemes Crafts Fair 21 October 2026

Mysuru Hosts Shilp Samagam Mela 2026

  • The nine-day Shilp Samagam Mela 2026 concluded at the Dasara Exhibition Grounds in Mysuru, Karnataka.
  • The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment organised the fair to market traditional handicrafts and handloom products made by eligible artisans.
  • Artisans from 12 States and Union Territories participated in the Mysuru fair.
  • The event featured 83 stalls, of which 80 were allotted to artisans; 37 women artisans participated.
  • The fair displayed products including handloom saris, handicrafts, spices, pickles, incense sticks, honey products, leather goods and wooden toys.
  • Ramdas Athawale, Minister of State for Social Justice and Empowerment, visited the fair.
  • Sudhansh Pant, Secretary of the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment, also visited the venue.
  • The ministry said it has organised Shilp Samagam fairs since 2022; 13 fairs had benefited 975 artisans by the time of the release.
  • Mysuru was the first Shilp Samagam Mela of financial year 2026–27.
  • The ministry’s Bharat-TULIP initiative presents artisans’ products under a brand to improve visibility and facilitate online sales.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
