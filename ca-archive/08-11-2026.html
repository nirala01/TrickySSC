International Relations Research Cooperation 8 November 2026

India and EU Conclude Horizon Europe Association Talks

  • India and the European Union (EU) concluded negotiations for India’s association with Horizon Europe, the EU’s research and innovation programme.
  • The association is intended to establish a long-term institutional framework for research cooperation between India and the EU.
  • It will build on existing cooperation under the India-EU Agreement on Science and Technology Cooperation and the India-EU Trade and Technology Council (TTC).
  • Indian researchers, institutions and industry are expected to be able to join collaborative research proposals seeking programme funding.
  • The proposed association would also give Indian participants access to EU research consortia and advanced research infrastructure.
  • It is intended to support researcher mobility and joint development of critical technologies, standards and solutions.
  • Horizon Europe’s Pillar II clusters include health; digital technologies; industry and space; climate; energy and mobility; and food and bioeconomy.
  • Exploration of the association began after the India-EU Summit of 27 January 2026; formal talks began following the TTC ministerial meeting in Brussels on 15 July 2026.

  • The proposed association is intended to widen access to collaborative research funding, EU research networks and research infrastructure.
  • The release links the cooperation to strengthening India’s innovation ecosystem and advancing Viksit Bharat 2047.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: Which EU programme is India seeking association with? A: Horizon Europe.
  • Q: Which two parties concluded the association negotiations? A: India and the European Union.
  • Q: Which Horizon Europe pillar’s clusters are highlighted? A: Pillar II.
  • Q: Where was the TTC ministerial meeting that preceded formal talks held? A: Brussels.
  • Q: On what date did formal talks begin following the TTC meeting? A: 15 July 2026.
  • Q: Which India-EU body is named alongside the science and technology agreement? A: The Trade and Technology Council.

Environment & Ecology Air Quality 8 November 2026

CAQM Invokes GRAP Stage III Across Delhi-NCR

  • The Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) activated Stage III of the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) across the National Capital Region (NCR) with immediate effect.
  • The decision followed a CAQM GRAP sub-committee meeting on 6 November 2026 to review air quality and forecasts.
  • The Central Pollution Control Board’s daily AQI bulletin recorded Delhi’s average AQI at 382 on 6 November, in the ‘very poor’ category.
  • The sub-committee cited unfavourable weather conditions that prevented pollutants from dispersing properly.
  • India Meteorological Department (IMD) and Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM) forecasts indicated that AQI could reach the ‘severe’ category in the coming days.
  • Stage III measures were to be implemented alongside all measures already in force under GRAP Stages I and II.
  • NCR agencies, including state pollution control boards and the Delhi Pollution Control Committee (DPCC), were asked to enforce all three stages strictly.
  • The public was advised to walk or cycle for short trips, use less-polluting transport, combine journeys and avoid burning coal or wood for heating.

  • Q: Which Delhi pollution-control body was named for enforcement? A: DPCC.

Economy & Banking Drug Pricing 8 November 2026

Revised Prices of 137 Anti-Cancer Drugs Take Effect

  • Revised prices for 137 anti-cancer drugs are scheduled to take effect from 9 November.
  • The National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) recently capped trade margins on the maximum retail price (MRP) of these medicines.
  • The Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers reported that 76 pharmaceutical companies had submitted revised MRPs.
  • The reported revisions covered 1,225 anti-cancer formulations, relating to 121 drugs.
  • MRP reductions were reported in more than 1,000 formulations of anti-cancer medicines.
  • The estimated annual savings to consumers from the price reductions are expected to exceed ₹3,500 crore.
  • Retailers, dealers, hospitals and institutions must prominently display revised MRP lists on their websites and at their premises.
  • The announcement concerns anti-cancer medicine prices and is associated with the Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers.

Awards & Honours Police Honour 8 November 2026

Uttarakhand Police Receives the President’s Colour

  • Union Home and Cooperation Minister Amit Shah presented the President’s Colour to Uttarakhand Police in Dehradun on 6 November 2026.
  • Uttarakhand became the 16th state whose police force has received this honour, described in the release as the highest honour for a police force.
  • Uttarakhand Chief Minister Pushkar Singh Dhami attended the ceremony.
  • The award recognised the service, discipline and courage of the force; the release dedicated the honour to 206 personnel who made the supreme sacrifice.
  • The state police force grew from 13,000 personnel to more than 26,000, according to the address.
  • The release highlighted the force’s response to forest fires, landslides, tunnel rescues and other emergencies, including the Silkyara tunnel rescue.
  • Uttarakhand Police helps manage security for major pilgrimages and destinations, including the Kumbh Mela, Char Dham Yatra, Hemkund Sahib and Kainchi Dham.
  • Shah said Uttarakhand Police was working on the new criminal laws and stated a goal of securing punishment within three years for cases registered after May 2027.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
