Defence Internal Security 9 November 2026

Leaders Praise Security Forces at Bastar Diwali

  • Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Union Home Minister Amit Shah praised security personnel for their role in overcoming Naxalism.
  • The celebrations took place with security personnel in Bastar, Chhattisgarh, during Diwali.
  • Modi described the Bastar Diwali celebration as special and memorable, and said development should follow the end of Naxalism.
  • Amit Shah said Modi has made it a tradition to celebrate Diwali with security personnel who are away from their families while serving the country.
  • Shah described the Bastar celebration as significant because the region had previously been affected by Naxal violence.
  • Shah called it the first Diwali in five decades after the country had been freed from the scourge of Naxalism.
  • The Home Minister dedicated the occasion to the courage and sacrifices of security personnel who lost their lives in service.
  • The leaders linked the change in Bastar with a new chapter of peace and development.

Indian Shuttlers Win Three Titles in Telangana

  • Indian players won three of the five titles at the Telangana India International Challenge 2026.

Defence Naxalism 9 November 2026

PM Marks Bastar Diwali After Naxalism’s Decline

  • Prime Minister Narendra Modi celebrated Diwali with security personnel in Bastar, Chhattisgarh, and addressed them.
  • Modi said every district in the country was free from Naxalism and that citizens had overcome the fear of Left Wing Extremism.
  • He recalled that at one point more than 230 districts across nearly 20 states were affected by Naxalism.
  • The Prime Minister named Bihar, Maharashtra, Odisha, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh among the affected states.
  • Modi said that during 2004–2013, annual security personnel fatalities exceeded the number of Naxalites killed.
  • He said that, for the first time in 47 years, no CRPF jawan had lost his life on duty in the past year.
  • Modi thanked personnel from the CRPF, CoBRA, BSF, ITBP, SSB, IRB, state police forces, Bastar Fighters and special forces, including women commandos.
  • The Prime Minister visited a CRPF security camp at Karanpur in Bastar.
  • He said that over the preceding 12 years, 50 crore people had been lifted out of extreme poverty while Naxal violence had ended in the country.
  • Modi credited security personnel’s sacrifices and service with helping secure the country’s unity and integrity.

Sports, Culture & Days Festivals 9 November 2026

Vice-President Opens Deepotsav 2026 in Ayodhya

  • Vice-President C. P. Radhakrishnan inaugurated Deepotsav 2026 at Ram Ki Paidi in Ayodhya, Uttar Pradesh.
  • The 2026 celebration was the 10th edition of Deepotsav.
  • The event began in 2017 with 1.7 lakh diyas; the release noted that more than 26 lakh diyas were lit in 2025.
  • For the 10th edition, the plan was to light more than 30 lakh diyas at Ram Ki Paidi and over 70 lakh across Ayodhya.
  • The Vice-President watched Ramayana-based cultural performances and musical fireworks and attended the Saryu Aarti at Naya Ghat.
  • More than 2,100 indigenous drones were used to depict episodes from the Ramayana.
  • Around 1,000 artists took part in the cultural programmes.
  • The celebrations supported local livelihood opportunities for potters, rural women, artisans, performers, accommodation providers, guides and transport-service providers.
  • Radhakrishnan also offered prayers at the Shri Ram Janmabhoomi Temple and attended special prayers and aarti at Ram Katha Park.
  • Uttar Pradesh Governor Anandiben Patel and Chief Minister Yogi Adityanath were among those present at the event.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
