Economy & Banking Fuel Standards 27 October 2026

E100 Ethanol Fuel Allowed in Certified Flex-Fuel Vehicles

  • The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways amended the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989, through a gazette notification.
  • The amendment recognises E100 fuel in the emission-related provisions for eligible flex-fuel vehicles.
  • E100 is petrol blended with up to 100% ethanol, as described in the amended rules.
  • The change applies only to vehicles specifically designed and certified to use the relevant ethanol fuel.
  • E100 has been added alongside E85 for eligible flex-fuel vehicles.
  • The amendment enables original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) to launch vehicles engineered to run on 100% bio-ethanol fuel.
  • Vehicles designed for E20 petrol or conventional fuel are not permitted to use E85 or E100 under the notification.
  • E85 and E100 are therefore not interchangeable with fuels intended for ordinary petrol vehicles.

Exam Background

  • An OEM is an original equipment manufacturer—the company that manufactures and markets a vehicle.
  • The notification concerns fuel use in specially designed vehicles; it does not authorise E85 or E100 for all petrol vehicles.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Which ministry amended the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989? — Ministry of Road Transport and Highways.
  • Which ethanol fuel was newly recognised in the rules? — E100.
  • Which other ethanol fuel is mentioned alongside E100? — E85.
  • Which vehicles can use E100 under the amendment? — Vehicles specifically designed and certified for it.
  • Can an E20-designed vehicle use E100 under the notification? — No.
  • What does OEM stand for? — Original Equipment Manufacturer.

International Relations Legal Metrology 27 October 2026

India Hosts 61st International Legal Metrology Meeting

  • The 61st Meeting of the International Committee of Legal Metrology began in New Delhi.
  • India hosted this international meeting for the first time.
  • The gathering brought together delegates, senior officials and technical experts from across the world.
  • The meeting focused on cooperation in measurement standards, consumer protection and fair trade.
  • Consumer Affairs Secretary Nidhi Khare addressed the gathering.
  • Khare highlighted the role of accurate, internationally harmonised measurements in fair trade and consumer confidence.
  • She also linked reliable measurement standards with industrial innovation and economic growth.
  • The meeting highlighted Government Approved Test Centres as a way to increase verification capacity.
  • The e-Maap National Portal is being developed to provide integrated digital services in legal metrology.
  • The address also referred to Jan Vishwas reforms of 2023 and 2026, including specified offence decriminalisation, improvement notices and graded penalties.

Economy & Banking Export Support 27 October 2026

RELIEF Export Scheme Extended Until March 2027

  • The Department of Commerce extended the timeline of Component II of the RELIEF initiative until 31 March 2027.
  • RELIEF stands for Resilience and Logistics Intervention for Export Facilitation.
  • RELIEF is a time-bound intervention under the Export Promotion Mission.
  • The extension was notified amid continued geopolitical disruptions in West Asia and effects on maritime logistics across the Gulf and adjoining regions.
  • Component II encourages exporters to obtain ECGC cover for upcoming shipments to specified regions.
  • The component provides 95% risk coverage through the relevant ECGC cover.
  • Eligible cover includes Standalone Policies and Whole Turnover Policies obtained on or after the date specified in the scheme notification.
  • For eligible exporters, the premium is protected from increases beyond pre-disruption levels during the eligible period.
  • Covered cargo categories include Full Container Load, Less than Container Load and refrigerated containers; energy shipments are excluded.
  • Exporters must submit claims and supporting documents through the ECGC customer portal at ecgcltd.in.

Coverage and Purpose

  • RELIEF was launched on 19 March 2026, according to the report, to support exporters facing freight-cost escalation, higher insurance premia and war-related export risks.
  • Component II covers specified shipment categories and excludes energy shipments; the extension responds to disruption along the Gulf and wider West Asian maritime corridor.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Which department extended the RELIEF timeline? — Department of Commerce.
  • Under which mission does RELIEF operate? — Export Promotion Mission.
  • What risk coverage does Component II provide? — 95%.
  • Which organisation’s customer portal is used to submit claims? — ECGC.
  • Which shipment category is excluded from the stated coverage? — Energy shipments.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
