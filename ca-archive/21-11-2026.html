Science & Technology Animal Health 21 November 2026

India Unveils Its First Indigenous H5N1 Bird Flu Vaccine

  • India’s first indigenous H5N1 bird flu vaccine was dedicated to the nation by Union Agriculture and Farmers’ Welfare Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan.
  • The vaccine was developed by ICAR–National Institute of High Security Animal Diseases (NIHSAD) in Bhopal.
  • NIHSAD is an institute of the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR).
  • The vaccine is intended to help protect the poultry sector from H5N1 bird flu.
  • The development is relevant to poultry farmers’ livelihoods as well as animal health.
  • The Minister congratulated NIHSAD’s scientists and researchers for developing the vaccine.
  • H5N1 is a subtype of avian influenza; the supplied report identifies it as the target of the vaccine.
  • The announcement concerns an indigenous animal-health vaccine developed at Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh.

Dedicated byShivraj Singh Chouhan

Background

  • ICAR is the Indian Council of Agricultural Research, the national body associated with agricultural research and education.
  • NIHSAD’s full name is the National Institute of High Security Animal Diseases.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: What is the target of India’s first indigenous bird flu vaccine? A: H5N1.
  • Q: Which institute developed the vaccine? A: ICAR–NIHSAD.
  • Q: Where is NIHSAD located? A: Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh.
  • Q: What does NIHSAD stand for? A: National Institute of High Security Animal Diseases.
  • Q: What does ICAR stand for? A: Indian Council of Agricultural Research.
  • Q: Which Union Minister dedicated the vaccine to the nation? A: Shivraj Singh Chouhan.
  • Q: Which sector is the vaccine intended to help protect? A: The poultry sector.

Defence Indian Navy 21 November 2026

Indian Navy Receives Diving Support Craft DSC A21

  • DSC A21, a Diving Support Craft, was delivered to the Indian Navy at the Naval Dockyard in Mumbai.
  • The craft was indigenously designed and constructed by Titagarh Rail Systems Limited of Kolkata.
  • DSC A21 is equipped with specialist diving equipment.
  • Its design provides enhanced stability and a larger deck area.
  • The vessel has improved sea-keeping characteristics for its specialist role.
  • It was designed and built under applicable Naval Rules and Regulations of the Indian Register of Shipping.
  • The craft will support diving operations and underwater inspections.
  • Its roles also include salvage assistance and coastal operational deployment.
  • The craft is part of the Diving Support Craft project and is associated with the Make in India initiative.

  • Q: Which company designed and built DSC A21? A: Titagarh Rail Systems Limited.

  • Q: Which national initiative is the craft described as supporting? A: Make in India.

Economy & Banking Core Industries 21 November 2026

Core Industries Index Grows 10.9% in October

  • The Index of Core Industries (ICI) recorded year-on-year growth of 10.9% in October.
  • October’s growth was higher than the upwardly revised 8.2% recorded in September.
  • The provisional figures were released by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry.
  • Six of the nine sectors covered in the report registered positive growth.
  • Iron ore production had the highest reported growth, at 26.4%.
  • Coal production grew by 23.1%, while electricity generation rose by 21.6%.
  • Cement production increased by 15.4% and steel output by 7.3%.
  • Refinery products grew by 3.4% year on year.
  • Natural gas fell by 7.6%, crude oil by 4.1% and fertiliser production by 12.2%.

Awards & Honours Dairy Awards 21 November 2026

National Gopal Ratna Awards 2026 Winners Announced

  • The Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying announced the winners of the National Gopal Ratna Awards 2026.
  • The awards recognise contributions to the conservation, development and promotion of indigenous bovine breeds.
  • The three categories are best dairy farmer rearing indigenous cows or buffaloes; best dairy cooperative, milk producer company or dairy FPO; and best artificial insemination technician.
  • A special award is also given for the North-Eastern Region and Himalayan States to encourage dairy development there.
  • In the best dairy farmer category, Muralidharan Nair of Kottayam, Kerala, won first place outside the special regional category.
  • In the dairy cooperative/company/FPO category, TND 93 Thachur MPCS of Kallakurichi, Tamil Nadu, won first place.
  • In the artificial insemination technician category, Shubhankar Nandi of Hooghly, West Bengal, won first place outside the special regional category.
  • The special regional awardees named were Sagar Singh Rawat (dairy farmer), The Chak Devia Dairy Cooperative Limited (dairy organisation) and Sudesh Kumar (AIT).
  • Applications were invited from 10 August to 30 September 2026 through awards.gov.in, and 1,583 applications were received.
  • The awards will be presented on 26 November 2026 at Bapu Sabhagar, Muradpur, Patna, during National Milk Day celebrations.

  • The National Gokul Mission was launched in December 2014 to support conservation and scientific development of indigenous breeds.
  • The awards are presented by the Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying under the Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying.

  • Q: Which organisation won first place in the dairy cooperative/company/FPO category? A: TND 93 Thachur MPCS, Tamil Nadu.
  • Q: Who won first place in the AIT category outside the special regional category? A: Shubhankar Nandi of West Bengal.
  • Q: Where will the 2026 awards be presented? A: Bapu Sabhagar, Muradpur, Patna.
  • Q: When are the awards scheduled to be presented? A: 26 November 2026, during National Milk Day celebrations.

Government Schemes Housing 21 November 2026

PMAY-U 2.0 Sanctions 1.75 Lakh More Houses

  • The government sanctioned 1.75 lakh houses under Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana–Urban 2.0 (PMAY-U 2.0).
  • The approvals were made at the scheme’s 11th Central Sanctioning and Monitoring Committee meeting.
  • An additional in-principle approval for 1.5 lakh houses was also recorded at the meeting.
  • The meeting was chaired by Satendra Singh, Secretary, Department of Urban Development.
  • In-principle approval covered over 1.27 lakh houses in Andhra Pradesh and over 22,000 in Maharashtra, subject to compliance with scheme guidelines.
  • With the approvals, the total number of houses sanctioned under PMAY-U 2.0 crossed 24 lakh.
  • The Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs implements the urban housing scheme.
  • Launched in September 2024, PMAY-U 2.0 aims to support one crore additional eligible EWS, LIG and MIG families.
  • The scheme supports eligible urban families to construct or purchase pucca houses through different verticals.
  • States and Union Territories were asked to speed up expenditure, release funds on time, strengthen monitoring and geo-tag houses.

  • Q: Which ministry is responsible for PMAY-U 2.0? A: Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs.
  • Q: When was PMAY-U 2.0 launched? A: September 2024.
  • Q: How many additional eligible families does PMAY-U 2.0 aim to support? A: One crore.
  • Q: Which income groups are named in the scheme’s target? A: EWS, LIG and MIG.
  • Q: How many houses were sanctioned in the reported meeting? A: 1.75 lakh.
  • Q: In which two states were the reported in-principle approvals made? A: Andhra Pradesh and Maharashtra.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
