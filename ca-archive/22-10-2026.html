Government Schemes Financial Inclusion 22 October 2026

Finance Ministry Launches Nationwide Financial Inclusion Campaign

  • The Union Finance Ministry launched a nationwide Financial Inclusion Campaign at Gram Panchayat and Urban Local Body (ULB) levels on 22 October 2026.
  • The campaign will run until 31 December 2026 and cover 2.7 lakh Gram Panchayats and ULBs.
  • The Department of Financial Services (DFS) is coordinating the campaign with banks and other stakeholders.
  • Bank camps will support enrolment in Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY), a life-insurance scheme.
  • The camps will also promote Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY), which provides accident insurance.
  • Enrolment in Atal Pension Yojana (APY), a pension scheme, is another focus of the campaign.
  • The campaign will promote Kisan Credit Card (KCC) applications and enrolment in Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) ahead of the Rabi season.
  • Progress will be monitored by stakeholders including state administrations, banks and the DFS.
  • The stated aim is to widen access to formal financial services and help eligible people receive the benefits of key government schemes.

  • PMJJBY is a life-insurance scheme; PMSBY is an accident-insurance scheme.
  • APY is a pension scheme, while KCC facilitates farmers' access to credit.
  • PMFBY is the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana, a crop-insurance scheme.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: Which ministry launched the Financial Inclusion Campaign? A: Union Ministry of Finance.
  • Q: Which department is coordinating the campaign? A: Department of Financial Services.
  • Q: At which local levels is the campaign being conducted? A: Gram Panchayat and Urban Local Body levels.
  • Q: Until when will the campaign run? A: 31 December 2026.
  • Q: Which life-insurance scheme is included? A: Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY).
  • Q: Which accident-insurance scheme is included? A: Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY).
  • Q: Which pension scheme is included? A: Atal Pension Yojana (APY).
  • Q: Which crop-insurance scheme will be promoted ahead of Rabi? A: Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY).

Sports, Culture & Days Para Sports 22 October 2026

India Wins 27 Medals on Day Three of Asian Para Games

  • India won 27 medals on Day 3 of the Asian Para Games in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan: 5 gold, 11 silver and 11 bronze.
  • India's overall tally rose to 62 medals—14 gold, 22 silver and 26 bronze—and the country stood fifth in the medal table.
  • Dilip Gavit won the men's 100m T46/47 combined gold in 10.69 seconds; Mohammed Morssinganakath Basil took silver in a Games-record 10.72 seconds.
  • Sachin Sarjerao Khilari won the men's shot put F46 gold with a record throw of 15.87 metres; Mohd. Yasser won silver and Parveen bronze.
  • Ayush Verma claimed gold in the men's shot put F53 event, adding a second medal to his personal tally.
  • Praveen Kumar won men's high jump T44/64 gold with a Games-record 2.05m; Banti took silver with a personal best of 1.92m.
  • Simran won the women's 100m T12 gold in a Games-record 12.02 seconds, her second gold after winning the 200m the previous day.
  • Sheetal Devi and Payal Nag won silver in the women's compound doubles para-archery final.
  • Krishna Nagar and Sivarajan Solaimalai took men's doubles SH6 badminton silver; three medals had also been assured in the SL4 category.
  • Swimmer Ravi Budigina won men's 100m backstroke S13 silver, while Himanshu Nandal earned bronze in men's 100m breaststroke S11.
  • Bronze medallists included Anubhav Choudhary (100m T35), Rakeshbhai Bhatt (100m T37), Sharath Makanahalli (1500m T12) and Sukhchain (javelin F55).
  • Other Indian bronze winners included Shailesh Kumar (long jump T42/T61/T63), Haney (discus F37) and Karamjyoti Dalal (women's discus F55).

Defence Joint Exercise 22 October 2026

Ninth IBSAMAR Naval Exercise Concludes in South Africa

  • The ninth edition of the India-Brazil-South Africa Maritime Exercise, IBSAMAR-IX 2026, concluded on 20 October 2026.
  • The exercise took place at Simon's Town, South Africa, and involved the navies of India, Brazil and South Africa.
  • It was a ten-day joint exercise with both harbour and sea phases.
  • Activities included professional exchanges, operational discussions and joint training among personnel from the three navies.
  • The exercise aimed to improve interoperability, strengthen operational cooperation and deepen mutual understanding among the navies.
  • Training helped participants understand one another's procedures, capabilities and methods of operating at sea.
  • The exercise was intended to build trust and improve the navies' ability to work together on maritime challenges.
  • INS Tarkash's Commanding Officer addressed the closing ceremony and highlighted the role of naval exercises in translating diplomatic ties into practical cooperation.
  • The release described IBSAMAR as a trilateral maritime-cooperation exercise among countries located on three different continents.
  • The exercise supports cooperation for a safe, stable and open maritime environment and a rules-based maritime order.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
