Government Schemes Manufacturing 29 October 2026

Cabinet Approves Manufacturing and Panchayat Schemes

  • The Union Cabinet approved the Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme and the Khelo India Sports Goods Manufacturing Scheme.
  • The Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme has an outlay of ₹10,000 crore over 13 years, up to FY 2039–40.
  • The container scheme aims to build a globally competitive Indian container industry, reduce import dependence and strengthen maritime and logistics supply chains.
  • Its four support components are interest subvention, testing and certification, land leasing, and operational expenditure assistance.
  • The container scheme is expected to generate 3,000 direct jobs and over 50,000 indirect jobs, with support for related industries such as specialised steel and corner castings.
  • The Khelo India Sports Goods Manufacturing Scheme has an approved outlay of over ₹1,856 crore for 2026–27 to 2033–34.
  • The sports goods scheme envisages dedicated Sports Goods Manufacturing Zones (SGMZs) to encourage cluster-based manufacturing and integrated industrial ecosystems.
  • Its two broad approaches are incentives for manufacturing and interventions to build the wider ecosystem; Anchor Investment Support and Turnover Linked Support are key components.
  • The Cabinet also approved the Pradhan Mantri Viksit Gram Swaraj Yojana (PMVGSY) as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme for five years, from 2026–27 to 2030–31.
  • PMVGSY has a financial outlay of ₹7,395 crore and aims to strengthen Panchayats as empowered, self-reliant, accountable and high-performing local self-government institutions.
  • PMVGSY applies across States and Union Territories, including rural local governance institutions in non-Part IX areas where Panchayats do not exist, and is to cover over 2.78 lakh Rural Local Bodies.

Key Components

  • Container manufacturing support includes interest subvention, testing and certification, land leasing, and operational expenditure assistance.
  • Sports goods support includes Anchor Investment Support to attract manufacturers and Turnover Linked Support to encourage production and capacity expansion.
  • PMVGSY is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme focused on Rural Local Bodies and Panchayat-level governance.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: What is the outlay of the Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme? A: ₹10,000 crore.
  • Q: Up to which financial year does the container scheme run? A: FY 2039–40.
  • Q: What are the proposed Sports Goods Manufacturing Zones called? A: SGMZs.
  • Q: What are the two key named support components of the sports goods scheme? A: Anchor Investment Support and Turnover Linked Support.
  • Q: What is the full form of PMVGSY? A: Pradhan Mantri Viksit Gram Swaraj Yojana.
  • Q: What is the duration of PMVGSY? A: Five years, from 2026–27 to 2030–31.
  • Q: Which type of scheme is PMVGSY? A: A Centrally Sponsored Scheme.

Sports, Culture & Days Education and Sports 29 October 2026

MoU to Bring Clean Sport Values into Classrooms

  • The Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports, the Ministry of Education and UNESCO exchanged an MoU on the Sports Values in Every Classroom Toolkit.
  • The initiative aims to bring clean sport values into classrooms across India through education, sport and everyday classroom activities.
  • The toolkit seeks to foster respect, integrity and fairness among children.
  • The MoU was exchanged in the presence of Union Youth Affairs and Sports Minister Dr Mansukh Mandaviya.
  • Union Education Minister Pralhad Joshi was also present at the exchange.
  • The UNESCO Regional Office for South Asia was represented by its Director, Dr Tim Curtis.
  • Toolkit video modules are to be made available in 13 Indian languages to reach students across the country.
  • The initiative also aims to strengthen the link between education and sport and support a fairer sporting ecosystem.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date

Frequently Asked Questions

Which schemes did the Union Cabinet approve for manufacturing and rural governance?
The Cabinet approved the Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme and the Khelo India Sports Goods Manufacturing Scheme. It also approved PMVGSY as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme.
What is the aim of the Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme?
It aims to develop a globally competitive container manufacturing ecosystem in India. The scheme also seeks to reduce import dependence and strengthen maritime and logistics supply chains.
How long will the Khelo India Sports Goods Manufacturing Scheme run?
The scheme covers eight years, from 2026–27 to 2033–34. It includes dedicated Sports Goods Manufacturing Zones and support for investment and production.
What is PMVGSY and who does it cover?
PMVGSY is the Pradhan Mantri Viksit Gram Swaraj Yojana, a Centrally Sponsored Scheme running from 2026–27 to 2030–31. It applies across States and Union Territories and is intended to cover over 2.78 lakh Rural Local Bodies.
Who won the third India–Zimbabwe Women’s ODI?
India won by eight wickets in Vadodara and completed a 3–0 series sweep. Vaishnavi Sharma took five wickets and was named Player of the Match.
What is the Sports Values in Every Classroom Toolkit?
It is the focus of an MoU exchanged by the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports, the Ministry of Education and UNESCO. Its classroom activities aim to promote respect, integrity and fairness through sport and education.
Is current affairs enough to score well in SSC General Awareness?
No. Current affairs are important but static GK — History, Geography, Polity, Economics and Science — still carries the larger share of the General Awareness section. The most reliable approach is to read current affairs daily for about ten minutes and revise static GK chapter-wise alongside, using chapter-wise tests to check retention.
