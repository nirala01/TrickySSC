Government Schemes Census Rules 17 October 2026

Census (Amendment) Rules, 2026 Expand Enumerator Eligibility

  • The Ministry of Home Affairs issued a Gazette notification on the Census (Amendment) Rules, 2026.
  • The amended rules are made under the Census Act, 1948.
  • The changes revise who may be appointed as a census enumerator.
  • Eligible teachers now include staff from government, local authority, aided and unaided private educational institutions.
  • Teachers at institutions run by societies or trusts may also be appointed.
  • Clerks and other officials working in the Central and State Governments are included.
  • Employees of local authorities may serve as enumerators under the revised rules.
  • Eligible establishments also include PSUs, statutory bodies, banks, companies, institutions, societies and trusts.
  • State governments and Union Territory administrations may appoint other persons they consider suitable.

  • Q: Which ministry issued the Census (Amendment) Rules, 2026? A: Ministry of Home Affairs.
  • Q: Under which Act were the amended rules made? A: Census Act, 1948.
  • Q: What role does the amendment expand eligibility for? A: Census enumerator.
  • Q: Can teachers from unaided private institutions be enumerators? A: Yes.
  • Q: Can employees of local authorities be appointed as enumerators? A: Yes.
  • Q: Who may identify other suitable persons for appointment? A: The respective State governments or Union Territory administrations.

Economy & Banking Airfares 17 October 2026

Government Finalises Rules to Regulate Airfare Pricing

  • The Central Government informed the Supreme Court that rules to regulate airfare pricing had been finalised.
  • The rules have received administrative approval for publication.
  • The Government told the Court that publication was expected within two weeks.
  • The Court directed that the matter be listed again on 16 November.
  • The published rules are to be placed before the Court by the next hearing.
  • The case concerns a public interest litigation on airfare pricing by private airlines.
  • The petition raises concerns about steep and unpredictable fare increases, including dynamic pricing.
  • The proposed rules also address excessive passenger charges, including baggage fees.
  • The petitioner sought fair and transparent fares, particularly during festivals and periods of high demand.

Economy & Banking Forex Reserves 17 October 2026

India’s Forex Reserves Fall to 726 Billion US Dollars

  • India’s foreign exchange reserves declined by over US$8.5 billion in the week ended 9 October.
  • The reserves stood at US$726 billion at the end of that week.
  • The figures were reported in data from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).
  • Foreign currency assets, the largest reserve component, fell by US$7.1 billion to US$597.6 billion.
  • Gold reserves declined by US$1.3 billion to US$105 billion.
  • Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) fell by US$109 million to US$18.5 billion.
  • India’s reserve position with the International Monetary Fund declined by US$10 million.
  • The reserve position with the IMF stood at US$4.78 billion for the reported week.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body

Frequently Asked Questions

Who can be appointed as a census enumerator under the 2026 amendment?
Eligible people include teachers from government, local authority, aided and unaided private institutions, as well as government and local authority staff. The rules also include specified employees of establishments such as PSUs, banks, companies, societies and trusts.
When are the airfare pricing rules expected to be published?
The Central Government told the Supreme Court that publication was expected within two weeks. The Court directed that the published rules be placed before it by the next listing on 16 November.
Where did India win women’s Kabaddi gold at the 2026 Asian Games?
The Indian women’s Kabaddi team won gold at the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan. Union Minister for Railways Ashwini Vaishnaw met team members in New Delhi.
What is PM-SETU and how many ITI clusters were approved?
PM-SETU means Pradhan Mantri Skilling and Employability Transformation through Upgraded ITIs. Its sixth National Steering Committee meeting approved plans for five clusters across Maharashtra, Odisha and Jammu and Kashmir.
What were India’s foreign exchange reserves in the week ended 9 October?
The reserves stood at US$726 billion after declining by over US$8.5 billion during the week. Foreign currency assets were the largest component, at US$597.6 billion.
Who is India’s next Ambassador to Nepal?
Sudhakar Dalela was appointed as the next Ambassador of India to Nepal. He is a 1993-batch Indian Foreign Service officer and was serving as a Secretary in the Ministry of External Affairs.
Is current affairs enough to score well in SSC General Awareness?
No. Current affairs are important but static GK — History, Geography, Polity, Economics and Science — still carries the larger share of the General Awareness section.
