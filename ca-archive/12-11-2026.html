Cabinet Decisions Schemes and Metro 12 November 2026

Cabinet Extends Two Schemes and Approves Aqua Line Extension

  • The Union Cabinet approved continuing the Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units under PM Mudra Yojana up to 31 March 2031.
  • The Mudra credit-guarantee continuation has an approved outlay of ₹5,000 crore and is expected to support loans worth ₹12.60 lakh crore.
  • By September 2026, lending institutions had sanctioned over 60.36 crore Mudra loans amounting to ₹43.34 lakh crore since the scheme began.
  • The Cabinet also continued the Stand-Up India Scheme for five years, up to 31 March 2031.
  • Stand-Up India aims to provide financial assistance to an additional 2.5 lakh entrepreneurs over five years, focusing on women, Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
  • Since its launch in April 2016, Stand-Up India had facilitated loans to more than 2.75 lakh women, SC and ST entrepreneurs.
  • Stand-Up India provides loans from ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore to eligible women, SC and ST entrepreneurs; its credit-guarantee corpus is ₹4,111 crore for a four-year scheme duration.
  • The Cabinet approved an extension of the Noida Metro Aqua Line from Noida Sector 51 to Greater Noida Sector 04.
  • The approved metro extension is approximately 8.5 km long and will have five elevated stations.
  • Once operational, the extension is expected to take the active Metro Rail Network in Noida and Greater Noida to around 72 km.

Cabinet Decisions Fertiliser Subsidy 12 November 2026

Cabinet Approves Nutrient-Based Subsidy for Rabi 2026–27

  • The Union Cabinet approved Nutrient Based Subsidy (NBS) rates for phosphatic and potassic fertilisers for the Rabi 2026–27 season.
  • The approved rates apply from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027.
  • The tentative budgetary requirement for the season is approximately ₹62,370 crore.
  • The requirement is about ₹24,417 crore higher than the budgetary requirement for Rabi 2025–26.
  • NBS support is intended to help ensure fertilisers covered by the decision remain available to farmers at affordable prices.
  • The Cabinet decision took account of recent international price trends for fertilisers and inputs.
  • The inputs specifically mentioned in the announcement include urea, DAP and sulphur.
  • The decision concerns phosphatic and potassic fertilisers; the announcement does not provide individual subsidy rates for each fertiliser.

  • Q: Which Union ministry includes the Department of Fertilisers? A: Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilisers.

Defence Coastal Security 12 November 2026

Sea Vigil-26 Assesses India’s Coastal Defence Preparedness

  • The fifth edition of the pan-India Coastal Defence Exercise Sea Vigil-26 began on 12 November 2026 and runs until 19 November.
  • The exercise covers India’s entire coastline and Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ).
  • All coastal States and Union Territories are participating, alongside other maritime stakeholders.
  • Fishing and coastal communities are included among the stakeholders involved in the exercise.
  • The Indian Navy is coordinating Sea Vigil-26 as the lead agency.
  • The exercise has two phases: a Coastal Defence and Security Readiness Evaluation phase, followed by a Sea Phase.
  • The readiness-evaluation phase runs from 12 to 17 November; the Sea Phase takes place on 18 and 19 November.
  • Sea Vigil was conceptualised in 2018 to validate maritime security measures strengthened after the 26/11 Mumbai terror attacks.
  • The national-level exercise provides an opportunity to assess preparedness for maritime security and coastal defence.

International Relations Naval Exercise 12 November 2026

India and Oman Hold 15th Naseem Al Bahr Naval Exercise

  • The Indian Navy and the Royal Navy of Oman held the 15th bilateral exercise Naseem Al Bahr, also called Sea Breeze.
  • The exercise aimed to improve coordination, professional cooperation and mutual understanding between the two navies.
  • During the harbour phase, specialist teams held Subject Matter Expert Exchanges (SMEE).
  • Harbour-phase discussions covered Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD), locating and neutralising sea mines, and hydrography.
  • The sea phase included joint operational activities by Indian Navy ship INS Surat and Omani ship Al-Muazzar.
  • Activities included joint visits, Visit, Board, Search and Seizure (VBSS) drills, and cross-deck operations.
  • The drills gave personnel from both navies opportunities to practise coordinated procedures and improve operational interoperability.
  • The exercise supports the maritime partnership between India and Oman and strengthens naval cooperation with regional partners.

International Relations Naval Exercise 12 November 2026

Eighth India–Myanmar Naval Exercise Begins in Visakhapatnam

  • IMNEX-26 is the eighth India–Myanmar naval exercise and is being held in Visakhapatnam from 10 to 14 November 2026.
  • The Myanmar Navy ship UMS King Sin Phyu Shin arrived at Visakhapatnam on 10 November to participate.
  • Indian Navy ships INS Rana and INS Sumedha are also participating in the exercise.
  • The opening ceremony took place aboard INS Rana on 10 November, marking the start of the bilateral exercise.
  • IMNEX provides a platform for the neighbouring navies to advance maritime cooperation and share professional expertise.
  • The harbour phase includes professional interactions, Subject Matter Expert Exchanges (SMEE), ship visits, sports and operational discussions.
  • The subsequent sea phase includes coordinated maritime operations and tactical manoeuvres.
  • The activities are intended to improve mutual understanding and operational coordination between participating units.
  • The exercise reflects India–Myanmar maritime cooperation and aligns with India’s MAHASAGAR vision for regional security and development.

Environment & Ecology Blue Flag Beaches 12 November 2026

Three Maharashtra Beaches Receive Blue Flag Recognition

  • Ladghar and Guhagar beaches in Ratnagiri district, and Shrivardhan beach in Raigad district, received the International Blue Flag award for 2026–27.
  • This is the first time Maharashtra has received international recognition under the Blue Flag programme.
  • The beaches met standards covering environmental conservation, cleanliness, water quality and biodiversity protection.
  • Other stated criteria include tourist safety, infrastructure and sustainable tourism management.
  • The Blue Flag season for the three beaches is scheduled to begin on 15 November 2026.
  • The official flags are being brought from the Netherlands, after which a flag-hoisting ceremony is to be organised.
  • The recognition followed coordinated work by Maharashtra’s Environment and Climate Change Department and Tourism Department.
  • District administrations, local bodies and local communities also contributed to the effort.
  • The state plans to work towards bringing other eligible beaches up to international standards and promoting sustainable tourism.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
