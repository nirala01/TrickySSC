Economy & Banking Census 31 October 2026

Population Census to Begin on 25 January 2027

  • Population census is scheduled across the country from 25 January to 28 February 2027, according to the notification issued by the Ministry of Home Affairs.
  • The announced schedule excludes Manipur, Goa, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Uttarakhand, Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh and Jammu and Kashmir.
  • The excluded areas are described in the report as snow-bound and poll-bound states or areas.
  • Self-enumeration will be available from 10 to 24 January 2027, before the main census period.
  • A revisional round is scheduled for 1 to 5 March 2027.
  • The census covers states and Union Territories, subject to the listed exclusions.
  • The notification was issued by the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA).
  • The scheduled census period runs for 35 calendar days, from 25 January through 28 February.

Appointments Defence 31 October 2026

Inderpal Singh Walia Appointed IAF Vice Chief

  • Air Marshal Inderpal Singh Walia is appointed Vice Chief of the Air Staff (VCAS) from 1 November 2026.
  • At the time of the announcement, he was Air Officer Commanding-in-Chief of the Eastern Air Command.
  • Walia is a National Defence Academy alumnus and joined the Indian Air Force’s fighter stream on 11 June 1988.
  • He has flown the MiG-21, MiG-23, MiG-27, Jaguar and Sukhoi-30MKI aircraft.
  • His command appointments included a frontline fighter squadron and the Tactics and Air Combat Development Establishment (TACDE).
  • He also served as Air Officer Commanding at Air Force Station Bareilly and as Senior Air Staff Officer at Eastern Air Command.
  • His other assignments included heading the Directorate of Air Staff Inspection and serving as defence attaché at Indian missions in Japan and South Korea.
  • Walia received the Vayu Sena Medal (VM) in 2008 and the Ati Vishisht Seva Medal (AVSM) in 2018.
  • He holds three postgraduate degrees in defence and strategic studies and completed advanced command and staff training in the United Kingdom and training at Bangladesh’s National Defence College.
  • He succeeds Air Marshal Ashutosh Dixit; the release said Dixit would assume charge as the IAF’s 29th Chief on 31 October 2026.

Sports, Culture & Days Wrestling 31 October 2026

Manisha Bhanwala Wins Historic Wrestling World Gold

  • Manisha Bhanwala became the first Indian woman to win gold at the Wrestling World Championships.
  • The championships were held in Astana, Kazakhstan.
  • Manisha won the women’s 59 kg final against American wrestler Abigail Nette by 8–6.
  • She recovered from a 0–6 deficit to claim the title.
  • The victory made her only the second Indian wrestler to win a world championship title.
  • The previous Indian world champion was Sushil Kumar, who won the men’s 66 kg gold in 2010.
  • Manisha reached the final after defeating Kazakhstan’s Altyn Shagayeva in the semifinal.
  • For the first time, three Indian women reached gold-medal finals in the same edition of the championships.
  • Neelam Sirohi, in the 50 kg category, and Kajal Dhochak, in the 76 kg category, won silver medals.
  • Tanmu Sharma also secured a bronze medal at the championships.

Economy & Banking Banking Reforms 31 October 2026

High-Level Committee Formed to Review Banking Sector

  • The Government of India constituted a High-Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat to review India’s banking sector.
  • The committee is tasked with recommending reforms to make banking globally competitive, financially inclusive and supportive of Viksit Bharat 2047.
  • Its formation had been announced by Union Finance and Corporate Affairs Minister Nirmala Sitharaman in the Union Budget 2026–27.
  • Former Central Vigilance Commissioner Suresh N. Patel is the committee’s chairperson.
  • Members named in the announcement are RBI Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu, former Indian Bank MD and CEO Padmaja Chunduru, and former RBI Deputy Governor N. S. Vishwanathan.
  • The Joint Secretary (Banking) in the Department of Financial Services is the committee’s member-secretary.
  • The committee has a six-month tenure.
  • Its terms include assessing banking practices and prudential norms, identifying global best practices and recommending suitable approaches for India.
  • It will review regulatory and legal frameworks covering banks, including Regional Rural Banks and cooperative banks, and assess long-term capital needs including those of development finance institutions and NBFCs.
  • Its remit also includes AI and digital innovation, cyber-risk safeguards, workforce capability, and review of priority-sector lending and the Lead Bank Scheme.

Key Areas of Review

  • The committee will consider capital needs, credit growth, cost of capital and ways to support private investment.
  • It will also examine financial inclusion, data-based credit delivery, digital innovation and banking-sector skills.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: Who chairs the High-Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat? A: Suresh N. Patel.
  • Q: Which minister announced the committee in the Union Budget 2026–27? A: Nirmala Sitharaman.
  • Q: What is the committee’s tenure? A: Six months.
  • Q: Which central bank’s deputy governor is a committee member? A: Reserve Bank of India Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu.
  • Q: Which two types of banks are specifically included in its regulatory review? A: Regional Rural Banks and cooperative banks.
  • Q: Which lending framework will the committee review? A: Priority-sector lending.
  • Q: What technology-related areas are included in its remit? A: AI, digital innovation and cyber-risk safeguards.

Economy & Banking Poverty and Employment 31 October 2026

India’s Extreme Poverty Falls to 2.6% by 2023

  • Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman cited a fall in India’s extreme poverty rate from 27.1% in 2011 to 2.6% in 2023.
  • The figures were highlighted in the World Bank’s October 2026 Poverty and Equity report.
  • The report described India’s poverty reduction as one of the fastest in the world.
  • Over 49 crore people moved above the lower-middle-income poverty line during 2011–2023, according to the minister’s post.
  • Employment in India increased by 13.4 crore people between 2017 and 2023.
  • The reported employment increase was nearly twice the increase in the working-age population over that period.
  • Rural women accounted for much of the employment gain, while rural and urban households both experienced welfare gains.
  • Access to electricity reached 99% of the population, as cited by the Finance Minister.
  • The World Bank report identified strong consumption growth, rising employment—especially among women—and expanded social transfers, including food subsidies, as key drivers.

Science & Technology Broadcasting 31 October 2026

Prasar Bharati Engineer Wins ABU Award 2026

  • Nongmaithem Nandakumar Singh of Prasar Bharati won the ABU Broadcast Engineering Excellence Award 2026.
  • The award recognised his contribution to broadcast technology and innovation.
  • His innovations include DDPlayout, indigenous automated playout software used by multiple Doordarshan Kendras.
  • He also developed DDSlowMo, a cost-effective slow-motion replay system used at national-level sporting events.
  • The award was presented by the Asia-Pacific Broadcasting Union (ABU), a regional organisation of broadcasting and media organisations.
  • The 63rd ABU General Assembly and Associated Meetings are being held in Colombo from 30 October to 3 November 2026.
  • The assembly’s theme is “Media for Peace and Humanity: Truth, Trust, and Transformation”.
  • The five-day gathering includes technical, programme and sports group meetings, as well as the Broadcasting for All Forum, Media Leaders’ Forum and Super Panel.
  • ABU brings together around 224 broadcasting and media organisations across 66 countries, according to the report.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
