Defence Indigenous Aviation 18 September 2026

HAL Delivers LCA Tejas Trainers, HTT-40 and Dhruv NG Helicopters

  • Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL) handed over two LCA Tejas FOC two-seater trainers and three HTT-40 basic trainers to the Indian Air Force (IAF).
  • HAL also handed over four Dhruv Next Generation (NG) helicopters to public sector helicopter operator Pawan Hans Limited (PHL).
  • The handover ceremony took place in Bengaluru, Karnataka, in the presence of Defence Minister Rajnath Singh and Civil Aviation Minister K. Rammohan Naidu.
  • The HTT-40 is an indigenously manufactured basic trainer aircraft designed to meet stage-1 pilot training needs, replacing foreign imports.
  • Defence Minister highlighted that induction of Dhruv-NG into civil aviation and LCA Tejas FOC trainers into the IAF reflects strong civil-military aerospace synergy.

100-Week 'Nasha Mukt Yuva Abhiyaan' Launched Across the Country

  • The Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports launched the 100-week 'Nasha Mukt Yuva Abhiyaan' across the nation on 18 September 2026.

Government Schemes Social Welfare 18 September 2026

Over ₹1,089 Crore Disbursed under Senior Citizen Schemes of AVYAY

  • The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment released data detailing the impact of the Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana (AVYAY) umbrella scheme.
  • A total of ₹1089.58 crore has been disbursed across the country under IPSrC and SAPSrC components.
  • Under the Integrated Programme for Senior Citizens (IPSrC), over 10.76 lakh senior citizens received support between FY 2014-15 and FY 2025-26 with an expenditure of ₹953.53 crore.
  • Under the State Action Plan for Senior Citizens (SAPSrC), launched in FY 2019-20, ₹136.05 crore has been released to States and UTs for customized local interventions.
  • Key pillars of AVYAY include shelter, food, healthcare, assistive living devices (RVY), geriatric caregiver training, and the Elderline national toll-free helpline.

Defence Joint Warfare 18 September 2026

Joint Operations Review and Evaluation (CORE) Programme 2026 Concludes

  • The Joint Operations Review and Evaluation (CORE) Programme 2026 concluded at the Manekshaw Centre in New Delhi.
  • The five-day programme, held from 14 to 18 September 2026, was organised by Headquarters Integrated Defence Staff (HQ IDS).
  • The academic and operational proceedings were conducted by the Centre for Joint Warfare Studies (CENJOWS).

International Relations Capacity Building 18 September 2026

5th ITEC Capacity-Building Programme on Human Rights Concludes in Delhi

  • The 5th ITEC Executive Capacity Building Programme on Human Rights for National Human Rights Institutions (NHRIs) of the Global South concluded in New Delhi.
  • The six-day capacity-building initiative was organised by the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) in collaboration with the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA).
  • A total of 38 senior delegates from 14 NHRIs across the Global South participated in the programme, which commenced on 14 September 2026.
  • Participating nations included Algeria, Chile, Ecuador, Ethiopia, Indonesia, Kazakhstan, Kenya, Kyrgyzstan, Malaysia, Oman, Samoa, South Africa, Tajikistan, and Zimbabwe.
  • NHRC Chairperson Justice V. Ramasubramanian addressed the valedictory session, describing human rights institutions as operating within a dynamic 'laboratory of human rights'.
  • Through this joint initiative, approximately 200 participants from 46 countries have received training across five editions.

Environment & Ecology Coastal Clean-up 18 September 2026

Union Minister Leads 'Swachh Sagar, Surakshit Sagar' Drive at Juhu Beach

  • Union Minister of State (IC) for Earth Sciences Dr. Jitendra Singh led a coastal clean-up drive at Juhu Beach, Mumbai.
  • The drive was held on the eve of International Coastal Cleanup Day, which is observed globally on the third Saturday of September.
  • The 2026 edition of the 'Swachh Sagar, Surakshit Sagar' campaign is being observed nationwide from 12 to 19 September 2026.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Frequently Asked Questions

What aircraft and helicopters did HAL deliver on 18 September 2026?
HAL handed over two LCA Tejas FOC two-seater trainers and three HTT-40 basic trainers to the Indian Air Force. Additionally, four Dhruv Next Generation (NG) helicopters were delivered to Pawan Hans Limited for civilian operations.
What is the Nasha Mukt Yuva Abhiyaan launched by the Sports Ministry?
It is a 100-week nationwide campaign organised with MY Bharat-NSS aimed at mobilizing Indian youth against drug abuse. The campaign began with the 'Rashtriya Sankalp Samaroh' and features weekly themed activities across 100 Sundays.
What are the key components of the Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana (AVYAY)?
AVYAY includes the Integrated Programme for Senior Citizens (IPSrC), State Action Plan for Senior Citizens (SAPSrC), Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana (RVY), and the Elderline national toll-free helpline for elderly welfare and care.
Where was the 17th Kathakali Festival held and what was its special performance?
The 17th Kathakali Festival was held at Kalakshetra Foundation in Chennai under the theme 'Katha Rasa Sangamam'. A major highlight was Kerala Kalamandalam's Kathakali adaptation of Ernest Hemingway's novel 'The Old Man and the Sea'.
What was the objective of the CORE Programme 2026 conducted in New Delhi?
The Combined Operational Review and Evaluation (CORE) Programme 2026, held at Manekshaw Centre, aimed to enhance tri-service integration, joint operational review, and readiness in domains like cognitive warfare, AI, and strategic communications.
When is International Coastal Cleanup Day celebrated?
International Coastal Cleanup Day is observed worldwide on the third Saturday of September. In India, it is marked through the 'Swachh Sagar, Surakshit Sagar' coastal clean-up campaign led by the Ministry of Earth Sciences.
Is current affairs enough to score well in SSC General Awareness?
No. Current affairs are important but static GK — History, Geography, Polity, Economics and Science — still carries the larger share of the General Awareness section. The most reliable approach is to read current affairs daily for about ten minutes and revise static GK chapter-wise alongside, using chapter-wise tests to check retention.
