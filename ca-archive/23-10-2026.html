Environment & Ecology Agriculture 23 October 2026

Khet Bachao Campaign Launched from Haryana

  • Union Agriculture and Farmers’ Welfare Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan launched the nationwide Khet Bachao Abhiyaan from Tarawadi in Karnal district, Haryana.
  • The nationwide awareness campaign is scheduled to run from 22 October to 30 November 2026.
  • Agricultural science centres and other agriculture-related institutions joined the launch virtually, along with farmers, scientists and officials.
  • The campaign promotes balanced use of chemical fertilisers and pesticides to protect soil health and human health.
  • Farmers were advised to apply nutrients according to their Soil Health Card, using only the nutrients the soil requires.
  • The Minister urged farmers to diversify beyond a repeated paddy–wheat cycle by including pulses such as chickpea, lentil and pigeon pea.
  • Farmers were encouraged to begin natural farming on at least half an acre, using preparations such as Beejamrit and Ghanjeevamrit.
  • The campaign also calls for avoiding stubble burning, which harms soil organisms, beneficial insects and earthworms.
  • Chouhan said the government was bearing more than ₹1.5 lakh crore in fertiliser subsidies; the release cited retail prices of ₹266 for a urea bag and ₹1,350 for DAP.
  • At the event, Chouhan handed Haryana’s Agriculture Minister Shyam Singh Rana an approval letter for 28,422 rural houses under Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana–Gramin, with ₹1,343.06 crore approved.

Campaign at a glance

Launch locationTarawadi, Karnal district, Haryana
Campaign period22 October–30 November 2026
Lead ministryMinistry of Agriculture and Farmers’ Welfare
Key focusSoil health, balanced fertiliser use and natural farming
Housing approval announced28,422 PMAY-G houses for eligible families in Haryana

Soil-protection measures

  • Use the Soil Health Card to guide fertiliser application and maintain balanced soil nutrition.
  • Include pulses in crop rotation; pulse roots can fix nitrogen and support soil fertility.
  • Avoid stubble burning to protect beneficial soil organisms, insects and earthworms.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: Where was the Khet Bachao Abhiyaan launched? A: Tarawadi, Karnal district, Haryana.
  • Q: Who launched the campaign? A: Union Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan.
  • Q: What was the campaign period announced at the launch? A: 22 October to 30 November 2026.
  • Q: Which card should guide farmers’ fertiliser use? A: The Soil Health Card.
  • Q: Which farming practice was recommended alongside crop rotation? A: Natural farming.
  • Q: Which scheme covered the rural-house approval announced for Haryana? A: Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana–Gramin (PMAY-G).
  • Q: Which state received approval for 28,422 rural houses at the event? A: Haryana.

Source: PIB release

Environment & Ecology Waste Management 23 October 2026

India Has Sector-Specific Rules for Nine Waste Streams

  • Union Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav said India has introduced sector-specific rules for nine waste streams.
  • The rules were discussed at the Circular Economy Conference in New Delhi.
  • The regulated streams include plastic waste, battery waste and electronic waste (e-waste).
  • The framework covers waste prevention, recycling, reuse, remanufacturing and resource recovery across a product’s lifecycle.
  • The Minister reported that around 500 lakh metric tonnes of waste had been processed across regulated waste streams.
  • He called for stronger coordination between producers and recyclers to address concerns about recyclable material availability and recycling capacity.
  • The Minister highlighted recovery of critical minerals from e-waste and batteries as an area requiring greater attention.
  • He also called for industry participation in developing secondary raw-material markets and improving supply-chain traceability.

Conference venueNew Delhi

Circular-economy focus

  • The stated approach spans waste prevention through resource recovery across the product lifecycle.
  • The Minister identified critical-mineral recovery and secondary raw-material markets as areas for stronger industry involvement.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: How many waste streams have sector-specific rules, according to the report? A: Nine.
  • Q: Which ministry is led by Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav? A: The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change.
  • Q: Which three waste types were specifically mentioned as examples? A: Plastic waste, battery waste and e-waste.
  • Q: Where was the Circular Economy Conference held? A: New Delhi.
  • Q: What resource recovery did the Minister highlight for e-waste and batteries? A: Recovery of critical minerals.
  • Q: What is the broad lifecycle approach described for the rules? A: Waste prevention, recycling, reuse, remanufacturing and resource recovery.

Science & Technology Road Safety 23 October 2026

Draft Proposal Seeks AEBS for New Car Models from 2030

  • The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH) has proposed making Advanced Emergency Braking Systems (AEBS) mandatory for all new car models from October 2030.
  • The proposal was set out in a draft notification; it is a proposed requirement, not a rule already in force.
  • For car models already in production before 2030, the draft proposes mandatory AEBS from October 2032.
  • AEBS can apply automatic braking if a driver does not respond to an emergency situation.
  • The system is intended to help avoid a collision or reduce its impact.
  • A revised braking standard for trucks is scheduled to take effect from 1 October 2027.
  • Electronic stability control systems are also to be mandatory for trucks from 1 October 2027.
  • Electronic stability control is intended to improve vehicle stability, particularly when braking on curves.

Proposed and scheduled measures

Pre-2030 in-production modelsAEBS proposed from October 2032
Revised truck braking standardEffective 1 October 2027
Truck electronic stability controlMandatory from 1 October 2027
Responsible ministryMinistry of Road Transport and Highways

System purpose

  • AEBS provides automatic braking when the driver fails to respond to an emergency.
  • Electronic stability control supports stability, including during braking on curves.

Important Facts for Exams

  • Q: What is the full form of AEBS? A: Advanced Emergency Braking Systems.
  • Q: Which ministry proposed AEBS for new car models? A: Ministry of Road Transport and Highways.
  • Q: From when is AEBS proposed for all new car models? A: October 2030.
  • Q: When is AEBS proposed for pre-2030 in-production models? A: October 2032.
  • Q: What does AEBS do if a driver fails to respond to an emergency? A: It can apply automatic braking.
  • Q: From when is the revised truck braking standard scheduled to apply? A: 1 October 2027.

International Relations Counter-Terrorism 23 October 2026

India and Uzbekistan Hold Tenth Counter-Terrorism Meeting

  • India and Uzbekistan held the tenth meeting of their Joint Working Group on Counter Terrorism in New Delhi.
  • The two countries agreed to strengthen cooperation against national and regional security threats.
  • They discussed threats posed by terrorist groups globally and in their respective regions.
  • The discussions covered countering extremism and radicalisation and combating the financing of terrorism.
  • The two sides also addressed the prevention of technology being used for terrorist purposes.
  • They considered the links between transnational organised crime and terrorism.
  • Proposed areas for stronger bilateral cooperation included information sharing, capacity building and sharing best practices.
  • Both sides reaffirmed cooperation in multilateral forums including the United Nations, SCO-RATS and EAG.
  • The meeting was co-chaired by representatives of India’s Ministry of External Affairs and Uzbekistan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

Government Schemes Drug Awareness 23 October 2026

Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan Reaches Over 35 Crore People

  • The Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan has reached more than 35 crore people across India, according to the report.
  • The campaign operates under the National Action Plan for Drug Demand Reduction.
  • It was launched in 2020 and initially focused on 272 vulnerable districts.
  • The campaign has since expanded its reach across the country.
  • Its reported outreach includes more than 13.94 crore youth and 10.86 crore women.
  • More than 32 lakh educational institutions have been engaged through the campaign.
  • The initiative emphasises youth, women and communities in preventing substance use and promoting healthy lifestyles.
  • The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment highlighted community participation and collective responsibility as part of drug-demand-reduction efforts.

  • Q: Under which national plan does the campaign operate? A: National Action Plan for Drug Demand Reduction.
  • Q: Which ministry is associated with the campaign? A: Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.
