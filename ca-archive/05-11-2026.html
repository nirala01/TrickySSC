Cabinet Decisions Statistical System 5 November 2026

Cabinet Approves VISTAR and Critical Mineral Processing Schemes

  • The Union Cabinet approved the VISTAR Scheme for 2026–27 to 2030–31, with an outlay of over ₹4,371 crore.
  • VISTAR stands for Viksit Bharat Initiative for Statistical Transformation, Analytics & Research.
  • The scheme covers statistical and data-management activities of the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI).
  • VISTAR aims to modernise India’s official statistical system through more timely, granular and interoperable data.
  • Its planned work includes large-scale surveys, methodological research, digital transformation and national data governance.
  • The Cabinet also approved the Capex Support Scheme to Promote Critical Mineral Processing.
  • The critical-mineral scheme has a financial outlay of ₹2,814 crore over five years.
  • It will provide capital-expenditure support to establish and operationalise domestic critical-mineral processing facilities.
  • The scheme addresses risks from processing being concentrated in a few global locations, including supply disruption and price volatility.
  • Domestic processing is intended to support supply-chain resilience, economic security and decarbonisation targets.

Cabinet Decisions Agnipath 5 November 2026

Cabinet Approves Two-Year Extension for Agniveers

  • The Union Cabinet approved an additional two-year engagement period for Agniveers who were previously due to leave after four years.
  • The extension applies to the 75% of Agniveers who were earlier set to exit after their initial service period.
  • After the extra two years, Agniveers who are willing to continue may be enrolled in the Armed Forces.
  • The report also states that 25% of Agniveers are to be enrolled in the regular forces on merit.
  • The extended engagement will come with higher remuneration, according to the report.
  • The first batch of Agniveers was nearing completion of its original four-year tenure.
  • The Army’s feedback on Agniveers’ performance over the four-year period was reported as positive.
  • Chief of Defence Staff General N. S. Raja Subramani briefed the media after the Cabinet meeting in New Delhi.
  • The Agnipath Scheme was introduced on 14 June 2022.
  • Recruitment under Agnipath has expanded to almost all districts across the country, and the process was described as more transparent and streamlined.

International Relations US Elections 5 November 2026

Democrats Win Control of Both Houses in US Midterms

  • The Democratic Party secured control of both chambers of the US Congress in the 2026 midterm elections.
  • Democrats won at least 228 seats in the 435-member House of Representatives, above the 218-seat majority mark.
  • In the Senate, Democrats won 54 seats and Republicans won 44, with two seats still undecided in the reported results.
  • The results ended Republican control of both chambers, according to the report.
  • Democratic gains included traditionally Republican states such as Texas, South Carolina, Kansas, Iowa and Ohio.
  • The report identified inflation and rising fuel prices among the key economic campaign issues.
  • The prolonged US-Israeli war with Iran and its effects on global energy supplies also featured as a campaign issue.
  • Control of both chambers gives Democrats greater scope to scrutinise the administration, investigate and block proposals or presidential nominations.
  • President Donald Trump remained in office and retained executive powers, including the ability to veto legislation.

Appointments Election Commission 5 November 2026

Supreme Court Seeks Replies on Election Commissioners’ Immunity

  • The Supreme Court of India sought responses from the Centre and the Election Commission of India on a petition challenging legal immunity for election commissioners.
  • The petition challenges Section 16 of the Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023.
  • Section 16 bars civil and criminal proceedings for acts performed by the CEC and Election Commissioners in the discharge of official duties.
  • The petitioner argued that the immunity is blanket in scope and has no exception for acts allegedly committed without good faith.
  • The plea said voters, candidates and citizens could be left without legal recourse against alleged wrongful or mala fide actions.
  • The petitioner argued that safeguards against frivolous complaints and prior judicial scrutiny could protect commissioners without fully barring legal remedies.
  • The notice was issued by a bench comprising Chief Justice of India Surya Kant, Justice Joymalya Bagchi and Justice V. Mohana.
  • The Court’s notice sought responses; the report did not state that the Court had ruled on the provision’s constitutional validity.

Science & Technology 6G 5 November 2026

Silicon Chip Demonstrates Low-Loss Terahertz Transmission

  • Researchers at the Jawaharlal Nehru Centre for Advanced Scientific Research (JNCASR) developed a silicon photonic-crystal chip for terahertz signals.
  • JNCASR is an autonomous institute of the Department of Science and Technology (DST).
  • The chip demonstrated data transmission at up to 100 gigabits per second (Gbps) across a deliberately created obstacle.
  • The demonstration could help develop compact terahertz circuits for future sixth-generation (6G) wireless networks.
  • The tested frequency range was 310–360 GHz; terahertz frequencies lie between microwaves and infrared light.
  • The photonic crystal used carefully patterned triangular holes in a thin silicon layer, with a structure inspired by graphene’s honeycomb lattice.
  • Adding one obstacle caused about 1–2% signal loss; two obstacles in sequence caused about 2–3% loss.
  • In high-speed tests, the double-obstacle and omega-shaped devices transmitted at 48 Gbps and 60 Gbps, respectively.
  • A power splitter supported 100 Gbps using QAM-32 modulation, which encodes information through signal amplitude and phase.
  • The research was published in the journal *Advanced Materials* and was led by JNCASR assistant professor Abhishek Kumar.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.
