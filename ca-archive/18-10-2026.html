Economy & Banking Drug Pricing 18 October 2026

NPPA Rationalises Margins on 137 Anti-Cancer Drugs

  • NPPA undertook Trade Margin Rationalisation (TMR) for 137 identified non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs in the public interest.
  • The measure aims to curb excessive margins in distribution and retail while making cancer medicines more affordable and accessible.
  • The revised prices are scheduled to take effect from 9 November 2026.
  • The MRP of the identified medicines may fall by up to 70%, depending on their existing price structures.
  • Retailers and hospitals must charge the reduced prices and display the revised prices in their pharmacies.
  • Consumers can check revised drug prices through the Pharma Sahi Daam app and the NPPA website.
  • State Drug Controllers are to help ensure compliance by manufacturers, dealers, retailers, hospitals and medical institutions.
  • The measure builds on NPPA’s 2019 pilot, which capped trade margins at 30% for 42 anti-cancer drugs.
  • Following the 2019 exercise, the MRP of 526 brands of non-scheduled anti-cancer medicines was reduced by up to 90%.
  • The intervention seeks to control excessive margins while protecting legitimate manufacturer revenues and avoiding artificial shortages.

Economy & Banking Space 18 October 2026

India’s Space Economy May Reach $40–45 Billion

  • Dr Jitendra Singh, Minister of State for Atomic Energy and Space, said India’s space economy is valued at around $9 billion.
  • He projected that it could grow to $40–45 billion over the next seven to eight years.
  • The remarks were made at the Bloomberg New Economy Forum’s media roundtable on Space, Sovereignty and New Economy in New Delhi.
  • The Minister said higher domestic and international investment would support the sector’s expansion.
  • He highlighted faster regulatory approvals, lower business costs and stronger supply-chain integration as growth needs.
  • The opening of India’s space sector to private participation had produced significant results within five to six years, he said.
  • The stated priority is to turn technical capabilities into commercially viable companies able to compete globally.
  • The Minister stressed that generating demand is important for the growth of private space companies.
  • He said international partnerships and capital can support technology development, manufacturing and access to overseas markets.
  • The remarks also linked domestic space capabilities with national security and strategic autonomy.

Government Schemes Public Grievance 18 October 2026

Supreme Court Panel Opens Portal for July Violence Complaints

  • The High Powered Enquiry Committee (HPEC), constituted by the Supreme Court, launched a portal for complaints and representations.
  • The committee is examining incidents of violence at Jantar Mantar in Delhi and at places outside Delhi during July 2026.
  • The portal is hpec.nic.in and is intended for affected people, including affected police personnel, and other stakeholders.
  • The HPEC is to examine the issues specified in the Supreme Court’s order.
  • Priority matters include the use of pellet guns, targeted violence and harassment of female protestors, and excessive violence and property destruction on either side.
  • The portal allows vulnerable witnesses to receive protection and affected people to keep their identities confidential if they wish.
  • Complaints and supporting material may also be submitted physically at the HPEC office at Shiksha Sadan, New Delhi.
  • Physical submissions are accepted on working days from 2 pm to 4 pm; submissions may also be sent by post or email.
  • The Ministry of Home Affairs said people may use the portal to provide relevant information or suggestions as well as complaints.

Sports, Culture & Days Handicrafts 18 October 2026

Shilp Samagam Mela 2026 Runs in Mysuru

  • The Shilp Samagam Mela 2026 is being held at the Mysore Dasara Exhibition Grounds in Mysuru, Karnataka.
  • The nine-day fair began on 11 October and is scheduled to conclude on 19 October 2026.
  • The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment is organising the fair for artisans and beneficiaries from SC, OBC and sanitation-worker communities.
  • The exhibition has 83 stalls, of which 80 are allotted to artisans; 37 women artisans are among those allotted stalls.
  • Artisans from 12 states and Union Territories are taking part in the fair.
  • The event is jointly organised by NSFDC, NBCFDC and NSKFDC, three national corporations under the Ministry.
  • Products on display include handicrafts, handloom saris, spices, leather goods, wooden toys, terracotta items and block-printed goods.
  • The fair aims to strengthen market connections, promote traditional crafts and support sustainable livelihoods through direct customer access.
  • The Mysuru fair is the first Shilp Samagam Mela of financial year 2026–27.
  • The Ministry has organised Shilp Samagam fairs since 2022; 13 fairs had benefited 975 artisans, according to the release.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:
