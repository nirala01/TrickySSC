International Relations Haj Agreement 11 November 2026

India Secures 1,75,025 Pilgrim Quota for Haj 2027

  • India secured a quota of 1,75,025 pilgrims under the Bilateral Haj Agreement for 2027.
  • The agreement was signed by Union Minority Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju and Saudi Arabia’s Minister of Hajj and Umrah, Tawfiq bin Fawzan Al-Rabiah.
  • The two ministers reviewed Haj 2027 preparations and operational priorities for Indian pilgrims.
  • Their discussions covered arrangements aimed at a safe, smooth and well-organised pilgrimage.
  • Rijiju visited Saudi Arabia to attend the 6th Haj Conference and Exhibition 2027 and sign the bilateral agreement.
  • He arrived in Jeddah for the conference and agreement-signing programme.
  • Rijiju also attended a dinner hosted by Saudi Arabia’s Minister of Hajj and Umrah.
  • During the visit, Rijiju celebrated Diwali with members of the Indian diaspora and Saudi guests at a dinner hosted by Indian Consul General Fahad Suri.

Science & Technology Cybercrime 11 November 2026

Supreme Court Seeks Steps to Curb Digital Arrest Scams

  • The Supreme Court asked WhatsApp and other intermediaries to outline measures to prevent digital arrest scams and related cybercrimes.
  • The intermediaries were asked to submit their proposals to the Court and the Indian Cybercrime Coordination Centre (I4C).
  • The matter is a suo motu case titled Victims of Digital Arrest Related to Forged Documents.
  • The Court’s order followed a hearing held on 29 October.
  • WhatsApp told the Court that it had taken preventive action against 9,400 accounts and would file an affidavit detailing its steps.
  • The Court said financial institutions must be held accountable for digital arrest scams and other cyber frauds.
  • It said banks should compensate victims if an investigation finds that fraud resulted from the institution’s failure to exercise due diligence.
  • Law enforcement agencies in all States and Union Territories were directed to report FIRs registered in digital arrest cases and the resulting investigations.
  • The Court listed the matter for further consideration on 23 November.

Government Schemes PLI Scheme 11 November 2026

Fresh Applications Invited Under Medical Devices PLI Scheme

  • The Government invited fresh applications under the Production Linked Incentive Scheme for Promoting Domestic Manufacturing of Medical Devices (PLI-MD).
  • The scheme has been extended up to 2035–36.
  • Approximately ₹2,700 crore is available as the scheme outlay.
  • Applications are to be submitted through an online portal, expected to become operational within 10 days of the announcement.
  • The Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers said eligibility and incentive-claim procedures had been simplified using lessons from the first phase.
  • Applicants will have three years to establish manufacturing facilities in one or more of the scheme’s four target segments.
  • The segments include medical consumables, disposables and implants, and critical components.
  • The other segments cover high-end medical devices with nascent domestic manufacturing and high-end devices currently only assembled in India.
  • Under PLI-MD 2.0, the minimum sales threshold for claiming incentives has been discontinued and the minimum investment requirement reduced.
  • Applicants may submit any number of applications across one or more target segments, while the application window will remain open continuously over a long period.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
