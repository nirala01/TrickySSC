India–Argentina Strengthen Strategic and Economic Partnership

  • The Fourth Meeting of the India–Argentina Joint Trade Committee (JTC) was held on 24 August 2026 at Palacio San Martín, Buenos Aires.

India–Argentina Trade

  • Bilateral trade between India and Argentina crossed USD 6.5 billion in 2025.
  • Bilateral trade registered annual growth of more than 17%.
  • India has emerged as Argentina's fifth-largest trading partner.
  • Both countries discussed reducing non-tariff barriers, facilitating foreign trade and promoting investment.

Lithium and Mining Cooperation

  • India and Argentina discussed cooperation in mining, energy and infrastructure.
  • Indian state-owned company KABIL is engaged in Argentina's lithium sector.

Agricultural Products and SPS Measures

  • Both sides noted progress in facilitating the entry of Indian agricultural products into the Argentine market.
  • Technical discussions on Sanitary and Phytosanitary (SPS) measures are progressing.
  • The products under discussion include:
    • Onions
    • Milk and milk products
    • Grapes
    • Potatoes
    • Bananas
    • Pulses
  • These discussions aim to improve market access for Indian farmers and exporters.

Pharmaceutical Market Access

  • Argentina has committed to work towards upgrading India from Annex II to Annex I under its pharmaceutical regulatory framework.
  • The move is intended to reduce barriers to entry for Indian pharmaceutical products in Argentina.
  • The cooperation builds on the 2019 Memorandum of Understanding between Argentina's ANMAT and India's CDSCO.
  • Both sides also agreed to strengthen cooperation through joint initiatives in Ayurveda and Yoga.

Digital, Space and Emerging Technologies

  • Aviation, space technology, telecommunications and digital services were identified as areas with further cooperation potential.
  • Emerging areas specifically highlighted include 5G, Artificial Intelligence (AI) and digital infrastructure.
  • Discussions also covered space and nuclear cooperation, including a proposed meeting between ISRO and CONAE.

India–MERCOSUR Preferential Trade Agreement

  • The India–MERCOSUR Preferential Trade Agreement (PTA) remains an important avenue for expanding market access.
  • Both sides discussed progress on the Terms of Reference (ToR).
  • The adoption of digital certificates of origin is expected to make cross-border trade more efficient.
  • MERCOSUR is a regional economic bloc in South America.

26th Summit of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation

  • The 26th SCO Summit — formally the 26th Meeting of the SCO Council of Heads of State (CHS) — is being held on 31 August – 1 September 2026.
  • Venue: Bishkek, the capital of the Kyrgyz Republic.
  • Host and Chair: The Kyrgyz Republic holds the SCO Chairmanship for 2025–26; President Sadyr Japarov chairs the summit.

  • Chairmanship theme: “25 Years of the SCO: Together Towards Sustainable Peace, Development and Prosperity.”

  • Leaders of all 10 SCO member states are expected to attend, along with observer states, dialogue partners and heads of SCO bodies.

  • On the sidelines, PM Modi is also scheduled to attend the opening ceremony of the 6th World Nomad Games in Kyrgyzstan.

About Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO)

  • The SCO is a permanent intergovernmental international organisation covering political, economic, security and defence cooperation across Eurasia.
  • Established: 15 June 2001 in Shanghai, China.
  • Predecessor: the “Shanghai Five” grouping formed in 1996 — China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan.
  • Founding members (6): China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and Uzbekistan (Uzbekistan's entry converted the Shanghai Five into the SCO).
  • SCO Charter: signed in 2002 at St. Petersburg and came into force in 2003.
  • Present strength — 10 full members: India, China, Russia, Pakistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, Iran and Belarus.
    • India and Pakistan joined as full members in 2017 (Astana Summit)
    • Iran joined in 2023
    • Belarus joined in 2024 as the 10th member
  • Permanent bodies:
    • SCO Secretariat — Beijing, China
    • Regional Anti-Terrorist Structure (RATS) — Tashkent, Uzbekistan
  • Highest decision-making body: the Council of Heads of State (CHS), which meets once a year in summit form.
  • Official languages: Russian and Chinese.
  • The SCO's founding security doctrine is the fight against the “three evils” — terrorism, separatism and extremism.
  • The “Shanghai Spirit” — mutual trust, mutual benefit, equality, consultation, respect for diverse civilisations and common development — is the organisation's guiding principle.
  • India hosted the 2023 SCO Summit (New Delhi, held in virtual format) and has been an active participant in SCO military exercises such as the “Peaceful Mission” series.
  • The SCO covers roughly 60% of the Eurasian landmass and about 40% of the world's population, making it one of the largest regional organisations in the world.

PARIVARTAN Scheme for Replacement of Old Trucks and Buses

  • The Union Cabinet approved the PARIVARTAN Scheme on 3 June 2026.
  • PARIVARTAN stands for Programme for Accelerated Renewal and Incentivization of Vehicle Assets for Reducing Transport Air Pollution and Network Emissions.
  • The scheme aims to replace more than 2 lakh old trucks and buses.
  • It targets vehicles conforming to Bharat Stage (BS)-IV or earlier emission norms.
  • Eligible old vehicles must be registered in:
    • National Capital Territory (NCT) of Delhi
    • NCR districts of Haryana
    • NCR districts of Rajasthan
    • NCR districts of Uttar Pradesh
  • The old vehicles are to be replaced with BS-VI or stricter norm-compliant vehicles or electric vehicles (EVs).

Environment & Geography Disaster Management Himalayan Region

Nepal Flash Floods Triggered by Glacier Collapse and Landslide

  • A devastating flash flood struck the Nepal–China border region in August 2026, causing major destruction to towns, roads and bridges and resulting in loss of life.
  • Scientists investigating the disaster are focusing on the possibility that it was triggered by a major glacier collapse and landslide.
  • The event occurred in the Himalayan region, an area highly vulnerable to climate-related disasters.

Glacier Collapse and Debris Flow

  • The first indication of the disaster came at around 8:37 a.m. local time, when strong ground shaking was detected along the Nepal–China border.
  • The shaking was initially identified by the US Geological Survey (USGS) automated system as an earthquake.
  • The USGS later clarified that the shaking was caused by an exceptionally powerful glacier collapse and debris flow, equivalent to a magnitude 5.2 earthquake.
  • Satellite imagery indicated that a huge section of glacier had broken away from a mountain and plunged towards the river below.
  • Scientists believe a landslide may have occurred simultaneously, producing a destructive mixture of rock, ice and water.
  • The debris entered the Lhende Khola River, a tributary of the Bhote Koshi River.
  • The Bhote Koshi flows from China into Nepal.

Flash Flood Wave

  • The collapse generated a powerful flood wave containing:
    • Water
    • Pulverised ice
    • Rock
    • Sediment
  • According to scientists quoted in the report, the flood wave travelled the first 22 km at an average speed of about 193 km/h.
  • This corresponds to approximately 120 mph.
  • The rapid movement of the flood wave made the event particularly destructive and difficult to predict.

What is a Flash Flood?

  • A flash flood is a sudden, rapid and highly localised flood that develops within a very short period — generally within six hours of the triggering event, and often within minutes.
  • It is distinguished from an ordinary riverine flood by its speed of onset, short duration and extremely high destructive energy, not by the volume of water alone.
  • Main causes:
    • Cloudbursts and intense, short-duration rainfall (a cloudburst is rainfall of 100 mm or more in one hour over a small area)
    • Glacial Lake Outburst Floods (GLOFs) — the sudden bursting of a moraine-dammed glacial lake
    • Glacier or ice avalanche collapse, as suspected in the Nepal event
    • Landslides and rockfalls that temporarily block a river and then breach
    • Failure of a dam, embankment or barrage
    • Poor urban drainage and encroachment on natural drainage channels
  • Typical characteristics: very high velocity, a steep and sudden rise in water level, and a heavy load of debris — boulders, silt, ice and uprooted trees — which is what destroys bridges and roads.
  • Why the Himalayas are vulnerable: steep gradients, young and fragile fold mountains, heavy monsoon rainfall, rapid glacial retreat due to global warming, expanding glacial lakes, and unplanned construction of roads, hydro projects and settlements along narrow river valleys.
  • Mitigation measures: early warning systems and automatic weather stations, satellite and drone monitoring of glacial lakes, controlled lowering of dangerous lake levels, hazard-zone mapping, regulated construction in river valleys, and community-level evacuation drills.
  • In India, disaster response is coordinated by the National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA), headed by the Prime Minister, with the NDRF as the specialised response force. Both were established under the Disaster Management Act, 2005.
Geography link-up: The Bhote Koshi is one of the headstreams of the Koshi (Kosi) river system, which rises in Tibet/Nepal and joins the Ganga in Bihar. The Kosi is popularly called the “Sorrow of Bihar” because of its frequent floods and its tendency to shift its course.

Sardar Patel National Unity Award — First Award Expected in 2026

  • The Sardar Patel National Unity Award was instituted by the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA), Government of India.
  • The award was instituted in September 2019.
  • It is the highest civilian award in the field of contribution to the unity and integrity of India.
  • The award is named after Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel.
  • Its objective is to recognise notable and inspiring contributions by citizens, institutions and organisations of India towards:
    • National unity
    • National integrity
    • Strengthening the idea of a strong and united India
  • The award is associated with National Unity Day, observed on 31 October, the birth anniversary of Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel.
  • According to a report published by The Indian Express on 30 August 2026, the government is likely to announce the first recipients of the award on 31 October 2026.
  • The report states that although more than 100 nominations are understood to have been received each year, no nomination had been considered deserving enough for an award to be announced so far.
  • The possible announcement in 2026 coincides with the conclusion of the two-year celebrations marking Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel's 150th birth anniversary, which began on 31 October 2024.
  • The award is conferred by the President of India through a Sanad under his hand and seal.
  • It is presented at a ceremony along with the Padma Awards at Rashtrapati Bhavan.

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel

  • Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel is associated with the cause of national unity and integration.
  • His birth anniversary, 31 October, is observed as National Unity Day (Rashtriya Ekta Diwas).
  • The Sardar Patel National Unity Award was instituted in his name to recognise contributions towards national unity and integrity.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which important day is observed on 29 August?
National Sports Day is observed in India on 29 August every year. It commemorates the birth anniversary of Major Dhyan Chand, who was born on 29 August 1905 and is popularly known as the “Wizard of Hockey”.
Where is the 26th SCO Summit being held in 2026?
The 26th Summit of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation is being held in Bishkek, the capital of the Kyrgyz Republic, on 31 August and 1 September 2026. The Kyrgyz Republic holds the SCO chairmanship for 2025–26, and the summit marks the 25th anniversary of the organisation.
What is the full form of the PARIVARTAN Scheme?
PARIVARTAN stands for Programme for Accelerated Renewal and Incentivization of Vehicle Assets for Reducing Transport Air Pollution and Network Emissions. It was approved by the Union Cabinet on 3 June 2026 and launched on 14 July 2026, with a total outlay of ₹9,585 crore.
What is ‘Gnani Artha’?
Gnani Artha is a sovereign AI stack developed by GNANI AI and launched by Vice President C. P. Radhakrishnan at Uprashtrapati Bhavan, New Delhi. It has two components — Evon 3.3, which is a Large Language Model, and Plexus, a platform that connects AI intelligence with real-world work and institutions.
What is a flash flood and how is it different from a normal flood?
A flash flood is a sudden, highly localised flood that develops generally within six hours of the triggering event, and often within minutes. It differs from an ordinary riverine flood in its speed of onset, short duration and very high destructive energy, and it usually carries a heavy load of debris such as boulders, silt and ice. Common triggers include cloudbursts, glacial lake outburst floods, glacier collapse, landslides and dam failure.
When was the Sardar Patel National Unity Award instituted?
It was instituted by the Ministry of Home Affairs in September 2019. It is the highest civilian award in the field of contribution to the unity and integrity of India, is conferred by the President of India through a Sanad, and is presented at Rashtrapati Bhavan along with the Padma Awards.
