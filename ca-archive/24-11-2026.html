Economy & Banking Economic Outlook 24 November 2026

S&P Global Sees India as Asia-Pacific’s Fastest-Growing Economy

  • S&P Global projects India to be the fastest-growing economy in the Asia-Pacific region in 2027.
  • GDP growth is projected at 7% in both the current fiscal year and the next, ending March 2028.
  • The projections appear in S&P Global’s Economic Outlook Asia-Pacific Q1 2027 report.
  • Goods exports, public investment spending and household consumption supported growth in the June and September quarters.
  • Household consumption was supported by buoyant credit growth, according to the report.
  • S&P Global noted India’s deeper integration into global supply chains and the benefits of trade diversification.
  • Electronics, engineering goods and chemicals were among the export sectors showing strength.
  • Inflation is projected to average 5.3% in the fiscal year ending March 2027, with risks tilted upwards.
  • Inflation is projected to ease to 4.7% in the fiscal year ending March 2028.
  • S&P Global expects the RBI to raise policy rates by 25 basis points in December and said the chance of another early-2027 hike had increased.

Government Schemes Poverty and Agriculture 24 November 2026

India’s Extreme Poverty Rate Reported at 2.6%

  • Union Agriculture and Rural Development Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan said India’s extreme poverty rate had fallen from 27.1% to 2.6%.
  • He cited a recent World Bank report and said nearly 500 million people had moved out of poverty.
  • Chouhan said the next challenge was preventing people who had crossed the poverty line from falling back into poverty.
  • A workshop on the issue was held at the Dr Ambedkar International Centre.
  • Officials from Agriculture and Farmers’ Welfare, Rural Development, Land Resources, and Agricultural Research and Education attended.
  • The Agriculture Ministry reported a 4.45% decadal growth rate for the agricultural sector, compared with about 3–3.5% during the Green Revolution.
  • Reported foodgrain production stood at 376.56 million tonnes and horticulture production at 386.91 million tonnes.
  • The Ministry cited more than ₹4.66 lakh crore disbursed under PM-KISAN.
  • Other agricultural initiatives mentioned included crop insurance, agricultural credit and natural farming.
  • The initiatives also included establishing 10,000 Farmer Producer Organisations and digital measures such as AgriStack.

Government Schemes Public Health 24 November 2026

India Administers Over One Crore HPV Vaccine Doses

  • Prime Minister Narendra Modi welcomed the administration of more than one crore HPV vaccine doses in less than nine months.
  • The doses were delivered under the National HPV Vaccination Campaign, launched in February 2026.
  • The campaign targets 14-year-old girls and offers a free, safe and voluntary single-dose vaccination.
  • Vaccination is available at government health centres and requires parental consent.
  • The campaign has reached about 87% of its annual target group of approximately 1.2 crore girls.
  • Six states achieved 100% coverage of their targeted population, while 12 states exceeded 80% coverage.
  • Uttar Pradesh led with more than 29.33 lakh girls vaccinated, followed by Bihar with over 13.70 lakh and Gujarat with over 7.75 lakh.
  • The WHO South-East Asia Region congratulated India on administering over 10 million doses to 14-year-old girls.
  • The campaign aims to protect girls against HPV infection, which can cause cervical cancer.
  • Vice-President C. P. Radhakrishnan and Union Health Minister Jagat Prakash Nadda also praised the milestone.

Cabinet Decisions Mining Rules 24 November 2026

Recognition Period for Mining Agencies Extended to Five Years

  • The Ministry of Coal notified the Mineral Concession (Third Amendment) Rules, 2026.
  • The amendment was notified through G.S.R. 999(E) dated 17 November 2026.
  • It extends the recognition cycle for Accredited Prospecting Agencies (APAs) from three years to five years.
  • The recognition cycle for Mining Plan Preparation Agencies (MPPAs) is also extended from three years to five years.
  • The change reduces the number of periodic assessments and re-recognition processes for these agencies.
  • The longer recognition period is intended to reduce time spent on repeated evaluations and recognition procedures.
  • It is expected to support continuity in preparing geological reports and mining plans.
  • Geological reports and mining plans are required for bringing coal and lignite blocks into operation.
  • The Ministry described the change as a step towards ease of doing business and trust-based governance in the coal sector.

Why daily current affairs matter in the SSC exam

In SSC CGL Tier I, the General Awareness section carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, that is 50 marks out of 200, and it must be completed in a locked 15-minute sectional window under the 2026 pattern. Unlike Quantitative Aptitude or Reasoning, General Awareness rewards recall rather than calculation, so a well-prepared candidate can finish the whole section in seven or eight minutes and carry the saved time as a psychological cushion into the rest of the paper.

Within that section, current affairs typically account for a meaningful share of questions, and the questions are almost always factual one-liners — who launched a scheme, where a summit was held, which ministry implements a programme, what a scheme's full form is. That is exactly why this page is written as short bullet facts rather than paragraphs of analysis.

  • Current affairs questions are the fastest questions in the paper — no working, no rough sheet.
  • They repeat across SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD and state-level exams in the same cycle.
  • They connect naturally with static GK: a summit question pulls in headquarters and capitals, a scheme question pulls in the ministry.
  • Reading ten minutes a day is far more effective than revising a 300-page compilation a week before the exam.

How to use this daily current affairs page effectively

  1. Read the “Today at a glance” box first. A handful of lines, under two minutes. If you are short on time on a busy day, this alone keeps you current.
  2. Then read each topic once, slowly. Do not try to memorise on the first pass — understanding what happened makes the facts stick far better than rote repetition.
  3. Revise only the Important Facts for Exams boxes. These are deliberately written in question–answer shape, so they double as a revision sheet in the last week before the exam.
  4. Attempt the practice MCQs the same day. Active recall on the day you read something is worth several passive re-readings later.
  5. Do a weekly sweep every Sunday. Open the last seven date pages from the archive and read only the fact boxes — that takes about fifteen minutes and covers a full week.
  6. Link every current affair to static GK. When you read about a summit, revise the organisation's headquarters and member capitals at the same time. SSC frequently mixes the two.

Standard categories SSC asks current affairs from

Over the last several exam cycles, current affairs questions in SSC papers have clustered into a predictable set of buckets. Reading with these buckets in mind makes your notes far easier to revise:

  • Government schemes and Cabinet decisions — full form, ministry, outlay, launch date
  • Appointments — new chairpersons, chiefs, CEOs, and heads of constitutional bodies
  • Awards and honours — instituting body, field, first recipient
  • Summits, conferences and agreements — host city, edition number, theme
  • Science, technology and space — missions, launches, indigenous technology
  • Defence — exercises, inductions, indigenous platforms
  • Economy and banking — reports, indices, rankings, RBI decisions
  • Environment and ecology — disasters, protected areas, climate reports
  • Sports — tournaments, winners, venues, important days
  • Important days and themes — date, theme of the year, observing body
