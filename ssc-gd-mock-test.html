SSC GD Constable Mock Test Series 2027

The SSC GD Constable computer-based examination is a short, fast paper: 80 questions in 60 minutes across four parts, with a cut-off that moved past 146 out of 160 for unreserved male candidates in the 2026 result. The questions are rarely hard on their own. What decides selection is accuracy under a clock that gives you 45 seconds a question, and the discipline not to lose quarter-marks to guesses. This series is built for exactly that: 60 full-length papers modelled question-type by question-type on real GD shifts from the 2024, 2025 and 2026 cycles.

Every mock follows the SSC GD part order — General Intelligence & Reasoning first, then General Knowledge & General Awareness, Elementary Mathematics and English/Hindi — so the paper you practise on is the paper you will sit. Each one opens in English or Hindi, and every question has a step-by-step solution; GK solutions add one related fact you can carry to the next paper.

SSC GD Syllabus 2027 — Part-wise Topics

Every mock in this series is built against the official CBE syllabus below, so practising the full series means meeting every topic SSC lists.

General Intelligence & Reasoning (20 questions)

Analogies, similarities and differences, spatial visualisation and orientation, visual memory, discrimination, observation, relationship concepts, arithmetical reasoning and figural classification, arithmetic number series, non-verbal series, coding and decoding, blood relations, direction sense, order and ranking, and figure-based questions such as mirror images, paper folding and embedded figures. Practise this part alone with the SSC GD reasoning mock test.

General Knowledge & General Awareness (20 questions)

India and its neighbouring countries, sports, history, culture, geography, economic scene, general polity, the Indian Constitution, scientific research and current events of national and international importance. Practise it with the SSC GD GK mock test.

Elementary Mathematics (20 questions)

Number systems, computation of whole numbers, decimals and fractions, relationships between numbers, fundamental arithmetical operations, percentages, ratio and proportion, averages, interest, profit and loss, discount, mensuration, time and distance, ratio and time, and time and work. Practise it with the SSC GD maths mock test.

English / Hindi (20 questions)

Basic comprehension and writing ability: spot the error, fill in the blanks, synonyms and antonyms, spelling, idioms and phrases, one-word substitution, sentence improvement, active and passive voice, direct and indirect narration, cloze passage and reading comprehension in English; in the Hindi paper, the equivalent Hindi vyakaran, shabdavali and gadyansh questions. Practise it with the SSC GD English mock test.

What Every Mock Contains

The 60 papers follow a fixed plan so that nothing in the syllabus is left to chance. Each mock mirrors one real GD shift for its question mix, and the plan rotates across recent shifts so that you meet every pattern SSC has used since the 80-question format began.

  • Reasoning with figures: figure questions in every mock — mirror images, paper folding, embedded figures, figure series and counting figures — because they are the easiest marks in Part A once you have seen enough of them.
  • Maths at the exam level: one- and two-step arithmetic on the real GD pattern, with the tricks written into the solutions, so speed comes from method rather than from hard questions.
  • English and Hindi on the GD pattern: a fresh cloze passage, spelling, synonyms, antonyms, idioms, one-word substitution, voice and narration — with no vocabulary word repeated across the series.
  • Complete GK coverage: every static GK chapter is asked at least once across the series, with current-affairs questions in every paper tilted towards awards, sports achievements and women achievers that recent SSC papers favour, and a short related fact in every GK solution.
  • No repeats: no question is repeated anywhere in the series, and every question stays at the real exam level.

Practice Mocks and Challenge Mocks

Mock 1 to Mock 48 are pitched at the real GD level: mostly direct questions, a share of two-step problems and a few that test the ceiling of the paper. Mock 49 to Mock 60 are challenge papers, set at the level of the hardest real GD shift and never above it. They keep the same question types but push the harder version of each — longer series, two-step blood relations, three-step arithmetic and GK questions where every option is a real fact.

How to Use These Mocks Before the Exam

Start with a free mock early, before you have finished the syllabus, and treat the score as a baseline rather than a verdict. Then move to one full mock every two or three days, and spend at least as long reviewing each paper as you spent writing it. The review is where marks are found: sort your wrong answers into concept gaps, careless slips and time losses, because each needs a different fix.

In the last three weeks, shift to a mock every day at the same time as your exam slot, and use the subject-wise tests to repair the weakest part without sitting a whole paper. Keep the challenge mocks for the final fortnight, when a tough paper tells you more than an easy one. Remember the physical tests follow the CBE: keep your running and the PST standards in your weekly plan alongside the mocks.
