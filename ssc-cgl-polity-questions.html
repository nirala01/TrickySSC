Indian Polity is a fact chapter built on numbers — Article numbers, Amendment numbers, Schedules, ages and time limits. SSC does not ask you to discuss federalism; it asks which Article deals with the Finance Commission, which Amendment lowered the voting age to 18, or within how many days the Rajya Sabha must return a Money Bill. The four options are always taken from the same table — four neighbouring Articles, four Amendments, four office-holders — so a half-remembered number is exactly what the paper is built to catch.

Marks are lost in three places. The first is Article–subject pairs: Art 32 and constitutional remedies, Art 72 and the President’s pardon, Art 110 and the Money Bill, Art 280 and the Finance Commission, Art 324 and the Election Commission. The second is Amendment–change pairs: the 42nd and the Preamble, the 44th and the right to property, the 61st and the voting age, the 73rd and 74th and local bodies, the 86th and Art 21A. The third is who appoints, who administers the oath and who removes the President, Governor, judges, CAG and Election Commissioners. The tests here are built around exactly these three.

The three traps that cost the most marks

  • Neighbouring Articles. Art 72 (President’s pardon) and Art 161 (Governor’s pardon), Art 32 and Art 226, Art 352, 356 and 360, Art 123 and Art 213 — SSC puts the pair side by side. Learn each Union Article with its state twin.
  • Two Amendments of the same year. The 73rd and 74th (1992), the 42nd and 44th (Emergency and after), the 101st GST and 102nd NCBC — read what the question asks before picking the familiar number.
  • “Only”, “cannot” and “both Houses” in statements. Who can introduce a Money Bill, which motion lies only in the Lok Sabha, who votes in the President’s election — the wrong statement usually hides in one absolute word.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Polity

Q1. Which Article of the Constitution did Dr B.R. Ambedkar call the ‘heart and soul’ of the Constitution?
(a) Article 14
(b) Article 19
(c) Article 21
(d) Article 32
Article 32, the right to constitutional remedies, lets a person move the Supreme Court directly for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights through writs — habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari and quo warranto. Article 14 is equality before law, Article 19 the six freedoms and Article 21 the right to life and personal liberty. Answer: (d)
Q2. Consider the following statements about the election of the President of India:
1. Elected members of both Houses of Parliament are part of the electoral college.
2. Nominated members of the Rajya Sabha are part of the electoral college.
3. Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of Delhi and Puducherry are part of the electoral college.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 2 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3
Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Under Article 54 only the elected members of Parliament and of the state Legislative Assemblies vote, and the 70th Amendment (1992) added the elected members of the Delhi and Puducherry Assemblies. Statement 2 is wrong — nominated members do not vote for the President, though they do vote in the Vice-President’s election. Answer: (a)
Q3. Read the Assertion (A) and Reason (R) carefully.
Assertion (A): The Council of Ministers remains in office only as long as it enjoys the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
Reason (R): Under Article 75(3), the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
(c) A is true, but R is false
(d) A is false, but R is true
Because Article 75(3) makes the ministers collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha, a no-confidence motion passed there forces the whole Council of Ministers to resign, including ministers who are members of the Rajya Sabha. Answer: (a)
Q4. 73वें संविधान संशोधन अधिनियम, 1992 द्वारा संविधान में कौन-सी अनुसूची जोड़ी गई?
(a) दसवीं अनुसूची
(b) ग्यारहवीं अनुसूची
(c) बारहवीं अनुसूची
(d) नौवीं अनुसूची
73वें संशोधन ने भाग IX (अनुच्छेद 243–243O) और ग्यारहवीं अनुसूची जोड़ी, जिसमें पंचायतों के 29 विषय हैं। बारहवीं अनुसूची (नगरपालिकाओं के 18 विषय) 74वें संशोधन से, दसवीं अनुसूची (दल-बदल विरोधी कानून) 52वें संशोधन, 1985 से और नौवीं अनुसूची पहले संशोधन, 1951 से जोड़ी गई। उत्तर: (b)

How to Prepare Polity for SSC CGL

  1. Keep an Article table. Article – subject – Part, for the 60 or so Articles SSC keeps asking: 12–35, 36–51A, 52–78, 79–123, 124–147, 280, 324, 352–360 and 368. More Polity questions come out of this one table than any other.
  2. Learn the landmark Amendments as a list. Number, year and change — 1st (Ninth Schedule), 42nd, 44th, 52nd, 61st, 73rd, 74th, 86th, 91st, 101st, 103rd, 104th and 106th.
  3. Make one “who appoints, who removes” chart. President, Vice-President, Governor, judges, CAG, Election Commissioners, UPSC members — with the age, term, oath and removal procedure for each.
  4. Read the solutions, not just the answer. Each solution here carries six or seven related facts — reading them is how 350 questions cover the whole chapter.
  5. Revise in short rounds. One test a day keeps the Articles fresh while you move ahead, and then check yourself on a previous year paper.
