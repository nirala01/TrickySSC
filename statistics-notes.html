Statistics

Statistics in the SSC syllabus means three averages — mean, median, mode — and a few measures of spread. Know how each is found and when each is used.

1. Key formulas

MeasureHow to find it
MeanΣx / n; for grouped data Σfx / Σf
Median (ungrouped)Middle value after sorting; if n is even, average of the two middle values
Median position(n + 1)/2-th value
ModeMost frequent value
Empirical relationMode = 3 × Median − 2 × Mean
RangeLargest − smallest
Variance (population)Σ(x − mean)² / n
Standard deviation√Variance
Add k to every valueMean, median, mode all increase by k; SD unchanged
Multiply every value by kMean, median, mode and SD all multiply by k

2. Shortcuts that save time

Empirical relation in either direction If mean = 12 and median = 11, mode = 3 × 11 − 2 × 12 = 9. The relation also gives mean or median when the other two are known.
  • Adding or subtracting a constant changes the mean but not the spread.
  • For a skewed distribution: mean > median > mode (right-skewed), the reverse for left-skewed.
  • Sum of deviations from the mean is always 0.

3. Solved examples

Example 1 Data: 3, 5, 7, 7, 8. The sum is 30, so mean = 6; median = 7; mode = 7.
Example 2 Data: 2, 4, 6, 8. Median = (4 + 6)/2 = 5; range = 6; mean = 5.
Example 3 If every value is multiplied by 3 and then increased by 5, a mean of 10 becomes 3 × 10 + 5 = 35; an SD of 2 becomes 6.

4. Traps to avoid

  1. Sort the data before finding the median.
  2. For an even count, the median is the average of two values, not one of them.
  3. Standard deviation does not change when a constant is added.
  4. Don't confuse range (one number) with spread (SD).
