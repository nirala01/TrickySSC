Statistics in the SSC syllabus means three averages — mean, median, mode — and a few measures of spread. Know how each is found and when each is used.

1. Concepts in brief

Three kinds of "average"

The mean uses every value, so one very large or small value can pull it a long way. The median is the middle value and is not disturbed by extremes — it suits incomes or house prices. The mode is the most common value and suits things like shoe sizes.

Grouped data

When data comes as values with frequencies, multiply each value by its frequency, add them up and divide by the total frequency. This is just the ordinary mean with repeats counted.

Spread

Two data sets can have the same mean but very different spread: 49, 50, 51 and 0, 50, 100 both average 50. Range uses only the two extremes; variance and standard deviation use every value's distance from the mean.

3. Shortcuts that save time

Empirical relation in either direction If mean = 12 and median = 11, mode = 3 × 11 − 2 × 12 = 9. The relation also gives mean or median when the other two are known.
  • Adding or subtracting a constant changes the mean but not the spread.
  • For a skewed distribution: mean > median > mode (right-skewed), the reverse for left-skewed.
  • Sum of deviations from the mean is always 0.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 Data: 3, 5, 7, 7, 8. The sum is 30, so mean = 6; median = 7; mode = 7.
Example 2 Data: 2, 4, 6, 8. Median = (4 + 6)/2 = 5; range = 6; mean = 5.
Example 3 If every value is multiplied by 3 and then increased by 5, a mean of 10 becomes 3 × 10 + 5 = 35; an SD of 2 becomes 6.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 4 The mean of 6, 8, 10, x and 12 is 9. Total = 45, so x = 45 − 36 = 9.
Example 5 Median of 10, 3, 7, 15, 9, 12: sorted 3, 7, 9, 10, 12, 15 → (9 + 10)/2 = 9.5.
Example 6 Values 2, 4, 6 with frequencies 3, 5, 2. Mean = (6 + 20 + 12)/10 = 3.8.
Example 7 Variance of 2, 4, 6, 8: mean 5; squared deviations 9, 1, 1, 9 sum to 20 → variance 5, SD √5.

Answer: 5.

Q2. Mean 20 and median 18. Mode by the empirical relation?

Answer: 14 (54 − 40).

Q3. Range of 15, 9, 22 and 4?

Answer: 18.

Answer: 11.

Q5. The mean of 5 numbers is 30. If 6 is added to each, the new mean?

Answer: 36.

6. Traps to avoid

  1. Sort the data before finding the median.
  2. For an even count, the median is the average of two values, not one of them.
  3. Standard deviation does not change when a constant is added.
  4. Don't confuse range (one number) with spread (SD).
