Both halves of this chapter rest on one idea each. For the calendar, count the odd days — the days left over after removing whole weeks — and move the weekday by that many. For the clock, remember that the minute hand gains 5.5° per minute on the hour hand; every angle, meeting and right-angle question follows from it.

Marks are lost in three places. The first is the leap day — counting a 29 February that does not lie between the two dates, or calling 1900 and 2100 leap years. The second is counting the starting day too, which moves the answer one day on. The third is forgetting that the hour hand moves: at 3:20 it is not on the 3, it has gone 10° further.

For a given angle between two hours, solve 5.5M = 30H ± θ and also check the case with 360° added or taken away — the plain formula can miss a time.

The three mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Counting the wrong leap days. A 29 February counts only if it falls between the two dates; 1900 and 2100 have none.
  • Counting the starting day. From the 5th to the 12th is 7 days, not 8.
  • Freezing the hour hand. It moves 0.5° every minute, so at 7:20 it is 10° past the 7.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Calendar & Clock

Q1. What was the day of the week on 15 August 1947?
Completed years 1946 = 1600 + 300 + 46: odd days 0 + 1 + (11 leap × 2 + 35 ordinary = 57 → 1) = 2. Days of 1947 up to 15 August: 31 + 28 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 15 = 227 → 3 odd days. Total 2 + 3 = 5. Answer: Friday
Q2. If 26 January 2023 was a Thursday, what day of the week will 26 January 2027 be?
4 years, and one 29 February (2024) lies in between: 4 + 1 = 5 odd days. Thursday + 5 = Answer: Tuesday
Q3. What is the angle between the hands of a clock at 7:20?
|30 × 7 − 5.5 × 20| = |210 − 110| = 100°. Answer: 100°
Q4. At what time between 4 and 5 o’clock will the hands of a clock be together?
At 4 o’clock the minute hand is 20 minute spaces behind. It gains 11/12 of a space each minute, so it needs 20 × 12/11 = 21 9/11 minutes. Answer: 21 9/11 minutes past 4

How to Prepare Calendar & Clock for SSC CGL

  1. Learn the century odd days by heart. 5, 3, 1, 0 for 100, 200, 300 and 400 years saves a minute on every old date.
  2. Keep the month odd days ready. January 3, February 0 (1 in a leap year), March 3, April 2, May 3, June 2, July 3, August 3, September 2, October 3, November 2, December 3.
  3. For the same date in another year, just count the 29 Februaries. Years + leap days crossed, divided by 7, gives the shift.
  4. For any clock angle, write |30H − 5.5M| straight away. There is no need to draw the dial.
  5. For a given angle, check every case. Solve with + and −, and with 360° added or taken away, then keep the times between 0 and 60 minutes.
  6. Do Level 1 untimed first, then with the 15-minute clock. Move to Level 2 once Level 1 is steady, and then check yourself on a previous year paper.

SSC CGL Calendar & Clock — Frequently Asked Questions

How many calendar and clock questions are asked in SSC CGL?
Usually none or one question in a Tier I reasoning section. Calendar questions (day of the week, odd days) come up more often than clock questions, and the same forms appear in SSC CHSL.
What are odd days?
The days left over after taking out complete weeks. An ordinary year has 365 days = 52 weeks + 1 day, so 1 odd day; a leap year has 2. Odd days are what move a date to a different day of the week.
How many odd days are there in 100, 200, 300 and 400 years?
100 years have 5 odd days, 200 years 3, 300 years 1 and 400 years 0. That is why the day of the week repeats every 400 years.
Is every century year a leap year?
No. A century year such as 1900 or 2100 is a leap year only if it is divisible by 400. So 2000 and 2400 are leap years, but 1700, 1800, 1900 and 2100 are not.
What is the formula for the angle between the hands of a clock?
Angle = |30H − 5.5M|, where H is the hour and M the minutes. If the result is more than 180°, subtract it from 360° to get the smaller angle.
How do I find the mirror image of a clock time?
Subtract the time from 11:60 (which is the same as 12:00). For example, the mirror image of 8:20 is 11:60 − 8:20 = 3:40. With seconds, subtract from 11:59:60.
Are the tests available in Hindi?
Yes. Questions, options, detailed solutions and shortcut tricks are available in Hindi, and you choose English or Hindi before the test starts.
What is the difference between Level 1 and Level 2?
Level 1 is set at real SSC CGL difficulty: days within a year, a few years apart, odd days, clock angles, hands together or opposite, mirror images and simple fast or slow clocks. Level 2 is harder than the exam: dates from 1600 to 2399, century years, birthdays many years later, day puzzles like 'the day before yesterday', angles of any size, clocks that run fast over several days and counting how often the hands meet.
