Every homonym question asks one thing: which meaning does this sentence need? A homonym has the same spelling and sound as another word but a different meaning — the bank of a river and the bank where you keep money. A homophone sounds the same but is spelt differently — stationary (not moving) and stationery (pens and paper). SSC papers call both kinds “homonyms”.

Marks are lost in two places. The first is trusting the sound — both words sound right when you say the sentence aloud in your head, so only the meaning can decide. The second is knowing only one meaning — a student who knows quarry only as a stone pit cannot see that “the hounds chased their quarry” uses a different word.

The two mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Choosing by sound. Read the sentence for meaning, decide what the blank must mean, and only then look at the spellings.
  • Choosing the “different” sentence for the wrong reason. In the highlighted-word pattern, three options share one meaning. Do not pick an option just because its sentence is about a different topic — pick the one where the word itself means something else.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Homonyms

Q1. Select the most appropriate homonym to fill in the blank.
The drummer struck the ______ at the end of the song.
(a) symbol   (b) cymbal   (c) simple   (d) sample
A cymbal is a round brass plate struck in music; a symbol is a sign that stands for something. The drummer strikes an instrument. Answer: (b)
Q2. Select the sentence containing the homonym of the highlighted word.
A spoke of my bicycle wheel is bent.
(a) The mechanic replaced the rusted spoke.
(b) She spoke to the principal about the fees.
(c) Each spoke was tightened with a key.
(d) The spoke snapped on the rough road.
In the given sentence, “spoke” is a rod of a wheel. Options (a), (c) and (d) use it the same way. In (b) it is the past tense of “speak” — same spelling, different meaning. Answer: (b)
Q3. Select the meaning of the highlighted word as it is used in the sentence.
Do not stall any longer; give me your answer.
(a) a stand in a market   (b) a compartment for an animal   (c) to delay on purpose   (d) a seat in a theatre
“Any longer” and “give me your answer” show that the speaker is being kept waiting, so “stall” is a verb meaning to delay. Answer: (c)
Q4. Select the most appropriate pair of homonyms to fill in the blanks in the given order.
The guide ______ the tourists past an old ______ mine.
(a) lead, led   (b) led, lead   (c) led, led   (d) lead, lead
The first blank needs the past tense of “lead”, which is led. The second needs the metal, lead (said “led”). Answer: (b)

How to Prepare Homonyms for SSC CGL

  1. Read the sentence before the options. Decide what the blank must mean in your own words; then the right spelling is easy to see.
  2. Learn pairs, not single words. Write each homophone next to its partner with a short sentence — stationary bus, stationery shop.
  3. Collect second meanings. When you meet a familiar word used strangely (quarry, fawn, hamper, temple), note the new meaning; these are exactly what the highlighted-word pattern tests.
  4. Say the heteronyms aloud. Wound, refuse, minute and entrance change their sound with their meaning — saying them helps you remember which is which.
  5. Do Level 1 untimed first, then with the 15-minute clock. Move to Level 2 once Level 1 is steady, and then check yourself on a previous year paper.

SSC CGL Homonyms — Frequently Asked Questions

How many homonym questions are asked in SSC CGL?
Usually none or one in an SSC CGL Tier I shift, and a few more across CHSL and the Tier II English paper. Since the 2025 cycle, the ‘sentence containing the homonym of the highlighted word’ pattern has appeared in Tier I English, and confused homophones also turn up inside Spot the Error and Fill in the Blanks.
What is the difference between a homonym and a homophone?
A homonym is a word that has the same spelling and sound as another word but a different meaning, such as bank (of a river) and bank (for money). A homophone sounds the same as another word but is spelt differently and means something else, such as stationary and stationery. SSC papers use the word ‘homonym’ for both kinds of question.
How do I solve ‘select the sentence containing the homonym of the highlighted word’?
First decide what the highlighted word means in the given sentence. Then read the four options: three of them use the word in that same meaning, and one uses it in a completely different meaning. That odd one out is the answer.
How do I choose between two words that sound the same?
Ignore the sound and read the meaning the sentence needs. Then match it to the spelling: stationery has an ‘e’ like envelope, principal is a person (the head of a school), and dessert has two s’s because you want seconds of something sweet.
How many questions are in each test?
Twenty-five questions per test, with an optional 15-minute timer and the real exam marking of +2 for a correct answer, −0.5 for a wrong one and 0 for an unattempted question.
Are the tests in English or Hindi?
The homonyms tests are in English, as the English Language section is in SSC CGL and CHSL. Every solution states the rule and gives the meaning of each word that sounds or looks alike, in simple English.
What is the difference between Level 1 and Level 2?
Level 1 is set at real SSC CGL difficulty — common homophones like pane and pain, and everyday homonyms like bank, bat and match. Level 2 is harder than the exam: less common pairs such as discrete and discreet or censer and censor, rare meanings like quarry, fawn and pall, words spelt alike but said differently such as wound, refuse and entrance, and two-blank questions where the order of the pair decides the answer.
