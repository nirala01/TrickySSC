Mixture & Alligation

Formulas, tricks and traps on one page · Quantitative Aptitude

Alligation is a quick way to mix two things. The cheaper-to-mean gap and the dearer-to-mean gap give the ratio directly — no equations needed.

1. Key formulas

SituationRule
Alligation ruleCheaper qty : Dearer qty = (d − m) : (m − c)
Mean price(c·q₁ + d·q₂)/(q₁ + q₂)
ReplacementFinal pure = initial × (1 − x/V)ⁿ
Mixture of ratio, gain %SP = CP of mixture × (100 + gain%)/100
Water to add to milkuse alligation with water at 0 price

Here c = cheaper price, d = dearer price, m = mean price, x = quantity removed and replaced each time, V = total volume.

2. Shortcuts that save time

The cross method Write c on the left, d on the right, m in the middle. The ratio of quantities is the crosswise difference: cheaper gets (d − m), dearer gets (m − c).
Rice at ₹20 and ₹30 per kg mixed to sell at ₹26: cheaper : dearer = (30 − 26) : (26 − 20) = 4 : 6 = 2 : 3.
  • For concentration, use the same cross method with percentages of milk, alcohol or salt.
  • For replacement of V litres by x litres, the fraction left after one step is (V − x)/V.

3. Solved examples

Example 1 In what ratio must tea at ₹60/kg be mixed with tea at ₹75/kg to get a mix worth ₹66/kg? (75 − 66) : (66 − 60) = 9 : 6 = 3 : 2.
Example 2 A 40-litre vessel is full of milk. 4 litres are drawn out and replaced with water, and this is repeated once more. Milk left = 40 × (36/40)² = 40 × 0.81 = 32.4 litres.
Example 3 How much water should be added to 20 litres of 80% milk so that it becomes 50% milk? Pure milk = 16 litres, final volume = 16/0.5 = 32. Water added = 12 litres.

4. Traps to avoid

  1. The ratio gives quantities, not prices — don't invert it.
  2. In replacement, the factor is raised to the number of repetitions, and uses the volume left, not the volume removed.
  3. Water has a price (or milk percentage) of zero — use it as the cheaper item.
  4. Keep units the same (litres or kg) throughout.
