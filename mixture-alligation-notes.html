Mixture & Alligation

Alligation is a quick way to mix two things. The cheaper-to-mean gap and the dearer-to-mean gap give the ratio directly — no equations needed.

1. Concepts in brief

A mixture has an average

When two things are mixed, the price (or strength) of the mixture is a weighted average of the two. It must lie between them: mixing ₹20 rice with ₹30 rice can give ₹26 rice, but never ₹35 rice.

Why the alligation cross works

The cheaper item sits below the mean and the dearer item above it. The further an item is from the mean, the less of it you need to balance. That is why each item's quantity is the distance of the other item from the mean.

Concentration questions

Treat the percentage of milk, acid or alcohol like a price. Water counts as 0% milk. Pure milk counts as 100%.

Repeated replacement

Each time x litres are removed from V litres and replaced with water, the milk left is multiplied by (V − x)/V. After n rounds, multiply n times.

3. Shortcuts that save time

The cross method Write c on the left, d on the right, m in the middle. The ratio of quantities is the crosswise difference: cheaper gets (d − m), dearer gets (m − c).
Rice at ₹20 and ₹30 per kg mixed to sell at ₹26: cheaper : dearer = (30 − 26) : (26 − 20) = 4 : 6 = 2 : 3.
  • For concentration, use the same cross method with percentages of milk, alcohol or salt.
  • For replacement of V litres by x litres, the fraction left after one step is (V − x)/V.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 In what ratio must tea at ₹60/kg be mixed with tea at ₹75/kg to get a mix worth ₹66/kg? (75 − 66) : (66 − 60) = 9 : 6 = 3 : 2.
Example 2 A 40-litre vessel is full of milk. 4 litres are drawn out and replaced with water, and this is repeated once more. Milk left = 40 × (36/40)² = 40 × 0.81 = 32.4 litres.
Example 3 How much water should be added to 20 litres of 80% milk so that it becomes 50% milk? Pure milk = 16 litres, final volume = 16/0.5 = 32. Water added = 12 litres.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 4 30 litres of milk and water are in the ratio 7 : 3. How much water must be added to make it 3 : 7? Milk = 21, so water must become 49; it is 9 now. Add 40 litres.
Example 5 Acid solutions of 20% and 50% are mixed to give 30%. Ratio = (50 − 30) : (30 − 20) = 2 : 1 (20% : 50%).
Example 6 A milkman sells milk at cost price but gains 20% by adding water. He sells 6 litres for the price of 5, so water : milk = 1 : 5.
Example 7 Two vessels hold milk and water in the ratios 3 : 1 and 5 : 3. Equal quantities are mixed. Milk fractions 3/4 and 5/8 average to 11/16, so milk : water = 11 : 5.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. Sugar at ₹40/kg and ₹55/kg is mixed to make ₹50/kg. Ratio?

Answer: 1 : 2 (5 : 10).

Q2. 50 litres of milk: 5 litres replaced by water, done twice. Milk left?

Answer: 40.5 litres (50 × 0.9²).

Q3. Juice at ₹60 and ₹90 per litre mixed 2 : 1. Price of the mixture?

Answer: ₹70 per litre.

Q4. How much water to add to 12 litres of 75% juice to make it 60% juice?

Answer: 3 litres (9 litres juice → 15 litres total).

Q5. Selling at cost price after adding water gives a 25% gain. Water : milk?

Answer: 1 : 4.

6. Traps to avoid

  1. The ratio gives quantities, not prices — don't invert it.
  2. In replacement, the factor is raised to the number of repetitions, and uses the volume left, not the volume removed.
  3. Water has a price (or milk percentage) of zero — use it as the cheaper item.
  4. Keep units the same (litres or kg) throughout.
