Economic SurveyKey highlights

Survey at a glance

  • Tabled – Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman placed the Economic Survey 2025-26 in Parliament on 29 January 2026.
  • Prepared by – the Economic Division of the Department of Economic Affairs, Ministry of Finance, led by Chief Economic Adviser V. Anantha Nageswaran.
  • Size – one volume of about 739 pages in 16 chapters, with a short chart-based 'Highlights' booklet.
  • Budget link – it came just ahead of the Union Budget 2026-27, presented on 1 February 2026 (a Sunday).
  • Big idea – a 'disciplined Swadeshi' that takes India from strategic resilience to strategic indispensability in world supply chains.
Economic Survey: the yearly report card of the Indian economy; first presented for 1950-51; released separately from the Budget since 1964; drafted under the CEA in the Finance Ministry.

Growth numbers

  • FY26 GDP – real growth put at 7.4% by the First Advance Estimates.
  • FY26 GVA – real Gross Value Added to rise 7.3%.
  • FY27 outlook – real GDP growth projected at 6.8% to 7.2%.
  • Potential growth – raised to about 7%, helped by reforms, capex and productivity.
  • Rank – India stayed the fastest-growing major economy for a fourth straight year; nominal GDP for FY26 near ₹357 lakh crore.
GDP and GVA: GVA is the value of output minus inputs used; GDP = GVA + product taxes − product subsidies; both are estimated by the National Statistics Office (NSO) under MoSPI.

Who is driving demand

  • Consumption – Private Final Consumption Expenditure (PFCE) grew 7.0% in FY26.
  • Consumption share – PFCE reached 61.5% of GDP, the highest since 2012 (FY23 had the same share).
  • Investment – Gross Fixed Capital Formation grew 7.8% and held at about 30% of GDP.
  • Reasons – low inflation, steady jobs, higher real incomes, public capex and a pick-up in private investment.

Sector-wise growth in FY26

  • Agriculture – estimated to grow about 3.1%.
  • Industry – 6.2%, up from 5.9% in FY25; 7.0% in the first half of FY26.
  • Services – about 9.1%, up from 7.2% in FY25.
  • Manufacturing – GVA rose 7.72% in Q1 and 9.13% in Q2 of FY26.
  • Services share – a record 56.4% of GVA in the FY26 advance estimates.

Fiscal deficit on a downward path

  • Centre's fiscal deficit – 4.8% of GDP in FY25; budgeted at 4.4% for FY26.
  • Revenue deficit – budgeted at 0.8% of GDP for FY26, the lowest since FY09.
  • Debt – general government debt-to-GDP fell about 7.1 percentage points since 2020.
  • States – their combined fiscal deficit rose to 3.2% of GDP in FY25 from about 2.8% earlier.
  • Capex – the Centre's effective capital spending touched 4% of GDP in FY25, against 2.7% before Covid.
Fiscal deficit: total expenditure minus revenue receipts and non-debt capital receipts, i.e. what the government must borrow; targets are set under the FRBM Act, 2003.
SASCI: Special Assistance to States for Capital Investment – 50-year interest-free loans from the Centre to states for capital works, begun in 2020-21; it helped states hold capex near 2.4% of GDP in FY25.

Tax base widens

  • Revenue receipts – the Centre's receipts rose to 9.2% of GDP in FY25 from an average of about 8.5% in FY16–FY20.
  • Income tax returns – up from 6.9 crore in FY22 to 9.2 crore in FY25.
  • Non-corporate taxes – mainly personal income tax; rose from about 2.4% to about 3.3% of GDP after the pandemic.
  • Direct tax share – now over 58% of total tax revenue, against about 52% before Covid.
  • Why – tech-based tax administration, better compliance and rising incomes.

GST 2.0

  • Decision – cleared by the 56th GST Council meeting on 3 September 2025, chaired by the Finance Minister.
  • Start – new rates applied from 22 September 2025.
  • Two main rates – 5% (merit) and 18% (standard); the 12% and 28% slabs were scrapped.
  • Special rate – 40% on sin and luxury goods such as tobacco and high-end cars.
  • Aim – fix inverted duty structures, lift consumption and help small firms join the formal system.
GST Council: constitutional body under Article 279A (101st Amendment, 2016), chaired by the Union Finance Minister; GST began on 1 July 2017.

Three rating upgrades in 2025

  • Morningstar DBRS – May 2025, from BBB (low) to BBB.
  • S&P Global – 14 August 2025, from BBB- to BBB; its first upgrade of India in 18 years (last in 2007).
  • R&I, Japan – September 2025, from BBB to BBB+ with a stable outlook.
  • Reason – the Survey links the upgrades to steady fiscal consolidation and better quality of spending.

RBI eases policy

  • Repo rate – cut by a total of 125 basis points since February 2025 to 5.25% (December 2025).
  • CRR – lowered by 100 bps to 3.0% in stages between September and November 2025.
  • Liquidity – bond buying through OMOs and a $5 billion three-year USD/INR buy-sell swap in December 2025.
  • Operating target – the Weighted Average Call Rate (WACR) stayed slightly below the repo rate in FY26, showing smooth transmission.
  • Broad money (M3) – grew 12.1% in December 2025 against 9% a year before.
Monetary Policy Committee: six members chaired by the RBI Governor; set up in 2016 under the amended RBI Act, 1934; it aims at 4% CPI inflation with a band of 2% to 6%.

Banks in their best health in decades

  • GNPA – gross bad loans of scheduled commercial banks fell to 2.2% in September 2025, a multi-decade low.
  • NNPA – net bad loans at a record low of 0.5%.
  • CRAR – capital adequacy stood strong at 17.2% (September 2025).
  • Credit growth – bank credit grew 14.5% as of 31 December 2025, against 11.2% a year earlier.
  • Small firms – loans to micro and small enterprises jumped 24.6% in November 2025.
NPA: a loan becomes non-performing when interest or principal stays unpaid for over 90 days; GNPA is the gross figure, NNPA is after provisions.

Inflation at a record low

  • Headline CPI – averaged 1.7% in April–December 2025, the lowest since the CPI series began.
  • Lowest month – 0.3% in October 2025, the lowest reading of the series.
  • Main driver – food and fuel, which make up 52.7% of the CPI basket; vegetable and pulse prices fell.
  • Global view – India's drop of about 1.8 percentage points in 2025 was among the sharpest of major emerging economies.
  • RBI forecast – FY26 inflation estimate cut from 2.6% to 2.0% in December 2025.

Why prices stayed soft

  • Weather – a good monsoon and mild temperatures gave strong harvests.
  • Stocks – comfortable foodgrain stocks with the government.
  • Tax cuts – lower GST rates were passed on to buyers.
  • Core inflation – kept up mainly by costly gold and silver; global metal prices stayed moderate.
CPI: Consumer Price Index released every month by the NSO (MoSPI); the RBI's inflation target is based on CPI (Combined).

Money reaching more people

  • Jan Dhan – 55.02 crore accounts by March 2025, 36.63 crore of them in rural and semi-urban areas.
  • FI-Index – RBI's Financial Inclusion Index rose from 64.2 (March 2024) to 67.0 (March 2025).
  • Investors – unique investors crossed 12 crore in September 2025, nearly a quarter of them women; demat accounts above 21.6 crore.
  • Mutual funds – 5.9 crore unique investors at the end of December 2025.
  • FSAP 2025 – IMF and World Bank review found financial sector assets near 187% of GDP (2024).
PM Jan Dhan Yojana: launched on 28 August 2014; zero-balance bank accounts with a RuPay debit card and accident cover; run by the Department of Financial Services.

Micro-credit schemes

  • Mudra loans – over ₹36.18 lakh crore given across 55.45 crore loan accounts by October 2025.
  • Women – about two-thirds of all Mudra loans have gone to women.
  • Mudra slabs – Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh).
  • PM SVANidhi – recast in August 2025 and extended to 31 March 2030; loans of ₹15,000, ₹25,000 and ₹50,000 in three rounds.
  • Stand-Up India – loans of ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore for SC, ST and women entrepreneurs setting up new units.
PM Mudra Yojana: launched on 8 April 2015; collateral-free loans for non-farm micro and small units through banks, NBFCs and MFIs; MUDRA Ltd works under SIDBI.
PM SVANidhi: launched on 1 June 2020 by the Housing and Urban Affairs Ministry for street vendors; outlay ₹7,332 crore up to 2030; good repayers get a UPI-linked RuPay credit card.

New insurance law

  • Act – Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025, passed in December 2025; President's assent on 20 December.
  • FDI – foreign investment limit in Indian insurers raised from 74% to 100%.
  • Laws amended – Insurance Act 1938, LIC Act 1956 and IRDA Act 1999.
  • Penalty – maximum fine raised from ₹1 crore to ₹10 crore; stronger protection for policyholders.
  • In force – most provisions applied from 5 February 2026.
IRDAI: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India, set up under the IRDA Act, 1999; headquarters Hyderabad.

One code for the share market

  • Bill – Securities Markets Code Bill, 2025 introduced in the Lok Sabha on 18 December 2025.
  • Merger – it would replace the SCRA 1956, the SEBI Act 1992 and the Depositories Act 1996 with one law.
  • Next step – sent to the Standing Committee on Finance for study.
  • SEBI moves – investor tools 'SEBI Check' and 'SEBI vs Scam', an ESG debt framework and electricity derivatives.
SEBI: Securities and Exchange Board of India; formed in 1988 and made statutory in 1992; headquarters Mumbai.

Exports and safety buffers

  • Total exports – a record $825.3 billion in FY25, up 6.1%.
  • Services exports – an all-time high of nearly $388 billion in FY25, up 13.6%.
  • Remittances – $135.4 billion in FY25; India remains the world's largest receiver.
  • Forex reserves – $701.4 billion on 16 January 2026, enough for about 11 months of imports and over 94% of external debt.
  • Current account deficit – about 0.8% of GDP in the first half of FY26, against 1.3% a year earlier.

India's global trade footprint

  • Goods – share of world merchandise exports rose from 1% (2005) to 1.8% (2024).
  • Services – share rose from 2% to 4.3%; India is the seventh-largest services exporter.
  • UNCTAD – its Trade and Development Report 2025 placed India third in the Global South for variety of trade partners, after China and the UAE.
  • FDI – gross inflows of $64.7 billion in April–November 2025.
  • Digital investment – India was the top destination for greenfield digital investment in 2020–24 (about $114 billion).

Trade deals signed or sealed

  • UK – Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA) signed on 24 July 2025.
  • Oman – CEPA signed in Muscat on 18 December 2025.
  • New Zealand – FTA talks concluded on 22 December 2025, in about nine months.
  • European Union – FTA concluded on 27 January 2026, called the 'mother of all deals'.
  • EFTA – the TEPA, signed in March 2024, came into force on 1 October 2025; earlier pacts were with Mauritius (2021), UAE (2022) and Australia (2022).
EFTA: European Free Trade Association of Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein; formed in 1960; secretariat in Geneva.

Record farm output

  • Foodgrains – a record 357.73 million tonnes in 2024-25, up 25.43 million tonnes.
  • Horticulture – 362.08 million tonnes, more than foodgrains; it forms about one-third of farm GVA.
  • Livestock – its GVA rose nearly 195% between FY15 and FY24.
  • Fisheries – fish output up over 140% in 2014–2024.
  • Farm GVA – grew 3.6% in H1 FY26 against 2.7% a year before.

Support to farmers

  • PM-KISAN – over ₹4.09 lakh crore paid in 21 instalments.
  • PM-KMY – 24.92 lakh farmers enrolled in the pension scheme by 31 December 2025.
  • e-NAM – 1,522 mandis, 1.79 crore farmers and 4,698 FPOs across 23 states and 4 UTs.
  • Other help – crop insurance (PMFBY), Kisan Credit Cards, irrigation under PMKSY, Soil Health Cards and the Digital Agriculture Mission.
  • Survey advice – link fertiliser use to soil needs, balance ethanol with food security, and pay per acre to shift crops.
PM-KISAN: launched on 24 February 2019 at Gorakhpur; ₹6,000 a year in three equal instalments by DBT to landholding farmers; Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare.
PM Kisan Maandhan Yojana: started in 2019 for small and marginal farmers aged 18–40; gives ₹3,000 a month pension after age 60.

Services as the growth engine

  • GDP share – services made up 53.6% of GDP in H1 FY26.
  • FDI magnet – services drew about 80% of FDI inflows in FY23–FY25.
  • Jobs – services give about 30% of all jobs and about 62% of urban jobs.
  • Export mix – software and professional consulting lead the rise in services exports.
  • GCCs – India hosts over 1,700 Global Capability Centres of foreign firms.

Industry and innovation

  • Global Innovation Index – India rose to 38th in 2025 from 66th in 2019, top among lower-middle-income nations.
  • Startups – DPIIT-recognised startups grew from about 500 in 2016 to over 2 lakh in 2025.
  • Tech share – medium- and high-tech industries now give 46.3% of manufacturing value added.
  • PLI – across 14 sectors, over ₹2 lakh crore invested, ₹18.7 lakh crore of extra output and 12.6 lakh jobs (September 2025).
  • Entrepreneurship – WIPO ranks India 12th for entrepreneurship policy and culture.
Global Innovation Index: yearly ranking by the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO), Geneva; Switzerland topped GII 2025.

Electronics and chips

  • Mobiles – production value up nearly 30 times, from ₹18,000 crore (FY15) to ₹5.45 lakh crore (FY25).
  • Position – India is now the second-largest maker of mobile phones.
  • Electronics exports – moved from 7th to 3rd-largest export item (FY22 to FY25); $22.2 billion in H1 FY26.
  • PLI electronics – output of about ₹9.34 lakh crore and exports of ₹5.12 lakh crore up to September 2025.
  • Semiconductors – 10 projects in 6 states worth about ₹1.60 lakh crore approved by August 2025.
India Semiconductor Mission: launched in December 2021 with ₹76,000 crore to build chip fabs, packaging and display units; works under MeitY.

Roads, rail and air

  • Highways – national highway length up about 60%, from 91,287 km (FY14) to 1,46,572 km (December 2025).
  • High-speed corridors – from 550 km to 5,364 km, nearly ten times.
  • Railways – 99.1% of the network electrified by October 2025; 69,439 route km in March 2025.
  • Airports – from 74 in 2014 to 164 in 2025; India is the third-largest domestic aviation market.
  • Capex – Centre's capital outlay grew from ₹2.63 lakh crore (FY18) to ₹11.21 lakh crore (FY26 BE).

Power sector turnaround

  • Capacity – installed power capacity of 509.74 GW in November 2025, up 11.6%.
  • Green share – renewables about half of capacity; India is third in the world in renewable and solar capacity.
  • DISCOMs – first-ever profit after tax of ₹2,701 crore in FY25, against a loss of ₹67,962 crore in FY14.
  • AT&C losses – down from 22.62% (FY14) to 15.04% (FY25).
  • Shortage – the power demand-supply gap fell from 4.2% in FY14 to nil.

Phones, water and space

  • Telecom – tele-density 86.76%; 5G reaches 99.9% of districts.
  • Internet – users have crossed about 95 crore.
  • Tap water – over 81% of rural homes have tap connections under Jal Jeevan Mission (October 2025).
  • SpaDeX – India became the fourth nation to dock satellites autonomously.
Jal Jeevan Mission: launched on 15 August 2019; aims at a working tap connection for every rural home ('Har Ghar Jal'); Ministry of Jal Shakti.

Clean energy push

  • Renewables – capacity rose from 76.38 GW (2014) to 253.96 GW (November 2025).
  • This year – 38.61 GW added in April–December 2025, led by solar (30.16 GW).
  • Nuclear – 8,780 MW today; target 100 GW by 2047.
  • Nuclear Energy Mission – ₹20,000 crore (Budget 2025-26) for at least five home-grown Small Modular Reactors by 2033.
  • Storage – need of about 411 GWh by 2031-32; a ₹18,100 crore PLI backs 50 GWh of battery-cell making.
SHANTI Act, 2025: Sustainable Harnessing and Advancement of Nuclear Energy for Transforming India; passed on 18 December 2025, assent on 20 December; replaces the Atomic Energy Act, 1962 and the Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act, 2010; allows licensed private players in nuclear power.

Greener rules and green money

  • Audits – Environment Audit Rules, 2025 bring in certified third-party checks.
  • Decriminalising – small offences under green laws such as the Air Act and Environment (Protection) Act moved to penalties via Jan Vishwas reforms.
  • BRSR – SEBI's sustainability reporting is compulsory for the top 1,000 listed firms since FY23.
  • Green finance – Sovereign Green Bonds framework (2022) and RBI's green deposits framework (2023).
  • Waste – circular economy plans for 10 waste types, Extended Producer Responsibility and the single-use plastic ban.

Schools and colleges

  • School scale – 24.69 crore students, 14.71 lakh schools and over 1.01 crore teachers (UDISE+ 2024-25).
  • GER – primary 90.9, upper primary 90.3, secondary 78.7 and higher secondary 58.4.
  • Higher education – institutions rose from 51,534 (2014-15) to 70,018 (June 2025).
  • Top institutes – 23 IITs, 21 IIMs and 20 AIIMS; IIT campuses abroad at Zanzibar and Abu Dhabi.
  • NEP goals – 100% school GER by 2030 and 50% higher-education GER by 2035.
NEP 2020: replaced the 1986 policy; brought the 5+3+3+4 school structure and the APAAR ID ('One Nation, One Student ID').

Learning gains

  • PARAKH survey – the 2024 Rashtriya Sarvekshan tested over 21 lakh students in about 74,000 schools.
  • Maths – Grade III proficiency rose from 42% (2021) to 65% (2024).
  • Language – Grade III proficiency rose from 39% to 57%.
  • Credits – Academic Bank of Credit covers 2,660 institutions; 2.2 crore APAAR IDs carry credits.
PARAKH: Performance Assessment, Review and Analysis of Knowledge for Holistic Development – national assessment centre under NCERT; its survey replaced the National Achievement Survey.

Health gains

  • IMR – infant deaths fell from 40 to 25 per 1,000 live births (2013 to 2023), a drop of over 37%.
  • MMR – maternal deaths down 86% since 1990, against 48% worldwide.
  • Child deaths – under-five mortality down 78% and newborn mortality down 70% (1990–2023).
  • Life span – life expectancy is now over 70 years.
  • Primary care – 1.82 lakh Ayushman Arogya Mandirs with over 500 crore visits; 10.98 crore hospital admissions under AB-PMJAY.

New health worries

  • Lifestyle – obesity, diabetes and digital addiction flagged as rising threats.
  • Tele-MANAS – free 24x7 mental health helpline 14416; its app came in 2024 and calls crossed 32 lakh.
  • SHUT clinic – at NIMHANS, Bengaluru, treats unhealthy use of technology.
  • Laws – Online Gaming (Regulation) Act, 2025 and the Pragyatah guidelines on screen time.
  • Anti-obesity – call to cut cooking oil use by 10%, with Fit India and Eat Right campaigns.
Tele-MANAS: Tele Mental Health Assistance and Networking Across States; started by the Health Ministry on 10 October 2022 (World Mental Health Day).

Jobs picture

  • Employment – 56.2 crore people aged 15+ employed in Q2 FY26, 8.7 lakh more than in Q1.
  • December 2025 – LFPR 56.1%, worker population ratio 53.4% and unemployment rate 4.8%.
  • Women – female LFPR rose from 23.3% (2017-18) to 41.7% (2023-24); their jobless rate fell from 5.6% to 3.2%.
  • e-Shram – over 31 crore unorganised workers registered; more than 54% are women.
  • Gig work – gig workers grew from 77 lakh (FY21) to about 1.2 crore (FY25).
PLFS: Periodic Labour Force Survey run by the NSO (MoSPI) since 2017; gives LFPR, WPR and unemployment rate.

Labour codes in force

  • Date – all four codes came into force on 21 November 2025, replacing 29 central labour laws.
  • The four – Code on Wages 2019, Industrial Relations Code 2020, Code on Social Security 2020 and OSH & Working Conditions Code 2020.
  • Wages – the Centre will fix a national floor wage; appointment letters are compulsory.
  • Gig workers – gig work, platform work and aggregators defined in law for the first time.
  • Impact – inspectors turn into 'Inspector-cum-Facilitators'; an SBI study sees about 77 lakh new jobs.

Skilling push

  • ITI upgrade – 1,000 government ITIs to be modernised on a hub-and-spoke model (200 hubs, 800 spokes).
  • PMKVY 4.0 – training in NSQF-aligned job roles with industry-led sector skill councils.
  • Apprentices – NAPS 2.0 pays 25% of the stipend straight to apprentices by DBT; NATS 2.0 has made over 12 lakh DBT payments.
  • Finance – a wider Model Skill Loan Scheme, skill vouchers and NSDC's Skill Impact Bond.
  • Job portal – National Career Service has about 5.9 crore job seekers and 8 crore vacancies posted.
PMKVY: Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana, launched in 2015 by the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship and run through the NSDC.

Poverty and rural progress

  • New poverty line – the World Bank raised its global line from $2.15 to $3.00 a day (2021 prices).
  • India – extreme poverty at 5.3% in 2022-23; 23.9% under the lower-middle-income line.
  • Social spending – 7.9% of GDP in FY26 (BE), against 7% in FY24.
  • SVAMITVA – drone surveys done in 3.28 lakh villages; 2.76 crore property cards ready (December 2025).
  • Drone Didis – 1,094 drones given to women SHGs in 2023-24, 500 of them under Namo Drone Didi.
SVAMITVA: launched on 24 April 2020 (National Panchayati Raj Day) by the Ministry of Panchayati Raj; maps village homesteads by drone and gives owners property cards.
Namo Drone Didi: central scheme approved in 2023 to give drones to women self-help groups so they can rent out farm services such as spraying.

Village governance and livelihoods

  • eGramSwaraj – single online platform for panchayat plans, work and accounts.
  • Apps – Meri Panchayat for citizens to track work; Samarth for collecting panchayat taxes.
  • DDU-GKY – placement-linked skilling for poor rural youth.
  • RSETIs – Rural Self-Employment Training Institutes run by banks in almost every district.
  • DAY-NRLM – supports self-employment through women's self-help groups.
DAY-NRLM: Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana – National Rural Livelihoods Mission; launched in 2011 as 'Aajeevika'; Ministry of Rural Development.

AI path for India

  • Focus – useful, sector-specific AI (health, farming, education) rather than racing for the biggest models.
  • Compute – smaller models and spread-out computing suit Indian needs and costs.
  • Data – home-grown data from about 1 billion connected people is treated as a strategic asset.
  • Rules – reward firms that train models locally or fund local R&D with lighter checks and faster approvals.
  • Workforce – earn-while-you-learn routes and hands-on trades that AI cannot easily replace.

Making cities work

  • Building space – permitted floor space (FSI) in Indian cities is far lower than in Singapore or New York, pushing sprawl.
  • Traffic – in 2024 rush hours cost commuters 117 hours in Bengaluru, 103 in Mumbai and 76 in New Delhi.
  • Fixes – more digitised e-buses, transit-oriented development and congestion pricing.
  • Waste – door-to-door collection now in 98% of urban wards; waste processed up from 16% to 80% since 2014-15.
  • Model city – Indore cited for changing public habits on cleanliness.

Swadeshi as a strategy

  • Three tiers – act first on critical weak spots, then on doable high-payoff skills, and skip costly low-priority substitution.
  • Input costs – a National Input Cost Reduction Strategy that treats cheap, reliable inputs like infrastructure.
  • Path – Swadeshi → strategic resilience → strategic indispensability.
  • Base – deregulation and stronger state capacity.
