What You Will Practice in the Number-Pair & Triad Classification Chapter

Pairs of numbers

  • Fixed difference or fixed sum between the two numbers
  • Fixed multiple or ratio — the second is 3 times the first
  • Squares and cubes — the second is the square or cube of the first
  • Both numbers prime, or both multiples of the same number

Digit-based pairs

  • One number is the reverse of the other
  • The second is the digit sum or digit product of the first

Triads and two-step rules

  • Third number = first × second, first + second or first − second
  • Numbers in equal steps, or the middle number halfway between the others
  • Two-step rules — square plus one, cube minus the number, double then add

Test one relationship at a time on all four pairs or triads — the odd one is the group that fails the test the other three pass.

The three mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Reading the pair as two separate numbers. The rule is in the relationship between them — difference, ratio or operation — not in either number alone.
  • Using the same rule on the digits. When a note says operations are on whole numbers, do not add or multiply the digits separately.
  • Stopping at the first rule that fits three. A rival rule can fit a different three; check that yours leaves exactly one out.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Number-Pair & Triad Classification Questions

Q1. Choose the odd pair: 5 : 25, 6 : 36, 7 : 49, 8 : 56.
5 : 25, 6 : 36 and 7 : 49 are a number and its square; 8 : 56 is 8 × 7. Answer: 8 : 56
Q2. Choose the odd pair: 14 : 22, 31 : 39, 45 : 53, 27 : 37.
The second number is 8 more than the first in three pairs; in 27 : 37 it is 10 more. Answer: 27 : 37
Q3. Choose the odd triad: (2, 8, 32), (3, 12, 48), (5, 20, 80), (4, 12, 40).
In the first three each number is 4 times the one before; (4, 12, 40) goes × 3 and then × 3.33. Answer: (4, 12, 40)
Q4. Choose the odd triad: (3, 4, 12), (5, 6, 30), (7, 2, 14), (4, 9, 32).
Third = first × second in three triads; 4 × 9 = 36, not 32. Answer: (4, 9, 32)

How to Prepare Number-Pair & Triad Classification for SSC CGL

  1. Test one relationship at a time. Difference, sum, ratio, square, cube and reverse cover most pairs.
  2. Write the rule as a sentence. “Second is the square of the first” is a rule; “both are two-digit” is not.
  3. For triads, test the third number first. It is usually the first two combined by one operation.
  4. Watch the whole-number note. Operate on the number itself unless the question is clearly digit-based.
  5. Look for the quieter rule at Level 2. When all four share a gap or a parity, compare the squares, cubes and common factors.
  6. Do Level 1 untimed first, then with the 15-minute clock. Move to Level 2 once Level 1 is steady, and then check yourself on a previous year paper.

SSC CGL Number-Pair & Triad Classification Questions — Frequently Asked Questions

How many classification questions are asked in SSC CGL?
Classification (odd one out) usually gives two to three questions in every Tier I shift and a few more in Tier II Paper I, split across words, letters, numbers, number pairs and word pairs.
What types of number-pair classification come in SSC CGL?
Pairs with a fixed difference or sum, a fixed multiple or ratio, squares and cubes, reversed digits, and digit sums or products, plus triads where the third number comes from the first two.
How do I solve odd number-pair questions quickly?
Test one relationship at a time — difference, sum, ratio, square, cube, reverse — on all four pairs. The pair that fails the test the other three pass is the answer.
What does the whole-number note mean?
Some questions say the operation must be done on the whole number, not on its digits. Then 15 : 45 is a multiple of 3, not a rule applied to 1 and 5 separately.
How many questions are in each classification practice test?
Twenty-five questions per test, with an optional 15-minute timer and the real exam marking scheme of +2 for a correct answer, −0.5 for a wrong one and 0 for an unattempted question.
Are the classification tests available in Hindi?

What is the difference between Level 1 and Level 2?
Level 1 is set at real SSC CGL difficulty — one clear relationship per question. Level 2 is harder than the exam: all four pairs share the obvious relationship and a quieter rule decides, and wrong options are built from rules that almost fit.
