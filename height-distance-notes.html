Every question here is a right-angled triangle. Draw it, mark the angle, and use tan θ = height ÷ distance. Standard angles give clean numbers.

1. Concepts in brief

The picture behind every question

An observer, an object (a tower, a pole, a kite) and the ground form a right-angled triangle. The line from the eye to the top is the line of sight. The angle it makes with the horizontal is the angle of elevation (looking up) or depression (looking down).

Which ratio to use

The height is the side opposite the angle and the ground distance is the side next to it, so tan links them directly. Use sin or cos only when the question involves the slanted length — a ladder, a wire, a string.

Two observations

When the angle is measured from two points on the same line, write the height in terms of each distance, then use the gap between the points. The standard-angle results in the table save the algebra.

2. Key facts and formulas

Basictan θ = height / distance
30° trianglesides 1 : √3 : 2 (opposite : adjacent : hypotenuse)
45° trianglesides 1 : 1 : √2
60° trianglesides √3 : 1 : 2
Two angles from two points, distance d between themh = d / (cot α − cot β) for β nearer the object

3. Shortcuts that save time

Standard-angle ratios At 45°, height = distance. At 30°, distance = height × √3. At 60°, height = distance × √3.
  • If a pole's elevation changes from 30° to 60° after walking d toward it, its height is d√3/2.
  • If the shadow is as long as the height, the angle of elevation of the sun is 45°.
  • Always draw the figure and mark the right angle at the base.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 A tower's top is seen at 60° from 20 m away. Height = 20 × tan 60° = 20√3 ≈ 34.6 m.
Example 2 Elevation of a tower from a point is 30°; after walking 100 m closer it becomes 60°. Let height h: h cot 30° − h cot 60° = 100 → h(√3 − 1/√3) = 100 → h × 2/√3 = 100 → h = 50√3 m.
Example 3 A 10 m ladder leans against a wall at 60° to the ground. It reaches 10 sin 60° = 5√3 m up the wall.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 4 A pole's shadow is √3 times its height. tan θ = 1/√3, so the sun's elevation is 30°.
Example 5 From the top of a 60 m building, the angle of depression of a car is 30°. Distance of the car = 60 cot 30° = 60√3 m.
Example 6 A pole breaks; the top touches the ground 10 m from the foot, making 30° with the ground. Standing part = 10 tan 30° = 10/√3; broken part = 10/cos 30° = 20/√3. Original height = 30/√3 = 10√3 m.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. Elevation 45°, distance 50 m. Height?

Answer: 50 m.

Q2. A 20 m ladder makes 60° with the ground. How far is its foot from the wall?

Answer: 10 m (20 cos 60°).

Q3. A tower is 100√3 m high. From a point 100 m away, its elevation?

Answer: 60° (tan θ = √3).

Q4. Elevation rises from 45° to 60° on walking 20 m closer. Height?

Answer: 30 + 10√3 ≈ 47.3 m.

Q5. From the top of a 75 m lighthouse the depression of a ship is 45°. Distance?

Answer: 75 m.

6. Traps to avoid

  1. Measure angles from the horizontal, not from the vertical.
  2. Depression from the top equals elevation from the bottom — draw it to see why.
  3. Don't use sin or cos when the question gives distance and asks height — tan is the direct link.
  4. Add the height of the observer when the angle is measured from his eye level.
