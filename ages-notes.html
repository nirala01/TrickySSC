Problems on Ages

Concepts, formulas, solved examples and practice · Quantitative Aptitude

Age questions are small equation puzzles. Two facts do all the work: the difference between two people's ages never changes, and everyone ages by the same number of years.

1. Concepts in brief

Three time points

Every age question talks about at most three moments: now, some years ago and some years later. Write each person's age at each moment in terms of one unknown — usually the present age — and the conditions become simple equations.

The age gap never changes

If A is 6 years older than B today, he is 6 years older at every moment of their lives. When a ratio question feels stuck, equate the difference in parts between the two ratios.

Groups and averages

When every member of a group grows t years older, the total grows by t × (number of members) and the average grows by t. A member born later adds nothing to the total until he exists.

3. Shortcuts that save time

Use the constant difference If the ratio changes from a : b to c : d after t years, the age gap stays the same, so: (a − b)k = (c − d)m where k and m are the multipliers now and later. Match the gap in both ratios, then read off the ages.
  • Never write the ratio as the age — write the ages as ratio × k.
  • Test answer options: add t to a likely option and see if the stated condition holds.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 Father is 3 times as old as son. After 12 years he will be twice as old. Let son = x, father = 3x. 3x + 12 = 2(x + 12) → x = 12, father = 36.
Example 2 Present ages of A and B are in the ratio 4 : 5. Five years ago their ages were in the ratio 7 : 9. Ages 4k, 5k: 9(4k − 5) = 7(5k − 5) → 36k − 45 = 35k − 35 → k = 10. A = 40, B = 50.
Example 3 Average age of a family of 5 is 24. After 3 years, a baby is born; average age of the six then? Sum now = 120; after 3 years = 120 + 15 = 135; baby is 0. Average = 22.5.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 4 The sum of the ages of a mother and daughter is 50. Five years ago the mother was 7 times as old as the daughter. m + d = 50 and m − 5 = 7(d − 5) → d = 10, m = 40.
Example 5 A : B is 3 : 5 now and 5 : 7 after 10 years. The gap is 2 parts in both ratios, so 2 parts = 10 years and 1 part = 5. Ages are 15 and 25.
Example 6 Ten years ago A was half as old as B. Their present ages are in the ratio 3 : 4. Ages 3x and 4x: 2(3x − 10) = 4x − 10 → x = 5. Ages 15 and 20; sum 35.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. A is 5 years older than B. In 10 years the sum of their ages will be 45. A's present age?

Answer: 15 (A + B = 25, A − B = 5).

Q2. A father is 30 years older than his son. In 5 years he will be 3 times the son's age. Son's age?

Answer: 10.

Q3. Ages are 4 : 3 now and 11 : 9 after 6 years. Present ages?

Answer: 16 and 12.

Q4. The average age of 4 people is 20. Their average after 4 years?

Answer: 24.

Q5. Ages are 2 : 5 now and were 1 : 3 four years ago. Present ages?

Answer: 16 and 40.

6. Traps to avoid

  1. "Five years ago" means subtract 5 from every person in the question.
  2. A newborn contributes 0 at the time of birth and grows by the same years as everyone else.
  3. Don't forget that "X years later" the ratio changes — you cannot reuse the old ratio.
  4. Check the answer against both conditions in the problem before marking.
