What You Will Practice in the Series Chapter

Number series

  • Constant gaps, gaps that rise or fall by a fixed step, gaps that double or triple
  • Gaps that are squares, cubes or primes; gaps of gaps
  • Multiply by a constant or a growing number; multiply then add (×a + b, ×n + n)
  • Squares and cubes of a running number, n(n + 1), n³ + n²
  • Two or three interleaved series, alternating operations, sum of previous terms
  • Wrong-term questions, two missing terms, decimals, fractions and negative terms

Letter series

  • Single letters with constant, growing or alternating skips, and wrap-around after Z
  • Two-, three- and four-letter clusters, each position on its own step
  • Opposite letters (A–Z, B–Y), reversed groups and groups of changing length
  • Continuous letter strings with two question marks, and word or number-word series

Letter-repeat and alphanumeric series

  • Fill the blanks — fixed, rotating, growing, mirror and reversing units
  • Capital-letter blocks with one moving letter, and digit strings
  • Letter–number terms — a letter with its position, its square or its reverse position
  • Root and subscript expressions, and numbers and letters taking turns

Turn letters into numbers first. Once a letter series is written as positions, it is just another number series.

The three mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Trusting the last two terms. 2, 4, 8 can continue as 16 (×2) or 14 (gaps 2, 4, 6); test the rule on every term.
  • Ignoring alternate places. When the series zig-zags, split odd and even places before looking for a rule.
  • Counting letters on fingers. One slip in a position value gives exactly the option that is placed next to the answer.

Solved Examples — SSC CGL Series Questions

Q1. Find the next term: 4, 9, 19, 39, 79, ?
Each term is the previous one × 2 + 1: 4 → 9 → 19 → 39 → 79 → 159. Answer: 159
Q2. Find the next letter: B, E, I, N, ?
B(2) → E(5) → I(9) → N(14): the gaps are 3, 4, 5, so the next gap is 6 and 14 + 6 = 20 = T. Answer: T
Q3. Fill the blanks in order: a_ca_cab_ab_
Cut into blocks of three: a_c | a_c | ab_ | ab_. The unit is abc, so the blanks are b, b, c, c. Answer: bbcc
Q4. Find the next term: A2, C6, E10, ?
The letters move +2 (A, C, E, G) and each number is twice the letter’s position: A = 1 → 2, C = 3 → 6, E = 5 → 10, G = 7 → 14. Answer: G14

How to Prepare Series for SSC CGL

  1. Write the gaps under the series. Most number series show their rule in the first or second row of gaps.
  2. Know squares to 25 and cubes to 15. Gaps such as 25, 36, 49 or 27, 64, 125 should be recognised at a glance.
  3. Split long or zig-zag series into odd and even places. Two simple runs are easier than one strange one.
  4. Convert letters to positions. A = 1 to Z = 26, and opposite letters add to 27.
  5. For blanks, cut the string into blocks. Find the unit length from a complete block, then fill by place.
  6. Do Level 1 untimed first, then with the 15-minute clock. Move to Level 2 once Level 1 is steady, and then check yourself on a previous year paper.

SSC CGL Series Questions — Frequently Asked Questions

How many series questions are asked in SSC CGL?
Series is a regular reasoning chapter in SSC CGL — usually two to three questions in every Tier I shift (number series, letter series or a letter-repeat blank) and more in Tier II Paper I.
What types of series come in SSC CGL?
Number series (gaps, squares and cubes, multiply-then-add, interleaved and wrong-term questions), letter and letter-cluster series, letter-repeat series where you fill the blanks, and alphanumeric series that mix letters and numbers.
How do I solve a number series quickly?
Write the gaps first. If the gaps do not settle, take the gaps of the gaps, then test ratios, squares and cubes, and finally split the series into odd and even places.
How do I solve letter-repeat (fill the blank) questions?
Cut the string into blocks of the unit length, read the unit from a complete block, and fill each blank by its place in the block — then check the rotating, growing or reversing patterns.
How many questions are in each series practice test?
Twenty-five questions per test, with an optional 15-minute timer and the real exam marking scheme of +2 for a correct answer, −0.5 for a wrong one and 0 for an unattempted question.
Are the series tests available in Hindi?
Yes. Questions, options, detailed solutions and shortcut tricks are available in Hindi, and you choose English or Hindi before the test starts.
What is the difference between Level 1 and Level 2?
Level 1 is set at real SSC CGL difficulty — one clear rule per series. Level 2 is harder than the exam: rules are stacked two deep, such as gaps of gaps, growing multipliers or three interleaved runs, and wrong options are the slips students actually make.
