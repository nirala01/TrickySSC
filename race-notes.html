Races

A race question is a speed-ratio question in disguise. Convert "A beats B by 20 metres" into how far each runs in the same time, and the ratio of speeds follows.

1. Concepts in brief

Races are speed ratios

Two runners start together and run for the same time until the winner finishes. In that time, the distances they cover are in the ratio of their speeds. So "A beats B by 20 m in a 200 m race" just means A : B = 200 : 180.

Start and dead heat

"A gives B a start of 20 m" means B begins 20 m ahead and runs 20 m less. A dead heat means both finish at the same moment, so the start exactly cancels the speed difference.

Beating by time

"A beats B by 10 seconds" means B needs 10 seconds more than A to run the whole race. If a question gives both a distance and a time, the loser covers the distance gap in that time — which gives his speed directly.

2. Key terms and formulas

Dead heatBoth reach the end together
Race of D metres, A beats B by dA : B (speed) = D : (D − d)
Meeting on a circular track (opposite)Time = Track length ÷ (S₁ + S₂)
Meeting on a circular track (same direction)Time = Track length ÷ (S₁ − S₂)

3. Shortcuts that save time

Beat to ratio In a 100 m race, A beats B by 10 m: when A runs 100, B runs 90. Speed ratio A : B = 10 : 9.
  • A beats B by x, B beats C by y, in the same race length D: A beats C by x + y − xy/D.
  • To find "A beats B by how many seconds", use B's time over the full race minus A's time.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 In a 200 m race A beats B by 20 m. Speed ratio A : B = 200 : 180 = 10 : 9. B's distance when A finishes = 180 m.
Example 2 A and B run at 10 m/s and 8 m/s. For a dead heat in 400 m, B needs a head start: A takes 40 s, B covers 320 m in that time, so head start = 80 m.
Example 3 Two runners start together on a 400 m circular track at 5 m/s and 3 m/s, in the same direction. They meet after 400/(5 − 3) = 200 seconds; in opposite directions after 400/8 = 50 seconds.

More solved examples (exam level)

Example 4 In a 1 km race A beats B by 100 m or 20 seconds. B runs 100 m in 20 s, so his speed is 5 m/s and his time is 200 s. A's time = 180 seconds.
Example 5 In a 100 m race A beats B by 10 m and B beats C by 10 m. A beats C by 10 + 10 − 100/100 = 19 m.
Example 6 A can give B a 20 m start in a 100 m race and still finish level. Speed ratio A : B = 100 : 80 = 5 : 4.

5. Practice questions (with answers)

Tap a question to see its answer.

Q1. In a 500 m race A beats B by 50 m. Speed ratio?

Answer: 10 : 9.

Q2. A runs at 5 m/s and B at 4 m/s in a 200 m race. By how many metres does A win?

Answer: 40 m (B covers 160 m in A's 40 s).

Q3. In 100 m, A beats B by 20 m and B beats C by 25 m. A beats C by?

Answer: 40 m (20 + 25 − 5).

Q4. On a 600 m circular track, runners at 7 m/s and 5 m/s go in opposite directions. When do they first meet?

Answer: 50 seconds (600/12).

Q5. Same runners, same direction. When does the faster first lap the slower?

Answer: 300 seconds (600/2).

6. Traps to avoid

  1. "Beats by 20 m" is a distance gap at the finish — not the difference in speed.
  2. A head start for B means B runs less distance than A.
  3. Don't confuse beats by distance and beats by time.
  4. On a circular track use relative speed with the full circuit as the distance.
