Races

Formulas, tricks and traps on one page · Quantitative Aptitude

A race question is a speed-ratio question in disguise. Convert "A beats B by 20 metres" into how far each runs in the same time, and the ratio of speeds follows.

1. Key terms and formulas

TermMeaning
A beats B by d metresWhen A finishes, B is d metres short of the end
A beats B by t secondsB takes t seconds more than A for the whole race
Head start of d metresB starts d metres ahead of A
Dead heatBoth reach the end together
Race of D metres, A beats B by dA : B (speed) = D : (D − d)
Meeting on a circular track (opposite)Time = Track length ÷ (S₁ + S₂)
Meeting on a circular track (same direction)Time = Track length ÷ (S₁ − S₂)

2. Shortcuts that save time

Beat to ratio In a 100 m race, A beats B by 10 m: when A runs 100, B runs 90. Speed ratio A : B = 10 : 9.
  • A beats B by x, B beats C by y, in the same race length D: A beats C by x + y − xy/D.
  • To find "A beats B by how many seconds", use B's time over the full race minus A's time.

3. Solved examples

Example 1 In a 200 m race A beats B by 20 m. Speed ratio A : B = 200 : 180 = 10 : 9. B's distance when A finishes = 180 m.
Example 2 A and B run at 10 m/s and 8 m/s. For a dead heat in 400 m, B needs a head start: A takes 40 s, B covers 320 m in that time, so head start = 80 m.
Example 3 Two runners start together on a 400 m circular track at 5 m/s and 3 m/s, in the same direction. They meet after 400/(5 − 3) = 200 seconds; in opposite directions after 400/8 = 50 seconds.

4. Traps to avoid

  1. "Beats by 20 m" is a distance gap at the finish — not the difference in speed.
  2. A head start for B means B runs less distance than A.
  3. Don't confuse beats by distance and beats by time.
  4. On a circular track use relative speed with the full circuit as the distance.
